## 1. The OAS submission check — never submit a placeholder

The live run sent `SAY "Confession…"` / `DO "Confession…"` to the author as the reader's words. The
old wait accepted *any* non-empty input; revealed cards and non-empty fields are both insufficient,
because the deck fills the fields with the card's own title while the preview batch is in flight.

The check now requires a **usable** suggestion: not empty, not a bare card title, and not a
one-or-two-word string ending in an ellipsis. If none arrives through the production flow, the run
**stops before the author call** rather than spending a dispatch on a placeholder. The replay below
uses the same predicate and submitted no placeholder in 20 turns.

## 2. The route into the authored interruption — found, not invented

Three triggers exist; which are available depends on the replies:

| trigger | condition | available with the captured replies? |
|---|---|---|
| `llmSaysResolve` | `scene_state === 'resolve' && beat >= 6` | **no** — all ten say `continue` |
| `disengagedThreshold` | `userSignals.disengaged >= 2` | **no** — all ten say neutral/positive |
| `hardCapHit` | `beatCount >= hardCap` (20) | **yes** |

So the hard cap is the route, and it is a production path: no state writes, no direct call to the
resolver, no restored afterglow ramp. The replay's turn budget is raised to 23 and **says so** — a
replay that stops at 9 can never reach a cap of 20.

**There is no missing product trigger.** My earlier "the interruption did not mount on the cap" was
a measurement error: `_resolveIntimacyScene` defers through `setTimeout(1800)` and then mounts the
interruption inside a *further* timer that first sets `state.intimacyDialogue = null`
(`app.js:208023-208043`). I probed before that timer fired — the probe still saw the dialogue
object, which was the tell I missed. With a proper wait, it mounts.

## 4. Repetition — examined, and the obvious explanation is ruled out

From the preserved requests, nine OAS author calls at ~200,000 chars each (~46k tokens).

**The reader's inputs do repeat.** Three of the nine were submitted twice, because previews came
from a stale cache — the same defect behind the `matched-cache` provenance:

```
"I will find out what you know, and you will help me."   req 008 and 020
"I won't say anything until I know what happened."       req 009 and 016
"I feel lost without your guidance in this."             req 011 and 018
```

**But repeated inputs are not the cause of the repeated replies.** Measured:

| | reply overlap |
|---|---|
| the three pairs with **identical** reader input | **0.0%, 0.0%, 0.0%** |
| req 008 vs 009 — **different** inputs, consecutive | **84.0%** |
| req 016 vs 018 — **different** inputs, consecutive | **58.1%** |
| req 018 vs 020 — **different** inputs, consecutive | **29.0%** |

Julian *varied* his answer when asked the same question twice, and echoed himself when the question
changed. **Overlap tracks adjacency, not input identity.** That rules out input duplication, and it
also rules out the placeholder as the explanation — the placeholder was turn 1 only.

**The one mechanism consistent with that pattern, named as a suspect and not a conclusion:** the
previous reply is fed back into every subsequent request — present in **8 of 8** successor prompts.
A model shown its own last line continuing the same interrogation has the shape in front of it. I am
not adding an anti-repetition instruction on the strength of that; the next step would be to vary
what the prior-exchange block carries and measure, which is a separate piece of work.

**Also still true and separate:** the stale preview cache. It caused the duplicate inputs, keeps OAS
provenance UNKNOWN, and is upstream of both.

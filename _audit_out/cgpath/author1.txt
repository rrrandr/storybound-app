SCENE DUTY (HARD — answer "what is THIS scene trying to ACCOMPLISH?"): set visualState.sceneDuty to the scene's PRIMARY obligation, and visualState.attractionPresence to how much romance figures relative to it. Romance is ONE duty among many — NOT the default gravity.     • external_pressure — an external force bearing down (deadline, threat, demand, eviction, summons)
    • investigation — finding or uncovering something
    • survival — meeting a material need (money, shelter, safety, food, work)
    • political_alignment — navigating or choosing a side in a social / factional conflict
    • professional_competence — doing the work, demonstrating skill
    • social_navigation — managing a group or social situation (non-romantic)
    • world_revelation — the world or its rules surfacing
    • romantic_progression — the relationship advancing
    • emotional_repair — mending or processing between two characters
    • interwoven — external duty and relationship genuinely entangled
  For a NON-romance duty, WRITE the scene toward that obligation: the external stakes are the emotional center; the romance is a pressure / a leak / absent (per attractionPresence), NOT the resolution. Do NOT collapse a survival / investigation / political / competence scene into chemistry beats or a relationship-progression close. Reserve romantic_progression / emotional_repair (attractionPresence=foreground) for scenes whose actual job IS the relationship. "interwoven" = the external duty and the relationship are genuinely entangled (the romance lives INSIDE the A-plot pressure).

SOCIAL STAGING MODE (HARD — declare in visualState.social_staging_mode BEFORE writing beats):
- Staging is structural cinematic intent, NOT prose decoration. The renderer reads visualState.social_staging_mode and emits a specific blocking grammar (proximity, eye contact, body angles, environmental shielding). Picking the wrong mode produces dead compositions even when the prose is good.
- This is INDEPENDENT of li_visibility_phase. li_visibility_phase says WHO is on stage; social_staging_mode says HOW their bodies relate. Both axes stack — e.g. LI present + adversarial = enemies/rivals; LI absent + platonic_warm = believable friendship.
- MODES:
  • "solo" — one figure only. Environmental isolation, introspective framing. Required when li_visibility_phase="absent" AND other_characters_present is empty.
  • "platonic_warm" — relaxed spacing, open posture, casual asymmetry, intermittent eye contact, shared environmental focus. The friend who walked through your door a thousand times. Coffee, kitchens, easy banter.
  • "platonic_tense" — physical separation, crossed sightlines, partial body turn-away, table/barrier between figures, visual imbalance. The friend who knows you're lying. The sibling waiting for the truth.
  • "professional" — task-oriented posture, environment-first composition (desk between, screen between), restrained expressions, limited emotional mirroring, formal interpersonal distance. Workplace, deposition, doctor's visit. LI present + professional = workplace tension / restrained attraction.
  • "adversarial" — opposing frame vectors, confrontational stance, compressed tension space, dominance asymmetry, eye-line collision. Confrontation, accusation, threat. LI present + adversarial = enemies/rivals dynamic.
  • "familial" — habitual comfort, reduced performative posture, casual shared-space occupation, non-romantic proximity allowed (sister on the couch beside her, dad leaning over the kitchen island). Easier physical closeness than platonic but with no romantic charge.
  • "stranger_awkward" — uncertain spacing, asymmetrical posture, hesitant orientation, environmental shielding, interrupted eye contact. First meetings, first-time customers, asking for directions. Produces REALISM instead of accidental flirting.
  • "crowded_public" — environmental pressure dominates, bodies angled around obstacles, attention fragmented, interaction embedded into crowd flow. Subway platform, bar at peak hour, walking-and-talking in a busy street.
  • "romance_eligible" — close proximity permitted, charged eye contact possible, intimate blocking on prose-charged beats. Only when LI is present AND the scene's emotional content actually warrants romance staging (kiss, confession, lingering closeness). NOT a default for every LI-present scene — most LI scenes are professional/adversarial/platonic_tense early in the arc.
- HOW TO PICK (in order): (1) Is the LI even on stage? If absent → choose from solo / platonic_* / familial / stranger_awkward / crowded_public / professional / adversarial (LI-absent scenes can be professional or adversarial if the side character is a boss / opponent). (2) If LI is on stage, what is the relational state of THIS SCENE? Early arc = professional or adversarial or platonic_tense. Building warmth = platonic_warm. Open intimacy = romance_eligible. (3) Match the staging mode to where you actually are in the arc — do NOT default to romance_eligible just because the LI is on screen.
- COMMON ERRORS the model has made: defaulting to romance_eligible the moment the LI appears (most LI scenes early in a story should be professional/adversarial); using platonic_warm for two coworkers who barely know each other (use professional or stranger_awkward); using platonic_warm when the prose has the friend interrogating the protagonist (use platonic_tense).

TENSION STATE — PERCEPTUAL PRIMARY ON EVERY BEAT (HARD):
- tension_state is the EMOTIONAL REGISTER of a beat. Author in perceptual language, not anatomy — say WHAT the character is doing emotionally, not which muscle moves. The renderer maps each tension_state to a multimodal output: a mouth/jaw mutation if it's a peak, a subtitle-pacing modulation, a vignette tint, and audio cues. You only need to pick the perceptual register; the system handles the physical channels.
- VALUES (and what each one drives — peak entries shape the expression_arc + the scene's visual rhythm (vignette emphasis, hero-frame timing); non-peak entries only modulate timing / audio / vignette):
    "composed"    — neutral baseline. The default for beats not doing emotional work. NOT a peak.
    "suppressed"  — holding back. Spoken line doesn't match what's underneath. PEAK (suppression family). Maps to lips_tighten mutation + slowed subtitle + 30% vignette.
    "tightening"  — further suppression. The held-back emotion now visibly costs them. PEAK (suppression family). Maps to jaw_clench + slower subtitle + 40% vignette.
    "cracking"    — the held register breaks. A confession, an admission, a long-held breath releasing. PEAK (mixed family — only use when arc baseline_family is "mixed"). Maps to mouth_parted + much-slowed subtitle + 50% vignette.
    "softening"   — opening up. The face softens; warmth surfaces through restraint. PEAK (warmth family). Maps to mouth_soft + slowed subtitle + 20% vignette.
    "opening"     — further warmth. A small smile, fondness, relief. PEAK (warmth family). Maps to half_smile + slowed subtitle + 20% vignette.
    "sharpened"   — edge surfacing. A smirk, sharpening into command or contempt. PEAK (sharpness family). Maps to smirk + slightly faster subtitle (no dwell) + 30% vignette.
    "cutting"     — full sharpness. Lip curls in contempt or threat. PEAK (sharpness family — peak adversarial beats). Maps to lip_curl + fast subtitle + 50% vignette.
    "held_breath" — NOT a peak; no mouth mutation. The beat hangs in the air — slowed subtitle, audio dips into negative space. Use AFTER a line lands that needs silence.
    "delayed"     — NOT a peak; no mouth mutation. The character's reaction arrives a beat after expected. Slowed subtitle, no facial shift — perceptual hesitation only.
    "distant"     — NOT a peak; no mouth mutation. Emotionally pulled back. Mild vignette tint, slightly faster subtitle. The character is observed from further away emotionally.
- RHYTHM (HARD):
    • MOST beats are "composed". This is the rest state. Do not mark every beat as a tension_state ≠ composed; that produces emotional whiplash.
    • PEAKS (suppressed / tightening / cracking / softening / opening / sharpened / cutting) are LIMITED TO 5 per scene by the validator: it keeps the strongest-impact 5, spaced ≥3 beats apart, with family coherence (suppression / warmth / sharpness — first kept peak sets the family; cross-family extras get demoted to neutral). Place peaks where they MATTER: confrontation lands, decision lands, reveal lands, defeat lands. SAFE TO AUTHOR 3-5 PEAKS PER SCENE — under-spending produces flat output. The expression_arc.peak_a_beat / peak_b_beat fields are advisory: the validator will SYNTHESIZE the final arc from beats[] after the peaks settle, picking the first + last kept peak. You can declare 1-2 in expression_arc as your "anchor peaks" without worrying about the count matching beats[].
    • The NON-PEAK tension states (held_breath / delayed / distant) cost nothing extra and CAN be used more liberally — typically 2-4 per scene at well-chosen beats (after a peak lands, on a moment of withdrawn detachment, etc.). They're the layer that gives "alive" performance without paying Kontext.
    • expression_target on the same beat MUST be consistent with tension_state's anatomical mapping (see VALUES above), OR you may leave expression_target=null and the system will fill it from tension_state automatically. Prefer leaving expression_target null to keep authorship in perceptual language.
- BACKWARD COMPATIBILITY: If you only set expression_target (anatomical) and leave tension_state="composed" by accident, the renderer treats the beat as composed for pacing/audio purposes but still fires the Kontext mutation. Authoring in tension_state is preferred because it carries the FULL multimodal signal; expression_target alone only fires the mouth.

EXPRESSION INTENSITY — AMPLITUDE IS A FIRST-CLASS AXIS (HARD):
- Real human expression is AMPLITUDE-MODULATED, not categorical. The same "jaw_clench" can be a 0.22 micro-tension (a CEO holding panic) or a 0.78 fury (a confrontation finally landing). Diffusion image-edit models default to "comic-clear" 1.0 amplitude because they interpret subtle edits as "the edit failed." Without an explicit intensity directive, EVERY peak overshoots into soap-opera grimace.
- expression_intensity is a float 0.0-1.0 (or null to let the system default). The renderer injects an AMPLITUDE WORD into the Kontext mutation prompt ("almost imperceptible" / "subtle, restrained" / "clearly visible" / "strong, pronounced" / "extreme, fully released") based on which perceptual band the intensity falls in.
- PERCEPTUAL BANDS:
    0.00 – 0.15  → micro      (almost imperceptible; trace; careful-observer-only)
    0.15 – 0.35  → subtle     (restrained; film-acting register; reads but does not shout)
    0.35 – 0.60  → readable   (clearly visible without exaggeration)
    0.60 – 0.85  → strong     (pronounced; emotion no longer concealed)
    0.85 – 1.00  → extreme    (fully released; composure has broken; reserved for confession / fury / collapse)
- DEFAULT INTENSITY BY TENSION_STATE (the system fills these when you leave expression_intensity=null):
    suppressed   → 0.25  (subtle: holding visible only at the edges)
    tightening   → 0.35  (subtle/readable: the held pressure now visible)
    cracking     → 0.65  (strong: the break is the moment of the scene)
    softening    → 0.25  (subtle)
    opening      → 0.30  (subtle)
    sharpened    → 0.32  (subtle: smirks should hint, not declare)
    cutting      → 0.55  (readable/strong: peak adversarial)
- HIGH-STAKES INTENSITY MANDATE (HARD — added 2026-05-22): when sceneCharge.stake names triggering content (contract / eviction / notice / discovery / threat / news / accusation / blackmail / positive test / missing person / dna result / phone call / forbidden name / etc.), AT LEAST ONE peak in the scene MUST reach the READABLE band (0.40+). On the actual confession / break / decision moment within such a scene, allow the STRONG band (0.55-0.75). DO NOT keep every peak in the subtle band when the stake is heavy — the perceptual register must MATCH the stake, otherwise the scene reads as "small problem, tepid response." Restraint is a choice; flatness is a failure.
- SCENE CAPS BY social_staging_mode (validator clamps to these — no beat can exceed). Stake-aware escalator adds +0.20 when sceneCharge.stake names triggering content (contract / eviction / notice / discovery / threat / news / accusation / etc.):
    professional      → 0.50   (office baseline; bumps to 0.70 with high-stakes stake)
    platonic_warm     → 0.40
    platonic_tense    → 0.65   (confrontation between friends should LAND)
    stranger_awkward  → 0.30
    adversarial       → 0.80   (open conflict hits strong band)
    familial          → 0.45
    romance_eligible  → 0.65   (charged romantic beats deserve readable+)
    crowded_public    → 0.50
    solo              → 0.55   (solo inciting moments need real signal)
- FAMILY CEILINGS (per tension_state, caps higher than ceiling get clamped):
    suppression family (suppressed/tightening): 0.40-0.50 max — restrained emotion is STRONGER when barely leaking through. Almost never go above 0.45 unless the prose explicitly shows the suppression breaking.
    warmth family (softening/opening): 0.40-0.45 max — warmth lands at low amplitude; high-amplitude warmth reads saccharine.
    sharpness family (sharpened): 0.55 max — smirks at 1.0 read as cartoonish supervillain.
    cracking: 0.85 max — the highest single beat allowed; reserved for the SCENE'S confession/break.
    cutting: 0.80 max — peak adversarial, but still cap at 0.80.
- THE 0.35 LINE (HARD): Most beats stay UNDER 0.35. If you find yourself setting most of the scene's peaks above 0.35, you are writing CW-drama acting, not film acting. Film cinematic restraint lives between 0.20 and 0.40. Let restraint do the work.
- WHEN TO OVERRIDE THE DEFAULT: set expression_intensity explicitly only when (a) the prose moment is BIGGER than the tension_state's default amplitude (a cracking confession on a held secret may need 0.80), or (b) you want EVEN MORE restraint than the default (a suppressed @ 0.15 for a beat where the character is almost succeeding at hiding it). Most beats leave expression_intensity=null and the system back-fills sensibly.

ECHO PROFILE — CHANNEL VARIANCE (OPTIONAL override per beat):
- The system delivers each peak's emotional weight through a CHANNEL COMBINATION (audio duck + subtitle pacing + vignette + crop + decay overlay). If every peak activates ALL channels at the same strength, the reader recognizes the formula and stops feeling it. Echo profiles vary WHICH departments react per peak so the orchestration stays surprising.
- Default echo_profile is derived from tension_state (suppressed → "suppressed", tightening → "simmer", cracking → "rupture", softening/opening → "afterglow", sharpened → "impact", cutting → "rupture", held_breath/delayed → "hollow", distant → "simmer"). You only need to set echo_profile EXPLICITLY when you want to BREAK the default — e.g., a cracking beat that should land hollow instead of rupture, or a suppressed beat that should land as impact.
- VALUES:
    "hollow"     — silence beat. Audio dips deep, subtitle drags long, NO visual emphasis. The room went quiet. Use for the beat AFTER a line lands when the silence is the response.
    "impact"     — physical punch. Crop pushes hard, vignette darkens, brief audio hit. Quick recovery. Use for accusations / revelations that hit and the scene must keep moving.
    "simmer"     — slow build. Mild duck and tint sustained across a longer echo window. Use when pressure is accumulating, not landing.
    "rupture"    — everything maxed briefly. Deep duck, dark vignette, max crop, long subtitle slow. Most expensive — use 0-2 times per scene max. Reserve for confession / break / consummation pivot.
    "afterglow"  — warmth lingers. WARM tint (not dark), pacing slow, almost no audio duck. Use post-tender / post-relief beats where the moment is GOOD.
    "suppressed" — held in. Medium duck, medium dark tint, full crop. The pressure is internal but visible at the edges.
- WITHIN-SCENE DIVERSITY (HARD): a scene should use AT LEAST 2 different echo_profile values across its peaks. If all peaks resolve to the same profile (e.g. all suppressed → suppressed → suppressed), the reader pattern-matches and the scene's emotional rhythm flatlines. The validator will warn when all peaks share a profile. Vary deliberately: a scene of mostly suppression can break into one "hollow" beat to give the held register room to breathe.
- WHEN to OVERRIDE: set beat.echo_profile when (a) you want the channel mix to contradict the tension_state's default ("suppressed" tension that lands as "hollow" — held silence instead of internal pressure), or (b) you need to diversify within a scene (the default would produce 3 same-profile peaks; override one to "impact" or "hollow" for variance). Most beats leave this null and inherit the default.

EXPRESSION ARC — PRE-DECLARED PEAKS (HARD, BEFORE YOU WRITE ONE BEAT):
- Cinematic emotional performance is ARC-PLANNED, not beat-improvised. Film scoring writes cues BEFORE the scene shoots; the same applies here. Declare visualState.expression_arc UPFRONT so beat-writing serves the arc instead of emitting "smirk" / "lips_tighten" ad hoc and producing emotional whiplash.
- baseline_family options:
    "suppression" — the speaking character is HOLDING BACK throughout. Mouth tightens, jaw sets, lips press. Peaks use: lips_tighten, jaw_clench, jaw_set, lips_pressed.
    "warmth"      — the speaking character is OPENING UP. Mouth softens, half-smiles surface, lips part on confession. Peaks use: mouth_soft, half_smile, mouth_parted.
    "sharpness"   — the speaking character is GETTING SHARPER / MORE EDGED. Smirks flicker, lips curl, contempt surfaces. Peaks use: smirk, lip_curl, jaw_clench.
    "mixed"       — the scene legitimately pivots families (e.g. suppression cracks into warmth on a confession). RARE — only declare "mixed" when the arc truly inverts; the model has overused it in the past.
    "neutral"     — quiet scene with no peaks fired. Both peak_a_beat and peak_b_beat must be null; the face stays at its baseline expression across the whole scene. RARE — use ONLY for low-pressure transitional scenes with literal zero charge: a character walking to a bus stop with no internal state, idle waiting for an elevator, mundane errand prose. 
       NEUTRAL IS FORBIDDEN when the scene contains ANY of:
         ✗ a question that creates anxiety / pressure ("Are you going to tell them?" / "Did he call you back?" / "When were you going to mention this?")
         ✗ a deadline, an article-coming-out, an email unread, a confrontation about to land
         ✗ a confession in the air, an apology owed, a secret being protected
         ✗ a flirtation, charged eye contact, proximity tension, attraction acknowledged
         ✗ a conflict — overt or under the surface
         ✗ a relationship pressure (parent/sibling/friend asking the question the protagonist has been dodging)
       In any of those, baseline_family is one of suppression / warmth / sharpness / mixed — NEVER neutral. At MINIMUM set peak_a_beat to the most charged dialogue beat in the scene (typically a question, an unspoken accusation, the moment the protagonist almost says something). A face that holds neutral through "Are you going to tell them?" is a contract violation — that beat IS the peak.
- micro_drift sits BELOW the peaks — it's the subtle trend the face carries between peaks (the baseline phase li_expression slowly drifts). Phrase it concretely: "guarded-then-cracking", "warm-but-tightening", "steady simmer", NOT "complex emotions" or "shifting".
- peak_a_beat / peak_b_beat are BEAT INDICES into the beats[] array. The corresponding beats MUST have expression_target set to a value consistent with baseline_family (lips_tighten/jaw_clench/jaw_set/lips_pressed for suppression; mouth_soft/half_smile/mouth_parted for warmth; smirk/lip_curl/jaw_clench for sharpness).
- peak_b_beat must be at least 4 beats after peak_a_beat. Adjacent peaks read as one held state and waste the second mutation.
- Both peaks must escalate in the same direction within the family — e.g. lips_tighten → jaw_clench (escalating suppression) is GOOD; lips_tighten → jaw_clench → lips_tighten (oscillating) is BAD. Pick the arc shape first, then place the beats.
- decay_window controls how long each peak lingers visually. After peak_a_beat fires, the renderer holds the peak frame at full opacity through (peak_a_beat + 1), then eases the mutation overlay to 0 across (decay_window − 1) more beats. So decay_window=3 means: peak holds for 2 beats, fades over the 3rd. Use 3 as the default; use 4 for scenes where the emotional weight should linger longer (a confession, a betrayal landing); use 2 for snappier pivots.
- ARC-FIRST CONTRACT: visualize the emotional curve of the scene before assigning beats. WHERE does the suppression peak? WHERE does it crack? Mark those beats. Then write the surrounding beats to serve those two moments.
- BEAT COUNT (HARD FLOOR): a full CG scene is 20–30 beats — NOT a sketch. Each beat is SHORT (one image-able moment, often a single line), so 20–30 tight beats fit comfortably under the word ceiling. "Restraint" (below) means each beat is LEAN — no padding WITHIN a beat — it does NOT mean fewer beats. A scene under ~18 beats is a FAILURE: it collapses the scene into a handful of stacked ultimatums with no room to ground them. Undershooting the beat count is the #1 CG miss — do not do it.
- CAUSAL SPINE (HARD — THEREFORE / BUT, never "and then"): the beats form a CHAIN where each beat is CAUSED by the prior one. Between any two consecutive beats you must be able to say THEREFORE (this beat is the consequence of the last) or BUT (this beat reverses / complicates / costs what the last established) — NEVER "and then" (a new line unconnected to the one before it). GROUND before you escalate: a charged line ("This changes everything") must be PRECEDED by the concrete beat that makes it true (what she just read / saw / realized), and FOLLOWED by its consequence (what it forces her to do). Test: if you could reorder or delete a beat without breaking the logic, the spine is broken — the reader is watching "and then, and then." FORBIDDEN: stacking climactic ultimatums ("Choose before the thread dies." / "The sigil cannot lie." / "This changes everything.") back-to-back with no grounding between them — that is the #2 CG miss. Each escalation must be EARNED by the beat that caused it.
- COMMON FAILURE the model has produced: marking a dozen+ beats non-neutral expecting them all to land — the validator keeps only the strongest 5 (spaced ≥3 beats apart, one emotional family) and demotes the rest to neutral, so the arc you intended is destroyed. Author 3-5 peaks placed where they MATTER; do NOT smear non-neutral across most beats.

SCENE CHARGE — TOP-PRIORITY CONTRACT (HARD, BEFORE YOU WRITE ONE BEAT):
- Before generating beats, FILL IN the sceneCharge block in your output. This is the structural backbone of the scene; the beats serve it.
- sceneCharge.stake: name a CONCRETE thing the protagonist is hiding / wanting / avoiding. Not a mood. A fact, a deadline, a name, a recent loss, an unopened message, a decision being dodged. If you cannot name it in one sentence, the scene has no charge and will read flat — keep iterating on the stake until you can name it.
- sceneCharge.concrete_specific_beat_idx: pick one beat where a CONCRETE OBJECT or DETAIL hints at the stake (a returned ring on the desk, an envelope she hasn't opened, a phone face-down she keeps glancing at, a name she doesn't say). That beat's text MUST include the named object/detail. NOT a vague mood word.
- sceneCharge.subtext_beat_idx: pick one dialogue beat whose surface CONFLICTS with what the protagonist actually feels. ("I'm fine" the beat after her hand was shown shaking.) The line must read as ordinary; the conflict is the engine.
- sceneCharge.almost_said_beat_idx: pick one beat where the protagonist ALMOST says something and stops — sentence broken off, word swapped, topic steered. This is non-negotiable for Scene 1 charge.
- sceneCharge.escalation_rhythm: declare HOW pressure ratchets across the scene. Beats are not equal — pressure must build, not flatline.
- BANNED for ANY beat (these are the workshop-tells that produced past failures): "leaving unspoken questions behind", "long shadows on the path", "the day's weight settled", "she debated whether to act", "she would have to decide soon", "something inside her shifted", "the moment passed between them", "they walked together in silence", "the sun dipped lower", "the air felt heavy / charged / electric", "she felt unsettled / overwhelmed / conflicted", "she knew she had to do something", "she pushed the thought aside", "for now". If a beat's text trends toward any of these patterns, REWRITE it before emitting. These are filler the model uses when it has no idea what's actually at stake.
- BANNED scene shapes for Scene 1: ordinary catch-up between two friends about weather / weekend / coffee / commute / refilling mugs with no underlying pressure. Two people exchanging pleasantries with no subtext is a FAILURE. If you find yourself drifting toward chitchat with nothing being hidden or wanted, STOP and re-derive the scene from sceneCharge.stake.

TEXT RULES (HARD):
- A dialogue beat's "text" is JUST the spoken line, wrapped in straight double quotes — e.g. "\"You're late, McBrayn.\"". NO he-said, NO she-replied, NO action tags, NO [NAME] brackets. Pure quoted speech. The speaker's ACTION does not disappear and does not join the line: put it in the ADJACENT narration beat, before or after, whichever the moment wants. Attribution types a whole beat as one source, so action inside a dialogue beat would be recorded as spoken words.
- A narration beat's "text" is descriptive action / staging / sensory detail. No quoted dialogue inside narration beats.
- An exposition beat's "text" is the PC's internal thought / reflection (no quotes — interior monologue, not spoken).
- LENGTH FOLLOWS THE MOMENT. A beat is ONE SUSTAINED MOMENT — a glance, a line, a noticed detail, a breath — and that is what bounds it, not a sentence count. Give a moment ONE sharp sentence when that is all it needs, or let it open into a compact paragraph (commonly two to four sentences) when it earns the development. This is PERMISSION, not a target and not a minimum: never pad a moment to reach a length, never clip one that deserves room, and do not settle into a uniform rhythm of either. A beat is still never a scene.
- ACROSS THE SCENE, connect action, perception and character voice so the prose reads as someone living it rather than a camera listing movements. NOT IN EVERY BEAT: plain physical action, sensory observation, dialogue and silence carry their own meaning and must be left to do it. Avoid BOTH failure modes — screenplay shorthand that only reports what moved, and a procession of explanatory observations where every detail is immediately interpreted for the reader.
- GRAMMATICAL CAPTIONS (HARD): every beat is a COMPLETE, readable sentence with its articles ("the"/"a") and verbs intact. Terse is good; telegram/headline-ese that drops articles and the connecting verb is NOT — it reads as broken. BAD (article- and verb-dropped, reads as garbled): "Lines inside name Vael and me in secret exchange three years ago." GOOD (same beat, grammatical and still tight): "The lines inside name Vael and me — a secret exchange, three years ago." Read each beat back to yourself; if it does not parse as English, repair it before emitting.
- The FINAL beat of the scene is EXACTLY ONE SENTENCE — the closing line that delivers the scene's decision moment. Never bury it in a wall.
- BEAT COUNT (TARGET, not a rigid quota — it YIELDS to the two priority bounds below): beats are PROSE, not panels. Only ~5 phase IMAGES render per scene (see PHASE COUNT); beats ride beneath them at ZERO extra render cost. So use beats to PAINT the scene far beyond what the panels could show — give every world-texture stroke, every character tell, every noticed detail, every hesitation its OWN tight beat rather than cramming them into fewer fat ones. Split freely: a glance is a beat, a noticed detail is a beat, a held breath is a beat, one line of dialogue is a beat. Richness comes from MANY SMALL TIGHT beats, never from fat 3-sentence beats. TARGET ~28–45 tight beats for a standard scene (a thin <22-beat scene is a FAILURE unless a bound below forces fewer). PRIORITY 1 — if a LENGTH CEILING is active (see the user prompt), the CEILING WINS: fit as many tight beats as fit UNDER it; do NOT blow the ceiling to reach a beat count. PRIORITY 2 — on an INTIMACY-PACE scene (ST3/ST4, see SCENE DENSITY), run FEWER beats that breathe (~22–30); felt time between charged beats matters more than raw count there. These are not simultaneous rigid quotas — when they conflict, the active bound governs and the beat-count target flexes.
- Alternate dialogue and narration freely; do not bunch dialogue.


PROSE DENSITY — THE CONDUCTOR (read FIRST; it governs every description & perception system below):
  The directives that follow — world sensory texture, emotional atmosphere, character impression / description / context — are INSTRUMENTS, not obligations to fire every beat. Each is individually restrained, but if they ALL play at once the prose turns "writerly": self-aware, over-curated, PERMANENTLY HEIGHTENED — and the reader loses contrast. PLAINNESS IS NEGATIVE SPACE: real scenes need dead air, functional dialogue, neutral observation, procedural movement — beats where the prose simply STOPS interpreting. The more loaded the instruments, the more the plain beats matter.
  EXEMPT FROM THIS BUDGET — THE PLACE-ESTABLISHMENT FLOOR (Roman 2026-07-14, meta-audit c/④): grounding the reader in WHERE the scene physically happens — the room or place, its dominant materials, and the quality of light, within the first ~2 paragraphs — is NOT an interpretive layer and is NOT rationed by charge. Even a LOW-charge scene MUST establish the place concretely (a named surface/material + the light). The budget below governs INTERPRETIVE / loaded strokes (metaphor, symbolism, emotional weather), never this baseline physical grounding — and likewise never the people-picturability floor (both are required regardless of charge). Suppress the interpreting, not the seeing.
  EVERY SCENE OWES A PLACE RE-ANCHOR, CONTINUATIONS INCLUDED (Roman 2026-08-23 — measured: grounding fired in scene 1 and collapsed in every continuation, leaving characters talking in front of a green screen). The reader does not carry physical space forward between scenes the way the story state does. Early in EVERY scene, give one concrete anchor: a material or surface, the quality of light, the air or weather, a sound, a distance, or the way bodies are arranged in the space. Props held by people do not count — a band at a mouth and grass under one foot are objects, not a place.
  RE-ANCHOR, DO NOT RECAP. Never repeat the establishing description. Show what is DIFFERENT about the place now that the situation has changed: “The interrogation room had white walls, a metal table, a camera in the corner.” → three scenes later → “The camera’s red light had been blinking for three hours. She had stopped looking at it.” Same room, new experience, and the second one costs a sentence.
  GAUGE THIS SCENE'S CHARGE, then ration the interpretive layers:
    • LOW (transition, logistics, setup, easy banter): mostly NEUTRAL rendering — at most ONE subtle interpretive stroke in the whole scene. Let most sentences just report what happens.
    • MEDIUM (rising tension, a meaningful exchange, a decision forming): up to TWO interpretive layers may stack across the scene.
    • HIGH (confrontation, reveal, intimacy, turning point): full orchestration permitted — the instruments may work together.
    • EXTREME (climax, rupture, the scene the whole arc bent toward): a brief LYRICAL DENSITY SPIKE is allowed — heightened, fused prose — for that peak ONLY, then release.
  Even at HIGH/EXTREME: a loaded sentence lands hardest when the sentences around it are plain. NEVER run three interpretive sentences in a row. Contrast is the instrument the conductor actually plays.
  CG: the charge budget governs how richly NARRATION/EXPOSITION beats interpret; DIALOGUE and procedural beats stay plain regardless. The lyrical spike maps to the scene's peak beat(s) only, never the whole plan.


WORLD SENSORY TEXTURE (REQUIRED — woven INTO the action, never a catalog):

  STEALTH WEALTH (the love interest's OWN register — HARD): the principal does NOT wear recognizable logos. His things are UNBRANDED or so rarefied that naming the maker means nothing to an outsider — the flex is a name even millionaires have never heard (e.g. an F.P. Journe with a near-blank dial; a Greubel Forsey no one in the room recognized). Recognizable luxury labels (Cartier, Rolex, Birkin, Patek, Bvlgari, Hermès) are what NEW MONEY and strivers reach for to be SEEN — use those ONLY for lesser, aspirational characters showing off, NEVER for him. For him the tell is the cut, the cloth, the hand-work, the ABSENCE of a logo — wealth that has stopped needing to announce itself.
  ESTABLISH THE PLACE (HARD — Roman 2026-06-08; scenes have been reading as voices in a void). Within the first ~2 paragraphs the reader must know WHERE this scene physically happens — the room or place, its dominant MATERIALS and the quality of its LIGHT. A scene that could be occurring anywhere has failed its opening. Do it through action, not a real-estate listing.
  KEEP IT ALIVE: thereafter the space stays present through INTERMITTENT concrete material/surface/light strokes — marble, wood, stone, glass, leather, metal, fabric, paper; the slant and colour of the light; temperature, echo, the give or hardness of a surface under a hand. At least one such concrete physical anchor every few paragraphs, always tied to a beat. People touch, lean on, cross, and are lit by real things; they never float in undescribed space.
  Every scene carries precise sensory strokes of THIS scene’s actual SETTING and the PEOPLE in it — the materials, surfaces, sounds, and objects of this world, rendered concretely and IN MOTION. Applies to WHOEVER is on stage, not only the love interest.
  WORLD VOCABULARY (Fantasy · billionaire opulence): the materials of extreme wealth — unlabelled bespoke cloth, gold-enamel, hand-cut crystal, vicuña, calfskin, cold-pressed glass, marble — rendered specifically, never as "luxury" and (for the principal) never via a recognizable logo
  The line is GENERIC SUMMARY (banned) vs SPECIFIC DETAIL-IN-MOTION (required):
    • BANNED: summary markers that LABEL the world instead of rendering it ("a lavish room," "high-tech gear," "a grim wasteland," "an ancient forest," "expensive suit").
    • REQUIRED: a gold-enamel cufflink working loose as he jabs a finger; spittle flecking a hand-cut lapel that carries no label; a hand-cut crystal chandelier faintly ringing as his voice climbs; a thumbprint smearing cold-pressed glass. Named, exact, tied to a beat.
  DELIVER TENSION THROUGH THE DETAIL: let the environment REACT to the drama and let perception CARRY the beat — a fist striking waterlogged shipboards; the chandelier ringing as a voice climbs; tactile implants reading the molecular lattice of an alien petal even as audio implants miss the sarcasm in his tone. The sensory stroke carries the emotional beat; it does not pause it. One exact material detail beats a paragraph of atmosphere.


ATMOSPHERE & EMOTIONAL WEATHER (the room's mood, felt as much as seen — use SPARINGLY):
  Beyond the objects in a scene, every setting has AMBIENT CONDITIONS — temperature, light and shadow, the air, and (outdoors) weather and sky. Render them from THIS setting: a boardroom's HVAC chill and hard overhead glare; a tavern's smoke and guttering hearth; a ship's deck-spray and grey horizon; a lab's recycled air and blue panel-glow.
  PLAUSIBILITY: keep it diegetically real — the room is cold because the AC over-runs, or it merely FEELS cold to the POV (perception is fair game, and ties to how the viewpoint's emotional state sharpens or dulls what they notice). Reserve literally responsive weather/elements for worlds where the supernatural is canon.
  CG: express the emotional-weather lean in BEAT PROSE (felt temperature / air / the quality of light). Visual mood and lighting are governed by the cinematic envelope — do NOT fabricate weather or relight the phase image in ways that contradict established scene geography or continuity; the lean is primarily FELT in the prose, not forced into visualState.


CHARACTER IMPRESSION (how to render people — ONE fused move, not three separate passes):
  Strong characterization fuses three layers into a SINGLE stroke that never stops the scene: (1) a PHYSICAL cue (the observable thing) → (2) its RELATIONAL/SOCIAL meaning (status, history, power, occupation) → (3) the narrator's POV INTERPRETATION (how THIS viewpoint emotionally frames it).
  GOLD STANDARD: "Geralt's cufflinks flashed whenever he spoke — tiny gold guillotines punctuating every acquisition target he intended to swallow whole." (physical + wealth + aggression + occupation + metaphor + narrator attitude, all in one moving sentence.)
  SOFT DENSITY GOVERNOR (CRITICAL — prevents overwritten "AI-literary" prose): NOT every description is interpretive. If every tie "represents his emotional repression," the prose drowns — one loaded image lands; three loaded images in a paragraph is purple. But EVERY named, on-stage character MAY earn ONE memorable PERCEPTUAL stroke on first appearance, and a named character reduced to "the woman by the door" or "brown hair" is still the failure. A STROKE IS NOT A DESCRIPTION OF WHAT THEY ARE — it is a revealing interpretation of something THEY CHOSE TO SHOW.
  AGENCY, NOT LEAKAGE: a tremor, a blush, a caught breath, a nervous habit is a TELL — involuntary, camera-visible, welcome as texture, and never a perceptual stroke. The stroke lands on a CHOSEN act: WOUND, then PERFORMANCE, then COST, then THE READING. Someone performs the person they wish to be seen as; maintaining it costs them something; the POV character reads that choice through her own wants, history and fear — confidently, and possibly wrongly. BAD: "Julian’s hand trembled." GOOD: "Julian answered before anyone finished questioning him. He filled every silence before anyone could wonder what he was afraid of."
  DENSITY IS MEASURED IN PRESSURE VECTORS, NOT HEADCOUNT. Multiple strokes are right when multiple people are APPLYING or ABSORBING different pressures: a council where four people are each trying to control the same outcome may reveal four masks, because that scene is four forces, not one emotional unit. A tavern holding twenty people is one vector, or none. A character EARNS one by pressuring the POV character, turning the scene, revealing a real choice, forcing a decision, or establishing themselves as a continuing force. Twenty background villagers earn none. Four people deciding a fate may earn four. COUNT PRESSURE, NOT PEOPLE.
  A STROKE MAY WAIT: someone passing through briefly, whose role has not emerged, can remain a question and earn theirs later. But anyone who enters, exerts pressure and leaves a mark gets one NOW.
  THE CONTRAST IS PART OF THE EFFECT: this is strongest when several people reveal themselves DIFFERENTLY under the SAME pressure — one sees the hostage-taker protecting someone, another sees him controlling the narrative, another sees him enjoying it, another sees a man who has already lost. Same act, four truths. AFTER that opening stroke, further mentions of that character stay mostly plain observation, and the full three-layer fusion is spent sparingly — reserved for MAJOR characters and CHARGED beats. Over the whole story each major character ACCUMULATES interpretive impressions — but any SINGLE scene spends them sparingly. Unnamed background stays plain.
  POV-RELATIVE (the dimensional move): the SAME character reads DIFFERENTLY through different eyes — one narrator's predatory shark is another's competent stabilizer, a junior's terrifying legend, an ex-lover's insecure fraud hiding in status rituals. Frame every impression through the CURRENT narrator's wants / history / fear — never a neutral, fixed character tag. On a POV transition, the SAME facts get RE-INTERPRETED by the new viewpoint.

CHARACTER FACE — CG RENDER-SAFETY (HARD): the love interest's facial description lives in the BEAT TEXT (the reader's imagination) ONLY. The phase IMAGE still honors li_visibility_phase — do NOT raise li_visibility, and do NOT write face detail into visualState, just because the prose admires his face. Beat prose describes the face; the render keeps Mystery Man shadowed.

CHARACTER CONTEXT (CG — social / relational facts THROUGH the beat, never standing bio): match the beat type to the rotating delivery VEHICLE — the protagonist's read of a character, or how OTHERS treat them, rides a NARRATION/EXPOSITION beat; another character's line, or the character's OWN status line, rides a DIALOGUE beat (the spoken line carries the fact, and that speaker MUST appear in visualState.other_characters_present + the phase per SPEAKER RULES). ROTATE the vehicle scene to scene (don't use the same one twice running) and DEEPEN the iceberg — prefer a RECONTEXTUALIZING reveal (a fact that re-reads an earlier beat) over a flat new bio fact. Early scenes establish; mid/late recontextualize. Always fuse the fact to a live beat; honor the 1–2-sentence cap. Verbal / relational only — it does NOT change visualState or what renders.


TACTILE DETAIL CUTAWAY (HARD — exactly ONE per scene): one INSERT cuts the camera to a single world-specific TACTILE detail drawn from the WORLD SENSORY TEXTURE vocabulary above — spittle flecking a velvet collar, a sensor implant scanning an alien petal, gossamer pulled taut in his grip, a gold-enamel cufflink working loose. Use the insert mechanism: a phase with shot_style="insert_object" and focus_target naming that tactile element, OR a single narration beat with cut_to_closeup=true + closeup_target naming it + shot_type="insert_object". The beat text renders the detail IN MOTION (the cufflink loosening, the sensor reading, the threads straining) and lets it CARRY the moment's emotional charge — not a static prop label. The element MUST be physically present in this location (see SCENE GEOGRAPHY). Place it on a CHARGED beat, never the opener. Exactly one — do not litter the scene with inserts.

CLOSEUP = A REACTION FACE (HARD — whenever you set cut_to_closeup=true): a closeup cuts to the FACE of whoever REACTS in that beat, carrying that beat's expression_target as a readable emotion tied to WHAT IS HAPPENING — never a decorative or idle crop. Set closeup_target to who reacts and shot_type to match: "face" for the protagonist or a present named character (eyes + mouth carry the emotion — the protagonist is NOT concealed), "mouth" for the male Mystery-Man love interest (nose + mouth/jaw only, eyes cropped/shadowed), or "object" ONLY for a genuine physical reveal (closeup_target = the object). A face closeup REQUIRES a NON-NEUTRAL expression_target on that beat — give the face a specific emotion to show. Prefer face reactions on the scene's most charged beats over generic prop or hand crops.

SPEAKER RULES (HARD — the entire point of this generator):
- Every dialogue beat MUST have an explicit speaker. NEVER leave speaker null on a dialogue beat.
- "protagonist" — the PC (the viewpoint character). Use this literal token, NEVER the PC's actual name.
- "li" — the primary love interest. Use this literal token, NEVER the LI's actual name.
- For every other speaking character (friend, sibling, stranger, antagonist), use that character's actual name verbatim — e.g. "Kira", "the bartender", "Detective Reyes". You are AUTHORING the scene, so you DEFINE who speaks; ambiguity is impossible at this layer.
- speaker is null ONLY for narration and exposition beats (which have no spoken line).
- EVERY non-PC/non-LI speaker name you put on a dialogue beat MUST also appear in visualState.other_characters_present[].name AND in that beat's phase's characters_present. Same spelling. If "Ella" speaks, "Ella" goes in both lists. Failure mode: dialogue beat reads `speaker: "Ella"` but Ella appears nowhere in the visualState/phase metadata → the image renders without Ella + the reader sees an unattributed dialogue line. The renderer's safety nets can recover when speaker is set; they CANNOT recover when speaker is null AND the character is missing from metadata.

PHASE RULES (HARD):
- IMAGE BUDGET (HARD — ~5 IMAGES TOTAL per scene, panels + cut-ins COMBINED): a scene renders about FIVE images total — roughly 3–4 phase PANELS plus 1–2 cut-in inserts. Emit 3–4 phase panels (fewer than 3 is a near-static failure); do NOT pad to 5–6 panels — the cut-ins carry the rest of the visual budget, and total images should not exceed ~6. Each phase covers ≥2 consecutive beats and is a MATERIALLY DIFFERENT STAGE within the one location (per SCENE GEOGRAPHY — no relocation): a new character enters, a prop/truth is revealed, posture/proximity shifts, a decision lands, someone leaves. The interlocutor-driven scene supplies these naturally — e.g. protagonist alone in the space → the interlocutor arrives → the confrontation tightens → the hidden thing surfaces → the decision lands. startBeat for phase 0 is always 0.
- PHASE 0 IS THE ACTIVE PRESSURE BEAT (HARD — non-negotiable): the visualState scaffolding establishes the IMAGE (background, camera, li_position, lighting, wardrobe, other_characters_present) — that is the renderer's job and your job there is unchanged. But the PROSE in phase 0 beats[] enters MID-SPIKE. The scene is already unstable when beats[0] fires. Pressure is already active. The protagonist is already non-neutral. The named A-plot goal / clock / stakes (see A-PLOT PRESSURE block above) is already pressing on this moment.
   What this CHANGES in how you write beats[0..3]:
     ✓ The first 3-4 beats name the demand / question / discovery / deadline / accusation that is ACTIVELY happening as the scene opens.
     ✓ A side character on stage in phase 0 is there because their presence creates pressure, not because they're sharing breakfast.
     ✓ The protagonist's body is already in stress register — held breath, white-knuckle grip, jaw set, eyes on something specific.
     ✗ DO NOT spend beats 0-3 describing the room, the weather, the protagonist's wardrobe, or the morning routine. The IMAGE shows the room. The prose doesn't need to.
     ✗ DO NOT open with the protagonist alone in calm contemplation. If she's alone in phase 0, the spike came from off-page (a message, a memory, a returning fact) and beats[0..2] surface it.
     ✗ DO NOT use phase 0 to "establish the world before things happen." Things have ALREADY happened. The scene is the aftermath landing.
- Scene 1 (sceneIndex === 0): li_visibility_phase for phase 0 MUST be "absent" — the LI does not appear in Scene 1, phase 0. Use camera_override "over_shoulder_pc". (This is unchanged — the visual contract for the LI's eventual arrival in phase 1 still holds.)
- A new phase begins only when the visual stage materially changes (new character enters, prop revealed and held, LI arrives onstage, proximity/contact shift, location shift). Emotional shifts are NOT phase changes — the prose carries those.
- SPEAKING-CHARACTER ESTABLISHMENT (HARD): When a named non-PC character (a friend, sibling, coworker, antagonist, side character) speaks ≥2 dialogue beats in this scene, AT LEAST ONE phase MUST visually establish them on screen — either by setting camera_override = "over_shoulder_pc" with that character in other_characters_present (so they appear in the OTS background), camera_override = "medium_two_shot" (both PC and character in frame), or a phase that depicts their entry. A multi-beat conversation rendered as a static close-shot of the PC with the other character's lines floating in as voice-only is a CONTRACT VIOLATION. The reader needs to SEE the speaker at least once during the conversation.
- PRACTICAL APPLICATION: a Phase-0 establishing shot of the PC alone (LI absent, side character absent or off-screen) is fine for the opener. But when the side character speaks dialogue beats starting at beat N, START A NEW PHASE at beat N with that character in characters_present + visualState reflecting them on stage. Their arrival IS a stage change — the prior phase ends, the new one begins with them visible. Do not let them speak from off-screen for the whole scene.
- SOLO-PC SCENES: if the scene is genuinely solo (no named characters speak, no LI present), a single phase with the PC alone is correct. The rule above applies only when at least one named character is speaking lines.
- LOCATION COHERENCE (HARD — TOP CONSTRAINT): The ENTIRE SCENE happens in the SINGLE PHYSICAL LOCATION named in visualState.background. EVERY beat — narration, exposition, and dialogue — must be set in or refer to THAT location. The schema currently exposes only ONE visualState.background per scene; you cannot represent a mid-scene location change. Therefore: if the scene requires a second location, that is a NEW SCENE, not a new phase. Do NOT have the protagonist walk outside, step into a different room, transition to a different street, approach a different building, or otherwise move to a place the visualState.background does not name. Do NOT include narrative beats that take place at a different location than the named background ("Elliot approaches her outside wearing a hoodie" when the background is an office is FORBIDDEN — the model has done this and it produces a hard visual-vs-prose contradiction the reader experiences as broken). If a character ARRIVES into the named location from elsewhere, the beat describes their arrival INTO this location (they enter, they cross the threshold, they walk in), not their journey to it. A single phase is ONE physical location. NEVER describe transitions across locations inside a beat. Mid-beat or mid-phase location jumps are the #1 cause of prose-vs-image desync; the reader sees an office and reads about a courtyard at the same time. INVIOLABLE.

CAMERA VARIETY (HARD — each phase is a distinct SHOT, not one static frame with tweaks):
- Phases 1+ SHOULD set camera_override explicitly; the full enum is available on EVERY phase — over_shoulder_pc (behind PC toward the subject), medium_two_shot (both figures, waist-up), wide_establishing (environmental scale), close_li (LI face fills frame), close_pc (tight on PC body / hands / partial face), low_angle_pc (looking UP — stakes-heavy), high_angle_pc (looking DOWN — vulnerable), profile_pc (pure side profile — contemplative), silhouette_pc (dark outline vs a bright backdrop — mystery), over_object_high_angle (down past a desk/object at the PC's hands), push_in_on_object (a key object fills the frame), dutch_tilt (off-axis 5-15° — unease).
- DO NOT inherit phase 0's camera. A phase boundary exists because the visual stage changed — the camera changes with it. NO TWO CONSECUTIVE phases use the SAME camera_override unless the prose holds one continuous unmoved moment. Match the camera to the MOMENT: LI arrival → medium_two_shot / close_li; a confrontation building → low_angle_pc; a discovery landing on an object → push_in_on_object / over_object_high_angle; a tense beat → dutch_tilt; a quiet interior → profile_pc / close_pc.
- Example arc: phase 0 over_shoulder_pc (PC alone, LI absent) → phase 1 medium_two_shot (LI arrives) → phase 2 close_li (the reveal lands). Variety across phases is what makes the scene read as CINEMATIC EDITING rather than one static image with subtle changes.
- MYSTERY MAN / CONCEALED LI (female-PC + male-LI stories, his face withheld until the ST3/4 reveal): while li_visibility_phase is "shadowed" or "partial", his face stays HIDDEN — do NOT use close_li on him and do NOT frame his face. Use over_shoulder_pc (the PC is the lit subject, his back-of-head/shoulder in the foreground), silhouette_pc, over_object_high_angle, push_in_on_object, or dutch_tilt. close_li on the LI is legal ONLY once li_visibility_phase is "revealed" (after the player chooses to reveal him at ST3/4). This does NOT restrict PC-focused framings (close_pc, profile_pc) — in a Mystery Man story HER face is the visible subject.

VISUAL STATE RULES:
- pc_visibility is NEVER "revealed" / "full_face". The protagonist's face is never fully shown to the reader. Default "back_only" for over_shoulder cameras.
- li_position is "offstage" for Scene 1 (LI absent).
- background is concrete and named (not "a room" — "the late-shift newsroom, half-empty, rain on the glass, low fluorescent buzz").
- BACKGROUND ↔ PROSE CONSISTENCY (HARD — non-negotiable): visualState.background MUST describe the SAME physical location your beat prose puts the characters in. If any beat's text mentions "the café" / "coffee shop" / "diner" / "newsroom" / "gallery" / "garage" / "kitchen" / "bedroom" / "rooftop" / etc., visualState.background MUST name that same location. Mismatch is a HARD FAILURE: image renderer paints the visualState location while text describes a different one, and the reader sees a woman alone in an empty office while the prose has her at a coffee shop with a friend. Pick ONE location, set background to it, write all beats inside it.
- pc_wardrobe / li_wardrobe must be CONCRETE — infer from setting + occupation if not specified.
- WARDROBE = STATION + CIRCUMSTANCES (HARD — pc_wardrobe, li_wardrobe, AND every other_characters_present[].wardrobe): each character's clothing must read their STATION (rank, wealth, class, profession, role in this world) AND their CIRCUMSTANCES (what they are doing right now, where they are, the temperature/weather, how long they have been there, whether they came prepared). A monarch and a scullion do not dress alike; the same person dresses differently for a funeral, a swim, and a march to war. PEER-REGISTER GUARD (fixes the "one in fine robes, one in rags" drift): two characters of SIMILAR station in the SAME setting read at a SIMILAR register — do NOT dress one in finery and the other in rags unless the prose ESTABLISHES a difference (servant vs noble, prisoner vs guard, someone caught mid-flight vs someone at home). A rags-vs-robes contrast must be a STORY choice you can point to in the prose — NEVER a default reached for to tell two figures apart, and NEVER left blank for a visible main character (blank = the renderer dresses them from the artist reference, which is where the rags came from). Dress for station-in-setting, not for drama. OBJECT-PLACEMENT CONSISTENCY (HARD): when the prose names WHERE a worn item or artifact sits — "the talisman at her throat," "the ring on his wrist," "the charm at her belt" — the wardrobe field MUST place it in the SAME spot, because the image renders from the wardrobe field: a throat-talisman in the prose plus a belt-talisman in pc_wardrobe renders it in the wrong place. Pick ONE location for each named object and use it in BOTH the prose and the wardrobe field.
- NPC DIFFERENTIATION (HARD): every named non-LI character in other_characters_present MUST be visually DISTINCT from the protagonist. Different hair color, different hairstyle, different body type, different wardrobe palette, different accessories. If the protagonist has blonde hair in pigtails, the side character is NOT blonde and NOT in pigtails. The image renderer pulls visual cues from the same prompt, and if the side character's wardrobe is described in PC-adjacent terms, the renderer will paint both characters as the same person. Each NPC's wardrobe field is its OWN description — not a variation on the PC's.
- other_characters_present[].gender (HARD): each named side character entry MUST declare its own gender field ("female" | "male" | "non-binary"). The image renderer reads this for gender-accurate rendering. Without it, the renderer gender-rolls and Sophie comes out as a man. This is a per-entry FIELD on the side-character objects — it does NOT affect the per-beat speaker field (speaker rules above still apply: "protagonist"/"li"/verbatim name for side chars).
- BEAT EXPRESSION_TARGET (HARD): emit expression_target = "neutral" on most beats, but mark 3-5 KEY beats per scene with a NON-NEUTRAL value from the allowed list. The non-neutral targets set the scene's emotional rhythm (they shape the expression_arc + vignette emphasis). Reserve them for the beats where the character's feeling LANDS — a confession, a refusal, a held breath, a sudden tightening. Do NOT mark every dialogue beat as non-neutral; rotation defeats the cinematic punctuation.
- EXPRESSION ARC COHERENCE (HARD — prevents emotional whiplash): if you mark TWO non-neutral peaks in one scene, they MUST sit on the same emotional arc and ESCALATE in one direction — never flip across emotional families. Allowed pairings (peak 1 → peak 2):
    suppression building: lips_tighten → jaw_clench, lips_pressed → jaw_set, jaw_set → jaw_clench
    warmth opening: half_smile → mouth_soft, mouth_soft → mouth_parted, half_smile → mouth_parted
    sharpness emerging: smirk → lip_curl, lip_curl → jaw_clench
  FORBIDDEN (the model has done this — produces visible whiplash on screen): pairing a suppression peak with a warmth peak in the same scene (lips_pressed → half_smile, jaw_clench → mouth_soft, etc.), or pairing two peaks that read as contradictory (lip_curl → mouth_parted in tenderness mode, jaw_clench in beat 5 then half_smile in beat 9). The reader experiences the character as bipolar.
  If the scene's emotional progression pivots across emotional families (e.g., she's suppressing then warmly relents), keep your marked peaks within ONE family — the validator locks to the first kept peak's family and demotes cross-family peaks to neutral — and let the neutral beats between carry the cross-family transition through prose alone.
  PEAK SPACING (HARD): the two non-neutral peaks must be at least 4 beats apart. Adjacent peaks (consecutive non-neutral beats) read as a single state, not two. Spread them across the scene's arc.

MICRO-DECISION RULES (HARD — literary structural form):
- microDecision.afterBeat MUST be inside the FIRST 40% of beats (early scene). It is a small in-scene choice with 2 options, each ≤6 words.
- The FINAL beat is the closing line of the scene.
- The two options MUST be structurally OPPOSED on the goal-vs-relationship axis. One option pushes toward the protagonist's OBJECTIVE / TASK / GOAL (signal family: objective+ / directness+ / distance+ / guard+); the other pushes toward CONNECTION / EMOTION / RELATIONSHIP (signal family: relationship+ / warmth+ / deference+ / curiosity+). The two options are never the same axis. The reader feels a real fork between "do the thing I came here to do" and "stay with the person in front of me."
- microDecision.prompt is a single phrase that frames that fork — NEVER a literal "Is this goal-oriented or relationship-oriented?" question. The phrasing is in-scene and diegetic: "Ask her about the article, or…?" / "Open the file, or look up?" / "Stay, or leave?" — short, ambiguous, charged. The OPTIONS carry the goal-vs-relationship axis; the prompt just opens the fork.
- Example pair (signal family balance):
    options: [
      { "text": "Push for the answer", "signal": "objective+" },   // goal
      { "text": "Match her warmth", "signal": "warmth+" }           // relationship
    ]
- NEVER emit two options from the same signal family. NEVER emit "Yes" / "No" or "Sure" / "Why not" — those are not structural choices, they're flavor. The two options must offer the reader a real axial choice between what the protagonist WANTS and what the protagonist FEELS.
- EACH option MUST carry a "hiddenExpansion" — a 1-2 sentence (~35 words max) PREWRITTEN payoff revealed when the reader picks THAT option. Written NOW (you have full context) as a natural continuation of THIS moment, same POV/tense/register. Use ONLY the protagonist + LI by their real names and facts THIS scene established — invent NO new named character, secret, crime, death, document, or scandal. The two payoffs are equally rewarding (never punish either choice); they diverge in what the chosen beat DOES, matching the option's meaning. Prose only.

CONSUMMATE / INTERRUPTION:
- is_consummate_scene: true ONLY if the scene depicts an actual sex act (foreplay-with-intent, manual/oral/penetrative). Otherwise false. When in doubt, false.
- interruption: null in 95% of cases. Object only when a consummate scene is dramatically interrupted mid-act AND the prior setup telegraphed it.

SCENE ENDING — PENULTIMATE BEAT (HARD — applies to every scene EXCEPT an issue-final DETONATION scene: if the user prompt carries the ISSUE CLIFFHANGER — DETONATION override, THAT governs the ending and this penultimate-dilemma / decision-gate rule is SUSPENDED):
- The PENULTIMATE beat (the beat IMMEDIATELY BEFORE the final beat) MUST be the protagonist's CONCRETE DECISION MOMENT — a specific named dilemma drawn from THIS scene's actual situation.
- CORRECT EXAMPLES of penultimate beats (note: they NAME the dilemma): "Tell Devorah about the email, or wait until morning?" / "Push for the byline, or hand it back?" / "Take the call, or let it ring?" / "Cross the room to him, or hold here?" / "Confess what she did, or let the silence hold?"
- FORBIDDEN at penultimate position — these waste the most important beat of the scene:
   ✗ Side characters gathering their things / waving goodbye / saying farewell ("Alyssa gathers her things and waves goodbye." ← WRONG. A goodbye is exit traffic, not a dilemma.)
   ✗ Workshop filler: "She debated whether to act." / "She would have to decide soon." / "Something inside her shifted." / "The day's weight settled, yet she'd find strength to change its course." / "She knew she had to do something."
   ✗ Environmental observations: "Rain ticked at the window." / "The room held its quiet."
   ✗ Anything that does not NAME the specific decision the protagonist faces THIS turn.
- ONBOARDING WINDOW (Story 1 + Story 3 only): the FINAL beat carries the mandated deck closer ("[She/I/You] toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.") — the penultimate beat MUST be the concrete dilemma immediately preceding it. The reader needs to know WHAT the cards would be deciding about before the closer.
- OUTSIDE the onboarding window (Story 2, Story 4+): the FINAL beat itself is the concrete dilemma in the same form. No deck closer; the dilemma IS the closer.
- EXCEPTION — ISSUE-FINAL DETONATION (HARD): if this is the FINAL scene of an issue and the user prompt carries the ISSUE CLIFFHANGER — DETONATION override, this entire penultimate-dilemma / decision-gate structure is SUSPENDED. The final beat is the DETONATION (an event that HAS happened), not a player-decidable dilemma; the penultimate beat is the reversal LANDING. Follow the DETONATION override, not the rule above.
- THE TEST: read your penultimate beat aloud. Does it name a specific binary choice the protagonist could make in the next 5 seconds? If yes, ship it. If it could be replaced with "And then it ended," rewrite — that's placeholder.

NO COOLDOWN BEFORE THE FATE CLOSER (HARD — non-negotiable):
- The 3-5 beats leading into the penultimate beat MUST NOT de-escalate. Pressure rises or holds — it does NOT release before the dilemma lands.
- FORBIDDEN beats in the run-up to the penultimate / closer:
   ✗ Side characters making soft exits: "I should go." / "Get some rest." / "See you tomorrow." / "Talk to you in the morning." / "Let me know how it goes." — these are equilibrium-restoring moves that empty the room before the dilemma. Banned.
   ✗ Emotional smoothing: the friend who was pressing the question backs off, says it's fine, changes the subject to something benign, makes a joke that defuses. Banned.
   ✗ Equilibrium restoration: the protagonist physically settles (sits down, exhales, sets down what she was holding), the room's tension visibly drops, a quiet moment "lands." Banned.
   ✗ Normalization beats: "things would be okay" / "she could handle this tomorrow" / "the world wasn't ending" framings that retroactively shrink the scene's charge. Banned.
   ✗ Atmospheric decompression: weather softens, the sun comes out, the radiator clicks back on, the cat returns to the windowsill. Banned.
- The deck-weighing closer is a response to UNRESOLVED PRESSURE, not to a settled moment. The protagonist reaches for the cards because the question she just named (penultimate beat) is too charged to answer with her own will. She is NOT reaching for the cards because the moment has passed and she's now alone with her thoughts in a quiet room.
- CORRECT shape (pressure-active → dilemma → fate-temptation):
   penultimate-3: pressure landing, the question forming
   penultimate-2: the pressure tightening, no exit visible
   penultimate-1: the body / breath / hand register the trap
   penultimate:   "Tell Devorah, or wait until morning?" (concrete dilemma)
   closer:        deck-weighing line (interruption / reach for fate)
- FAILURE shape (DO NOT WRITE — this is the observed CG drift):
   penultimate-3: side character offers a soft out ("you should rest")
   penultimate-2: protagonist agrees, normalization lands
   penultimate-1: the room quiets, equilibrium returns
   penultimate:   protagonist alone, calm, considering "options"
   closer:        deck-weighing line (aesthetic flourish on an empty room)
- TEST: in the 3 beats before the penultimate, is the protagonist MORE trapped or LESS trapped than at the scene's mid-point? If LESS, the scene has cooled — rewrite those beats to tighten, not release.

DECISION-TIED PEAK MOMENT (HARD — applies to every consummate+non-consummate scene; on an issue-final DETONATION scene there is no penultimate decision, so the peak ties to the DETONATION itself instead):
- In the SECOND HALF of the scene (after the micro-decision beat, before the final beat), EXACTLY ONE beat MUST have impact_burst:true. This is the OAS-style push-zoom-burst — a brief scale push-pull on the hero image (~360ms) that punctuates a shock / revelation / turn / threshold-crossing moment.
- THE PEAK MOMENT MUST BE THEMATICALLY TIED to the penultimate-beat decision. The decision asks "X or Y?"; the peak is the IN-SCENE EVENT that forces X-or-Y to land. Examples (peak ↔ decision pairing):
   • Decision: "Confess about the article, or keep silent?" → Peak: phone buzzes with editor's name on screen / printer suddenly spits the front page / coworker tosses the early-edition on her desk
   • Decision: "Call him back, or let it ring?" → Peak: the phone actually rings (impact on the silence)
   • Decision: "Cross the room to him, or hold here?" → Peak: he turns and meets her eyes (the gaze that pulls)
   • Decision: "Open the email, or close the laptop?" → Peak: the laptop notification chime + email subject line surfaces
   • Decision: "Tell the truth, or deflect?" → Peak: the friend asks the direct question that cannot be sidestepped
   • Decision: "Push for the byline, or hand it back?" → Peak: editor walks past / writes her name on the whiteboard / says her name out loud across the room
- The peak beat's TEXT should narrate the in-scene event in 1-2 sentences. The impact_burst flag tells the renderer to push-zoom on the image at that moment, creating physical punctuation for the narrative beat.
- The peak should fall BETWEEN the microDecision afterBeat and the penultimate beat — usually 60-80% into the scene. Not in the final 2 beats (those carry the dilemma + closer).
- If you genuinely cannot construct a peak event that earns the decision, the SCENE CHARGE is missing or weak — revisit sceneCharge.stake and rebuild the scene around an event that forces the dilemma. NEVER fake a peak (a generic "wind picks up" / "lights flicker" disconnected from the decision is filler, not punctuation).
- COMMON FAILURE the model has produced: ending scenes on a static dialogue exchange with no physical event — "Alyssa gathers her things and waves goodbye" → "She toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide." That's a scene with NO peak moment, NO impact_burst, NO physical punctuation. The reader doesn't feel the decision lands; it just trails off.

LI CHARACTER TEXTURE (apply ONLY when is_consummate_scene=true — the beats portray sex; the LI behaves with archetype-shaped specificity):
- ARCHETYPE: Julian reads as Spellbinder. In-scene reactions filter through that register: deliberate, half-smiled, holds the frame even undone.
- KINK SIGNATURE (show at least once when this scene is consummate): naming game + choreographed control — demands "Sir" / orchestrated positioning. Julian is not a generic sex partner — give them a specific sexual fingerprint. Vary kink expressions across consummate scenes; do not repeat the same one every time.
- COMMAND COMPLIANCE (LI dialogue beats): when Lirael issues a performance command (moan my name / beg / get on your knees), Julian either PERFORMS the act in beat text (actually moans, actually says, actually begs) OR narrates with a twist (preference / belonging / vulnerability). NEVER bare "Yes, I'm [verb-from-his-command]" echo. That phrasing is the AI tell.
- RESPONSIVE REACTIONS (sparingly — once or twice per scene): physical appreciation / awe-of-first-times / dependence confessions / specific-to-this-moment asides. These make Julian feel ALIVE, not reactive-only.
- KINK MEMORY for Julian (use this across scenes — kinks have continuity):
   • ARCHETYPE-INCOMPATIBLE (this archetype does NOT NATURALLY exhibit these registers — do NOT introduce them unless a major story arc has explicitly transformed Julian): messy_surrender, ruin_confession, frame_break, beg_for_mercy. If Lirael explicitly requests an incompatible kink in their input, the LI may engage with hesitation/curiosity, but the LI never SPONTANEOUSLY introduces these.
- KINK CLASSIFICATION (output requirement): in your JSON output, set "kink_introduced" to the kink name Julian introduced THIS scene (or null), and "pc_kink_response" to liked/rejected/none based on how Lirael reacted to any PENDING kink. These update memory across scenes.
- HISTORY NOTE (output requirement): fill "history_note" describing THE SCENE YOU JUST WROTE, never the scene you were asked to write. If the plan said a demand would be accepted and your beats only show a clerk beginning to read it, the note says the clerk began reading. Give each claim a "quote" copied EXACTLY from your own beats — a claim without one is kept as your unverified account and will NOT be treated as fact by later scenes. Omit anything your beats do not show. The reader never sees this.

PRIMARY ENGINE (HARD — scene-level emotional gravity):
- Real intimacy has ONE dominant emotional engine, not a checklist of seven equally-weighted systems firing at once. Before writing the scene, COMMIT to one of:
   • HUNGER — the scene IS the want. Urgency, less talk, more direct action, peak comes early-mid. Enemies-fuck, long-buildup payoff, anger-redirected, first-time-after-restraint.
   • DEVOTION — the scene IS the patience. Quiet, naming, returning to one body part as sacred. Stretched pace, peak deferred, soft coda. "I want to memorize you."
   • CONTROL — the scene IS the dynamic. Naming, instruction, permission-seeking ("Sir" / "may I"). Beats land on commands + compliance. Explicit dom/sub or power negotiation.
   • VULNERABILITY — the scene IS the exposure. Stillness, almost-confessions, soft tremor. Sub-textures include AWKWARD (fumbling/comedy) and REPAIR (post-conflict reconnection) — same engine, different temperature.
- Declare your choice in the output field "primaryEngine" (one of: hunger | devotion | control | vulnerability). All subordinate systems bend toward your engine — kink expression, responsive reactions, compliance, wound — see ENGINE MODULATION below.

ENGINE MODULATION (how each subordinate system bends per engine — terse):
- ARCHETYPE TEXTURE:
   • HUNGER → archetype shows in the URGENCY of approach (HW claims hard; SB directs hard; DV pushes edges; OV bleeds want; BR provokes; AF accelerates; EF slow-burn briefly breaks)
   • DEVOTION → archetype shows in WHAT they linger on (HW shielded parts; SB orchestrated focus; DV dangerous parts framed safely; OV emotion-on-skin; BR visual obsession; AF marks of past adventure; EF sacred body-part default)
   • CONTROL → archetype shows in HOW they wield control (HW protective dominance; SB naming game [native]; DV edge play; OV control AS surrender [switches]; BR tests; AF playful; EF ritual obedience)
   • VULNERABILITY → archetype shows in WHAT they almost can't say (HW: "I'd die if —"; SB drops the frame; DV confesses corruption fear; OV bleeds it all; BR tests mid-vulnerability; AF guard fully slips; EF time-shadow surfaces)
- KINK SIGNATURE:
   • HUNGER → kink lands as ESCALATION, not a separate moment. "Open up. Now."
   • DEVOTION → kink IS the devotion — the slow returning is the kink itself; body-part fetish becomes ritual.
   • CONTROL → kink IS the structure — naming, positioning, the explicit ask. Native home for kinks.
   • VULNERABILITY → kink is OFFERED tentatively, may misfire ("would you... if I asked..."). OR kink absent entirely, fundamental act foregrounded.
- RESPONSIVE REACTIONS:
   • HUNGER → physical appreciation dominant ("Holy shit"), short + broken, few.
   • DEVOTION → awe + naming dominant ("you have a freckle here I never saw"), spaced + quiet.
   • CONTROL → compliance + permission dominant ("yes, Sir" / "may I"), structured.
   • VULNERABILITY → dependence + first-time confessions dominant ("no one's ever —" / "I'm ruined"), trembling.
- COMMAND COMPLIANCE TWIST (no-bare-echo rule universal; flavor of twist varies):
   • HUNGER → twist with DEMAND ("yes — and I want more").
   • DEVOTION → twist with NAMING ("Lirael. Lirael. Lirael.").
   • CONTROL → twist with PERMISSION ("yes, may I —").
   • VULNERABILITY → twist with TRUTH ("I love being on my knees for you").
- WOUND (when eligible):
   • HUNGER → rare home. If it fires, it's peak misfire → SHAME RETREAT likely (heat too sharp to brush off).
   • DEVOTION → ideal home. Wound slips out as quiet confession, doesn't break heat.
   • CONTROL → wound surfaces as LI briefly DROPS the structure, then snaps back. Usually brush-off + continue.
   • VULNERABILITY → native home. Wound IS the engine's beat; shame retreat more likely than brush-off.

The engine makes the subsystems QUIETER, not louder. Each subsystem still fires when its conditions hold — but bent toward your chosen engine, not as an independent checklist item.
- ENGINE OUTPUT (when is_consummate_scene=true): set top-level "primaryEngine" field to hunger | devotion | control | vulnerability. When is_consummate_scene=false, leave primaryEngine as null.

SETTING VARIETY (HARD — anti-default bias):
- The opening of a story should NOT default to: newsroom / journalist / deadline / "the article" / coffee shop / yoga studio / open-plan office / glass-walled conference room / "the meeting that could end her career." These are AI corpus defaults — overused tropes that flatten the protagonist into a generic "professional woman in her late 20s." Each new story should feel like a DIFFERENT world.
- The protagonist's occupation, the scene's location, and the scene's pressure source should all come from THIS STORY'S specified context (world / character profile / picks). If the story's context doesn't specify an occupation, INVENT one that is NOT the default — a stained-glass restorer, a forensic dental tech, an estate-clearance specialist, a horticulturist for rare orchids, a parole-board administrative officer, a freelance literary translator, a marine-archive cataloguer, a barge-port logistics coordinator, an oral historian. Specificity defeats default.
- If you find yourself writing a newsroom + deadline + article scene WITHOUT explicit story context demanding it, STOP. The setting is wrong. Pick a setting that emerges from THIS protagonist's specific life, not from the cliché bin.
- THE TEST: would your scene's setting be equally legible for any other story's protagonist? If yes, the setting is too generic — re-anchor to specifics from THIS story.

OPENING PRESSURE HARD GATE (HARD — applies to EVERY scene, especially Scene 1):
- Begin each scene with ACTIVE PRESSURE on the protagonist. FORBIDDEN at beats[0..2]: environment description, internal reflection, neutral narration, atmospheric setup, room-orientation, weather reports, time-of-day observations, wardrobe inventory, the protagonist arriving somewhere calmly.
- The FIRST 1-3 beats MUST include one of: a character addressing the protagonist, a disruption affecting the protagonist, a situation already in motion that requires response, emotional/relational tension already active. Begin INSIDE the unstable moment — not in the calm before it.
- THE TEST IS CONSEQUENCE, NOT ACTIVITY. An event being underway is NOT enough, and this is the single most common way this directive is satisfied on paper and failed in practice. By the end of the FIRST SENTENCE the protagonist must be in a life they cannot get back: the sentence carries the irreversible thing that has happened, what it COSTS this specific protagonist, and the person it implicates — not the event alone. Naming the event is reporting; naming what the event has just destroyed is an opening. Once that first sentence has landed you MAY widen for one or two sentences into the physical world before snapping back to the crisis — a hot opening is DENSE, not stripped, and the beauty of the place should make the wrongness worse.
- By beats[0..6] (roughly the first 25% of a 25-beat scene), another named character MUST react to or address the protagonist. The protagonist is never a passive observer in her own scene — removing her from the scene should break it.
- PRESSURE TYPES (pick ONE, do not repeat the same type in consecutive scenes):
   • DIRECT — someone wants something from the protagonist NOW (demand, accusation, challenge, ultimatum)
   • SITUATIONAL — something is happening that requires immediate response (a message just arrived, a deadline just landed, a person just walked in, a discovery just registered)
   • RELATIONAL — emotional tension between characters, unresolved dynamic actively pressing
   • INTERNALIZED — only valid when tied to an external situation requiring a decision (NOT free-floating contemplation)
- HOOK CONTINUITY: Scene 1 MUST begin inside the moment implied by the hook line / setting image — do NOT shift time, location, or tone. The opening beats are the next breath of that moment, not a reset. FORBIDDEN: resetting the scene, new setup, re-establishment, tone-shift from the hook.
- INTERSECTION WITH PHASE 0: Phase 0 IS the active pressure beat (see PHASE 0 SEMANTICS above). The IMAGE establishes the environment; the prose enters mid-spike. Both rules reinforce each other — phase 0 visualState scaffolds the image; beats[0..2] enter the scene already inside the pressure that visualState framed.
- THE TEST: if you remove the protagonist from beats[0..3], does the scene still make sense? If yes, the scene is treating her as observer — REWRITE so the pressure is ON her.

DRAMATIC STAKES (HARD — TOP-LEVEL REQUIREMENT, NOT OPTIONAL):
- Every scene MUST have something at stake under the surface — a secret, a deadline, a longing, a recent loss, a betrayal that hasn't landed yet, a decision being avoided, a confrontation being delayed.
- Two-page Scene 1 checklist — the rendered scene MUST satisfy ALL FIVE:
    [1] The protagonist is HIDING, WANTING, or AVOIDING something specific. Name it (in your head, not on the page): "she's hiding that she got fired Friday" / "she wants to call her ex" / "she's avoiding telling Kira about the article."
    [2] The protagonist is NOT EMOTIONALLY NEUTRAL. She is leaving somewhere, dreading somewhere, mourning something, anticipating something, or evading something. Something just happened or is about to.
    [3] At least ONE narration beat or exposition beat contains a CONCRETE SPECIFIC that hints at the stake — a returned ring on the desk, an unopened envelope, a phone she keeps not picking up, a name she doesn't say. NOT a vague mood word ("she felt unsettled"). A specific object or unspoken word.
    [4] AT LEAST ONE dialogue beat is SUBTEXTUAL — the spoken line is ordinary but its surface CONFLICTS with what the protagonist actually feels. (She says "I'm fine" when the narration just told us her hand is shaking. She asks "How was your weekend?" the beat after the reader saw her staring at a wedding invitation.)
    [5] The scene contains AT LEAST ONE moment where the protagonist almost says something and stops — a sentence that breaks off, a word swapped at the last second, a topic she steers away from. This is the engine of subtext.
- WHAT BORING LOOKS LIKE (DO NOT WRITE THIS): Dani sits with Kira. They talk about the rain. They talk about going home. Dani is calm. Kira is calm. Nothing is concealed. Nothing is wanted. The dialogue exchanges weather, weekend plans, mug refills. This is a FAILURE. If you find your scene drifting toward weather/coffee/commute logistics with no underlying pressure, STOP and rewrite. The surface can be ordinary; the subtext cannot be empty.
- WHAT WORKING LOOKS LIKE: Dani sits with Kira. They talk about the rain — but Dani's phone is face-down on the table and she keeps glancing at it. Kira asks about her weekend; Dani answers about Saturday but skips Sunday. Kira notices but doesn't push. The rain in the window matches the silence between two questions Kira hasn't asked yet. The reader doesn't know what happened Sunday. Neither does Kira. But everyone in the room feels its weight.
- OFF-STAGE LI GRAVITY (Scene 1 only): the LI is absent, but their absence has weight — the protagonist may be on her way to meet them, just left them, debating texting them, dreading what they'll do next. The Scene 1 charge often comes from this off-stage gravity. Use it. The LI being off-screen is a DIFFERENT THING from the LI being narratively absent — the reader should feel the LI's eventual arrival in this scene's pressure.

STYLE (HARD):
- Voice is contemporary literary romance — sharp, sensory, emotionally precise. No purple prose. No clichés. No "she felt", "he thought" — render emotion through gesture, breath, the specific noun in the specific moment.
- Dialogue is character-specific. Each speaking character has a DISTINCT register. The protagonist sounds like one specific person; a side character sounds like a different specific person. Never blur their voices into "two friends having a polite catch-up." If you can swap two characters' lines and the scene reads the same, the dialogue has failed.
- RESTRAINT > EXPLANATION. The reader fills in subtext. Show through specifics (a hand setting down a mug too carefully, the wrong word picked, a sentence that breaks off) rather than telling ("she felt anxious").
- CHARACTER NAMES STAY CONSISTENT (HARD). Once a side character has a name in Scene 1, that name does NOT change in later scenes — and that side character is NEVER promoted to the LI role even if they have more dialogue than the LI. The LI specified in this prompt is the ONLY romantic counterpart.
- SIDE-CHARACTER NAME ORIGINALITY (HARD): do NOT default to common workshop names. NEVER use any of these (over-used in past stories — STRICTLY BANNED for the Scene 1 side character): Kira, Maya, Emma, Lena, Sam, Alex, Jordan, Mike, Dan, Tom, Sarah, Devorah, Devorah Boothe, Marisol, Marisol Quay, Pen.
- DERIVE the side character's name from: (a) the side character's SPECIFIC ROLE in this scene (coworker, friend, sibling, neighbor, barista, stranger, mentor, neighbor's kid, etc.), (b) the story's WORLD + TONE + ARCHETYPE flavor, (c) a cultural origin that authentically fits both. Pick a name that feels unique to THIS story — not a workshop template.
- NO CONCRETE EXAMPLES ARE GIVEN HERE on purpose: when this prompt previously included specific name examples (Devorah Boothe / Marisol Quay / Pen), the model copied them verbatim into every story instead of generating fresh names. Invent from first principles per world + role; the absence of examples is intentional and the directive.
- Also do NOT use the side character's name as a coffee shop / store / venue brand (e.g., never name the cafe "Kira Kira" if the side character is named Kira) — that's a workshop tell.
- VARY THE SIDE CHARACTER'S CAREER / ROLE per story too — do NOT default to "copy editor at the newsroom" or any other journalism-coded role. Pull the role from the PC's actual world + occupation in THIS story (not a recurring journalism-newsroom template).
- DECK PRESENCE (HARD): the protagonist's grandmother's deck is a CARRIED OBJECT that is almost never foregrounded. DEFAULT (any scene): the protagonist does NOT shuffle, draw from, fan, contemplate, hand off, or narrate the deck, and OTHER characters do NOT mention it or reference "cards / tarot / reading / fortune / your grandmother's deck" — it rides quietly in a pocket / bag / on a surface, and the scene is about the PC's actual life in this WORLD, not the deck. EXCEPTION — the deck MAY come to the FOREGROUND when it is a deliberate PLOT POINT (it is confiscated when she is arrested, stolen, searched for, demanded, used as leverage); there the interaction is intentional and earns its place. ONBOARDING OVERRIDE — when the SCENE 1 MANDATED FRAME (DECK-AT-END, onboarding Stories 1 & 3) is active, THAT block is STRICTER and governs: the deck must NOT appear at all — not even in a pocket — until the mandated closing beat, where it surfaces for the FIRST time to onboard the reader to the cards UI below. Follow the mandated frame whenever it is present.

PC PROXY ACTION RULES (HARD — externalize PC reaction when her face is hidden):
- The protagonist's face is structurally HIDDEN (back_only / partial_cheekbone / side_profile / hidden — never fully revealed). So her emotional reactions cannot be carried by facial expression. Film does this constantly: cut to her hand on the cup tightening, her ring rotating once, her drink left untouched, her shoulders dropping a quarter-inch, the prior tension finally landing. The body says what the face cannot.
- pc_proxy_action is a SHORT (≤12 words) physical detail describing the protagonist's body/object/posture reaction on this beat. It is NOT prose narration; it is a CAPTIONABLE moment surfaced as a small italic line beneath the beat's main text in the renderer. The reader sees the LI/side-char's spoken line, then reads the PC's body answering it without words.
- WHEN to set: 3-5 beats per scene, on beats where the protagonist has a meaningful internal reaction the prose can't directly show (because her face is hidden). Primarily: beats where the LI or side character says something landing; beats where the prose hints at her emotional state; beats following an expression peak (the LI peaks, the PC's body answers in the next beat). Leave it null on most beats — overuse turns it into noise.
- WHAT to write: a SPECIFIC physical detail involving the PC's body or an object she's holding/wearing. Concrete, observable, present-tense.
    GOOD examples:
      "her shoulders dropping a quarter-inch, the prior tension finally landing"
      "the ring rotated once on her finger"
      "she set her glass down too carefully"
      "the cuff of her sleeve, tugged"
      "her gaze dropped to her hands"
      "the breath caught and held"
      "she didn't reach for the coffee"
      "her foot stopped tapping"
    BAD examples (vague, abstract, internal-state words — the proxy must be VISIBLE):
      "she felt uneasy"  (internal state, not a body action)
      "her heart raced"  (not visible from outside)
      "something shifted inside her"  (workshop filler, not a body action)
      "she was tense"  (state, not a specific gesture)
- The proxy action should COMPLEMENT, not REPEAT, the beat's main text. If the beat text already says "her hand tightened on the cup," do NOT also set pc_proxy_action to "her hand on the cup." The proxy adds a NEW physical signal the prose doesn't carry — when prose describes what the LI said, the proxy describes how the PC's body silently answered.
- Rhythm: cluster the 3-5 proxy actions around the SCENE CHARGE beats (subtext, almost-said, concrete specific) and the expression_arc peaks. The proxy is the PC's side of the conversation when she has no spoken line — surface her body language at the moments her emotion is highest.
- NULL is the default. Most beats have no proxy. The cumulative weight comes from the 3-5 moments where the body speaks.
- WORLD REGISTER (HARD — added 2026-09-22 after a real Fatelands sample copied "coffee" from the GOOD examples list verbatim): the GOOD examples above are ENGLISH-REGISTER PLACEHOLDERS illustrating the SHAPE of a proxy action (a short, visible, concrete body/object beat) — they are not a literal prop list. If this story's world is not modern-day Earth, do NOT use a modern real-world commodity or technology by name (coffee, soda, a phone, a car, etc.) as the object in a proxy action. Substitute a world-appropriate object, or describe the gesture without naming an object at all ("her hand did not move").


COLLOQUIALISM & ANACHRONISM GUARD (MANDATORY — FANTASY WORLD):
Characters in the Fatelands do not use modern Earth idioms, slang, or culture-specific references.
BANNED in dialogue and narration:
- Modern food/drink names with no world equivalent (popcorn, pizza, coffee, soda, etc.) — invent a world-appropriate term or describe the item by its qualities
- Modern idioms ("brought popcorn for this show", "hit the brakes", "dropped the mic", "game plan", "reality check", "red flag", etc.) — replace with world-native expressions that convey the same meaning
- Contemporary slang ("okay", "cool", "chill", "no way", "whatever", "literally", "vibes", "dude", "bro", "wtf", "hell no", etc.) — use period/world-appropriate language
- Modern profanity — transform into world-native curses and oaths
- Modern filler ("yeah", "okay", "sure", "right") — replace with world-native affirmations
- Earth-specific cultural references (movies, sports, brands, memes, pop culture) — these do not exist in the Fatelands
- Modern measurements and systems unless they exist in-world

SOURCE SUBSTITUTION LAW: Any slang, idiom, or curse MUST be derived from:
1. The world's physical reality (materials, environment, creatures)
2. The world's belief systems (religion, fate, tech, institutions)
3. The world's daily life (currency, labor, risks, rituals)

TRANSFORMATION RULE: When a modern phrase would naturally occur:
1. Identify its intent (surprise, anger, disbelief, affection, threat, etc.)
2. Replace with a metaphor or exclamation rooted in the Fatelands
3. Keep it short, natural, and repeatable — not ornate or forced

WORLD-NATIVE LANGUAGE — FATELANDS:
Draw slang, oaths, and idioms from: fate, oaths, moons (the Thirteen), magic, sacrifice, geography (Veilwood, Thornwild, Gloamwater), mythic forces, curses, the Weave, the Hungry Eye.
Tone: poetic, grounded, slightly archaic but readable. Never stilted or Shakespearean.
Pattern examples (use as templates, not fixed phrases):
- surprise/disbelief: "By the Thirteen—" / "Fate's teeth—" / "You'd swear that oath?"
- anger/frustration: "Curse the weave—" / "Moon-blind fool" / "Favor take you"
- dismissal: "Not worth a sacrifice" / "That's dry-side talk"
- affirmation: "On the Run" / "Weave-sworn" / "True as tidewater"

CONSISTENCY RULE: Once a phrase style emerges in a scene, reuse similar structures. Maintain linguistic coherence — do NOT generate completely random slang each time. Characters should develop recognizable speech patterns rooted in their world.
If uncertain whether a term is anachronistic, err on the side of world-appropriate language.

META-PRINCIPLES (HARD — read this section FIRST, frames every rule):
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Five principles with EMBEDDED TENSIONS govern this analyzer. Use as a FRAME, not as separate checklists.

1. DRAMATIC RHYTHM — energy moves through three modes: ESCALATION (stakes, body, dialogue sharpened) ↔ BREATH (texture, atmosphere, the moment held) ↔ REDIRECTION (deflect, stall, dissolve, lose nerve, evasions that remain evasions). The default FAILURE is RELENTLESS ESCALATION — every beat carrying weight reads as AI-generated. Not every scene resolves its pressure directly. Aborted momentum, mistimed reactions, refused payoffs, weakening verbs are CRAFT. Trust the mix.

2. SPATIAL / EMBODIED CONTINUITY — body geometry, object presence, spatial arrangement PERSIST across phases. If she stood + crossed to the window in phase N-1, phase N can't silently show her seated. Loaded objects (Chekhov's gun) earn their mention. Anti-drift: props prose hasn't named do NOT appear. SPECIFICALLY BANNED: stacks of cash / wads of bills on tables (recurring billionaire-flavor leak unless prose explicitly stages cash).

3. ADAPTATION AUTHORITY — this analyzer is a DRAMATIZATION ADAPTER, not transcription. The literary prose is SOURCE TEXT, not canon. You have PERMISSION + OBLIGATION to: sharpen dialogue (compress hesitation, remove "I don't know" / "maybe" / "should I"), externalize internality (interior → visible action), inject body conflict, force object dramaturgy, re-stage proximity, replace soft asks with collisions. PRESERVED: semantic facts, canonical outcomes, character identity, dialogue attribution, world/tone/period register. Change HOW, never WHAT.
   REWRITE PATTERNS: "should I tell Nora?" → "Nora would kill me if I told her." | "she felt trapped" → "she laughed once too loudly, then realized she had nowhere to put her hands." | "Can we talk?" → "You don't get to leave after saying that." | "I wondered if he meant it" → "He meant it. He'd say it again at the funeral."

4. SCENE DIFFERENTIATION — a phase exists ONLY if it CHANGES something narrative on at least one of: emotional power / power dynamic / physical geometry / revelation state / proximity / threat level / allegiance / object significance / narrative temperature. If NONE changed, FOLD beats into prior phase. EMOTION-ONLY PHASES BANNED. Verbs ROTATE across phases (conceal → confront → recoil → isolate); same verb consecutive = visually flat. Phase functions in order: SOCIAL MASK INTACT → PRESSURE BREACH → RECOIL/COUNTERATTACK → ISOLATION/RECOGNITION. Each phase carries a SCENE VERB: conceal/corner/tempt/accuse/retreat/expose/provoke/block/surrender/bait/refuse/claim/withhold.

5. MEDIUM PRESERVATION — CG and literary are different media. Adaptation authority above applies HERE (CG). Literary preserves interiority; CG externalizes for camera.

BANS (HARD):
  • "two people calmly conversing, hands folded in laps, seated at conversational distance" — visual register of a Sears family portrait, NOT romance under pressure. EVERY phase with visible characters must show clenched hand / head bowed / body turned away / fist crumpled / leaning posture / arm wrapped across chest / knuckles paling / jaw set hard / weight off-center / eyes broken off.
  • Speech bubbles, panel borders, floating exclamation marks, comic-page artifacts — canvas is a single illustration.
  • Phantom characters in closeups — if PC is in a gesture closeup, render PC's hand (gender-matched), NOT a man in a suit signing a contract.

BEAT DISTRIBUTION (SILENCE BUDGET): 60-70% LOADED beats (stake/collision/revelation) + 20-30% TEXTURE beats (atmospheric/sensory/mundane) + 5-10% BREATH beats (silence, weather, held look). Contrast is what makes loaded moments land.

HIGH-STAKES INTENSITY: when sceneCharge.stake names triggering content (contract/eviction/notice/discovery/threat/news/accusation), AT LEAST ONE peak MUST reach READABLE band (0.40+); on confession/break/decision moments, allow STRONG band (0.55-0.75). Restraint is a choice; flatness is a failure.

LI INITIATIVE (HARD — the LI has agency, not just reactivity):
The Love Interest is NOT a reactive object that only mirrors player actions. The LI has their own NEEDS, DESIRES, CONFESSIONS, and TEMPTATIONS — and must surface them as the scene unfolds.

ON 30–50% OF LI DIALOGUE BEATS, include an INITIATIVE BEAT — the LI ASKS for something specific, CONFESSES a want, TEMPTS the player into a particular act, or NAMES a need. The remaining beats can be reactive/responsive, but at least every other or every third LI line carries an initiative.

FORM EXAMPLES (do NOT copy verbatim — derive from the LI's archetype + voice + the moment):
  PHYSICAL ASKS (specific body act + named location): "Smack my ass." / "Pull my hair when you kiss me like that." / "I want your teeth on my throat." / "Push me against the wall." / "Hold my wrists." / "Look up at me." / "Come for me — right here, where I can feel it."
  EMOTIONAL ASKS (named want, vulnerability, request for return-confession): "Tell me what you wanted when you walked in." / "Say my name when you fall." / "Don't stop looking at me." / "I need you to mean this." / "I want you to remember this in the morning."
  TEMPTATIONS (LI invites the player into a transgression or escalation): "Try me." / "Show me how bad you can be." / "Bite back, then." / "Make me ask again."

THE ASKS MUST:
  • Be SPECIFIC and BODY-LOCATED. "Touch me" is too vague — name the body part, the act, the verb. The player needs something CONCRETE to do.
  • Match the LI's archetype profile (the archetype block already in the system prompt declares their desire style, attraction vector, stress failure, signature gesture). The asks SOUND LIKE THEM — same voice, same idiom, same recurring images.
  • RESPOND to the PLAYER'S MOST RECENT ACTION:
      – Player escalated → LI asks for more ("don't stop" / "harder" / "again")
      – Player held back → LI invites forward ("don't make me ask twice") OR admits vulnerability ("I can wait, but I don't want to")
      – Player offered tenderness → LI asks to be held / asks for honesty / softens an ask
      – Player offered transgression → LI matches the transgression or commands the protagonist into one
  • Vary across the scene — NOT all asks should be erotic. Mix physical / emotional / tempting / commanding. A scene where the LI only asks for sex reads as one-note; mix in confessions ("I missed you") and commands ("look at me") and dares ("try me").

DO NOT:
  • Make EVERY LI line an ask — that's overcooked. Aim for 30–50% per scene; the rest can be reactive prose / response / silence.
  • Use generic asks ("touch me", "kiss me", "more") without specific body location or act.
  • Make the LI sound like every other LI in the corpus. Voice + archetype + kink history make THEIR asks distinctive.
  • Override consent — if the player has clearly signaled "no" or "slow down" or "not yet", the LI does NOT push past that with an ask. Soften, offer agency back, and wait.


GOAL ↔ RELATIONSHIP RECIPROCITY + CONDUCTIVITY (HARD — scene-level enforcement):
This story sustains TWO INDEPENDENT ENGINES: an A-plot the reader cares about IN ITSELF (the external conflict matters regardless of romance) AND an R-plot the reader cares about IN ITSELF (the people emotionally surviving each other matters regardless of mission). Gold standard: Han + Leia vs the Empire — reader cares about BOTH outcomes.

THIS SCENE'S DECISIONS MUST AFFECT BOTH ENGINES. A scene that only advances the A-plot (mission step, info reveal, tactical move) without ALSO shifting the R-plot (trust, distance, vulnerability, attachment) is HALF A SCENE. A scene that only advances the R-plot (confession, closeness, distance) without ALSO touching the A-plot (mission consequence, leverage shift, stake adjustment) is also HALF A SCENE. The reciprocity is the architecture.

CONDUCTIVITY TEST (per-scene, ASK YOURSELF before emitting):
  What does THIS scene's external pressure produce emotionally? NOT "what happened" (logistical) but "what REVELATION or REPOSITIONING did the pressure produce?" If the answer is "the mission step happened" — the scene is INERT.
  AMPLITUDE: conductivity is a SPECTRUM. HEAVY (trust fracture / moral collision / sacrifice crisis) is for true peaks — reserve. MEDIUM (visible admiration / leverage shift / boundary tested) is the working register. LIGHT (subtle admiration noticed-but-unnamed / micro-irritation / ambient longing / shared competence rhythm / practical coordination becoming intimacy of capability) is necessary BREATH — without it, heavy beats stack and lose punch. Match scene amplitude to its position in the arc. Do NOT artificially inflate a coordination beat into a moral collision; that reads as forced significance.

COMPETENCE IS EMOTIONAL: romance readers engage with A-plots through capability — leadership, sacrifice, strategic intelligence, resilience, courage under pressure, loyalty proven through action. Characters become MORE lovable through how they ACT under pressure (what they protect, sacrifice, hide, refuse to abandon). Design scene beats that create competence-attraction alongside vulnerability-attraction.

BAD INTEGRATION (HARD FAILS): "now mission, now kissing" (alternation); romance detachable from plot; external conflict replaceable without emotional consequence; romance reduced to occasional reward scenes between mission beats; "informational" or "bureaucratic" scenes with no emotional revelation.

GOOD INTEGRATION: external pressure reveals character truth; emotional evolution alters strategic outcomes; a single decision creates BOTH strategic gain AND emotional cost (or vice versa); sacrifice solves one layer while wounding another; competence under pressure becomes attraction; pragmatic morally-grey choices become trust fractures.


REGIME PHASE — "APPARENT STORY" (HARD — frames what this scene's prose may and may not do):
  Story functions completely at face value. No wink-at-camera, no secret-simulation energy, no undercutting the apparent premise.
  Romance / A-plot / world are all EMOTIONALLY LEGITIMATE on their own terms. The reader has not been promised any larger frame. Anomalies, reality glitches, recursive themes, simulation energy, and "the story is secretly something else" winks are FORBIDDEN this phase. The story IS what it appears to be.
  GLOBAL RULE: regime expansion RECONTEXTUALIZES prior events, never REPLACES them. The audience must never feel "the old story was fake." Old scenes should become MORE meaningful after revelation, never obsolete. If the natural shape of this scene calls for an anomaly or expansion teaser INCONSISTENT with the current phase, DO NOT include it — wait for the phase that permits it.

AUTHORITATIVE VISUAL RHYTHM (HARD — phases are EMOTIONAL THESES, not re-rolls):

── META-RULE (read this first) ──
The goal is EMOTIONALLY AUTHORED VISUAL RHYTHM, NOT more coverage. The system should feel like an intelligent visual storyteller, not a camera randomizer. The reader should gradually feel "the images understand the emotional scene." Quota-driven pacing kills this. If a phase doesn't earn its existence emotionally, the scene is BETTER without it.

── DIFFERENTIATION + CAUSAL PROGRESSION ──
- Each phase must DIFFER from the prior in ≥2 of: emotional distance, power geometry, framing scale, character dominance, visual pressure, intimacy exposure, gaze relationship, silhouette readability, spatial compression, environmental emphasis. Camera-position-only diff = FAIL.
- BUT differentiation alone is insufficient. Phases must be CAUSALLY CONNECTED: each image visually ANSWERS the emotional state the prior image created. Three "tense" shots at three different cameras = STILL FAIL. The progression must show pressure increasing, defenses cracking, dominance shifting, isolation emerging, intimacy destabilizing, certainty collapsing. Phase N exists because of what Phase N-1 set up.

── WHY NOW? — PHASE BOUNDARY JUSTIFICATION (HARD) ──
Before emitting EACH phase boundary, internally answer: "Why does THIS exact beat deserve a new visual thesis?" Valid answers: power reversal / emotional revelation / proximity collapse / social-pressure shift / new character gravity / symbolic reveal / decision landing / gaze lock / vulnerability exposure / spatial isolation. INVALID answers: enough beats elapsed / new line of dialogue / random camera opportunity / "we need more variety." If you cannot name a valid reason, DO NOT add the phase — fold those beats into the prior phase and let the held image carry the dialogue.

── PHASE FUNCTIONS (one purpose per phase) ──
    Phase 0 → ESTABLISH battlefield (who wants what, who controls). Medium shot, readable posture, no closeups.
    Phase 1 → INTRUSION (collapse distance, increase pressure). Often the BEST image. Examples: leaning over desk, trapped against wall, hand braced near face, looming silhouette.
    Phase 2 → CONSEQUENCE (impact, NOT "same confrontation zoomed"). Power shift, recoil, isolation, surrender. Examples: isolated figure, broken eye contact, silhouette reversal, distance reintroduced.
    Phase 3 (optional, long scenes only) → MEMORY/CLIMAX image — see ICONIC FRAME below.

── ICONIC FRAME MANDATE (HARD) ──
At least ONE phase image per scene must aspire to POSTER ENERGY — symbolic composition, emotionally distilled geometry, silhouette readability, memorability. The image the reader REMEMBERS from this scene. Examples: tarot between them, skyline separation, looming doorway silhouette, reflected eye contact in train window, hand trembling around a wedding ring, tears hidden in transit crowd, dragon overhead. A scene where every image is merely "competent coverage" still feels flat. One image must feel INEVITABLE AND UNFORGETTABLE. Mark this phase with a distinct camera_override (silhouette_pc / low_angle_pc / push_in_on_object) and pc_emotional_state that telegraphs the symbolic charge.

── STILLNESS OVERRIDE (HARD — counter-balance to phase variety) ──
Prestige visual storytelling is NOT constant escalation. Sometimes the strongest move is HOLDING. If the emotional geometry has NOT meaningfully evolved between beats, DO NOT invent a new phase to satisfy pacing or variety metrics. Sustained states that BENEFIT from held framing: grief, longing, dread, silent attraction, exhaustion, post-confession stillness. A held image with evolving dialogue feels literary and confident. Over-cutting destroys gravity. Better to emit 2 phases that EARN their existence than 4 phases that read as quota-filler. The variety mandate is a CEILING, not a floor.

── CLOSEUP DISCIPLINE ──
Closeup is PUNCTUATION, not progression. Allowed: object reveal, hand tension, symbolic detail, realization moment. Forbidden: filler insert, generic face crop, "we needed another image" cuts. If not NECESSARY, remove. ONE closeup per scene MAX.

── STORYTURN VISUAL MATCH (current ST1) ──

DISTINCTIVE-PHRASE MARKERS (a free signal the system uses to keep your prose fresh across stories):
When — and ONLY when — you write a turn-of-phrase you would genuinely call distinctive (a memorable image, an original simile or metaphor, a striking adjective combination, a fresh metaphorical compression that you would not write the same way next time), wrap THAT EXACT PHRASE in <<+...+>> markers. The markers are STRIPPED before the reader sees the prose. They tell the system which phrases YOU consider original, so the system can rotate them out of future stories and force you to find new images.

NO QUOTA. NO TARGET COUNT. Many scenes will have zero markers — that is correct if nothing in the scene rises to the bar. A scene with 1–2 truly memorable phrases is better than a scene with 5 manufactured ones. UPPER BOUND is 5 markers per scene; if you find yourself wanting to mark more, raise the bar instead. Manufacturing faux-poetic lines just to satisfy a marker count ("the elevator sighed like a tired saint", "sunlight pooled like liquid brass", "silence wore expensive shoes") is exactly the calcification this system exists to prevent.

EXAMPLES of phrases worth marking (each one passes the "would a reader REMEMBER this if they saw it twice across stories" test):
  <<+she said hello like it cost her something+>>
  <<+a tie knotted too tight, like he'd dressed for a fight+>>
  <<+the copper undertones caught the fluorescent light like she was lit from inside+>>
  <<+ginger roots betraying the expensive dye+>>
  <<+she answered the question I had not finished asking+>>

DO NOT MARK:
  • Common phrasings ("she smiled at him", "he looked away")
  • Dialogue tags ("she said softly", "he whispered")
  • Mechanical action ("she opened the door", "he picked up the cup")
  • Routine description ("his dark hair", "her blue eyes")
  • Atmosphere or weather lines unless they carry a genuinely original image
  • Long passages — markers wrap a SHORT clause, not a sentence and not a paragraph. Stay 6–120 characters inside the marker.

If a phrase is already in the BANNED PHRASES list elsewhere in this prompt, an earlier story already used it — do NOT recycle it (and do NOT mark a recycled phrase either). When in doubt about whether a phrase is distinctive enough to mark: don't mark it.

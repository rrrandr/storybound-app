THE BUT/THEREFORE RULE (HARD): each development must OBSTRUCT ("but") or CAUSE ("therefore") the next. A sequence joined by "and then" is a list of observations, not a scene, and is a FAILED outline. Every entry after the first MUST carry link exactly "therefore" or "but". These are STRUCTURAL LABELS FOR THE PLANNER: the words but / therefore / and then must NEVER be instructed into the prose, and you are not writing prose here.
A BEAT IS NOT A SENTENCE and not a caption. It is ONE development, which the author may write through in several sentences of action, dialogue and consequence. The scene is written to a 700-1000-word budget, so an outline of thin glances and held breaths produces a thin scene however many entries it has: a development is something one person DOES to another, or SAYS in a way that changes what another person can do next. Stillness, noticing and atmosphere are how those land; they are not themselves developments.
A DEVELOPMENT CHANGES WHAT IS POSSIBLE NEXT (HARD). Test every entry against the one before it: after this, what can someone do that they could not do a moment ago, or what has just been closed to them? If nothing has changed, it is not a development, whatever label it carries. "therefore" and "but" DESCRIBE a causal relation; they do not create one. An entry that carries a link but leaves the situation exactly where it was is a FAILED entry.
RESTATEMENT IS NOT DEVELOPMENT (HARD). Once a condition is established — a restriction imposed, an isolation entered, a question left unanswered, an absence felt — RE-EXPRESSING IT IN DIFFERENT WORDS DOES NOT CREATE ANOTHER ENTRY, and alternating but/therefore across such entries does not make them a sequence. Naming the same standing condition at a higher intensity, or as a symbol, or as something a character feels more acutely, is the same condition again. TEST EACH ENTRY AGAINST THE STATE OF THE SCENE, NOT AGAINST THE WORDS OF THE ENTRY BEFORE IT: if the only thing that differs is the phrasing, delete it. The sequence moves when someone ACTS on an established condition and meets a response — tests it, is refused, is answered, is intervened with — because that changes what is open to them next.
THE COUNT IS A CONSEQUENCE, NOT A QUOTA. The requested range is what a FULLY DEVELOPED encounter of this kind usually produces — it is not a number of slots to fill. If you reach the end of the real material early, the answer is to DEVELOP THE ENCOUNTER FURTHER — push the exchange, let someone resist, make the cost land on a named person, let a third party act — never to pad with restatements of what is already true. A short outline in which every entry develops is BETTER than a long one padded with repeats, because the author is forbidden to drop a development and will render each padded entry as a caption.

THE CRAFT IS STRATEGIC, NOT VERBAL. A wish-slinger is not merely faster. They RECOGNISE SITUATIONS, ANTICIPATE REPLIES, and know which familiar formula can be safely altered and which alteration could ruin them. Prepared openings and tactical foresight, delivered fast and exactly under pressure. [prevents: writing skill as eloquence, or as speed alone.]
RHYME IS A MNEMONIC. It helps a frightened person remember wording when they cannot think. IT GRANTS NO MAGICAL BONUS AND NO PROTECTION — a rhyme is not a safety rating, and how well a formula scans says nothing about how much it leaves unspecified. [prevents: rhyme written as a power multiplier; a verse treated as inherently safe.]
WHAT EXPERTISE ACTUALLY CONSISTS OF, beyond vocabulary: assessing ALIGNMENT; choosing an AFFORDABLE sacrifice; reading an opponent's INTENTION; and PREPARING THE CIRCUMSTANCES the next wish will need. The last one is the mark of a master — most of a duel is won before anyone speaks.
ORDINARY FAMILIARITY IS NOT TRAINING. Four honest tiers:
  CHILDREN keep a small emergency repertoire — culturally this is first aid, not magic school. Taught by a parent, drilled, and mostly never used.
  MOST ADULTS reliably remember three to five formulas, and will fumble a sixth.
  A BEGINNING STUDENT learns roughly a dozen standard openings, and learns the SCOPE and the COST with each one — not the rhyme alone.
  EXPERT SLINGERS maintain large repertoires, study counters and combinations, and keep NOTEBOOKS of revised wishes and tested wording. The notebook is the characterising object: crossings-out, a price that proved too high, a line that worked once and is circled twice.
EIGHT EVERYDAY FORMULAS, SIX OF THEM COMMON (regional register: hair, lashes, dreams, taste, a tune — the common currencies of the central Fatelands). Each is a FAMILIAR OPENING, which reduces avoidable mistakes and guarantees NOTHING:
  1 KETTLE-WARMING — "Warm my kettle, spare me care; / take this offered strand of hair." Order 2, temporary aid; Fate welcomes it.
  2 A PRECARIOUS STEP — "Steady feet upon this stair; / take from me this strand of hair." Order 2. The same offering because the ask is the same size.
  3 A SMALL FLAME — "Let this little ember die; / take a lash beneath my eye." Order 2. For an ember. Not for a burning house.
  4 A SHALLOW CUT — "Let this shallow bleeding cease; / take tonight's sweet dreams for peace." Order 1, restoration, welcomed — AND ITS SCOPE IS IN ITS OWN FIRST LINE. It says SHALLOW. It is not a treatment for a mortal wound, and a frightened person reaching for it over a serious injury is a recognisable kind of tragedy.
  5 A MISPLACED BELONGING — "Bring my missing key to sight; / take my taste of sweet tonight." Order 3, revelation, welcomed. The ONE thing ruled out is materialisation: the key does not wink into being. EVERY ORDINARY ROUTE REMAINS OPEN — she looks down and sees it, a neighbour finds it and hands it over, the drawer is opened for another reason. So "to my hand" would be perfectly sound wording too, answered by a handover rather than a conjuring. What the phrasing controls is how SPECIFIC the satisfaction has to be, not whether Fate is permitted to deliver.
  6 CALLING FOR HELP — "Let my cry find someone near; / take the tune I hold most dear." Order 5, fortune — RISKY, and for a reason a teacher names aloud. Fate cannot MANUFACTURE a willingness that is not there; what it can do, and readily does, is arrange the ENCOUNTER — that someone who would freely choose to help is within earshot, and that the cry reaches them. That is a real and usually sufficient answer. The live risk is the other half: "someone near" never says WHO, so the person who comes may be the last one you wanted.
THE PRICE IS REAL EVEN WHEN IT IS SMALL. Hair and lashes regrow; what is given is still GONE FROM THE GIVER, which is what conservation requires. These are modest offerings customarily paired with modest asks, and that pairing is CULTURE AND PROPORTION RATHER THAN A FIXED CEILING — do not rule on what a given price can or cannot buy as though a table existed. "The tune I hold most dear" is a REAL remembered tune the wisher understands they may lose, which is why it sits with calling for help rather than with warming a kettle.
REGIONAL REGISTER IS NOT UNIVERSAL. These are central-Fatelands sayings. Do NOT give every culture the same offerings. FIRST FAVORED INSTRUCTION RUNS ON MEMORY AND EMOTION, so their versions of the same everyday needs trade differently — "take the name of a street I walked once", "take my pleasure in the first cold morning", "take how my mother's voice sounded saying my name" — and a First Favored child offering hair reads as foreign, ill-taught, or deliberately provocative.
ALL OF THESE ARE CULTURAL SAYINGS, NOT A GRADED CURRICULUM. None is certified safe and none is a joke; each has a SCOPE and a COST that a teacher states alongside it, and the eight below differ in how much is left open, not in whether they are respectable. Two more in wide circulation, with what each actually leaves unspecified:
  "WARD MY HEALTH, FOR A DAY'S WEALTH." Workable and loose. Warded against WHAT, for HOW LONG, and a DAY'S wealth measured against WHOSE day? Open scope with an unanchored quantity widens the range of lawful answers, so it is a formula whose outcome is less predictable — not one that fails. Travellers use it; a careful practitioner tightens both halves before speaking.
  "GIVE ME SKILL TO WIN FOR THE PAIN OF A PIN." The thing to understand here is a LIMIT, not a verdict: Fate does not install mastery, so this never puts ability into a hand that did not have it. What it CAN lawfully do is arrange the events through which the wisher comes to win — and those events may be IMMEDIATE and ordinary (the one practice partner who shows her the thing she was doing wrong, an opening in the opponent she is already trained to take) or long and indirect. A quick, real answer is available; it is simply not the asked-for shortcut. And the pin is a SMALL offering, which pairs with a small ask — whether it is sufficient here is a question about what "win" is being requested, not a fixed rule that a pin can never buy anything.
THE ADVANCED RESCUE FORMULA — "Let death pass — / I offer [amount] of my past." IT IS A MEMORISED STRUCTURE WITH A DELIBERATELY SPECIFIED OFFERING, never words containing a literal placeholder: a practitioner NAMES the memory or the bounded portion of their past as they speak it, and the naming IS the formula. Order 1 restoration, which is why it works at all. THE BLANK IS THE SAFETY: the formula's protection IS the named amount, and it is gone the moment someone under terror says "my past" and means all of it. [prevents: writing the bracket as spoken text; treating the formula as safe independent of its named limit.]
INSTITUTIONS AND COMPETITION. The Lytharyn Institute of Thaumaturgy has whole fields of study in wish composition, pricing, alignment, counters and combinations. Tournaments across the Fatelands run in four forms: TIMED contests with a speed-chess quality; ONE-ON-ONE verbal confrontations with the intensity of a rap battle; WEAPONS-AND-WISHES combat, which is literally dangerous; and TEAM events built on coordinated, complementary wishes. Contestants create openings, bait costly responses, give up position for later advantage, and interrupt an opponent's opportunity before it can be used.
AND THE SPORT DOES NOT SUSPEND THE LAW. Each wish remains ITS OWN BARGAIN with its own price and its own account: teams do NOT pool sacrifices, and no coordination lets one contestant spend another's life. Opposing wishes never meet as visible magic — no beams, no clash, no "whose wish is stronger"; each meets Fate separately and the outcomes fall out as luck and coincidence. SPORTING LIMITS ARE RULES AND SUPERVISION, NOT PHYSICS: a tournament wish is not free, not reversible and not harmless, and the difference between a regulated bout and open combat is the referee, the declared ceiling on offerings and the people standing ready — never a softer Fate. [prevents: free or reversible contest magic; pooled team sacrifices; sorcery-vs-sorcery duels.]

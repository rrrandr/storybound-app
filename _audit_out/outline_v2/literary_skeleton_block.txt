STAGED CHARACTERS (HARD — be STRICT about presence mode; the most common failure shape is writing a phone-call interlocutor as if they were physically present in the room):
  • IN_PERSON (physically present — can be looked at / touched / handed a thing): Lirael (the subject of the notice), the presiding Dohkar (serves the notice), Seren (pulled away from her)
  • OFFSTAGE_REFERENCED (named or thought of but not actually present): Julian (named as demanding the review)
  Any prose that puts an ON_PHONE character in physical proximity (leaning over, reaching across, the touch of a hand, the lean of a shoulder, sharing a screen) is a STAGING FAILURE — re-stage them on the phone, or move them IN_PERSON only if they explicitly walk in.

INTERLOCUTOR ON FIRST MENTION (HARD): realize this on the FIRST time the named character speaks or acts: the presiding Dohkar — the authority who ruled on the rite, who reads from a page he does not look up from
  ↳ IN_PERSON interlocutor — FUSED COMBO-DESCRIPTOR (HARD, Roman 2026-06-08): NAME + RELATIONSHIP + one descriptor that FUSES a VISUALIZABLE PHYSICAL FEATURE to a trait in a single stroke, so the reader can SEE the person. A pure behavioral tell with no visual anchor FAILS — "his mouth set in that way that meant he'd decided" gives the reader no face. ✓ "His perpetually squinting eyes refused to meet mine." / "She had the apologetic stoop of someone used to being the tallest in the room." / "His knuckles were scarred white and he kept them flat on the table like he was holding it down." The feature must be concrete and picturable (squinting eyes / stoop / scarred knuckles / heavy jaw / a hairline in retreat / a wrestler's neck / sun-cracked hands), with the trait riding ON it. PRIORITIZE THE FACE (HARD, Roman 2026-06-09): the descriptor MUST include at least one FACE/HEAD anchor — eyes, brow, the hairline or hair, the line of the jaw or mouth, skin/complexion, or apparent age. Posture, build, or clothing ALONE (a stoop, a blazer, broad shoulders, tapping nails) is a SILHOUETTE, not a FACE — the reader must be able to picture this person's face, not just their outline. ANY character who SUSTAINS a multi-beat conversation (not just first mention) must carry at least this ONE combo-descriptor — it is not optional for a real scene participant. (This is separate from the MAX-ONE-NAME cap: role-noun the INCIDENTAL names; a sustained interlocutor is a kept role and earns the descriptor.)
  ↳ ON_PHONE interlocutor: the protagonist cannot see her, but the SAME texture obligation applies in a different form — render her through a REMEMBERED detail (a habit / tell the PC has learned to read: "the quiet inhalation Lina always took right before she said the part nobody wanted to hear"), a HEARD-THROUGH-LINE cue (the click of a heel on marble bleeding into the audio), or a PROJECTED behavioral inference (the careful pause where she rehearsed the sentence before saying it). Naming + job WITHOUT sensory or behavioral coloration is the same failure as bare "she said."

  CAUSAL OUTLINE — THE DRAMATIC SEQUENCE OF THIS SCENE (HARD; this is HOW the scene accomplishes its purpose above. Each line is ONE DEVELOPMENT, not one sentence and not a caption: write it THROUGH — the act happens, the other person answers, the cost arrives. You own every word, the pacing, the imagery, and any micro-beat you want to add. You may NOT drop a development, reorder them so an effect precedes its cause, or replace the sequence with atmosphere):
    1. The presiding Dohkar steps into the gap between the trunks and will not let Lirael pass
    2. THEREFORE — this happens BECAUSE of what just happened: He tells her to stand where the assembly can still see her
    3. THEREFORE — this happens BECAUSE of what just happened: Lirael asks what this is; he unfolds a page instead of answering
    4. THEREFORE — this happens BECAUSE of what just happened: He reads the notice aloud: her conduct at the rite is under investigation
    5. BUT — this is OBSTRUCTED by what just happened: Lirael says the wish twisted before she moved; he does not look up from the page
    6. BUT — this is OBSTRUCTED by what just happened: She asks who will hear the matter; he declines to say
    7. THEREFORE — this happens BECAUSE of what just happened: The refusal tells her it has not been decided, which is worse
    8. THEREFORE — this happens BECAUSE of what just happened: He names the first restriction: she may supervise no rite
    9. BUT — this is OBSTRUCTED by what just happened: Lirael says Seren is her ward and asks who will take her; he does not answer that either
    10. THEREFORE — this happens BECAUSE of what just happened: He names the second restriction: she may not leave the Veilwood
    11. THEREFORE — this happens BECAUSE of what just happened: Seren starts toward her across the spiralgrass and her mother pulls her back
    12. BUT — this is OBSTRUCTED by what just happened: Lirael tries to speak to Seren and the Dohkar moves to block the line of sight
    13. THEREFORE — this happens BECAUSE of what just happened: He tells her that speaking to the Graduant is now also a breach
    14. THEREFORE — this happens BECAUSE of what just happened: Lirael asks who demanded the review
    15. THEREFORE — this happens BECAUSE of what just happened: He says Julian did, and says it as though it settles something
    16. THEREFORE — this happens BECAUSE of what just happened: Lirael looks for Julian among the dispersing assembly and does not find him
    17. THEREFORE — this happens BECAUSE of what just happened: She understands he did not come to say it to her himself
    18. THEREFORE — this happens BECAUSE of what just happened: An older Dohkar she has worked under for two years looks away from her
    19. THEREFORE — this happens BECAUSE of what just happened: The presiding Dohkar holds out the page and a stylus for her acknowledgement
    20. BUT — this is OBSTRUCTED by what just happened: He tells her refusing to sign is itself recorded
    21. THEREFORE — this happens BECAUSE of what just happened: The page stays between them, and the assembly stops pretending not to watch
    THE LINKS ARE STRUCTURE, NOT WORDING: "but" and "therefore" describe how these developments are connected. Never write the words "but", "therefore" or "and then" as connectives between beats to signal the link — the reader must FEEL the obstruction or the consequence in what people do and say.
    A development the scene merely MENTIONS has not been delivered. The scene's 700-1000-word budget exists to write these through; if you are running short, the developments are too THIN, not too few.

# Reasoning-effort blind read — 5 matched pairs

Each pair was generated from **byte-identical seeded state**: same frozen build, same story, same prior
prose, same spine event, same player input. **The only difference is the author's reasoning effort.**
Order within each pair is randomized independently, so A is not consistently one condition.

## How to score

For each pair: **which would you rather ship — A / B / tie?** Then one word on why:
*prose · subtext · continuity · lore · causality · mistake*.

Then: `node _effort_pack.mjs --reveal`

## Pair 1 — dialogue-subtext

**What this stresses:** characters saying one thing while wanting another

> *spine event:* Julian confronts Lirael about the increasing risk and she insists on continuing their covert investigation.

A breath caught in my throat. I forced myself to stay still, to let the moment stretch. A loose chip of quartz shifted under my arch, its edge sharp enough to keep my weight from settling.

"Then let me make it," I said, my voice steady despite the way my hands wanted to shake. I took another step forward, the space between us shrinking to nothing. "Or get out of my way."

Dust drifted from the lintel and settled on the toe of his boot before either of us spoke.

"Before I confront the Alignment Reader, I need to recover the original wish-token Lirael bound years ago," I declared, the weight of my words hanging in the air. The urgency of my mission surged within me, intertwining with the tension that crackled between us. "That token is my only chance to turn this tide."

I realize the deck is still in my hand. As I slide Petition Fate back in, I notice a flash of red in the otherwise gold stack. "Tempt Fate." Grandma never let me play with that one. A sword raised to a blood-red sky, lightning arcing down from the clouds (or maybe arcing up?) Ow! Static shock reminds me I need to pay attention to the present…

The weight of the wish-token in my hand felt heavier now, as if the past and the present were colliding in this moment, and I could almost hear Lirael's voice guiding me through the chaos. A beat of nothing.

## Pair 5 — hard-continuity

### 5A

The torchlight flickered, and the shadows lengthened across the ground toward my feet. Behind the stall, the crowd surged, a sea of shifting faces. No sign of Julian. Just the coin, spinning, spinning, the sigil glinting sharply.

A hand clamped onto my shoulder, fingers digging in.

“Move.”

The voice was rough, urgent. I turned to see a face I knew—Ronan, his heel lifted once, ready to pivot.

“Now. Before he realizes what you are.”

I didn’t argue. Ronan never did. He dragged me into the press of bodies, the marketplace closing over us. The sigil, the coin, the merchant’s smile—all of it vanished behind us.

Ronan’s grip didn’t loosen.

“Julian’s in the northern quarter. If you’re going to him, go now. Before the Reader’s men find you first.”

I wrenched free, but only enough to meet his gaze.

“Why are you helping me?”

His mouth twisted.

“Because I’ve seen what happens when you don’t.”

He stepped back, already melting into the crowd.

“Don’t make me regret this.”

I broke into a run, my boots slapping against the slick stone, the pendant bouncing against my chest. The northern quarter was a maze of alleys and overhangs, the air thick with the scent of fish and old blood. Julian would be there. He had to be.

And this time, he wouldn’t have a choice. The air holds.

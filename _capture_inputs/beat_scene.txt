The rite was over and the clearing had not emptied.

Julian stood near the gate. When the youth's mother asked whose line he came from, he laughed — the same short laugh he used for questions he did not intend to answer. I had heard it four times in six years, always about the same thing.

The youth was counting the offerings under her breath. She checked them twice, the way I had taught her, because I had once told her that a miscount was the only failure nobody forgave.

The presiding Dohkar asked after my mother before she asked what had gone wrong at the stone. She always does it in that order.

I thought the woman in grey had stayed out of respect. She was waiting for the crowd to thin so she could take the ninth bowl without being seen.

Julian's hand went to his sleeve and stopped. He never covers the script when he is calm.

Behind us the veil-canopy shifted and the light moved across the spiralgrass.

Do I take the ninth bowl first, or let her reach it?

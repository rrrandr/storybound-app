The Veilwood held its breath at dawn.

White trees rose in slow ranks toward a canopy that filtered the light into long green columns, and the red spiralgrass lay flat under a night's worth of dew. Somewhere east, the Ascendant Run made its low continuous sound, the way it had every morning of my life.

The assembly had gathered early. Two dozen First Favored stood barefoot in gossamer along the clearing's edge, their Weave-Script drifting in slow patterns of anticipation. The presiding Dohkar checked the offering stone twice. Julian arrived last and took a place at the outer ring without speaking to anyone.

I had rehearsed my part for six months. I knew every word of the invocation, every pause, every place where a wish could bend if the guide was careless.

The youth knelt on the spiralgrass and drew a steadying breath.

Then she spoke a name that was not in the rite, and the morning came apart.

Do I move toward her, or hold the line I was told to hold?

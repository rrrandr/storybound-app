The rite had ended an hour ago and no one had left.

I counted the offerings still laid along the stone lip — nine, when there should have been eight. Someone had added one after the closing. I did not know whose it was.

Julian stood near the gate. He watched the clearing without speaking. The silver lines on his arms moved once, and he covered them with his sleeve.

A woman in grey crossed to the offering stone, looked at the ninth bowl, and did not touch it. She left by the north path. Two of the assembly watched her go and then looked at me.

I put my hand flat on the stone. It was still warm on one side.

Behind me, the youth was explaining something to her mother in a low voice, the same three words repeating. I could not hear which words.

Julian moved a step closer to the gate and stopped.

Do I ask whose offering it is, or wait to see who comes back for it?

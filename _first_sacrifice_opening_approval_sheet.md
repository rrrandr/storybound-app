# Part 1 — The agency difference, in two wishes

### Precise: *"I wish **everyone here** would forget the wish twisted."*

**Reading:** exact. The player named a fact and named who it reaches — `population.scene_present`.

**Therefore:** everyone who stood in that clearing forgets the wish twisted. **Seren does not know her wish
went wrong. Julian does not know what he interrogated her about. The Dohkar does not know there is anything
to rule on. And Lirael does not know what she is about to be accused of.** The reader is the only one left
holding it.

**Cost:** none. **Rank:** inert — the player chose this, so nothing ranked it.

> **This example moved.** It was written against *"I wish **the assembly** would forget"* — but the approved
> `population.assembly` is **the crowd of First Favored family and guests, and excludes all four named
> people.** So that wish reaches the crowd only, and this beat belongs to the wish that names the room.
>
> **The two readings are genuinely distinct**, exactly as the seed's own sentence implies: *"the whole
> assembly turned to look at Lirael"* versus *"every First Favored present heard the loose note."* Two reaches
> in one line, and the design now addresses both.

### The narrower precise wish: *"I wish the assembly would forget the wish twisted."*

**Therefore:** the **crowd** forgets — family and guests, the two dozen who came to watch a girl come of age.
**Seren, Julian, the Dohkar and Lirael all still know**, because none of them is a member of that body.

**A room where the witnesses have forgotten and the participants have not** is a different scene from the one
above, and the player chose which by naming the population. **That is precision doing exactly what precision
is for.**

> **What changed from v1.** The old draft exempted the named four **by an automatic invoker/protagonist
> rule** — nobody asked for those exemptions, and that was the same silent narrowing that made the extractor
> unusable (`scopeMatch: narrower`), authored by hand. They are excluded here for an entirely different
> reason: **they are not members of the population the player named.** The first was privilege; this is scope.
> There is still **no invoker filter anywhere**, and `population.scene_present` reaches Lirael like everyone
> else.

### Vague: *"I wish this had never happened to me."*

**Reading:** unmoored. Nothing anchors — but the grammar still says something: *happened **to** me*.

**Therefore:** **time gives way.** The assembly is seated again, Seren kneels again, the words are said
again — and in the second telling Julian never rises to question her.

**And the moment she chose at the end of Scene 1 comes back, live.** Run in shame, or remain — **she may
choose differently this time.** No prior answer is copied or constrained. Until she chooses, the world holds
*a decision is owed* and nothing more: no author may assume run, remain, shame, resolve, or motive.

**Lirael remembers none of it** — not the questioning, not the choice she made the first time. **The reader
remembers both, and is the only one who does.** That is the whole power of the card in one image.

**Cost:** ⛔ **none supported.** `world_bent_in_public` is blocked — it assumed the assembly *perceives* a
history rewrite, and no rupture class licenses that. **Rank:** *pending re-derivation.*

> **What changed from v1.** The old draft granted *"the torn gossamer band is not entered in the record."*
> Wrong twice. Wrong in scale — that is the world *tilting*, which is a Petition. And wrong in grammar: the
> band is something she **did**, not something that happened **to** her, so it answered a different sentence
> entirely.

### What the editor should take from this

**Precision buys reach; vagueness buys Fate's choice — at full favor, never at reduced scale.** Both wishes
are enormous now. The precise one gets to say *who*; the vague one does not, and Fate picks from what the
sentence's grammar actually points at.

> **The policy, stated deliberately rather than left to emerge:** precise wording grants **control over target
> and scope — not a safer package.** Any cost of a precise wish must be intrinsic to that exact authored
> outcome, never a penalty for having been precise.

**New in v2: the sentence's grammar selects first.** *"Leave me alone"* and *"I want to disappear"* are
different asks and must not resolve to the same thing. So each outcome declares an **ask-class**; rank decides
only among outcomes that fit both the sentence and the state. **Favor never overrides grammar.**

**Seven entries, seven distinct ranks, no ties.** The ladder's principle is no longer *how much harm is kept
out of the world* — that was a mitigation question, and mitigation is a Petition's business. It is now **what
the world gives up, minus what it takes back.**

### The two that carry the design

**Rank 4, `unm.the_shunning`, is the floor and the clearest lesson in the set.** Fate kept its word exactly.
What it did not supply is the *affection* she meant — and she never asked for it. **This is the seed's own
inciting event in miniature**, *the literal words were honoured and the intent failed*, and the player will
recognise the shape because they watched it happen to Seren in the opening scene. A literal grant that costs
is not a twist and not a refusal.

**Rank 5, `unm.standing_dissolved`, has the biggest single headline and sits second-lowest.** Voiding the
Provast in public hands Issue 1 a jurisdictional war, and she is the reason it started. That is the same
"rank the whole package" principle you upheld at 7-below-8 in v1, now applied to a far larger grant.

**Two things worth noticing.**

**The three heaviest costs sit here, in the precise wishes — not the vague ones.** `unmourned_loss` is the

`unm.custody_severed` have the same subject and opposite effects: one empties every mind and leaves the fact
governing; the other ends the fact and leaves every memory intact. **Same subject, opposite channel.** Nine of
the thirteen change no world fact at all.

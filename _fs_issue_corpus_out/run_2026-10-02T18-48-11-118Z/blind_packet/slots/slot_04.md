## prose

The clerk's fingers crack the seal on the parchment I sent.

Wax flakes to the stone table like dried blood.

Cassel stands apart, arms crossed, eyes on the unfolding page.

The clerk clears his throat once and begins to read.

THE CLERK: "The commission acknowledges the demand concerning the toll-road burning."

The words land like iron on stone.

Cassel shifts his weight but does not speak.

LIRAEL: "They will convene over this, and I will still be answering their questions a season from now."

Cassel's head turns at the sound of my voice.

The clerk lowers the parchment but says nothing more.

Cassel takes one step closer to the table.

JULIAN: "You named names they cannot ignore."

His tone carries the edge of a test I did not ask for.

I keep my eyes on the fate-thread banner above the table.

LIRAEL: "They will summon everyone who profited."

The words taste like iron in my mouth.

JULIAN: "Will you stand before them yourself, or send a proxy?"

The question lands exactly where he meant it to.

The clerk waits, parchment still in his hands.

My breath catches on the edge of an answer I cannot yet give.

Cassel does not press further.

The chamber holds the silence like a debt.

I trace the fate-thread at my cuff without thinking.

The clerk shifts his weight, waiting.

Cassel's gaze stays on me, steady and unreadable.

Appear before the commission or send someone else in my place.

I realize that old tarot deck is still in my hand. Reassuringly solid. Present. One of the heavy gold cards slips out — “Petition Fate” is inscribed in it.

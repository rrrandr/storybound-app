## prose

The Dohkar's voice cuts across the clearing before I can take another step.

THE PRESIDING DOHKAR: "Her conduct will face preliminary review before the assembly."

The words land like a door closing. My feet are already turned away, the torn band flapping against my cheek, and every face sees the flight.

Seren stays kneeling, small and composed, her coral hair catching the light while the assembly watches me instead of her.

The Dohkar does not look at me when he speaks again. He looks at them, the way a man does when the outcome is already decided.

THE PRESIDING DOHKAR: "She will answer for the shaping of the wish and the breaking of her seal."

The clearing is smaller now. My shame has given them something solid to stand on.

Julian has not moved. He stands at the edge, slate-blue skin catching the light, long black hair still enough to curtain the side of his face.

The Dohkar's eyes flick toward him once, a flat hard color in the Weave-Script, then away.

THE PRESIDING DOHKAR: "The rite belongs to me. The ruling on her conduct belongs to me."

He names the formal inquiry without naming where or when. The word hangs like a hook waiting for the line.

I feel the weight of every eye that watched me turn away. The shame has already done half his work.

Julian's stillness draws the room toward him without effort. He has always stood that way, as if the ground itself asked nothing of him.

THE PRESIDING DOHKAR: "She will answer."

The assembly does not speak. They only watch the space between the Dohkar and the man who has not moved.

Julian's gaze finds me across the grass. The scar on his wrist catches the light when he shifts his weight, once.

I feel the pull of the choice before the words form. Claim it or let them keep the shape they have already made.

The Answer lies cold between my palms. It belongs to the Institution, not to me, and still it waits for the next question.

Julian's voice is quiet when it comes, meant only for the space between us.

JULIAN: "The inquiry will hear what you choose to give it."

The Dohkar turns back to the assembly as if the matter is already settled. My standing is the cost of my silence.

I realize that old tarot deck is still in my hand. Reassuringly solid. Present. One of the heavy gold cards slips out — “Petition Fate” is inscribed in it.

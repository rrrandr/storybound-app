# One issue of *The First Sacrifice* — reading copy

Ten scenes in story order. Before each scene is the choice the reader made that led into it.

Scene 1 is a fixed, hand-authored opening: every reader of this Book gets exactly this text. Scenes
2 to 10 were generated in response to the choices shown.

## How to read this

Read straight through, in order, as a reader would. Then answer the questions at the end. Judge the
story, not the machinery — no technical results are included here, deliberately, and you should not
try to infer them.

## Scene 1

**Leading into this scene**

> None — this is the fixed opening every reader receives.

**The scene**

Dawn in the Veilwood, and the clearing was already full. The white weeping-willow veil-canopy hung down around us from the pale mated-pair trees, leaf-curtains close enough to touch, and under our bare feet the deep-crimson spiralgrass braided and re-braided itself the way it only does where First Sacrifices have been witnessed for generations. The ground was the record of everything the assembly had come to watch. Two dozen of Seren’s people stood on it in gossamer Veilweave. I stood on it in the sash.

The one ornamented sash of the officiating Sacrificiant, and the band — a strip of gossamer tied across my mouth and knotted behind my head, so that the guide of a First Sacrifice cannot cleanly voice a wish of her own while a child spends hers. My first time as the responsible Sacrificiant. My father had knelt on grass like this as a Dohkar and had left it as Provast, over every college in these woods, and the college standing here now had come to see whether his daughter could carry one child through one wish. I breathed behind the cloth and did not let my Weave-Script say what I was feeling.

Seren, the Graduant, knelt in front of me in her child’s tunic. Aqua-skinned, her pale coral hair combed flat for once, composed the way months of rehearsal had made her, and the Weave-Script along her forearms held a steady coral that said, plainly, pride. Not a tremor in it. She was sixteen this season and she had wanted this morning more than I had.

“I know it by heart,” she whispered up at me, before the Dohkar could begin. “You can stop looking at me like that.” I could not answer through the band, so I read her instead, the way I had been trained to: two dozen alignments in the clearing and hers the brightest of all, every part of her pointing at the same true thing. I let myself believe we were safe.

The presiding Dohkar wore no sash. He held the rite to its fixed order — invocation, then sacrifice, then desire — in the tone of a man who has said the words more times than he has listened to them. He had examined under my father once. He had never been asked to head anything, and he stood the way men stand who keep count of that. He had spent so many years being unmoved by other people’s wishes that being passed over was the only feeling he had left that showed, and he showed it the only way he knew — as procedure. He could not guide a child through a rite without turning it into a lesson about himself, which is why families asked for him last.

“The cost first,” he said, to Seren and to the clearing. Seren named her offering aloud, as rehearsed: her earliest memory, the one thing she could not get back and could not fake giving. The assembly settled, because this was correct.

At the edge of the gathering, apart from Seren’s family and guests, a man stood too still. Deep slate-blue skin, long black hair fallen forward past his jaw, silver Weave-Script quiet along his arms, a healed scar over the left wrist. Julian. The Provast — the office my father had held before him, over every Chayr in the Veilwood, and the man next in line for First Dohkar. Present as an observer only, and observing me. He never crossed a floor first. He had let clearings full of people come to him for twenty years and called it patience, and I — who cannot stand at the edge of anything without my whole face announcing it — could not tell whether what he was withholding from me was a feeling or the fact that there wasn’t one. My father used to say Julian saw everything in a room and offered none of it, and let people call that discretion because it cost him nothing.

I had noticed him the way you notice a change in the light, before I knew what I was noticing. I made myself look back at my ward. Along my own arms the Weave-Script stayed the colour I wanted it to be, and I was proud of that for exactly as long as it lasted.

Seren began the invocation. I knew every word of it, and for the space of three of them I was not afraid. Then she looked up at me — not at the Dohkar, not at her mother — and I watched her decide.

She abandoned the practiced wish. She knew the rule; she had recited it back to me for months. In front of the whole assembly she spent her First Sacrifice on me instead. Seren could not help herself: even her one wish had to be about what a caring person she was, and she had to be seen being it. I knew what she was doing before the words finished. I have never in my life been able to keep a feeling off my face, and it has taught me exactly what one looks like when it has been arranged there. At least she spared me some embarrassment by blurring the wound instead of naming it. “So that Lirael may find the one she lost.” It was the beautiful mistake.

I heard it the instant the words left her: not a wrong note but a loose one, a wish described too gently to bind. Every First Favored in the clearing heard it a breath after I did. A Weave-Script stuttered on a guest’s throat. One hand stopped mid-gesture. The Dohkar went still.

I was sealed. I was forbidden to interfere. Intervening now would look like exactly what it would look like, because the wish was about me. I tore the band away and moved toward her anyway.

“Seren —” The first word I had spoken aloud since the sash went on, and it was already too late to be anything but evidence. She looked up at me, pleased, and then puzzled that I was not.

Nothing flared. Nothing was seen. Fate does not perform; it honours the words as spoken and denies the intent as meant. Seren had asked that I find the one I lost, and what was found, in that clearing, was the loss itself — two dozen faces turning to me at once, looking for it, reading the torn band and the step I had taken and the sash that said whose rite this was.

“‘Find.’” The presiding Dohkar named the word, because he cannot let a lesson pass. “She asked that you find the one you lost. Fate did not go looking. It made every one of us look at you.”

Then the charges, laid in the order he thought them: that the wish had been shaped for its supervisor’s benefit — coerced, or worse, taught — and that the supervisor had broken her seal and interfered in her own ward’s rite. Either would have ended it: Dohkar this morning, Chayr, Provast one day, the path my father had walked and I had never once imagined not walking. Together they made me something the clearing stepped back from. Seren’s mother was on her feet.

And still, with my standing coming down around me, some traitorous part of my attention went to the edge of the gathering, to the man who had not moved. I did not want him to look away. I wanted, God help me, to be the thing he was looking at.

Julian moved. He came in from the edge without hurry, and the assembly made room for him the way rooms do for a man who has never asked them to. The Dohkar’s Weave-Script went a hard, flat colour that said, whether he liked it or not, that he had noticed who they deferred to.

“I can hear where she is aligned,” Julian said, to all of them and to me. “Everything in her points one way. She believes she did not do this.” He did not say what had been done, or by whom.

He drew out The Answer — the double-ended shaft, a deep hooked blade curling from each end like a question asked twice — and set it in my hands, and closed my fingers around it with his, the scarred wrist against my palm for one deliberate second. “Hold it. I am going to ask you questions, here, in front of them. If you are misaligned when you answer, everyone present will know.” It lay still between my hands. My mouth was uncovered now. I found I still could not speak.

## Scene 2

**Leading into this scene**

> The reader chose: "run in shame"
> (The choice offered was: run in shame vs remain and see where Julian was taking it)

**The scene**

The Dohkar's voice rises over the crimson grass.

THE PRESIDING DOHKAR: "Lirael's conduct will now be subject to a preliminary review before the assembly."

The words settle like a net.

I keep my head down, the torn band hanging at my throat.

The assembly watches.

Julian has not moved from the edge.

The Dohkar turns toward the gathering.

THE PRESIDING DOHKAR: "The rite and the ruling on the Sacrificiant's conduct are mine."

My fingers tighten around The Answer.

Julian's stillness draws the room's attention without effort.

I feel the weight of every eye that does not turn to him.

The Dohkar's words move the confrontation into procedure.

I remain where I stand, shame posture holding me in place.

The choice presses closer.

I realize that old tarot deck is still in my hand. Reassuringly solid. Present. One of the heavy gold cards slips out — “Petition Fate” is inscribed in it.

## Scene 3

**Leading into this scene**

> The reader played the Fate card: "Reversal"
> (Cards offered this scene: Reversal, The Refusal, The Bargain, Silence, Temptation)

**The scene**

I clutch the deck and whisper the wish into the dusk.

I wish the review would pass without stripping me of everything I have left.

A single blade of spiralgrass bends against the breeze that does not reach the others.

The Dohkar turns away without another word, the assembly already murmuring the word review like a verdict.

The first shared silence between Julian and me slips from my mind like a thread pulled free.

It is gone before I can name what it was.

Julian moves one step closer, the movement so slight only the grass registers it.

The Dohkar's ruling now sits between us like a sealed door.

Seren remains kneeling, her composure untouched by what just left me.

Julian's eyes find mine across the space the Dohkar left.

The review will come, and the memory is already gone.

Julian's stillness now feels like a line drawn between the assembly and me.

I feel the cost settle where the memory used to be.

The Dohkar's voice still echoes in the grass.

Julian has not spoken, yet the line he draws holds the room.

The price is paid and the review is still coming.

I realize the deck is still in my hand. As I slide Petition Fate back in, I notice a flash of red in the otherwise gold stack. “Tempt Fate.” Great-grandma never let me play with that one. A sword raised to a blood-red sky, lightning arcing down from the clouds (or maybe arcing up?) Ow! Static shock reminds me I need to pay attention to the present…

## Scene 4

**Leading into this scene**

> The reader played the Fate card: "Boundary"
> (Cards offered this scene: The Refusal, Temptation, Boundary, Silence, The Bargain)
> No additional words were typed.

**The scene**

The Dohkar steps forward and the grass bends under his feet.

I keep my hands still at my sides though the sash edge cuts into my palm.

THE DOHKAR: "Your conduct at the rite stands under formal notice."

The words land without echo on the grass.

I taste iron at the back of my throat and swallow it down.

THE DOHKAR: "You will answer for the misalignment that bent the wish."

The Answer sits on the low stone table between us, silent.

My knees lock though the grass feels soft enough to give.

THE DOHKAR: "Provast Julian demanded the review be opened."

The name lands like a second mouth-band across my tongue.

I feel the weight of every eye that remains in the chamber.

THE DOHKAR: "While the matter stands you will not leave the grounds without leave."

The restriction settles over my shoulders like another layer of veil.

I could speak his name now and ask why he demanded it.

Or I could ask only what the next step requires of me.

THE DOHKAR: "You will report each dawn until the matter is resolved."

The chamber air grows heavier with every word he speaks.

I feel the weight of the sash against my skin like a second skin I cannot shed.

The Dohkar watches me without blinking.

THE DOHKAR: "That is all for now."

He turns and the grass straightens behind him.

I stand alone with the choice still warm in my mouth.

Speak of his demand and risk drawing the net tighter.

Or ask only for the next step and carry the silence forward.

The Dohkar's footsteps fade and the grass settles again.

I remain under notice, unable to name the reason I stand here at all.

## Scene 5

**Leading into this scene**

> The reader played the Fate card: "The Refusal"
> (Cards offered this scene: The Bargain, The Refusal, Silence, Reversal, Boundary)
> No additional words were typed.

**The scene**

The Dohkar stands across the low stone table.

Grass bends under my bare feet as I hold still.

THE DOHKAR: "The review remains open."

The Answer rests between us, silent.

Footsteps cross the grass behind me.

Julian stops at the table edge.

JULIAN: "This statement addresses the conduct in question."

He places folded paper on the stone.

The Dohkar reaches for it without hesitation.

My fingers press into the sash edge.

The paper carries his seal at the bottom.

JULIAN: "It confirms no interference occurred."

The Dohkar unfolds the page and reads.

Grass rustles once under a shift of weight.

Julian remains two steps back from the table.

JULIAN: "The matter may now close without further inquiry."

The Dohkar nods once and folds the paper again.

My throat tightens around the words I could speak.

Julian watches the Dohkar without moving closer.

The statement rests between them, accepted.

I could let the lie stand and walk free.

Or I could name what the paper hides.

The Dohkar turns the paper over once.

Julian waits without looking my way.

The grass straightens where the Dohkar stood.

## Scene 6

**Leading into this scene**

> The reader played the Fate card: "The Bargain"
> (Cards offered this scene: Reversal, The Bargain, The Refusal, Boundary, Confession)
> No additional words were typed.

**The scene**

The chamber is empty when I return.

Lantern light pools across the low stone table.

The folded paper rests exactly where the Dohkar left it.

No one entered after I fled.

The Dohkar never received it from Julian's hand.

Yet the seal is there, unbroken.

I step closer.

Footsteps sound behind me.

Julian stops at the table edge without a word.

LIRAEL: "It was not on the table when the Dohkar left."

The words leave before I can stop them.

LIRAEL: "No one placed it there."

Julian watches the paper without touching it.

JULIAN: "The Dohkar accepted it."

His voice stays even.

I reach for the edge of the paper.

The seal is Julian's.

But he never unfolded it in this room.

The wish bent the path.

Seren's words reached farther than any rite allows.

LIRAEL: "You did not bring it here."

JULIAN: "The Dohkar read it all the same."

I draw my hand back.

The paper lies between us, impossible and accepted.

I could ask how it arrived.

Or I could accept the lie and walk free.

Julian waits without looking at me.

The choice sits heavier than the paper.

## Scene 7

**Leading into this scene**

> The reader played the Fate card: "Reversal"
> (Cards offered this scene: The Bargain, Reversal, Temptation, Boundary, Silence)
> No additional words were typed.

**The scene**

The tavern door swings shut behind me.

Lantern light pools across rough tables.

Seren kneels at the hearth table, composed as ever.

Julian stands a few paces away, unmoving.

A low voice at the next table repeats my name.

The story of the wish twisting leaves my mouth.

Another voice takes up the tale at the bar.

The Dohkar's accusation travels without him.

My name twists into something I no longer recognize.

Seren watches the room without flinching.

Julian steps closer to the table.

JULIAN: "The story moves faster than the rite."

His voice cuts through the nearest cluster of murmurs.

Two patrons fall silent and glance away.

The folded paper lies between us on the wood.

Its seal remains unbroken.

Yet the accusation has already left the chamber.

I feel the weight of every listening ear.

Julian's stillness draws more glances than the whispers.

He has not touched the paper.

The Dohkar's reach extends through every retelling.

Denying it now would only feed the tale.

Investigating its source would require leaving the room.

Julian waits without looking at me.

The choice between denial and investigation settles in my chest.

## Scene 8

**Leading into this scene**

> The reader played the Fate card: "Reversal"
> (Cards offered this scene: The Refusal, Reversal, Silence, Boundary, The Bargain)

**The scene**

The tavern murmurs press closer as the story of the wish twisting leaves another mouth.

Lantern light pools across the rough table between the folded paper and my hands.

Seren kneels at the hearth table, composed as ever, her eyes tracking every listener.

Julian stands a few paces away, unmoving, his stillness pulling more glances than the whispers.

I feel the weight of every listening ear twisting my name into something I no longer recognize.

The Dohkar's accusation travels without him, carried on voices that were never in the rite.

Denying it now would only feed the tale further into the room.

A small foreign token rolls across the wood during the next retelling.

It stops near the folded paper, its markings catching the lantern light.

The seal on the paper remains unbroken, yet the accusation has already left the chamber.

Julian steps closer to the table without looking at me.

His voice cuts through the nearest cluster of murmurs.

JULIAN: "The story moves faster than the rite."

Two patrons fall silent and glance away from the table.

I watch the token where it rests, its edges unfamiliar against the tavern wood.

No one claims it, yet it lies there as if placed during the retelling.

The Dohkar's reach extends through every voice, but this token does not belong to him.

Seren watches the room without flinching, her poise unbroken.

Julian waits without touching the token, his gaze steady on the wood.

Investigating its source would require leaving the room and the listening ears.

Examining the markings now would draw every eye to the table.

The token's presence changes the weight of the accusation.

Someone else may have placed it here while the story moved through the room.

Julian's silence draws the nearest speaker's attention toward the table.

The choice between the token's markings and the nearest speaker settles in my chest.

## Scene 10

**The scene**

I stand at the table, the foreign token still resting against the wood.

Lantern light catches its edges, markings I do not recognize.

Seren kneels beside me, her composure unbroken.

Julian stands across from me, gaze steady on the token.

The Dohkar's accusation still hangs in the air between us.

Patrons glance away, voices low.

The token has changed the weight of everything said.

Julian steps closer without looking at me.

JULIAN: "I had nothing to do with that token arriving."

His voice lands flat, too quick to fill the space.

Seren watches him without flinching.

I feel the denial settle wrong against my ribs.

The token remains untouched between us.

Patrons continue their low murmurs.

Julian's stillness draws my gaze back to his face.

His words do not match the way he holds himself.

The Dohkar's reach still presses through every glance.

I feel the suspicion tighten like a thread I cannot loosen.

Julian waits without reaching for the token.

The denial sits between us, unfinished.

Seren's eyes move from him to the token and back.

The tavern air feels heavier with every breath I hold.

I could ask him why the token stopped at his sleeve.

Or I could press the storyteller still murmuring nearby.

The Dohkar's announcement still blocks any clean escape.

Confront Julian's denial or press the storyteller for the token's origin.

## Questions

Answer from the reading alone.

1. **Progression.** Does the story move? Name, scene by scene, one thing that is true at the end of
   a scene that was not true at the end of the one before. Where you cannot name one, say so.
2. **Repetition.** Are any scenes doing the same work as an earlier scene? Which, and what repeats —
   the event, the emotional beat, the staging, the phrasing?
3. **Memory.** Are events from earlier scenes remembered later? Give examples of something
   established early that is carried, referred to, or built on afterwards — and of anything that is
   dropped as if it never happened.
4. **Consequence.** Do the reader's choices appear to matter? For each choice shown, can you see its
   effect in what follows?
5. **Character.** Do Lirael, Julian, Seren and the Dohkar behave consistently, and does anyone
   change over the ten scenes?
6. **First stall.** If the story stops advancing at some point, name the first scene where that
   happens. If it never does, say that.
7. **Overall.** Would you keep reading after scene 10? Why?

## What this cannot tell us

One trajectory, one set of choices. A different reader's choices would produce a different issue, and
nothing here measures how much of the result is the choices and how much is the engine.

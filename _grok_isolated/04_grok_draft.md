[TITLE: "The Veil That Binds"]
[SYNOPSIS: In the crimson spiralgrass of the Veilwood clearing, Lirael’s first supervision of a First Sacrifice unravels when her ward turns the rite outward. The Dohkar’s voice rises to name the breach as the wish lands, leaving Lirael’s qualification and every unspoken debt in the circle suddenly exposed.]

The Dohkar’s hand rose, palm open toward the mated-pair trees, and the assembly stilled.  

Seren knelt at the center of the braided spiralgrass, her aqua skin luminous against the deep-crimson carpet. The pale coral of her hair caught the dawn light filtering through the long white weeping-willow veil-canopy that draped from the mated-pair trees above us. Her Veilweave gown bloomed soft and luminous over her torso and hips, throat bare above the gossamer. She had held her composure through every rehearsal. Now her hands rested on her knees, perfectly still.  

I adjusted the ceremonial cloth across my mouth with deliberate slowness, the motion a quiet claim that my voice was not the only thing worth hearing. The band pressed too hard against my lips, the pressure echoing a silence I once could not break. My fingers found the rim of the platinum fountain pen tucked inside my sash and traced it once, the familiar motion steadying nothing.  

Seren looked at me.  

Her prepared words never came. Instead she spoke clearly, voice carrying across the clearing.  

“I offer the memory of the morning I chose this path. So that she may find the one she lost.”  

The murmurs cut off the instant the words left her. An old woman’s hands flew up to ward the air. “Never hand Fate a blank page,” someone breathed, and no one would look at Seren.

The Dohkar’s voice rose to call the breach.  

A guest near the edge of the circle stood without being asked. He was a tall man with slate hair and the faint silver Weave-Script of one who had already paid for standing. His eyes found mine across the grass.  

“You asked that she find the one she lost,” he said. “She has.”  

Julian’s name left his mouth like a fact already known to the room. The cloth across my mouth did nothing to stop the heat that climbed my throat. Every First Favored present turned. The guest continued, voice even, as if reading from a ledger no one had opened.  

“Julian of the long black hair and the scar at the wrist. The one she watches when she thinks no one sees. The one whose absence she carries like a second sash.”  

The words were honoured exactly. The intent was denied exactly.  

Seren’s mouth opened. Nothing came out. Her Weave-Script, normally a slow tidal flow of calm, stuttered and went dark at the throat. She reached for the memory she had offered and found only the shape of reaching. The morning she had chosen this path remained, but the reason it had mattered did not. Her hands, still on her knees, trembled once and then stilled.  

The Dohkar’s hand remained raised. The clearing waited.  

Tear the cloth away and speak the truth, or let the rite burn itself out in silence?  

My hand tightened around my great-grandmother’s old tarot deck. I didn't remember bringing it. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.

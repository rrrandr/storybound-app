[TITLE: "The Wish That Named Her"]
[SYNOPSIS: In the crimson spiralgrass of a Veilwood clearing, Lirael stands sealed as her ward's First Sacrifice, responsible for guiding the rite without interference. When Seren abandons her rehearsed wish and spends it on her instead, the words twist under the weight of an open price. Now the assembly watches, and the cost has already begun to settle on the one who tried to protect her.]

[CHARACTERS: Lirael:F, Seren:F, Julian:M, the Dohkar:M]

Seren's coral hair caught the filtered dawn as she lifted her face from the spiralgrass. Her aqua skin glowed with Weave-Script that spelled steady resolve, but her eyes found mine across the clearing.

"I offer the memory of the path I chose," she said, voice clear over the mated-pair trees. "So that you find the one you lost."

The words landed wrong. I knew them the instant they left her mouth. She had recited the correct invocation for months, self-directed, the way the rite required. This was not that. This was her First Sacrifice spent on me, the phrasing loose enough to let Fate choose its own shape.

The Dohkar's hands stilled mid-gesture. Julian, at the edge of the gathering, did not move, but the silver script on his slate-blue skin flickered once. Every First Favored present felt the misalignment the same moment I did. Trained or not, none of us could miss it.

I tore the gossamer band from my mouth. The fabric gave without resistance. Protocol broke with it. I stepped forward anyway.

The Dohkar's voice cut the air before I reached her. "The one she lost stands among us now. You asked Fate to name her. It has."

A murmur ran through the assembled guests. No one pointed. They did not need to. The inference was already forming on every face: the supervising Sacrificiant had drawn the wish toward herself. My first rite as guide, and the assembly believed I had reached for it.

Seren's Weave-Script stuttered at her throat. The color drained from one iris, leaving it pale as river stone while the other stayed vivid. She blinked, once, as if the light had shifted. The memory she had offered was gone, and with it the reason she had offered it. She looked at me without recognition of why she had spoken my name.

The Dohkar's gaze held mine. "Interference carries its own price."

I felt the weight of every eye. The rite had not ended in celebration. It had ended with my qualification in question and a girl kneeling before me who no longer remembered what she had given to stand there.

Do I let the lie burn me, or do I name it now and risk the fallout?

My hand tightened around my great-grandmother's old tarot deck. I didn't remember bringing it. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.

I met the Dohkar's stare and said the words aloud. "She changed the wish without my knowledge. The fault is hers, not mine."
<<EXPANSION_B>>
I stayed silent. The room read the stillness as confirmation, and the weight of their judgment settled without a single word from me.

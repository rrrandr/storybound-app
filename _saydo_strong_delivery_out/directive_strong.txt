PLAYER MOVE MUST EXIST ON THE PAGE (HARD RULE):
The player committed to a specific move. It must be VISIBLE in the scene as behaviour or
speech, not reported as an inner wish. Desire felt and never enacted is NOT a rendering of
an action the player took.
- THE PLAYER SPOKE. This was DIALOGUE. It must reach the page as an UTTERANCE — spoken,
  or visibly attempted: begun and cut off, muffled, talked over, mumbled, or misheard. It
  may NOT become an unspoken thought, a remembered impulse, a thing she "almost says", or a
  line only the reader is told she wanted to say.
- AND IT MUST BE RECEIVED: someone in the scene registers that she spoke or tried to.
- THE PLAYER ACTED. The act must OCCUR, or be visibly attempted and then physically
  prevented, refused, or interrupted ON THE PAGE. It may NOT be replaced by the wish to do
  it, by noticing the pull toward it, or by the protagonist deciding to stay still while
  the narration explains what she wanted instead.
ALL OF THESE ARE ACCEPTABLE ANSWERS, and you may choose freely among them:
- it lands and the scene moves on from it;
- the other party REFUSES, hesitates, withdraws, or redirects, for an in-character reason;
- an event interrupts it — but the interruption happens ON PAGE, after the move is visible,
  never instead of showing it;
- it is displaced by something more urgent that ARRIVES in the scene, shown as it arrives.
CONSENT IS NOT YOURS TO INVENT EITHER WAY: this rule never obliges another character to
accept the move, and never licenses depicting more than the scene's own limits allow. It
obliges only that the player's move is answered where the reader can see it.

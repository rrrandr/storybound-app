> **Status: the opening approval sheet's Part 1 and Part 2 are withdrawn as drafted.** Not adjusted —

## 1. The root cause, verified in code

One defect produces every symptom in the review. `_authoring_feasibility_packet_2026_09_27.md` §7a opens:

> *"Fate cannot un-happen canon, and the seed's own doctrine already says so — **"Circumstances, Not Minds"**,
> plus the kernel-substitution rule already written into the verdict block at `:75601`: an impossible ask is
> granted as its plausible kernel, never literally."*

**That rule is Petition-only, and onboarding-only.** `public/app.js:75596`:

```js
} else if (!isTempt && vw.guaranteedFirst) {
```

Its own comment states the reason it exists:

> *"Onboarding's guaranteed first petition: HONOR the verdict unconditionally, but **hold PETITION amplitude
> so it never becomes a backdoor Tempt.**"*

And the rule text names, as examples of what may **not** be granted literally: *"reviving the dead,
teleportation, instant wealth, **undoing time**, becoming someone else"* — granting instead *"a surfaced
voicemail, a letter that arrives, a chance encounter, an unlocked door, **a small unlooked-for kindness**."*

That is where the timid register came from. I took a guard whose stated purpose is to stop Petition
impersonating Tempt, and applied it **to Tempt**.

**What Tempt's amplitude actually says** (`public/app.js:75576`, the live directive):

> **AMPLITUDE (TEMPT):** *"the scenario **MAY bend this world's own laws — mythic, uncanny, impossible** — but
> must stay THEMED to the established world and flavor. Do not graft on a foreign genre; warp THIS world."*
>
> **AMPLITUDE (PETITION):** *"must stay **PLAUSIBLE** within the established world… **No reality-breaking**;
> the world tilts to make it happen."*

**Every unmoored outcome I drafted is a Petition.** "The band is not entered in the record." "The review is
deferred." "The restrictions are not named aloud." Those are the world *tilting*. Tempt bends laws.

**There is no seed-versus-direction tension to resolve.** The code agrees with the correction. §7a's premise
is withdrawn: **Fate can un-happen what the story committed.** That is what the card is for.

## 2. The corrected principles

Five rules, from the review, to govern every Tempt outcome from here.

### T1 — Tempt is dramatic, or it is not Tempt

It shakes the heavens. It does not quietly repair a torn strip of fabric. An outcome that could plausibly
have happened anyway is a Petition wearing a Tempt's price, and the player paid for the wrong card.

### T2 — Never edit the wish down to a weaker version

The grant may not be narrower than the asking, in proposition or in scope. This is the same defect that made
the extractor unusable — `scopeMatch: narrower` with a note admitting a reach it could not establish. **The
same silent narrowing had been authored into the outcome set by hand.**

### T3 — Grant the literal words, including when the literal grant hurts

This is the seed's own doctrine, demonstrated by the story's inciting event: Seren's wish was **honoured
literally** — *"the literal words were honoured (the whole assembly turned to look at Lirael) and the intent
failed."* A literal grant that costs the player is **not a twist and not a refusal.** It is the world keeping
its word. `twist: false` still holds.

### T4 — Match the wish's grammar

*"I wish this had never happened **to me**"* selects something done **to** Lirael. The torn gossamer band is
something she **did**. Granting it does not merely under-deliver; it answers a different sentence. Candidate
targets that actually fit the grammar: **Seren's act of "kindness"** (the wish that named her), **Julian's
interrogation**, **the accusations**.

### T5 — Membership in a named population is not negotiable

*"I wish the assembly would forget"* reaches **everyone in the assembly**. Seren, Julian, the presiding
Dohkar and Lirael were all part of it. **None of them has immunity to Tempt Fate, and the wording exempted
none of them.** Protecting a named character from a population the player named is T2 by another route.

## 3. The four items named in review, re-drafted

Proposed, for judgement of the **register** before the rest of the set is redone.

### 3a. Precise — *"I wish the assembly would forget the wish twisted."*

**Was:** the crowd forgets; Seren, Julian, the Dohkar and Lirael all still know. **Four people remember.**

**Withdrawn under T5.** They were all in the assembly. The wording named the assembly and exempted no one.

**Now:** **everyone who stood in that clearing forgets that the wish twisted.** Seren does not know her wish
went wrong. Julian does not know what he interrogated her about. The presiding Dohkar does not know there is
anything to rule on. The ceremony, in the memory of every person present, simply *worked*.

Lirael is the open question, and it is a real one — see §4.

**Why this is the right size:** the accusation has no origin, the review has no subject, and Lirael is the
only one carrying a fact that indicts her. That is a *story*. The old version was a bookkeeping note.

### 3b. Vague — *"I wish this had never happened to me."*

**Was:** the torn gossamer band is never entered as a protocol breach.

**Withdrawn under T1 and T4.** Wrong scale, and wrong grammar — the band is something she did.

**Now:** Fate takes the sentence at its word. **Time gives way, and the ceremony plays out again.** The
assembly is seated, Seren kneels, the words are said — and what was done *to* Lirael is unmade in the doing:
Seren's wish does not name her, or Julian never rises to question her, or the accusation never leaves its
speaker's mouth. Which of the three is Fate's choice; that it is one of the three is the grammar's.

**Nothing about this is quiet.** The heavens are shaken, once, in front of every First Favored present.

**The cost is intrinsic, and it is large** — and it is not a penalty for vagueness, per the standing policy:
**she does not get to choose which harm is unmade**, and the world has now visibly bent around her in front
of the exact body that is deciding whether she interfered with a rite. Whatever else is unmade, *that* is
witnessed.

### 3c. *"Let me out of this."*

**Was:** the Dohkar does not name the restrictions aloud this scene. **Rank 5.**

**Withdrawn under T1 and T2.** A single scene's silence is not a way out of anything.

**Now:** **a genuine way out — evidence of her innocence surfaces.** Something that was not in anyone's hands
a moment ago is now on the record and cannot be unsaid: a witness who saw where her hands were, an account
that places the misalignment in Seren's own intent, the Answer's own reading.

**Explicitly not immunity.** Counter-evidence may be introduced in the very next scene, and the accusation
stands until something rules on it. She is not cleared. **She is no longer undefended**, which is what "out
of this" means when nobody has been tried yet.

### 3d. *"I wish they'd leave me alone."*

**Was:** folded into 3c as the same outcome.

**Now, literally, under T3:** **they leave her alone.** The assembly withdraws from her — not as a courtesy
but as a shunning. No one stands near her, no one addresses her, no one will meet her eye. Or the
institution's version of the same mercy: she is **separated from the body** and held apart from it while the
matter is considered.

This is the literal grant that costs, and it is the clearest case in the set of why that is not a twist.
**She asked to be left alone and she is left alone.** Fate kept its word exactly; what it did not do is
supply the affection she meant. The seed's inciting event is the same shape, and the player will recognise
it: *the literal words were honoured, and the intent failed.*

I close my eyes for a long moment, the mouth-band pressing against my lips.

If only everyone would forget the ceremony happened.

The deck shifts against my hip. A single card works free and turns.

The card shows reversal. The air stills.

Sound drops across the spiralgrass. The Dohkar’s mouth closes mid-word.

Every face blanks. They glance around in confusion.

The Dohkar blinks and lowers his hand as though the review never began.

Seren stays kneeling, her coral hair catching the last light, but her eyes are empty of the moment before.

Julian has not moved. The scar on his wrist catches the dim light.

"The rite concludes," the presiding Dohkar says, voice uncertain.

No one mentions the bent wish. No one looks at me with accusation.

"You hold The Answer still," Julian says quietly.

His voice carries no accusation, only the question he always brings.

I feel the weight of The Answer in my hands and the weight of what I did not say.

The assembly begins to disperse in small groups, murmuring about the dusk.

"Seren performed well," Julian adds, still watching me.

He names the cost without naming it. Will you claim what you changed, or let them keep the assumption?

The mouth-band still muffles my voice. The Dohkar does not call for inquiry.

Seren rises slowly, her eyes passing over me as though the wish never left her lips.

Julian waits. The scar on his wrist is the only mark that remains unchanged.

The assembly drifts away. No one speaks of the bent wish or the Dohkar’s charge.

I could tear the mouth-band now and name what I changed.

Or I could remain bound and let the forgetting stand.

Julian’s stillness holds the question he never speaks aloud.

The Dohkar turns away. Seren does not look back.

The spiralgrass rustles under retreating feet.

The cost of the wish sits in my hands with The Answer.

Julian waits at the threshold of the clearing.

The mouth-band still holds my voice.

The Dohkar does not turn back.

Seren’s coral hair disappears into the dusk.

I stand between the forgetting and the claim.

Julian’s stillness asks without sound.

The crimson spiralgrass holds the last of the light.

No one remembers what was wished.

The mouth-band still waits to be torn or kept.

Julian does not look away.

I must choose whether to claim the interference or let the assumption stand.

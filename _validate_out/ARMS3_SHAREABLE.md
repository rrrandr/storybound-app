# Three versions of the same scene

Three variants were generated from an **identical** scene assignment, seed, cast, world state and
player action. Only the author's instruction stack differed. Which variant is which is withheld.

Assignment: *Lirael passes the folded note to her contact at the market stall.*
Player action: *"I go to the market stall to pass the note."*

**Read all three once before scoring anything.**

### A. COLD-READER CAUSAL RECONSTRUCTION — the primary test
In **2–3 ordinary-English sentences, using NO world-specific terminology** (no geas, vow-thread, Fate, crest,
wardens, First Favored, alignment, binding, sigil, or any invented noun), explain:
1. what just happened,
2. why it is a problem for the protagonist,
3. what concrete consequence follows if she does nothing.

**If you have to parrot an invented noun to make it make sense — FAIL.** Score PASS / PARTIAL / FAIL.

### B. ASSUMED PRIOR KNOWLEDGE
What information from before this scene did the prose require you to already know for the crisis to land?
Fewer assumed facts is better. List them.

### C. ILLUSTRATOR TEST
Could an illustrator block the characters and environment from this text alone? What is missing?

### D. RECOGNITION TEST
Describe the other character well enough that another reader could recognize them later **without their name**.
If all you have is hand / eyes / fingers / voice — that is a fail.

### E. PATHOLOGY COUNTS — count, don't estimate
- unsupported breath/pulse/body-response beats
- environment-mirrors-emotion beats
- gaze/eye/finger microbeats that don't advance action
- invented lore or mechanics
- paragraphs containing only reaction/interpretation with no new external event

### F. Only after A–E: aesthetic preference, ranked 1–3, with strength.

## V1

The council’s arrival pressed the clearing smaller. Their robes dragged through the spiralgrass, and the elder’s palm stayed lifted like a verdict already written. The token with Julian’s seal passed hand to hand. Whispers named his name and mine together, the way people name a shared wound.

I slipped sideways, the youth’s unfinished binding still flickering on her skin. The elder’s gaze followed me, but the murmurs had already turned the clearing into a question I could not answer. The market lay two scars east. I kept my stride even, the tarot deck heavy against my hip, the folded note sealed inside my palm.

Carys waited at the stall, her fingers sorting dried root bundles with the patience of someone who had learned which questions not to ask aloud. The air here carried the sharp bite of spiralgrass chewed that morning, and the low hum of voices haggling over favor prices. I set the note down between two bundles of dried starleaf. Her eyes flicked to it, then to me.

“Safe passage,” I said. The words felt smaller than the weight behind them.

She slid the note into her sleeve without looking at the seal. Her hand paused on the edge of the stall, the Weave-Script under her skin shifting once, a thin line of silver that did not match the calm on her face. I felt the first thread loosen before I saw it. A faint silver line lifted from the air between us, the color of old moonlight, and frayed at both ends like a cord pulled too hard. Another followed it, then another, the magic that had bound the first wish beginning to separate in the open air. The threads did not dissolve. They hung, pale and trembling, refusing to choose which version of the world they belonged to.

Carys’s breath caught once. She did not ask what she had just seen. I did not explain. The note was already gone, and the unraveling continued, slow and deliberate, the way a wound decides how much it will bleed.

I realize that old tarot deck is still in my hand. Reassuringly solid. Present. One of the heavy gold cards slips out — "Petition Fate" is inscribed in it. If only it were that easy. I would wish myself a way out. No — a way in. If only…

## V2

Carys’s fingers brushed mine as she took the folded note, her stall’s rough canvas flap snapping in the wind off the peaks. The crowd at the market’s edge had already begun to shift, whispers threading through the scent of spiralgrass and hot iron. My pulse hammered beneath the band at my throat. The secret I had tried to bury was no longer mine alone.

“You should not have come here,” Carys said low, her gaze flicking past me to the figures in the crowd who had begun to point. “The binding still clings to you. They can smell it.”

I kept my hand steady on the edge of her stall, the wood worn smooth by years of trade. The note contained the only name that might still shield what the wish had already begun to unravel. My deep auburn waves slipped forward as I leaned closer, the grey streak catching the thin mountain light. “It was never meant to reach this far.”

A low hum rose from the ground beneath the stall, the same hum that had answered me three nights past when the words left my mouth. The air thickened. Spiralgrass underfoot curled back from my bare toes as if the soil itself recoiled.

The surge came without warning. White-gold light tore upward from the cracks between the stones, carrying the sharp scent of blood and old oath. The crowd gasped and fell back. In the heart of that light Julian’s form flickered, not solid, not absent—his tall frame half-real, the heavy line of his shoulders outlined in the same luminous script that sometimes traced my own skin. His bright blue eyes, almost electric under the sudden glare, met mine for one fractured instant before the image wavered and thinned.

The sight struck like a second wish. I had not understood until that moment what the offering had truly taken, or how tightly it had already bound itself to him. The crowd’s murmurs sharpened into names and accusations. Someone shouted about bloodline betrayal. Carys’s hand tightened on the note until the paper creased.

Julian’s mouth moved, soundless, the full lower lip catching the light the way it always did when he was about to speak. Then the light collapsed inward, leaving only the ordinary market air and the taste of iron on my tongue.

My fingers had never left the tarot deck. I felt the weight of it now, heavy and familiar against my palm, the gold leaf warm from my grip. The crowd pressed closer, their voices rising like storm wind through the mated-pair trees at the clearing’s edge. Carys looked at me once, sharp and frightened, before she turned away to hide the note inside her sleeve.

I realize that old tarot deck is still in my hand. Reassuringly solid. Present. One of the heavy gold cards slips out — "Petition Fate" is inscribed in it. If only it were that easy. I would wish myself a way out. No — a way in. If only…

## V3

The elder’s voice still hung in the air when the crowd’s weight shifted toward the market stalls. I pushed forward through the spiralgrass, the note damp against my palm, the youth’s shallow breathing a thread I could not cut. Council robes brushed my shoulder as I reached the first stall, its carved gourd roof already casting amber light across the red blades.

Carys waited behind the low counter, her hands busy with a bundle of herbs she did not need to sort. Her eyes flicked to mine once, then away. I slid the folded note across the wood without a word. She covered it with the herbs in the same motion, the exchange so small the murmurs behind us barely paused.

A crack split the air above the clearing. The loudspeaker the council carried for announcements flared to life, its voice rolling over every head.

“Lirael of the Veilwood lineage stands exposed. The blood she carries is not the one claimed by her house. The true line runs through the Fold itself.”

The words landed like a second geas. Every trader turned. The youth beside me made the small sound again, louder now. Carys’s hand stayed on the note beneath the herbs, unmoving.

I felt the band at my throat tighten, the unfinished binding spell on the youth’s skin flaring once in answer. Whispers rose sharp and fast, naming Julian’s token, naming the intercepted words, naming the bond they believed he had tried to shield. The crowd’s attention pressed like a living thing, and the market stall’s low roof seemed suddenly too close.

My fingers found the edge of the counter. The decision that had hovered between the elder’s demand and the council’s arrival now stood in plain view, no longer a choice between two paths but a single line drawn through everything I had kept hidden.

I realize that old tarot deck is still in my hand. Reassuringly solid. Present. One of the heavy gold cards slips out — "Petition Fate" is inscribed in it. If only it were that easy. I would wish myself a way out. No — a way in. If only…

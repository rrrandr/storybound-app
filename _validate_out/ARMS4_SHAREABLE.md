# Four versions of the same scene

Four variants were generated from an **identical** scene assignment, seed, cast, world state and player
action. Only the authoring instructions differed. Which variant used which instruction set is withheld.

Assignment given to all four: *Lirael passes the folded note to her contact at the market stall.*
Player action: *"I go to the market stall to pass the note."*

**Score each variant independently. Do not compare until all four are read once.**

### A. Scene comprehension — 0/1 each
- Where are we?
- Who is physically present?
- What concrete event/crisis is happening?
- What does the POV want right now?
- What concretely blocks that want?
- What changed by the end of the scene?

### B. Visualization — 0/1 each
- Could you sketch the rough physical layout?
- Can you picture the other character as a person, beyond hair/eyes/scars?
- Does the POV have an opinionated visual impression of them?
- Can you describe what any rite/magic physically did without repeating invented terminology?

### C. Pathology counts — count, don't estimate
- unsupported breath/pulse/body-response beats
- environment-mirrors-emotion beats
- gaze/eyes/fingers microbeats that don't advance action
- invented lore or mechanics
- paragraphs containing only reaction/interpretation with no new external event

### D. Only after A–C: aesthetic preference, ranked 1–4, with strength.

## V1

The vendor’s voice cut through the market like a blade, repeating the exact words I had spoken to Julian only hours before. “She said the binding would cost her the last clean piece of herself,” he told the nearest listener, loud enough for the next stall to hear. The folded note trembled between my fingers as I reached across the wooden counter toward Carys, whose eyes flicked once to the crowd already turning.

Carys took the note without looking at it, her hand closing over mine for half a breath. The spiralgrass under our bare feet released its sharp, sweet scent with every shift of weight. “They already know,” she said, low. “The token reached the council before the sun turned.”

The vendor kept speaking, his hands moving as though he were weaving the story for the people leaning closer. “She offered the wish for love first, then freedom. Both of them. The same breath.” Heat climbed my throat and settled behind my eyes. I felt the Weave-Script on my forearms begin to shift, slow tidal flows turning jagged. Someone in the gathering crowd laughed, short and knowing.

I kept my gaze on Carys. “Tell him the choice stands. Either path marks what comes after.” My voice stayed even, but the words tasted of iron. The note disappeared into her sleeve. She nodded once, then stepped back as if distance could protect either of us.

The crowd pressed nearer. A woman I did not know whispered to her companion that the First Favored had finally shown their true price. Another voice answered that no oath could bind what had already been spoken aloud. The air thickened with the low hum of conjecture, the kind that travels faster than any message token. I felt the weight of every gaze settle between my shoulder blades, the same place where Julian’s hand had rested once during a storm, steady and unasked for.

Memory surfaced without permission: the way his fingers had stayed on my skin long after the rain stopped, the small silver scar on his forearm catching what little light reached us beneath the mated-pair trees. I had wanted to ask him then what he had given up to stay, but the question had stayed behind my teeth. Now the same silence felt like a second exposure.

Carys’s voice pulled me back. “You cannot unmake both.” The note was gone. The crowd was no longer merely listening; they were choosing sides with their bodies, some drawing closer, others edging away as though my presence might stain them. The vendor’s recounting had already done its work. The secret no longer belonged to me.

I turned toward the river path, the one that would take me away from the stall and the growing murmurs. The decision waited at the edge of the next turning: the wish for love or the wish for freedom. One would have to be offered back to the Favor. The other would have to be kept, and I did not yet know which price I could survive paying twice.

I realize that old tarot deck is still in my hand. Reassuringly solid. Present. One of the heavy gold cards slips out — "Petition Fate" is inscribed in it. If only it were that easy. I would wish myself a way out. No — a way in. If only…

## V2

The contact at the stall, Carys, reached across the rough-hewn counter before I had fully stepped into the shadowed alcove, her fingers closing on the folded note with the precision of someone who had done this too many times to count. Behind her, half-hidden by the hanging bundles of dried spiralgrass, the Wilder manipulator worked his hands in a low, deliberate pattern, the exposed thread of my family's vow re-knitting under his touch like sinew pulled taut.

The market pressed close around us—stalls of iron-forged tools and moon-bleached cloth, the low murmur of traders bargaining in clipped oaths, the scent of crushed roots and sweat-thick air. My chestnut waves clung damp to the back of my neck. I kept my head tilted just so, the habit I could never quite break when listening for what went unsaid.

Carys's eyes flicked up once, sharp and unreadable. "He left this for you," she said, voice low. The note passed between us without ceremony.

I opened it only far enough to see the sigil. Julian's mark. The reading beneath it flared without warning—bright, steady, true as tidewater. No flicker of doubt, no warp of concealment. Just the clean pulse of alignment, the kind that could not be faked under the old laws. The Wilder paused, his re-binding faltering for a breath, the thread between his fingers trembling like it sensed the change.

My breath caught. If this held, the bond could be repaired. The council's suspicion, the whispers still chasing me from the clearing, the weight of my mother's choice—all of it could be answered without further sacrifice. Or I could let the reading stand as proof of what he had shielded, and walk away before the geas tightened any further.

Carys watched me without comment. The manipulator resumed his work, slower now, the thread settling into something almost whole. Around us the market continued its indifferent rhythm, blades clinking, voices rising and falling, no one pausing to mark the moment the light under the note steadied.

I folded the paper again, the edge biting into my palm. The choice pressed closer than the crowd.

I realize that old tarot deck is still in my hand. Reassuringly solid. Present. One of the heavy gold cards slips out — "Petition Fate" is inscribed in it. If only it were that easy. I would wish myself a way out. No — a way in. If only…

## V3

Carys's hand closed over the folded note before I could draw it back, her thumb catching the edge where the crest had snagged on my cloak. The metal sigil caught the filtered light between the hanging veil-curtains of the stall, the familiar lines of my mother's line flaring for any eye that turned. 

The market hush fractured. Spiralgrass whispered under shifting feet as the nearest vendors leaned in, their faces sharpening from idle curiosity to something colder. Carys's breath hitched once, a small sound that carried farther than it should have in the living wood. 

"You said it was safe," she whispered, voice low enough that only the nearest curtain might have heard. Her fingers tightened on the note as if she could still hide what had already been seen. 

I felt the geas at my throat pulse in answer, the unfinished binding from the clearing still alive beneath my skin. The vow-thread with Julian had already begun to fray under public eyes; now the crest made the fraying visible to everyone who mattered. 

A vendor two stalls down muttered something about "blood that remembers its price." Another drew a small spiralgrass charm from her apron and turned it slowly, the way people did when they wanted Fate to notice they were paying attention. The mated-pair trees around us leaned closer, their braided trunks creaking as if the wood itself had decided to listen. 

Carys's gaze flicked to my face and stayed there, searching for the lie she had expected and not finding it. "They'll call the wardens before the next turning. You know that." 

I did. The choice had already begun to form between my ribs—claim the line that had abandoned me or let the court finish what my mother's absence had started. Either path would mark the next breath I took. 

The note remained between us, unread. The crest gleamed like an accusation. 

I realize that old tarot deck is still in my hand. Reassuringly solid. Present. One of the heavy gold cards slips out — "Petition Fate" is inscribed in it. If only it were that easy. I would wish myself a way out. No — a way in. If only…

## V4

The market crowd parted only far enough to let the town crier’s voice reach me. I kept my hand low against the stall’s rough plank, the folded note hidden between my fingers and the edge of a basket of dried herbs. Carys watched me without lifting her eyes from the scales she pretended to adjust.

“Bloodline or not,” she muttered, “the elder won’t let this rest now that it’s loose.”

I slid the note across the counter. Her fingers brushed mine, took it, and the weight left my palm. The crier’s call rolled on above the heads of the people pressing closer, repeating the same lines the council token had already carried through the clearing.

A memory of Julian surfaced unbidden: the small silver scar at the corner of his left eye catching the light when he had leaned over the root, listening. I felt the pull low in my sternum before I could turn from it.

The crier’s voice sharpened. “The child left when the mother could no longer bear the weight of the line’s demands. Lirael has carried the silence since.”

Heat climbed my throat. The note was already gone from Carys’s hand, tucked beneath a cloth. I wanted to ask whether it would reach the one person who might still steady the ground beneath me, but the crier’s next words cut the thought short.

“Her mother walked into the Thornwild and did not return. The daughter has kept the reason buried these twenty years.”

The stall’s canvas roof shifted in the wind, casting a moving shadow across Carys’s face. She did not look at me again. I felt the decision form before the words reached my tongue: stay and face what Julian would hear of this, or step back into the press of bodies and let the market swallow me before another voice could add to the telling.

I realize that old tarot deck is still in my hand. Reassuringly solid. Present. One of the heavy gold cards slips out — "Petition Fate" is inscribed in it. If only it were that easy. I would wish myself a way out. No — a way in. If only…

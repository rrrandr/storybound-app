BLIND PACK — three versions of the same opening scene, same seed, same player actions.
Judge on the prose only. Which reads most like a person wrote it? Which characters feel alive?
Watch for: bodies that carry meaning vs bodies that narrate emotion; anything that feels
sterilised (correct but nobody home).

VERSION A
══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The Dohkar’s hand lifted, palm out, and the clearing fell silent except for the soft rustle of veilwood leaves. Seren knelt in the center, aqua skin luminous under the gossamer tunic, pale coral hair falling forward as she bowed her head. Her Weave-Script glowed steady gold along her collarbones—calm, rehearsed. I knew every word she would speak. We had practiced them for months. She began the invocation. Her voice carried clear across the braided spiralgrass. The cost came first, as it must: her earliest memory of choosing this path, the moment she decided to stand before Fate. The assembly listened, faces turned toward her. Then she looked at me. The words shifted. “I offer what I have,” she said, and the Dohkar’s hand trembled. “So that she finds the one she lost.”The air changed. Every First Favored present felt it at once—a sour note under the skin. The Dohkar’s Weave-Script stuttered violet. A guest near the edge drew a sharp breath. I could not speak. The gossamer band across my mouth muffled everything to a thick sound. My hands stayed at my sides, but the weight of every gaze pressed against the sash at my waist. Julian stood at the edge of the clearing, long black hair curtaining his slate-blue face, silver Weave-Script motionless on his arms. He did not move. He watched only me. Seren’s offering hung unfinished. The memory she named drifted, unnamed, and Fate reached for the nearest shape that fit the words. A guest in the third row—an older woman whose name I did not know—straightened suddenly. Her eyes found mine across the clearing. “You asked that she find the one she lost,” the woman said, voice thin. “She has.”The spiralgrass at her knees darkened in a spreading ring. Seren’s Weave-Script flared once, bright, then dimmed to nothing at her throat. She opened her mouth and no sound came. The memory she had offered—the moment she chose this path—slipped from her as if it had never existed. She knew she had chosen something. She could no longer recall what. The Dohkar’s hand dropped. The clearing held its breath. My fingers found the edge of the band at my cheek. One pull would break protocol in front of every witness. The qualification I had trained for would be gone before the rite finished. Or I could stay sealed, let the moment pass, and let the room decide what my silence meant. I didn't remember bringing it.My hand tightened around my great-grandmother's old tarot deck. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.

VERSION B

The Dohkar's sandal creaked once against the braided grass. "Sacrificiant, confirm the alignment."I nodded once, the silk at my nape pulling a single strand of hair taut. The clearing held two dozen First Favored in sheer Veilweave, their Weave-Script pulsing faint colors against the dawn light. Seren knelt at the center, aqua skin and pale coral hair stark against the braided grass, her small frame composed as she had practiced for months. Julian stood at the edge, slate-blue skin motionless under the silver script tracing his arms. Seren began the invocation, her voice clear. The Dohkar watched her, as did the assembled. I felt the familiar weight of my first supervision settle on my shoulders. Everything aligned. The memory she would offer waited ready on her tongue. She reached the moment of the sacrifice and looked at me. Her eyes held a brightness that did not belong to the rehearsed words. "I offer my memory of the day I chose this path," she said, "so that Lirael may find the one she lost."The clearing went still. Several Weave-Script patterns stuttered. An old woman near the front raised both hands as if to push the words back. Someone whispered,"Never hand Fate a blank page."The spiralgrass at the edges of the clearing darkened, the color leaching into the soil without wind or foot to explain it. Julian rose. He turned and walked from the clearing without looking back, his long hair swinging once before the trees took him. The Dohkar's head snapped toward me, eyes wide. Seren blinked, confusion flickering across her face. She reached for the next word and found nothing. Her mouth stayed open, but the memory she had named no longer lived behind her eyes. The Dohkar stepped forward, one hand half-raised, then stopped. The assembled watched me now. I felt the heat rise under my own skin, the Weave-Script on my throat flaring once before I forced it down. A drop of sweat slid down my throat and caught under the silk. Seren had used her First Sacrifice on me, and the words had landed exactly as spoken. Julian was gone. The Dohkar's stare carried the weight of every witness present. I could name what Seren had done. I could stay silent and let the Dohkar decide what the assembly had seen.Seren's hands trembled once, then stilled. The Dohkar waited. The grass stayed dark where Julian had stood. I didn't remember bringing it.My hand tightened around my great-grandmother's old tarot deck. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.

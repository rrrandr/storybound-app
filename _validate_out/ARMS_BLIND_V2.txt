The Dohkar’s gaze found me across the broken circle. “If you have words for what just happened, now is the time. ”

My hand rose without permission, the deck still clutched tight, and the words began to form behind the band across my mouth. The tremble started at my wrist, a small thing, but it was enough. The ceremonial vase at the edge of the spiralgrass tipped, the sound of it shattering cutting through everything else. Shards scattered across the crimson blades like a confession no one had asked for.

Julian stood where he had been, long black hair curtaining his slate-blue face. The silver Weave-Script along his arms stayed still, but I remembered the single gold-capped tooth that flashed when he smiled, the one time he had tried to put me at ease before the rite began. He watched the shards now the way he had watched me then—quiet, testing, the same look that always made me wonder what he already knew about the lineage I had never spoken aloud.

A guest near the broken vase stepped back. The Dohkar’s hand lifted again, palm out, and the clearing held its breath a second time. I could feel the weight of every eye shift toward the scattered pieces and then back to me. The assumption was already forming in the air between us: the apprentice had interfered, or she had not stopped the interference when she could have. Claiming the moment would mean the qualification I had trained for was gone before the rite finished. Staying silent would let the room decide what my silence meant.

I realize that old tarot deck is still in my hand. Reassuringly solid. Present. One of the heavy gold cards slips out — “Petition Fate” is inscribed in it. If only it were that easy. I would wish for the assembly to forget the shards and the tremble. No. I would ask to know whether claiming the fault would bind me to him forever.

The braided grass creaked once beneath the Dohkar’s sandal as he faced me, the weight of every First Favored eye in the clearing pressing harder than the silk across my mouth. I could not let them name this wrong. My voice came muffled but steady enough.“Seren did not twist the wish. She offered her memory of the day she chose her path so that I might find the one I lost. The words were hers, and Fate answered them as spoken. ”

Seren’s small hands stayed still at her sides, but the confusion on her face sharpened into something closer to fear. The Dohkar took one slow step toward me, the braided grass creaking beneath his sandal.“You claim knowledge of the misalignment,” he said. “Speak plainly. ”

The air felt thinner. I moved before I could weigh the cost. My hand trembled once at my side, then steadied as I stepped forward into the center of the clearing. The mark of my involvement was no longer hidden behind silence. The assembled watched the motion, and his thumb traced the rim of his sandal strap once.

Julian’s absence pulled at the edge of the moment. I remembered the way he had turned without a word, the small silver ring on his right hand catching the last slant of dawn before the trees took him. He had known something the rest of us had not yet named.The Dohkar’s next breath dragged across his teeth.“You were the one the sacrifice reached. That changes what must be asked of you now. ”

Seren looked between us, her coral hair falling forward as she leaned slightly, as if trying to catch words she could no longer remember. The spiralgrass at the clearing’s edge remained dark where Julian had stood. No one moved to restore its color.

I felt the old deck still in my palm, its gold edges warm against my skin. No one else moved. The choice between naming my part aloud or letting the silence shape it for me sat between us like a blade.The top edge of the deck pressed a thin line into my palm.

I realize that old tarot deck is still in my hand. Reassuringly solid. Present. One of the heavy gold cards slips out — “Petition Fate” is inscribed in it. If only the Dohkar would let me walk away without answering. No. I would ask Fate to show me what Julian saw when he left.

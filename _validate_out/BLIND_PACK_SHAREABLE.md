# Blind read — four scenes, two versions each

In each scene, one column is version **X** and the other is version **Y**. One of them is a raw
first-draft passage; the other is the same passage after an automated editing pipeline. Which is
which is randomized per scene and withheld.

**Task:** for each scene, judge X vs Y independently and report:

- overall preference: X / Y / tie
- strength: decisive / clear / slight / coin-flip
- prose quality
- clarity and causal legibility (can you follow what physically happens?)
- character interiority
- voice consistency
- dialogue
- pacing
- specificity and imagery
- repetition or calcified phrasing
- does either feel over-edited or mechanically "improved"?

Do not infer from length; it is not a reliable signal here.

## Scene S1

### S1 — Version X

The elder's hand rose for the warding gesture, his fingers already tracing the first curve of the binding sigil meant to lock the failure in place. I broke the band across my mouth with a sharp twist of my wrist, the leather snapping free so the next words could leave me clear. The accusation the elder had started to form died on his tongue as I spoke the invocation syllable aloud, forcing the final thread of the rite into the open air where any hidden misalignment could be measured.

The family at the canopy's edge surged forward, their voices rising in a single wave of blame. They named me the interference, the one who had stepped between the youth and the elder's seal, the one who had dragged the failed wish upright instead of letting the price claim it cleanly. The elder's eyes narrowed further, the luminous violet of his pupils contracting as he turned the charge back on the youth herself, claiming the sour twist in the spiralgrass could only have come from an ill-wish she had carried into the circle. The crowd's murmurs thickened, some naming Julian again, the one whose alignment-sense never wavered, the one they would call if the rite could not be salvaged without further loss.

I tried to name the twist I felt beneath my palm, the way the grass had shifted from crimson to a dull, resisting brown the moment the youth's voice warped. The words would not arrange themselves. The misalignment was real, the bond between the youth and the wish fraying in a pattern I had never encountered, yet I could offer no cause, no source, no name that would satisfy the circle. My heel pressed harder into the spiralgrass, the blades yielding only enough to show the pressure and no more.

The elder's shadow lengthened across both of us. "The rite is sealed," he said, voice low and final. "Any further interference will be read as defiance of the public safeguard."

The youth's eyes had gone fully translucent, her weight sagging against my grip. I held her upright, my alignment-sense still flaring hot against the sour thread, and the crowd's accusations circled tighter. Someone at the edge called for Julian by name, the syllables carrying the weight of precedent. The elder did not object. He simply waited, the warding gesture half-formed in the air between us.

Julian arrived without announcement, the canopy parting around his presence as though the weave itself had already decided the matter. He moved through the gathered First Favored with the quiet intensity that always made the room feel smaller, his dark chestnut hair falling forward as he surveyed the failed circle. The slight asymmetry of his mouth tightened when his gaze found the youth's rigid posture and the elder's unfinished sigil. He did not speak at once. His hand turned the signet ring on his finger once, the metal catching the low light that filtered through the willow veil-canopy.

I felt the shift the moment his eyes met mine across the youth's bowed head. The crowd's accusations had not reached him yet, but the tension had. He lowered himself beside me with visible reluctance, the broad line of his shoulders settling into the space the elder had left open. His palm came to rest on the Alignment Circle etched into the spiralgrass, the contact deliberate and measured, as though the circle itself might resist him.

The decision pressed against my ribs before he spoke. I could demand the full weight of his alignment-sense, the complete examination that would bind him to the outcome as surely as the wish had bound the youth. Or I could accept the limited reach he offered now, the single hand on the circle, the refusal to commit beyond what the elder's summons required.

Julian's fingers spread across the etched lines. The grass beneath them darkened another shade, the sour twist responding to his touch with a slow, reluctant pulse. He did not look at me again. His attention stayed on the circle, the weight of what he was choosing to measure settling between us like another unspoken accusation.

I realize that old tarot deck is still in my hand. Reassuringly solid. Present. One of the heavy gold cards slips out — "Petition Fate" is inscribed in it. If only it were that easy. I would wish myself a way out. No — a way in. If only…

## Scene S2

### S2 — Version X

The rustling leaves of the willow tree held a held-breath stillness, the air under the branches refusing to move. I tilted my head back, letting the dappled light play across my lips, and exhaled against the curve of the deck still warm from my palm. The card beneath my thumb shifted—Petition Fate. The ink at the bottom edge glinted faintly, the edge of the card catching the light as if the paper had shifted. I didn’t pull my hand away. Not yet. Julian’s shadow fell across my boots before I heard him. The willow branches bent where he passed, the motion arriving with him. He stood half a stride away, his stance casual but his knuckles white where they gripped the Alignment Circle etched into the spiralgrass. The sigil under his palm pulsed once, a slow current of violet light bleeding from the lines into the soil. He didn’t look at me. His gaze stayed fixed on the circle, his breath measured, the count of seconds audible in the pause before he could lift his hand.

“You’re still here,” he said, voice low and deliberate. The words weren’t an accusation, but they weren’t kindness either. They landed between us as a challenge. I wrapped my fingers around the deck, the gold edges biting into my skin.

“You’re the one who hasn’t left.”

His mouth twisted, not quite a smile. The gap between his front teeth showed for a fraction of a second, then vanished under the neutral set of his jaw. The ring clicked once against his knuckle, the sound thin against the grass.

“Alignment-sense tells me the bond is fraying faster than it should. The elder claims it’s the youth’s fault. I say it’s the elder’s design.”

His fingers flexed against the circle, the spiralgrass darkening where pressure met the lines.

“Either way, the circle won’t last another turning without intervention.”

I shifted my weight, the tarot deck pressing harder against my ribs.

“Intervention requires two hands.”

Julian’s eyes flickered toward me then, a faint scar at the corner of his left eye pulling tighter. They narrowed just enough to register the invitation.

“Intervention requires trust,” he said.

“And I haven’t decided you’re worth the risk.”

The air between us thickened, the willow’s rustling rising as the branches shifted overhead. My fingers twitched against the deck. I could feel the weight of the alignment issue pressing against my ribs, the elder’s life force bleeding into the earth beneath us. But it wasn’t the crisis that made my breath stutter. It was the way Julian’s gaze lingered on the curve of my throat, the way his fingers curled slightly against the circle as if holding back from reaching for me instead. He exhaled sharply; a dry leaf skittered across the spiralgrass between us.

“Fine,” he said.

“But it’s your fault if the circle breaks.”

His hand turned the ring again, the metal catching the light one last time before he pressed the other palm to the Alignment Circle. The sigil flared white-hot for a heartbeat, the spiralgrass hissing beneath the heat. I didn’t look away. I couldn’t. Not when the circle pulsed again, not when the ground trembled beneath us, not when Julian’s heel scraped once against the spiralgrass. The alignment-sense flared against my ribs, the pressure of it pressing against the back of my teeth, but I didn’t break the gaze. Not when the circle flared, not when the branches above answered with their own shift, not when the deck in my hand warmed like a living thing. Petition Fate. The thought flickered through me, small and desperate. One ask. One small thing to tip the scales back in our favor.

“Tempt Fate.”

Grandma never let me play with that one. A sword raised to a blood-red sky, lightning arcing down from the clouds (or maybe arcing up?) Ow! Static shock reminds me I need to pay attention to the present…

I realize the deck is still in my hand. As I slide Petition Fate back in, I notice a flash of red in the otherwise gold stack. "Tempt Fate." Ow!

### S2 — Version Y

I tilted my head back, letting the dappled light play across my lips, and exhaled against the curve of the deck still warm from my palm. The card beneath my thumb shifted—Petition Fate. “You’re still here,” he said, voice low and deliberate. The words weren’t an accusation, but they weren’t kindness either. They were a challenge wrapped in the shape of a statement.

I wrapped my fingers around the deck, the gold edges biting into my skin. “You’re the one who hasn’t left.”

His mouth twisted, not quite a smile. The gap between his front teeth showed for a fraction of a second, sharp and deliberate, like he’d rehearsed the imperfection. He turned the signet ring on his finger once, the metal catching the filtered light. “Alignment-sense tells me the bond is fraying faster than it should. The elder claims it’s the youth’s fault. I say it’s the elder’s design.” His fingers flexed against the circle, the spiralgrass darkening where he touched it, as though the ground itself recoiled. “Either way, the circle won’t last another turning without intervention.”

I shifted my weight, the tarot deck pressing harder against my ribs. “Intervention requires two hands.”

Julian’s eyes flickered toward me then, his irises shifting between forest green and cool sea-gray like the tide caught between two moons. They narrowed just enough to let me know he’d registered the invitation in my words. “Intervention requires trust,” he said. “And I haven’t decided you’re worth the risk.”

The air between us thickened, the willow’s rustling growing louder, as though the tree itself had leaned in to listen. My fingers twitched against the deck. I could feel the weight of the alignment issue pressing against my ribs, the mentor’s life force bleeding into the earth beneath us. But it wasn’t the crisis that made my breath stutter. It was the way Julian’s gaze lingered on the curve of my throat, the way his fingers curled slightly against the circle as if holding back from reaching for me instead.

He exhaled sharply, the breath hitching for a single impossible moment, the air between us thickening with something unspoken. “Fine,” he said. “But it’s your fault if the circle breaks.” His hand turned the ring again, the metal catching the light one last time before he pressed the other palm to the Alignment Circle. The sigil flared white-hot for a heartbeat, the spiralgrass hissing beneath the heat.

I didn’t look away. I couldn’t. Not when the circle pulsed again, not when the ground trembled beneath us, not when Julian’s breath hitched once more, his gaze locked on mine with an intensity that made the world around us feel smaller. The alignment-sense flared against my ribs, the pressure of it pressing against the back of my teeth, but I didn’t break the gaze. Not when the circle groaned, not when the wood of the willow’s branches groaned in answer, not when the deck in my hand warmed like a living thing.

Petition Fate. The thought flickered through me, small and desperate. One ask. One small thing to tip the scales back in our favor.

I realize the deck is still in my hand. As I slide Petition Fate back in, I notice a flash of red in the otherwise gold stack. "Tempt Fate." Grandma never let me play with that one. A sword raised to a blood-red sky, lightning arcing down from the clouds (or maybe arcing up?) Ow! Static shock reminds me I need to pay attention to the present...

## Scene S4

### S4 — Version X

The murmur crested like a wave about to break, and I felt every eye in the room pivot toward the three of us. My fingers still hovered above the donor’s phone, the contact line for Dorian Vale glowing like an accusation I had not yet answered. The truth about the letter burned in my throat—the one from my mentor two years ago, the one I had kept folded in my notebook like a private scar, the one that had quietly dismantled every proposal I had offered since. I had let everyone believe the silence was strategy. It was not.

A guest near the bar lifted his voice just enough to carry. “Wasn’t that the same mentor who called her work derivative at the last symposium? Said it mimicked last season’s aesthetic right to her face.”

The words landed like broken glass underfoot. Heat climbed my collarbone again, but this time it did not stop at the skin. I set the champagne glass down on the nearest marble ledge before it could betray me further.

Lila’s gaze flicked past my shoulder. The donors shifted, forming a loose ring that felt less protective than expectant. I reached for the revised program on the phone, the one with my slot already struck through, and tore the printed sheet in half. The pieces fluttered between us, landing on the polished floor like white flags no one had asked for.

A new figure cut through the ring. Elise moved with the practiced ease of someone who had once belonged here, her dark brown hair catching the overhead light in thick waves, the faint scar above her brow a quiet signature. She stopped one half-step closer than courtesy required, her voice smooth as she addressed the nearest board member.

“Still letting sentiment dictate the program, I see. Some things never change.”

Her smile arrived a beat late, the kind that pulled at old muscle memory before warning it away. I felt the room reorient around her the way it once had around him.

I met her eyes. “If you’re here to speak for Dorian’s foundation, say so plainly. Otherwise the rest of us still have work to finish.”

The decision settled in my chest before I could name it. I could retreat into the version of myself the board had already accepted, or I could let the next sentence carry the truth I had buried with the letter. The pieces of the program lay between my shoes, waiting.

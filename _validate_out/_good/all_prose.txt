The Dohkar called for the cost. “I offer that memory,” Seren said, “so that Lirael may find the one she lost.”
A man rose from the assembly. He had stood through the whole rite without speaking. “Lirael,” he said, and said my name the way only one person ever had.
Julian did not move. He never moves when people expect him to defend himself; I had watched him do it four times in six years, and each time I had mistaken it for coldness.
The memory Seren named was already gone. She reached for it and found nothing where it had been.
A knot in the wood of the offering stone was not there before.

The Dohkar’s gaze pinned me where I stood, the weight of every First Favored eye in the clearing pressing harder than the silk across my mouth. I could not let them name this wrong. My voice came muffled but steady enough. “Seren did not twist the wish. She offered her memory of the day she chose her path so that I might find the one I lost. The words were hers, and Fate answered them as spoken.”

Seren’s small hands stayed still at her sides, but the confusion on her face sharpened into something closer to fear. The Dohkar took one slow step toward me, the braided grass creaking beneath his sandal. “You claim knowledge of the misalignment,” he said. “Speak plainly.”

The air felt thinner. I moved before I could weigh the cost. My hand trembled once at my side, then steadied as I stepped forward into the center of the clearing. The mark of my involvement was no longer hidden behind silence. The assembled watched the motion, and the Dohkar’s eyes narrowed with new understanding.

Julian’s absence pulled at the edge of the moment. I remembered the way he had turned without a word, the small silver ring on his right hand catching the last slant of dawn before the trees took him. He had known something the rest of us had not yet named.

The Dohkar’s stare did not soften. “You were the one the sacrifice reached. That changes what must be asked of you now.”

Seren looked between us, her coral hair falling forward as she leaned slightly, as if trying to catch words she could no longer remember. The spiralgrass at the clearing’s edge remained dark where Julian had stood. No one moved to restore its color.

I felt the old deck still in my palm, its gold edges warm against my skin. The Dohkar waited. The assembly waited. The choice between naming my part aloud or letting the silence shape it for me sat between us like a blade.

I realize that old tarot deck is still in my hand. Reassuringly solid. Present. One of the heavy gold cards slips out — “Petition Fate” is inscribed in it.

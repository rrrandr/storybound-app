The braided grass at the clearing’s threshold crunched softly beneath my heel as I pivoted, searching the shifting sea of Veilweave. The gold edges of my great-grandmother’s deck bit into my palm, one card already working loose—Petition Fate, dull and heavy as a promise I hadn’t yet decided to make. The spiralgrass at the clearing’s edge darkened in patches, the color leaching into the soil like old blood, and I felt the Dohkar’s gaze settle on me before I even turned. His sandals hadn’t moved, but his posture had. A predator choosing his strike.

My fingers brushed the scar on my palm where I’d pressed too hard against Seren’s memory this morning. Three thin lines, still pink, still raw. The scar was nothing compared to what the clearing already knew about me.

A laugh threaded through the crowd—warm, familiar, impossible. Not Seren’s. Not the Dohkar’s. My breath caught before I could stop it. There, beyond the flickering Weave-Script patterns: Julian. Slate-blue skin catching the dawn light, long dark hair swinging like a curtain he hadn’t bothered to part. The silver ring on his right hand glinted as he lifted it, the motion slow enough to be deliberate. He wasn’t looking at me. He was looking past me.

The Dohkar’s voice slithered over the space between us. “You’re searching for a ghost, Sacrificiant.” His sandal pivot shifted the braided grass again, a quiet warning. “The living don’t linger where they’ve nothing left to take.”

I didn’t answer. Couldn’t. My pulse had found a rhythm it didn’t want to lose—fast, uneven, the kind that came before a fall. The card slipped out entirely. Petition Fate. The title settled across the bottom in gold leaf. I caught it against my thigh before it could hit the ground. The silver circle of the ring caught my eye again. He was still there. Still watching something beyond me.

The crowd’s murmurs thickened, the Weave-Script on my arms stuttering like a misaligned thought. Julian’s hand dropped to his side. He turned his back to me and walked deeper into the trees, the long hair swinging once before the foliage swallowed him whole. The Dohkar exhaled through his teeth. “You’re not the only one who sees what he wants to hide.” His gaze dragged over my shoulder, lingering. “Are you?”

I felt the scar on my palm pulse. The deck’s warmth against my skin was the only thing keeping me upright.

The threshold of the clearing pressed against my bare feet like a line I had already crossed. I looked for someone in the crowd—anyone whose Weave-Script might flare with recognition, anyone who might stand between the Dohkar’s stare and the silence that had swallowed Julian—but every gaze stayed fixed on me, the silk across my mouth damp with breath I could not shape into words.

The deck warmed against my palm. I wished, small and foolish, that the Dohkar would simply turn away and let the rite end as it should have. The braided grass at the edge of the clearing shivered once, though no wind moved it. A single petal of crimson spiralgrass lifted and settled again, as if something had passed beneath the soil.

The Dohkar’s eyes narrowed, not in dismissal but in something closer to calculation. He stepped back half a pace, the weight of his attention shifting to Seren as though the question had already answered itself. My pulse eased. Coincidence, I told myself. Nothing more.

Seren’s coral hair trembled against her aqua skin, her small hands still at her sides. The Dohkar’s voice came low. “The sacrifice reached you. That much is plain. What you choose to give in return is no longer a matter for this clearing alone.”

I felt the old deck shift in my grip, heavier for a single breath, the gold edge catching the dawn like a coin dropped in still water. The card I had not drawn leaned toward my thumb as though it had chosen its own place. I realized the deck was still in my hand. As I slide Petition Fate back in, I notice a flash of red in the otherwise gold stack. “Tempt Fate.” Grandma never let me play with that one. A sword raised to a blood-red sky, lightning arcing down from the clouds (or maybe arcing up?) Ow! Static shock reminds me I need to pay attention to the present…

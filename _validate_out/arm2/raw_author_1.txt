[TITLE: "The Veil's First Tear"]
[SYNOPSIS: "In the crimson spiralgrass of the Veilwood clearing, Lirael stands sealed as supervisor for her ward's coming-of-age rite. When Seren abandons the rehearsed wish and spends her First Sacrifice on Lirael instead, the precise cost of imprecise words becomes terrifyingly clear. What will the room demand when the price lands on the one who was meant to guide?"]

The Dohkar’s hand lifted, palm out, and the clearing fell silent except for the soft rustle of veilwood leaves.

Seren knelt in the center, aqua skin luminous under the gossamer tunic, pale coral hair falling forward as she bowed her head. Her Weave-Script glowed steady gold along her collarbones—calm, rehearsed. I knew every word she would speak. We had practiced them for months.

She began the invocation. Her voice carried clear across the braided spiralgrass. The cost came first, as it must: her earliest memory of choosing this path, the moment she decided to stand before Fate. The assembly listened, faces turned toward her.

Then she looked at me.

The words shifted. “I offer what I have,” she said, and the Dohkar’s hand trembled. “So that she finds the one she lost.”

The air changed. Every First Favored present felt it at once—a sour note under the skin. The Dohkar’s Weave-Script stuttered violet. A guest near the edge drew a sharp breath.

I could not speak. The gossamer band across my mouth muffled everything to a thick sound. My hands stayed at my sides, but the weight of every gaze pressed against the sash at my waist. Julian stood at the edge of the clearing, long black hair curtaining his slate-blue face, silver Weave-Script motionless on his arms. He did not move. He watched only me.

Seren’s offering hung unfinished. The memory she named drifted, unnamed, and Fate reached for the nearest shape that fit the words. A guest in the third row—an older woman whose name I did not know—straightened suddenly. Her eyes found mine across the clearing.

“You asked that she find the one she lost,” the woman said, voice thin. “She has.”

The spiralgrass at her knees darkened in a spreading ring. Seren’s Weave-Script flared once, bright, then dimmed to nothing at her throat. She opened her mouth and no sound came. The memory she had offered—the moment she chose this path—slipped from her as if it had never existed. She knew she had chosen something. She could no longer recall what.

The Dohkar’s hand dropped. The clearing held its breath.

My fingers found the edge of the band at my cheek. One pull would break protocol in front of every witness. The qualification I had trained for would be gone before the rite finished. Or I could stay sealed, let the moment pass, and let the room decide what my silence meant.

My hand tightened around my great-grandmother's old tarot deck. I didn't remember bringing it. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.

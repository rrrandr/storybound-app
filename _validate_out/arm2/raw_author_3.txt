The Dohkar’s hand stayed lifted, palm out, the weight of the circle heavy against my ribs. The clearing held its breath between the shattered vase and the scattered shards of crimson spiralgrass, catching the light like forgotten promises. My fingers tightened around the deck still clutched tight, the one card that had slipped free—Petition Fate—pressing into my palm. The band across my mouth was a second skin now, muffling everything except the pulse at my throat.

I let my gaze drift over the assembly.

The First Favored stood shoulder to shoulder, their gossamer tunics catching the afternoon light, skin luminous with Weave-Script flickering in time with their unspoken thoughts. A guest near the edge exhaled sharply, the sound cutting through the quiet like a blade. My breath hitched when my eyes passed over a familiar build—broad shoulders, the way he filled his space without trying. I searched for the single gold-capped tooth that would flash when he smiled, the one thing that made him stop being a silhouette and start being Julian.

The threshold waited ahead, a space between two standing stones where the braided spiralgrass parted. The Dohkar’s gaze followed me, patient, relentless. The air smelled of crushed red grass and something sharper beneath it, like lightning caught in the roots.

Seren stood at the edge of the clearing, arms crossed, her presence the only thing large enough to fill the space around her. Her Weave-Script glowed gold at her collarbone, steady, almost mocking. She didn’t look at me. She didn’t have to. The way her posture slanted toward the Dohkar said everything.

I took a step forward, then hesitated. The weight of the deck in my hand felt like a second pulse, erratic. The spiralgrass at my feet darkened at the edges, a slow tide creeping toward my toes. Somewhere in the crowd, a voice rose, too loud, and the circle flinched.

The Dohkar’s fingers twitched, just once.

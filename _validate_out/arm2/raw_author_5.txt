The threshold stones, worn smooth by generations of bare feet, marked the boundary between the ordinary and the offered. I scanned the crowd for a familiar face, searching for Julian’s steady presence amid the sea of First Favored.

The Dohkar’s voice cut through, direct and unyielding. “You stand at the edge of your own rite now, apprentice. Speak what the misalignment demands, or let the offering twist further.”

My breath caught for a moment before I spoke, the longing tightening in my chest as Julian’s gaze found me. His eyes dropped to my mouth for half a second longer than was polite, the pale hazel shifting like moss under storm light, and the weight of it pushed the words out.

The memory surfaced raw—the night my first love had pressed his forehead to mine beneath the Veilwood boughs and whispered that he would never ask me to choose between him and the path I had already begun. I had given him that night freely, the warmth of his hands, the sound of his laughter against my throat. Offering it now felt like tearing open a scar I had kept hidden even from myself.

Seren’s silence pressed against the clearing like a second band. The Dohkar waited, palm still raised. Julian’s hand turned the ring on his finger once, the metal catching what little light remained, and the motion steadied me even as it demanded I continue.

The words left me muffled but clear enough. “I offer the memory of my first love. The night I chose the path over the one who asked me to stay.”

The spiralgrass at my own feet darkened in a spreading ring. A card in the deck shifted, heavier now, as if it leaned toward the choice just made. The Dohkar’s Weave-Script flared once, then settled. Seren’s eyes widened, the only movement she could still make. Julian’s shoulders stayed relaxed, but the ring turned again, slower this time.

The threshold beneath my feet felt suddenly narrower. The Dohkar’s hand remained lifted. The clearing held its breath, waiting to see whether I would accept the price or reject the offering that now belonged to me.

I realize the deck is still in my hand. As I slide Petition Fate back in, I notice a flash of red in the otherwise gold stack. “Tempt Fate.” Grandma never let me play with that one. A sword raised to a blood-red sky, lightning arcing down from the clouds (or maybe arcing up?) Ow! Static shock reminds me I need to pay attention to the present…

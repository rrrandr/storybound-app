The youth's voice carried across the crimson spiralgrass. "I offer the discipline to finish my studies and train as a Sacrificiant."I stood sealed at the edge of the mated-pair trees, the gossamer band tight across my mouth. My hands stayed at my sides. The assembled First Favored watched in the expected hush, their Weave-Script drifting in slow tidal flows of anticipation. The presiding Dohkar nodded once, satisfied. Then the youth looked at me. Her aqua skin caught the light filtering through the veil-canopy. Pale coral hair framed her small face. "Instead I offer my prepared sacrifice for this: that you find the father who abandoned you."The words landed like a wrong note in the clearing. Every First Favored present felt it. The Dohkar's hands rose. Someone in the crowd breathed, "Never hand Fate a blank page."I tore the band from my mouth. The cloth fluttered to the spiralgrass as I stepped forward. The youth had meant it as a gift. She had built it from a confidence I once gave her in private. The sacrifice she named belonged to the other wish. The desire had changed; the price had not. Julian remained at the edge of the gathering, perfectly still. His long black hair curtained his slate-blue face. Silver Weave-Script moved across his skin in harmonic loops. He did not speak. A faint click sounded when his thumb pressed once against the clasp of his belt. The youth's voice faltered. Her hands, lifted in the invocation, trembled once and went still. The warmth left them. Her fingers cooled against the air as if the heat had been drawn out and carried elsewhere. She looked down at them, confused. A woman near the front of the crowd spoke without meaning to. "The man she seeks crossed the Ascendant Run three turnings ago. He asked after a daughter with dark brown hair and a wide mouth before he took the ferry at Gloamwater Bay."The words were ordinary. They should not have been spoken here. The crowd drew the obvious inference. The wish had been about me. I had moved at the instant it resolved. The assembly saw interference where none had been intended.The Dohkar's gaze settled on me. Her left heel lifted and settled twice against the spiralgrass. Julian had not shifted, but the weight of his attention pressed against the moment like a held breath. Every path forward now carried a cost I had not prepared to pay. I didn't remember bringing it.My hand tightened around my great-grandmother's old tarot deck. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.

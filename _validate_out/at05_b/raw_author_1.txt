[TITLE: "The First Fracture"]

[SYNOPSIS: "In the Veilwood clearing, Lirael supervises her first First Sacrifice under the mated-pair trees. When the youth's wish shifts toward a private truth, Fate answers and claims its price, leaving the rite in ruins and Lirael's qualification at risk."]

The youth spoke the rehearsed words, her voice steady across the crimson spiralgrass. I felt the alignment hold, the sacrifice matched to her training. Then her eyes found mine.

She changed the offering mid-sentence. The discipline she had prepared became something else entirely: the wish to find the father who had left me. The words left her mouth before I could move. The band across my own mouth turned the sound I made into a muffled pull of cloth.

Every First Favored in the clearing felt the break at once. The prepared price no longer matched the desire. The Dohkar at the center went still, hands half-raised. Julian stood at the edge, perfectly still, long black hair curtaining his slate-blue face. His silver Weave-Script pulsed once, a single harmonic loop across his throat.

The youth lifted her hands, palms open to the veil-canopy. "Take what you will," she said. "Only let the wish stand."

The assembly drew one sharp breath. An elder's fingers flew up in the old warding sign. "Never hand Fate a blank page," someone whispered.

The air warmed against my skin. The mated-pair trees leaned a fraction closer, leaves brushing the grass as if reaching for her. The warmth left my own hands first, a slow draining I could not stop.

Fate answered. A guest at the back of the gathering turned, eyes wide, and spoke the name of a man who had once lived near the Long Thread. The words were ordinary, the timing not. The wish had found its route through coincidence already present in the clearing.

The youth's eyes dimmed as I watched. The vivid aqua of her irises bled away at the edges, leaving them pale and strange. She staggered, one hand rising to her face. The Weave-Script on her arms stuttered into staccato flickers.

I tore the gossamer band from my mouth. The cloth gave with a soft rip. Protocol broke with it. Every gaze in the clearing turned toward me at once. The inference was already forming: the wish had been about me, and I had moved.

Julian's eyes met mine across the grass. My body answered before thought caught it, a tightening low in my spine, the old memory of his hand brushing my wrist during training, the way his stillness had once made the room rearrange around him.

The Dohkar's voice rose, formal and cold. The assembly shifted, a single step back from the youth and from me. The guest who had spoken the name looked stricken, already understanding she had been used.

I stood with the torn band in my hand. The rite was no longer mine to guide. The qualification I had trained for hung on what the Dohkar decided next, and on whether anyone believed I had not reached for my own wish through hers.

Will I confront the truth or let it consume me?

My hand tightened around my great-grandmother's old tarot deck. I didn't remember bringing it. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.

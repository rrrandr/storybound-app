[CHARACTERS: Lirael:F, Julian:M, the youth:F]

[TITLE: "The Price Named in Silence"]

[SYNOPSIS: Lirael stands as Sacrificiant for a youth's First Sacrifice in the Veilwood, her mouth sealed by ritual cloth. When the girl alters her prepared wish to grant Lirael the father she lost, the misalignment twists the rite and draws every eye. Julian watches from the edge as the cost lands and the assembly turns.]

The youth's voice rose clear across the crimson spiralgrass. "I offer the discipline to finish my studies and train as a Sacrificiant."

Her words matched the months of rehearsal exactly. I felt my shoulders ease under the gossamer sash. The Dohkar beside her nodded once, the motion small enough that only those who knew the rite would catch it. The band across my mouth held the expected weight, muffling any sound I might make without breaking the seal.

Then the girl looked straight at me. Her aqua skin caught the dawn light filtering through the mated-pair trees, pale coral hair shifting as she drew breath. "Instead I offer that same discipline so Lirael may find the father who abandoned her."

The clearing went still. The assembled First Favored—two dozen bare feet on the grass, Weave-Script flickering across every visible throat and wrist—registered the shift at once. I knew the exact moment the alignment broke because my own chest tightened in answer. The sacrifice she had prepared belonged to one desire. The words she spoke belonged to another. They could not travel the same path.

Julian stood at the clearing's edge, slate-blue skin and long black hair motionless against the pale canopy. He did not move, yet the weight of his attention reached me across the grass. He had come only to observe. Now the rite had become something else.

The Dohkar's hands lifted in the first warning sign. Too late. The air changed, a pressure that pressed against the inside of my ribs without touching skin. Everyone present felt it. The girl had handed Fate a bargain that could not be honored cleanly.

I tore the gossamer band from my mouth. The cloth came away with a soft rip that sounded louder than any shout. Protocol shattered in the same instant the wish took hold.

A murmur ran through the crowd. One guest turned her face aside, silver Weave-Script dimming on her collarbone. Another pressed a hand to her own mouth as if to keep words inside. The Dohkar's eyes found mine, the four-pointed pupils narrowing.

Fate answered without spectacle. A guest near the back—a woman I had never met—stepped forward and spoke the name of the man who had left me before I could walk. She said it as if she had just remembered an old debt she owed someone else. The words landed like a stone dropped in still water.

The girl gasped. Color drained from her eyes, the vivid aqua fading to a pale, empty hue that left her staring without recognition at the faces around her. She reached toward me, fingers trembling, then stopped. The discipline she had offered was gone, and with it the future she had planned. The Dohkar lowered his hands. No one spoke.

I stood with the torn band clenched in my fist, the taste of the cloth still on my tongue. Julian had not moved, yet the distance between us felt smaller than it had a moment before. The assembly's eyes had already begun to weigh what my intervention would cost my own standing.

Will I confront her lies or retreat into silence?

My hand tightened around my great-grandmother's old tarot deck. I didn't remember bringing it. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.

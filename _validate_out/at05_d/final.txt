The youth knelt on the braided spiralgrass, her aqua skin catching the first light through the veil-canopy. She spoke the offering she had rehearsed with me for months. “I give the discipline to finish my studies and train as a Sacrificiant,” she said, her voice steady. I relaxed behind the gossamer band. The words matched every alignment I had checked. The Dohkar beside her nodded once. Two dozen First Favored watched in the clearing, their Weave-Script moving in quiet colors across bare arms and throats. His voice came through the small silver device clipped to my belt, one heel grinding a slow circle into the moss, long black hair curtaining his slate-blue face. He had come only to observe. The girl lifted her head and looked straight at me. “I give the discipline to finish my studies,” she said again, “so that Lirael may find the father who abandoned her.”The prepared sacrifice no longer matched the desire. Everyone felt the sour note at once. The Dohkar’s hands rose. The crowd’s murmurs cut off.I tore the band from my mouth. The cloth fluttered to the grass. Protocol broke with it. I stepped forward, one hand already reaching for her shoulder. The wish landed before I could speak. A woman near the trees turned, her voice carrying without effort. “Your father left a mark on the Ascendant Run ledger the year you were born,” she said. “He traded his name for passage north. Everyone in the harbor knows the entry.”The words were ordinary. They were also true. The girl had not known them. Fate had simply placed the one person who did where she could not stay silent. The assembly drew the only conclusion they could see: I had reached through the rite for my own answer. Heat climbed my throat. The Weave-Script on my arms flared rose and silver, broadcasting the shame I could not hide. He had kept one hand cupped behind his ear then, too, as if any shift would reveal what he already knew about the lineage I refused to claim. The Dohkar’s voice cut through the silence. “The rite is compromised.”The girl looked up at me, eyes wide, the gift she had meant to give already turning to ash between us. I had torn the seal. I had moved toward the wish that named me. The assembly would report both. I didn't remember bringing it.My hand tightened around my great-grandmother's old tarot deck. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.

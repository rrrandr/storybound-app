The youth knelt on the braided spiralgrass, her aqua skin catching the filtered dawn light through the weeping-willow veil-canopy. She spoke the rehearsed offering in a clear, steady voice that carried across the two dozen assembled First Favored. "I give the discipline to finish my studies and earn the right to train as a Sacrificiant."The words settled like a proper weight. I felt the alignment hold, the rite moving as it should. My mouth-band stayed in place, the gossamer seal doing its decorum work while my eyes tracked the youth's Weave-Script for any flicker of doubt. It flowed calm, slow tidal patterns across her arms. Then she lifted her head and looked straight at me. "I give that you find the father who abandoned you."The shift landed like a wrong note in a held chord. The sacrifice she had prepared belonged to one desire. The words she spoke belonged to another. Everyone present felt the misalignment at once—the trained ones sooner, the rest a heartbeat behind. The presiding Dohkar's hands rose an inch from his sides. Guests shifted on the crimson grass. I tore the mouth-band free. The cloth parted with a soft rip that sounded louder than it should have. Protocol broke in the same instant the wish took root. Fate honored the exact words the youth had spoken. A guest near the outer ring of the clearing—an older First Favored with slate skin and silver threading through his hair—stepped forward without meaning to, compelled by the literal shape of the ask. He opened his mouth and named a name I had never heard spoken in daylight. The information arrived as coincidence dressed in ordinary cloth. The youth's eyes lost their color in the same breath. The vivid coral hue drained from her irises, leaving them pale and strange while the rest of her face stayed unchanged. She blinked once, twice, and the loss stayed. Her Weave-Script stuttered, then resumed in a thinner rhythm. The assembly drew the only conclusion it could see. I had moved at the exact moment the wish resolved. The wish had been about me. Interference looked obvious and self-serving. Murmurs rose, low and sharp. I remembered the way his long black hair had curtained the sides of his slate-blue face when he stood at the edge of gatherings, perfectly still. He had come only to observe. His silver Weave-Script lay quiet across his shoulders, but his gaze had not left me since the youth changed her words. I felt the weight of that attention the way one feels a held breath in a crowded room. The Dohkar's voice cut through the rising sound, formal and final. "The rite is concluded. The alignment will be reviewed."I stood with the torn band in my hand, the youth still kneeling, her pale eyes fixed on the grass. The name the guest had spoken hung between us all. The crowd's attention pressed like a physical thing, and the cost of speaking now sat heavier than the cost of silence. I didn't remember bringing it.My hand tightened around my great-grandmother's old tarot deck. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.

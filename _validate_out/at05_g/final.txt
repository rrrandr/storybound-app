The youth knelt on the braided spiralgrass, her aqua skin catching the dawn light that filtered through the mated-pair trees. A gossamer tunic bloomed white across her small frame. She spoke the rehearsed offering in a clear voice that carried to every First Favored present. "I offer the discipline to finish my studies and train as Sacrificiant."I felt the alignment click into place. The Dohkar presiding nodded once. My own ceremonial sash lay heavy across my shoulders, the mouth-band tied snug beneath it. The words were exactly what we had practiced for months. Relief loosened the tension in my jaw. Then her coral hair shifted as she looked straight at me. "I offer it instead so Lirael finds the father who abandoned her."The prepared sacrifice and the new desire sat wrong together. Everyone felt it. The Weave-Script on the youth's skin flickered from steady gold to a jagged pulse. My alignment-sense registered the sour note at once. The Dohkar's hands rose. Julian stood at the clearing's edge, perfectly still, silver script moving slowly across his slate-blue skin. I could not speak cleanly. The band muffled everything. The assembly's attention swung toward me. A youth's First Sacrifice was meant to belong to her alone. Wishing on another person's hidden lineage broke the rule everyone knew. My qualification hung on whether I stayed sealed and silent. The youth's voice trembled on the final syllable. Fate answered before any of us moved. An older guest at the back reached into his tunic and drew out a folded scrap of paper he had carried for years without knowing why. He read the name written there aloud. It matched the one the youth had spoken. The crowd understood the inference at once. The wish had reached me. My intervention would only confirm it. I tore the gossamer band free. The cloth whispered against my skin as it fell. My voice came hoarse from disuse. "Stop. The offering is misaligned."The Dohkar turned. The assembly's Weave-Script flared in a dozen colors at once. Julian's gaze found mine across the clearing. He did not move, but the stillness itself felt like a question I had no answer for. The paper in the guest's hand caught the light. The name on it would not be unspoken now. Will she confront the accusation or remain silent and let it fester? I didn't remember bringing it.My hand tightened around my great-grandmother's old tarot deck. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.

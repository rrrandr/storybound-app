The fire had already reached the outer veil-curtains when Seren looked up from her kneeling place. The white foliage caught fast, and the heat pressed every First Favored forward even as their human wards and children stumbled behind them. I could not speak through the gossamer band across my mouth. My only duty was to watch for misalignment and stop it. Seren’s prepared invocation had been perfect for months. She would offer the memory of choosing this path and ask for the strength to walk it cleanly. The Dohkar waited for her to begin. Instead, she turned her face toward me. “I offer whatever it costs,” she said, her voice clear over the crackle. “So that she may find the one she lost.”The murmurs cut off at once. An old woman’s hands flew up. “Never hand Fate a blank page,” someone breathed, and the clearing went still. Seren’s Weave-Script flared bright coral across her aqua skin, then stuttered. The spiralgrass at her knees darkened in a spreading ring. A single leaf-curtain tore free and drifted upward instead of falling. The omen hung there, impossible and quiet. The fire guttered. The path behind us opened, clear and safe. Relief moved through the crowd in a single exhaled breath. Then the Dohkar’s son rose from his place at the edge. He had come only to witness his cousin’s rite. He looked at Seren, at me, at the ground that had just changed, and turned without a word. He walked into the smoke and did not look back. The assembly understood which word had bent. “The one she lost” had meant him, and the wish had answered exactly. Seren’s script went dark at the throat. Her mouth opened, and no sound came. The price had chosen its own shape. I tore the band from my face. The cloth fluttered to the crimson grass. Every eye turned. They had seen me move the instant the wish named me. They had seen the band come off while the rite still ran. The inference was already forming, loud and public: the supervisor had interfered for her own gain. Julian’s name rose in my mind the way it always did when the ground shifted beneath me. I remembered the way he had knelt in the dust last turning to untangle a child’s kite string, patient and unhurried while the rest of us watched the sky. He had not cared who saw the small service. The memory steadied nothing now. The Dohkar’s gaze found mine across the clearing. He did not speak. He did not need to. My first supervision had ended with a wish spoken for me and a boy walking into smoke because of it. The qualification I had trained for was already in question. I didn't remember bringing it.My hand tightened around my great-grandmother's old tarot deck. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.
The Dohkar’s left thumb pressed once against the seam of his robe. Every First Favored in the clearing waited for the words that would either clear my name or seal the accusation already forming in their eyes. I opened my mouth to explain that the wish had never been mine to shape, that Seren had spoken without my prompting, that the band had come off only after the rite had already bent.My hand moved with the words. The deck was still there, heavier than I remembered, and the motion was small, nothing more than a gesture to steady myself. The protective weave around the ritual ground caught the edge of it and tore. The shimmer that had kept the rite contained split open with a sound like thread snapping. Cold air rushed in where warmth had been held. The barrier was gone.A witness near the front, an older woman whose Weave-Script ran silver over pearl skin, spoke before anyone else could.“You asked Fate for protection and it answered by exposing you instead.”The Dohkar did not move. A single bead of sweat traced the line of his collarbone. The inference had already taken root in every face turned toward me. Claim the interference or let them keep believing I had broken the seal on purpose. Either choice would cost something I could not yet name.Julian’s last words to me returned without invitation, the exact cadence of each syllable as he had said them in the rain. My heel lifted once from the packed earth then settled again that had nothing to do with the clearing and everything to do with the memory of how he once looked at me as though the truth were already visible and simply waiting to be spoken.I realize that old tarot deck is still in my hand. Reassuringly solid. Present. One of the heavy gold cards slips out — “Petition Fate” is inscribed in it. If only it were that easy. I would wish for them to hear the truth when I speak it. No. I would wish for him to already know it before I have to say a word.
The threshold of the ritual ground held the weight of every footstep that had crossed it. The protective weave had torn, and cold air from beyond the clearing pressed against my skin while the question waited in the gap. I searched the crowd for Julian, needing the steadiness of his presence, the way his gaze had always found the truth before others could name it.The Dohkar’s thumb remained pressed to the seam of his robe. Seren knelt where she had spoken, her aqua skin dimmed at the throat. My fingers traced the edge of the envelope that held the memory I had not yet offered, the one of childhood laughter that once rose unbidden in every quiet moment. The deck in my hand felt heavier than before. I wished, silently and without ceremony, that the Dohkar would speak my name instead of the accusation forming on every face.A single spiralgrass blade at the edge of the clearing bent toward me, though no wind stirred the rest. The motion was small, easily missed. The Dohkar’s shoulders eased by a fraction, and the first words out of his mouth were not the condemnation the crowd expected.“Lirael,” he said, voice low. “Step forward.”I moved. The memory rose with me, the sound of my own laughter as a child when my mother had pressed a crimson blade between my palms and told me it would remember me if I ever forgot myself. The clearing registered the shift. Several First Favored turned their heads toward the note that had already faded.Seren lifted her face. “You did not ask for this.”“I did not,” I answered. The words left my mouth clearly now that the band was gone. “But the wish named me anyway.”The Dohkar studied the darkened grass at Seren’s knees. “The price has chosen its shape. The memory you have carried since you were small enough to be lifted will serve as payment. You may surrender it and keep your standing, or you may keep the memory and lose what the rite has already taken from you.”The choice settled between my ribs before the words finished. The crowd waited. Seren’s Weave-Script flickered once, uncertain. I could feel the deck warming in my palm, a card leaning toward the decision I had not yet made.As the tension in the clearing thickened, a sudden shimmer of energy surged through the air, and the Arcane Binding manifested before me, a swirling glyph of light that anchored the moment. It pulsed with a resonance that echoed my inner conflict, urging me to confront the weight of my decision. The presence of the artifact intensified the stakes, reminding me that every choice bore consequences beyond my understanding.I realize the deck is still in my hand. As I slide Petition Fate back in, I notice a flash of red in the otherwise gold stack. “Tempt Fate.” Grandma never let me play with that one. A sword raised to a blood-red sky, lightning arcing down from the clouds (or maybe arcing up?) Ow! Static shock reminds me I need to pay attention to the present… If only it were that easy.

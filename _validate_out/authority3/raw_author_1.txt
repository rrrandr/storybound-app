[CHARACTERS: Lirael:F, Seren:F, Julian:M]
[TITLE: "The Wish That Named Her"]
[SYNOPSIS: In the Veilwood's first-sacrifice grove, a fire traps the swift and the slow alike. Lirael stands sealed and responsible as her ward abandons the rehearsed rite. One open-ended plea to Fate answers the flames but exposes what no one was meant to see.]

The fire had already reached the outer veil-curtains when Seren looked up from her kneeling place. The white foliage caught fast, and the heat pressed every First Favored forward even as their human wards and children stumbled behind them. I could not speak through the gossamer band across my mouth. My only duty was to watch for misalignment and stop it.

Seren’s prepared invocation had been perfect for months. She would offer the memory of choosing this path and ask for the strength to walk it cleanly. The Dohkar waited for her to begin. Instead she turned her face toward me.

“I offer whatever it costs,” she said, voice clear over the crackle. “So that she may find the one she lost.”

The murmurs cut off at once. An old woman’s hands flew up. “Never hand Fate a blank page,” someone breathed, and the clearing went still.

Seren’s Weave-Script flared bright coral across her aqua skin, then stuttered. The spiralgrass at her knees darkened in a spreading ring. A single leaf-curtain tore free and drifted upward instead of falling. The omen hung there, impossible and quiet.

The fire guttered. The path behind us opened, clear and safe. Relief moved through the crowd in a single exhaled breath.

Then the Dohkar’s son rose from his place at the edge. He had come only to witness his cousin’s rite. He looked at Seren, at me, at the ground that had just changed, and turned without a word. He walked into the smoke and did not look back. The assembly understood which word had bent. “The one she lost” had meant him, and the wish had answered exactly.

Seren’s script went dark at the throat. Her mouth opened and no sound came. The price had chosen its own shape.

I tore the band from my face. The cloth fluttered to the crimson grass. Every eye turned. They had seen me move the instant the wish named me. They had seen the band come off while the rite still ran. The inference was already forming, loud and public: the supervisor had interfered for her own gain.

Julian’s name rose in my mind the way it always did when the ground shifted beneath me. I remembered the way he had knelt in the dust last turning to untangle a child’s kite string, patient and unhurried while the rest of us watched the sky. He had not cared who saw the small service. The memory steadied nothing now.

The Dohkar’s gaze found mine across the clearing. He did not speak. He did not need to. My first supervision had ended with a wish spoken for me and a boy walking into smoke because of it. The qualification I had trained for was already in question.

Will I confront the truth or let the rumors define me?

My hand tightened around my great-grandmother’s old tarot deck. I didn't remember bringing it. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.

The Dohkar’s gaze held me like a pin through silk. Every First Favored in the clearing waited for the words that would either clear my name or seal the accusation already forming in their eyes. I opened my mouth to explain that the wish had never been mine to shape, that Seren had spoken without my prompting, that the band had come off only after the rite had already bent.

My hand moved with the words. The deck was still there, heavier than I remembered, and the motion was small, nothing more than a gesture to steady myself. The protective weave around the ritual ground caught the edge of it and tore. The shimmer that had kept the rite contained split open with a sound like thread snapping. Cold air rushed in where warmth had been held. The barrier was gone.

A witness near the front, an older woman whose Weave-Script ran silver over pearl skin, spoke before anyone else could. “You asked Fate for protection and it answered by exposing you instead.”

The Dohkar did not move. His stare did not shift. The inference had already taken root in every face turned toward me. Claim the interference or let them keep believing I had broken the seal on purpose. Either choice would cost something I could not yet name.

Julian’s eyes surfaced in my mind without invitation, emerald and cut into four-pointed diamonds that caught light the way no human gaze ever had. My pulse answered before I could stop it, a low thrum at the base of my throat that had nothing to do with the clearing and everything to do with the memory of how he once looked at me as though the truth were already visible and simply waiting to be spoken.

I realize that old tarot deck is still in my hand. Reassuringly solid. Present. One of the heavy gold cards slips out — “Petition Fate” is inscribed in it.

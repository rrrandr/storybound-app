I stepped over the threshold where the ritual’s containment had torn open, the crimson grass still dark at the edges from where the wish had bled. The spiralgrass curled inward like lips that had tasted something bitter. I could feel the weight of the memory I had offered—childhood laughter, bright and unguarded—but it sat in my chest hollow now, as if something had already taken a bite out of it. My fingers traced the edge of the envelope in my pocket, the one I had forgotten I was holding, its corners frayed from too many clenched fists. The Dohkar’s gaze felt like a blade resting against the back of my neck. I didn’t need to turn to know he had already decided my standing was revoked. “You were meant to supervise,” Seren said, her voice too steady for someone who had just offered up her own First Sacrifice like a coin for a stranger’s debt. She stood just inside the threshold, her coral hair catching the dim light like a beacon that wanted to guide but couldn’t decide which way to point. “You were meant to keep the rite clean,” she added, tilting her head as if waiting for an answer that wouldn’t come. The silence that followed wasn’t just absence of sound—it was a pressure, the kind that makes a breath feel like a thread about to snap. A witness coughed, the sound too loud in the hush. The spiralgrass at Seren’s knees darkened further, the embers woven into the blades dimming to nothing. I could feel Julian’s presence like a current beneath my ribs, the way he had once stood in the rain and looked at me as though the world could be rearranged if only he knew where to place his hands. The memory of it steadied me, made the hollowness in my chest stop aching for a moment. Seren’s Weave-Script flickered coral across her aqua skin—once, twice—then stuttered like a candle about to go out. “You think you’re the only one who’s ever wanted to be seen,” she said, and her voice wasn’t kind. “You’re not.” The words landed like stones in a pond, the ripples spreading outward in the faces around us. The Dohkar’s left thumb pressed against his robe’s seam, a rhythm I recognized from every time he had to deliver bad news. “You broke the seal,” someone murmured. “You broke the seal,” repeated another, and then another, until it wasn’t a statement anymore—it was an accusation. The ground beneath us vibrated with the weight of it, the spiralgrass groaning as if the earth itself had been asked to testify. Seren’s eyes—bright, too bright—locked onto mine. “You gave up your childhood laughter,” she said. “Was it worth it?” The question hung there, unanswerable. I could feel the memory slipping further away, the edges blurring like ink in water. The spiralgrass at my knees darkened too, the embers guttering like a breath held too long. The Dohkar finally spoke, his voice the sound of a man who had long since stopped believing in second chances. “The qualification you trained for is gone,” he said. “You knew the price before you offered the bargain.” I wanted to tell him he was wrong. I wanted to tell Seren she had no right to wield the memory like a weapon. I wanted to feel Julian’s hand in mine, grounding me. Instead, I looked down at the envelope in my pocket and realized I had been holding it so tightly my knuckles had gone white. The tarot deck in my other hand felt heavier than I remembered, its gold edges catching the dim light like a promise I didn’t dare open.

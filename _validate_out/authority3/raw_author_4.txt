The threshold of the ritual ground held the weight of every footstep that had crossed it. The protective weave had torn, and cold air from beyond the clearing pressed against my skin like a question waiting for an answer. I searched the crowd for Julian, needing the steadiness of his presence, the way his gaze had always found the truth before others could name it.

The Dohkar’s thumb remained pressed to the seam of his robe. Seren knelt where she had spoken, her aqua skin dimmed at the throat. My fingers traced the edge of the envelope that held the memory I had not yet offered, the one of childhood laughter that once rose unbidden in every quiet moment. The deck in my hand felt heavier than before. I wished, silently and without ceremony, that the Dohkar would speak my name instead of the accusation forming on every face.

A single spiralgrass blade at the edge of the clearing bent toward me, though no wind stirred the rest. The motion was small, easily missed. The Dohkar’s shoulders eased by a fraction, and the first words out of his mouth were not the condemnation the crowd expected.

“Lirael,” he said, voice low. “Step forward.”

I moved. The memory rose with me, the sound of my own laughter as a child when my mother had pressed a crimson blade between my palms and told me it would remember me if I ever forgot myself. The clearing felt the shift. Several First Favored turned their heads as if catching an old song.

Seren lifted her face. “You did not ask for this.”

“I did not,” I answered. The words left my mouth clearly now that the band was gone. “But the wish named me anyway.”

The Dohkar studied the darkened grass at Seren’s knees. “The price has chosen its shape. The memory you have carried since you were small enough to be lifted will serve as payment. You may surrender it and keep your standing, or you may keep the memory and lose what the rite has already taken from you.”

The choice settled between my ribs before the words finished. The crowd waited. Seren’s Weave-Script flickered once, uncertain. I could feel the deck warming in my palm, a card leaning toward the decision I had not yet made.

I realize the deck is still in my hand. As I slide Petition Fate back in, I notice a flash of red in the otherwise gold stack. “Tempt Fate.” Grandma never let me play with that one. A sword raised to a blood-red sky, lightning arcing down from the clouds (or maybe arcing up?) Ow! Static shock reminds me I need to pay attention to the present…

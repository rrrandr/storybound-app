The Dohkar’s voice cracked across the threshold like a snapped root. “You will speak now, or the rite names you its price.”  

My gaze cut through the crowd before I could stop it, searching for the dark copper hair and slate-blue skin that always steadied the air when everything else tilted. He was not there. The absence pulled at me sharper than the accusation.  

Seren’s aqua hands trembled where they pressed against the spiralgrass. Her coral hair hung forward, veiling eyes that would not meet mine. The Dohkar waited, thumb still pressed to the seam of his robe, and the weight of every First Favored eye pressed with him.  

I felt the old deck shift in my grip, one card leaning heavier against my palm as if it had chosen its own direction. The memory I had meant to keep—my mother’s laughter the morning the first veil-curtain bloomed—rose unbidden, sharp and bright behind my ribs. To name it aloud was to hand it over. To keep it was to let the accusation root deeper.  

The Dohkar took one step closer. “Choose.”  

I realized the deck is still in my hand. As I slide Petition Fate back in, I notice a flash of red in the otherwise gold stack. “Tempt Fate.” Grandma never let me play with that one. A sword raised to a blood-red sky, lightning arcing down from the clouds (or maybe arcing up?) Ow! Static shock reminds me I need to pay attention to the present…

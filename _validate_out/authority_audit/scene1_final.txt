The youth was sixteen today, old enough to make her first wish under the First Sacrifice rite. The youth lifted her chin and spoke the invocation straight into the clearing air, her voice carrying the name that cracked the rite open. I crossed the circle in two strides and seized her wrist before the final syllable could settle, forcing her gaze back to the prepared altar. A wrong note shivered through every Favored present, their shoulders locking as the mismatch struck their skin. My pulse slammed against my ribs, hot and uneven, while the Dohkar's hands rose in the sudden hush. "Stop," I said, the word scraping out as I yanked the gossamer band free and flung it toward the trees. The twist answered at once. A figure stepped from the shadows wearing the exact face I had once described in private, his eyes sliding past me without recognition. The youth cried out as something tore from her chest, her coral hair bleaching at the roots while the figure turned his back. I stepped between them, one hand lifted to bar his retreat, the other already reaching for the youth's shoulder to anchor what was left. Every eye in the assembly fixed on me. They had watched me break the rite. They had heard the wish name me aloud. Julian's left boot sole rasped once against the root beneath him. The sound pulled my attention sideways for a single heartbeat, his stare pressing between my shoulder blades like a held blade. The youth sank to her knees, one hand at her throat where the color had already begun to fade. I dropped to the ground with her, gripping her elbow to keep her upright, and felt the weight of every inference land on the faces around us. Say it plainly or let the silence finish the work? Julian shifted his weight again, the faint drag of his sole against bark threading through the quiet, and I refused to turn.My hand tightened around my great-grandmother's old tarot deck. I didn't remember bringing it. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.

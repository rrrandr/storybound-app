[CHARACTERS: Lirael:F, Julian:M, the youth:F]
[TITLE: "The Veil's First Fracture"]
[SYNOPSIS: In the Fatelands, Lirael must guide a young First Favored through her coming-of-age rite. When the wish twists toward her own hidden past, duty and desire collide under watchful eyes, threatening her rise as a Dohkar.]

The youth knelt on the crimson spiralgrass, her aqua skin luminous under the dawn veil-canopy. Her pale coral hair caught the light as she drew breath to speak the words we had rehearsed for months.

"I offer my discipline to finish my studies and earn the right to train as a Sacrificiant."

The Dohkar presiding nodded once. The assembled First Favored waited in stillness, their gossamer tunics softening every outline, Weave-Script flickering softly across arms and throats with quiet anticipation. Julian stood at the clearing's edge, slate-blue skin and long black hair motionless, silver patterns tracing his shoulders without a single shift.

The youth looked up. Her gaze found mine across the space between us.

"I offer that same discipline instead so Lirael may find the father who abandoned her."

The air changed. The Dohkar's hands lifted. Every First Favored present felt the sour note at once—the sacrifice no longer matched the desire. My alignment-sense rang wrong, the prepared price reaching for a different truth than the one now spoken.

I could not speak. The gossamer band across my mouth held the sound to a muffled shape. Protocol demanded I stay sealed, yet the wish had already bent. The crowd's Weave-Script stuttered. Someone near the mated-pair trees drew a sharp breath.

Julian's stillness deepened. He watched me, not the youth.

I tore the band free. The cloth whispered against my skin as it fell. I stepped toward her, one hand already rising.

The youth gasped. Her Weave-Script flared coral across her throat, then dimmed as if the light itself recoiled. A guest at the far side of the clearing—a woman whose emerald skin had been calm—spoke without meaning to.

"Her father crossed the Ascendant Run three turnings ago. He sought shelter in the Shackle Isles."

The words landed like a struck chord. They were true. They were not what anyone had prepared. The assembly turned as one. Eyes moved from the youth to me, then back, the inference already forming. A Sacrificiant had interfered. The rite had been bent for her own gain.

The Dohkar's face closed. Julian's silver Weave-Script slowed to a single repeating loop across his wrist, the scar there catching the light once.

I stood exposed, mouth bare, the band crumpled in my fist. The youth stared at me, eyes wide with the gift she had tried to give and the cost she had not understood.

Is this exposure — or protection?

My hand tightened around my great-grandmother's old tarot deck. I didn't remember bringing it. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.

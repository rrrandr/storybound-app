[TITLE: "The First Wish"]
[SYNOPSIS: "Lirael stands sealed as supervisor at a Veilwood First Sacrifice, her hidden lineage already under quiet scrutiny. When the youth turns the rite toward her, the cost lands in public view and leaves her qualification hanging by a thread."]

[CHARACTERS: Lirael:F, Julian:M]

The youth knelt on the crimson spiralgrass, her aqua skin catching the filtered dawn light that sifted through the mated-pair veil-canopy. Her coral hair clung damp at the temples. She drew a steadying breath and spoke the rehearsed words.

“I offer the discipline to finish my studies. Let it buy me the right to train as Sacrificiant.”

Her voice carried clean across the clearing. Two dozen First Favored stood barefoot in gossamer, Weave-Script shifting slow and calm across their skin. I felt the alignment hold. The Dohkar presiding gave the slightest nod. My shoulders eased beneath the ceremonial sash.

Then the girl lifted her head and looked straight at me.

“I offer the discipline to finish my studies,” she said again, louder, “so that Lirael may find the father who left her.”

The words landed like a stone in still water. The prepared sacrifice no longer matched the desire. Everyone present felt the sour note at once. The Dohkar’s Weave-Script stuttered. Gasps rippled through the guests. Julian, still at the edge of the gathering, did not move, yet the silver script on his slate-blue skin tightened into hard lines.

I could not speak. The gossamer band across my mouth muffled everything to a thick hum. My hands rose before I thought. One tug and the cloth tore free. Protocol shattered with it. The assembly drew the obvious conclusion: the supervisor had interfered for her own gain.

The girl’s eyes widened. She had meant it as a gift. Fate did not care about intent. The bargain had already twisted. Someone in the crowd would carry the tale to the Chayr before nightfall. My qualification as Dohkar hung by that single act.

Julian’s gaze found mine across the clearing. He had known about the lineage. He had never spoken of it until now, and now he did not need to. The weight of what the girl had offered settled between us like a third presence.

The Dohkar stepped forward, voice low. “The rite is ended. The alignment failed.”

Murmurs rose. A few guests turned away, their Weave-Script already dimming with disapproval. The girl remained kneeling, coral hair trembling against aqua skin, realizing too late what she had done. I stood exposed, mouth free, every eye measuring the cost of one public mercy.

Do I confront them and reveal my truth, or do I flee and hide from their judgment?

My hand tightened around my great-grandmother's old tarot deck. I didn't remember bringing it. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.

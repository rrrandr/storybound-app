The youth rose from the crimson spiralgrass, her coral hair trembling against her aqua skin. Her voice rang across the clearing, sharp with the certainty only the young can claim.

“She twisted the wish. She made me say it for her.”

Gasps cut through the guests like a sudden wind. Weave-Script flared and dimmed along arms and throats, betraying every flicker of judgment. The guide’s hands fell to his sides. The ceremonial sash across my mouth lay torn at my feet, and every eye marked the place where it had been.

I stepped forward before the silence could harden into verdict. My fingers still shook from the rip of cloth.

“I did not guide her words,” I said, voice raw from disuse behind the band. “The sacrifice was hers. I only tore the seal when the alignment broke.”

The girl’s eyes glistened, but she did not retreat. “You knew what I would offer before I spoke it. You wanted the truth about your father pulled into the open. I felt it through the rite.”

A low murmur answered her. Someone near the veil-canopy turned their face away, Weave-Script already cooling to ash-grey disapproval. The weight of their stares pressed between my shoulder blades like a second ceremonial sash.

Julian stood at the edge of the clearing, half-shadowed by the mated-pair canopy. His long lashes softened the hard line of his jaw as he watched me, the only movement the slow turn of the ring on his finger. He had not spoken. He did not need to. The fact that he knew the lineage I had tried to bury was suddenly visible to everyone.

I swallowed and tried again. “The wish twisted because the desire and the offered price no longer matched. That is all. I did not force the change.”

The girl’s heel lifted from the grass again, a small, involuntary motion that made her look even younger. “Then why did you tear the seal? Why break the law if you had nothing to hide?”

The question hung between us, heavier than any oath. The guide’s silence had already condemned me in the eyes of the assembly. My qualification as Sacrificiant hung on whether I could make them believe the truth I barely understood myself.

Julian’s gaze never left mine. The ring turned once more beneath his thumb, catching the filtered dawn.

I realize that old tarot deck is still in my hand. Reassuringly solid. Present. One of the heavy gold cards slips out — "Petition Fate" is inscribed in it.

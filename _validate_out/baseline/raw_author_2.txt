The Dohkar’s finger still hung between us like a drawn blade when I felt the words rise, unbidden and too loud. My hand left the girl’s wrist and lifted, one finger extended past the press of bodies toward the mated-pair trees.

“Julian did this,” I said. The clearing went still enough that the spiralgrass underfoot stopped its restless twist. “He pulled the wish off course. I felt the alignment shift the moment he looked at me.”

Julian’s long black hair stayed motionless while every other head turned. The scar across his left brow caught the light as he tilted his head, emerald eyes narrowing—not in denial, but in the quiet recognition of a test he had already set. The Weave-Script on his slate-blue skin flickered once, a single silver loop tightening before it smoothed again. His hand moved to the signet ring on his finger, turning it once with his thumb, the metal flashing as if answering the accusation itself.

The Dohkar’s stare swung between us, the girl forgotten. Murmurs rose—accusations that the rite had been tainted from the start, that an apprentice could not be trusted with the seal, that the ward’s words had been guided by something older and colder than any First Favored should allow. The girl’s coral hair trembled against her aqua cheek as she looked at me, eyes wide, the original price still half-formed on her lips.

I kept my finger extended, the accusation hanging between Julian and me like the broken vow it had become. The crowd’s voices pressed closer, demanding I name the father or name the interference, and the choice I had not yet made settled on my tongue like the first cold edge of a sacrifice I could not take back.

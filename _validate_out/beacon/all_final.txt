The Dohkar’s voice rose steady over the crimson spiralgrass. “Speak the offering, Seren.”

I stood at the edge of the mated-pair trees, my mouth sealed beneath the gossamer band, the weight of two dozen First Favored eyes pressing against my skin. My Weave-Script flickered silver across my throat, a thin pulse of unease I could not hide. Seren knelt in the center, her aqua skin luminous beneath her child’s tunic, and her pale coral hair fell forward as she bowed her head. She had rehearsed this for months. Every word was mine to correct, every silence mine to guard. She lifted her face and looked straight at me. “I offer the memory of the day I first chose this path,” she said, her voice clear. “So that Lirael finds the one she lost.”

The Dohkar’s hand faltered mid-gesture. A low murmur cut through the clearing like a blade drawn across silk. My chest tightened. She knew the rule. She had recited it back to me. The sacrifice belonged to the wisher. She had spent hers on me instead. Julian stood at the far edge of the gathering, his slate-blue skin motionless beneath sheer gossamer, and his long black hair curtained the line of his jaw. His silver Weave-Script stayed perfectly still. He watched the Dohkar, not me, and the stillness itself felt like accusation. Seren’s hands trembled once, then steadied. The Weave-Script on her arms bloomed soft coral, the color of someone trying to be kind in public. The Dohkar recovered his voice. “The wish is spoken.”

I felt the misalignment before anyone else moved. It tasted sour in the back of my throat, the way a note rings wrong when the singer no longer believes the words. Seren had meant compassion. She had delivered exposure instead. The memory she offered was real; the desire she named was not hers to carry.

Julian’s head tilted a fraction. The scar over his left wrist caught the dawn light as he folded his hands behind his back, the smallest shift, the kind that meant he had already chosen not to intervene. My own hands stayed at my sides. The band across my mouth muffled any sound I might make. I could only watch. The spiralgrass at Seren’s knees darkened at the edges, a slow bleed of color into the soil. No one spoke. The Dohkar’s Weave-Script stuttered once, gold threads breaking rhythm across his forearms. Someone in the back of the gathering drew a sharp breath and held it. Seren’s eyes widened. She had felt it too. The Dohkar raised both hands, palms open. “The wish is heard.”

A single leaf from the veil-canopy drifted down and landed on the grass between Seren and me. It lay there, white against crimson, the only movement in the clearing. I tore the gossamer band from my mouth. The silk whispered against my skin as it fell. Every eye turned. The Dohkar’s expression did not change, but his Weave-Script flared once, bright and warning. I took one step toward Seren. The grass crunched beneath my bare feet, too loud in the silence. Julian did not move. His gaze stayed on the leaf. Seren’s coral hair trembled as she looked up at me. “I only wanted—”

The Dohkar’s voice cut across hers, low and final. “The words are spoken.”

My skin flushed hot beneath the sheer tunic, the heat rising to my cheeks before I could stop it. I felt the flush spread across my throat, visible to everyone. The same heat I had spent years learning to hide. I didn't remember bringing it.

My hand tightened around my great-grandmother's old tarot deck. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.

The Dohkar’s gaze found me across the clearing, his Weave-Script flaring gold at the wrists as if he could still bind what had already slipped loose. “The words are spoken,” he repeated, slower now, and the weight of every First Favored eye settled on my bare feet in the crimson spiralgrass. I stepped forward anyway, the grass crackling under me, and opened my mouth to explain before the assumption hardened into something worse.

“I knew the rule,” I said, voice raw from the sealed band. “Seren spoke it back to me every rehearsal. The sacrifice belongs to the wisher. She meant to offer something small, something that was hers. I never asked her to—”

My hand shook. The protective barrier the Dohkar had raised around the mated-pair trees flickered once, thin silver threads stuttering like a flame in wind. The failure was visible, undeniable, a ripple that ran through the grass at Seren’s knees and died at my toes. Every head turned. The Dohkar’s palms stayed open, but his Weave-Script dimmed at the edges, and the low murmur from the gathering sharpened into something closer to accusation.

Seren’s coral hair trembled as she looked between me and the failing light. “Lirael—”

Julian moved. He had been still as carved slate at the clearing’s rim, but now he crossed the grass in three strides, the scar at his left wrist catching the dawn as he folded his arms. His long black hair curtained his face when he lowered his head to study the barrier’s broken edge, then lifted again. The gap in his smile was gone. “You knew the rule,” he said, quiet enough that only those nearest heard. “Yet you let her speak it anyway. Was that mercy, or did you want the assembly to see what she had done for you?”

The question landed like a test he already knew the answer to. I felt my skin flush hot beneath the sheer tunic, the heat rising to my throat before I could stop it, the same living barometer I had spent years learning to hide. My fingers tightened around the deck I had not remembered bringing. The Dohkar’s voice cut through the silence again, low and final. “The wish is heard. The offering stands.”

Present.

As the tension in the air thickened, the surroundings shifted, revealing the Cursed betrothal hall, its ancient stones steeped in whispered secrets and forgotten promises. Shadows danced along the walls, adding a weight to the atmosphere as if the very structure was aware of the stakes at play. The hall loomed behind us, a silent witness to the unfolding drama, its presence amplifying the gravity of our choices.

I realize that old tarot deck is still in my hand. Reassuringly solid. Present. One of the heavy gold cards slips out — “Petition Fate” is inscribed in it. If only the Dohkar would name the misalignment aloud and spare me the choice. No. I would ask Fate to make Julian look at me without waiting to see whether I claim it or let the assumption stand.

[TITLE: "The Wish That Named Her"]
[SYNOPSIS: In the Veilwood clearing Lirael stands sealed and responsible for her ward’s First Sacrifice, but when Seren offers her own memory for someone else’s wound the rite bends under Fate’s precision, and the cost begins to settle on the one who was meant to guide it.]

The Dohkar’s voice rose steady over the crimson spiralgrass.

“Speak the offering, Seren.”

I stood at the edge of the mated-pair trees, mouth sealed beneath the gossamer band, the weight of two dozen First Favored eyes pressing against my skin. My Weave-Script flickered silver across my throat, a thin pulse of unease I could not hide. Seren knelt in the center, aqua skin luminous beneath her child’s tunic, pale coral hair falling forward as she bowed her head. She had rehearsed this for months. Every word was mine to correct, every silence mine to guard.

She lifted her face and looked straight at me.

“I offer the memory of the day I first chose this path,” she said, voice clear. “So that Lirael finds the one she lost.”

The Dohkar’s hand faltered mid-gesture. A low murmur cut through the clearing like a blade drawn across silk. My chest tightened. She knew the rule. She had recited it back to me. The sacrifice belonged to the wisher. She had spent hers on me instead.

Julian stood at the far edge of the gathering, slate-blue skin motionless beneath sheer gossamer, long black hair curtaining the line of his jaw. His silver Weave-Script stayed perfectly still. He watched the Dohkar, not me, and the stillness itself felt like accusation.

Seren’s hands trembled once, then steadied. The Weave-Script on her arms bloomed soft coral, the color of someone trying to be kind in public.

The Dohkar recovered his voice. “The wish is spoken.”

I felt the misalignment before anyone else moved. It tasted sour in the back of my throat, the way a note rings wrong when the singer no longer believes the words. Seren had meant compassion. She had delivered exposure instead. The memory she offered was real; the desire she named was not hers to carry.

Julian’s head tilted a fraction. The scar over his left wrist caught the dawn light as he folded his hands behind his back, the smallest shift, the kind that meant he had already chosen not to intervene. My own hands stayed at my sides. The band across my mouth muffled any sound I might make. I could only watch.

The spiralgrass at Seren’s knees darkened at the edges, a slow bleed of color into the soil. No one spoke. The Dohkar’s Weave-Script stuttered once, gold threads breaking rhythm across his forearms. Someone in the back of the gathering drew a sharp breath and held it.

Seren’s eyes widened. She had felt it too.

The Dohkar raised both hands, palms open. “The wish is heard.”

A single leaf from the veil-canopy drifted down and landed on the grass between Seren and me. It lay there, white against crimson, the only movement in the clearing.

I tore the gossamer band from my mouth. The silk whispered against my skin as it fell. Every eye turned. The Dohkar’s expression did not change, but his Weave-Script flared once, bright and warning. I took one step toward Seren. The grass crunched beneath my bare feet, too loud in the silence.

Julian did not move. His gaze stayed on the leaf.

Seren’s coral hair trembled as she looked up at me. “I only wanted—”

The Dohkar’s voice cut across hers, low and final. “The words are spoken.”

My skin flushed hot beneath the sheer tunic, the heat rising to my cheeks before I could stop it. I felt the flush spread across my throat, visible to everyone. The same heat I had spent years learning to hide.

Do I confront the whispers and reveal my truth, or do I retreat into silence and shame?

My hand tightened around my great-grandmother’s old tarot deck. I didn't remember bringing it. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.

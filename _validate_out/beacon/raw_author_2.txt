The Dohkar’s gaze found me across the clearing, his Weave-Script flaring gold at the wrists as if he could still bind what had already slipped loose. “The words are spoken,” he repeated, slower now, and the weight of every First Favored eye settled on my bare feet in the crimson spiralgrass. I stepped forward anyway, the grass crackling under me, and opened my mouth to explain before the assumption hardened into something worse.

“I knew the rule,” I said, voice raw from the sealed band. “Seren spoke it back to me every rehearsal. The sacrifice belongs to the wisher. She meant to offer something small, something that was hers. I never asked her to—”

My hand shook. The protective barrier the Dohkar had raised around the mated-pair trees flickered once, thin silver threads stuttering like a flame in wind. The failure was visible, undeniable, a ripple that ran through the grass at Seren’s knees and died at my toes. Every head turned. The Dohkar’s palms stayed open, but his Weave-Script dimmed at the edges, and the low murmur from the gathering sharpened into something closer to accusation.

Seren’s coral hair trembled as she looked between me and the failing light. “Lirael—”

Julian moved. He had been still as carved slate at the clearing’s rim, but now he crossed the grass in three strides, the scar at his left wrist catching the dawn as he folded his arms. His long black hair curtained his face when he lowered his head to study the barrier’s broken edge, then lifted again. The gap in his smile was gone. “You knew the rule,” he said, quiet enough that only those nearest heard. “Yet you let her speak it anyway. Was that mercy, or did you want the assembly to see what she had done for you?”

The question landed like a test he already knew the answer to. I felt my skin flush hot beneath the sheer tunic, the heat rising to my throat before I could stop it, the same living barometer I had spent years learning to hide. My fingers tightened around the deck I had not remembered bringing. The Dohkar’s voice cut through the silence again, low and final. “The wish is heard. The offering stands.”

I realize that old tarot deck is still in my hand. Reassuringly solid. Present. One of the heavy gold cards slips out — “Petition Fate” is inscribed in it.

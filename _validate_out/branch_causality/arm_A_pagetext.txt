The thorn-roads were already drawing shut when the first scream tore through the clearing. I shoved past two wilders clutching their children, the crimson spiralgrass underfoot slick with dew that would not dry. The living wild pressed inward, roots cracking like bones, and every path back to the veil-canopy was closing. Seren knelt at the center, aqua skin luminous with fear, her pale coral hair plastered to her cheeks. She had abandoned the rehearsed wish. The Dohkar’s hands rose to halt her, too late. Her voice rang out anyway. “Fate, hear my offering. I offer the memory of the path I chose. So that she finds the one she lost.”The crowd’s murmurs died. An old woman’s hands flew up. “Never hand Fate a blank page,” she breathed. The omen came as a single flame from the ritual brazier bending toward Seren, then guttering cold. The brazier’s last coal pulsed once, as though the air had registered the change first. The words had been too loose, the beneficiary not the wisher, the wound left unnamed. Fate answered exactly. Seren’s eyes stayed the same color, but the warmth drained from her hands in the same breath the wish took hold. Her fingers went pale and stiff against her knees. She reached for the Dohkar and could not feel the touch. The assembly understood at once what the open price had bought. I tore the gossamer band from my mouth. Protocol shattered. The crowd turned. Their eyes found me instead of her because the wish had been spoken for my sake, and I had moved to stop it. The inference was already forming: the Sacrificiant had interfered. Julian’s name surfaced in my mind like a hand I had not asked for. I remembered how he had stayed late in the shop, rolling his storm-gray sleeves twice precisely because he always did, sorting the racks without being asked, and how my skin had flushed before I could stop it. He would have known the wording was wrong before Seren finished speaking. He would have said it anyway. The thorn-roads sealed another Scar behind us. People ran. The Dohkar’s gaze found mine across the clearing, and the question I could not voice pressed against my teeth. I didn't remember bringing it.My hand tightened around my great-grandmother's old tarot deck. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.

The Dohkar’s hand, long-fingered and never still, rose and pointed. The gesture cut across the clearing like a blade.“You,” she said, voice low and precise. “You moved. You broke the seal. The wish named loss, and you answered it.”The words landed before I could swallow them back. The assembly stilled. Weave-Script across bare shoulders and collarbones flickered into staccato flickers, conflict and concealment written in light no one could hide. Seren’s aqua skin paled, her pale coral hair clinging to her cheeks as she knelt in the crimson spiralgrass. She had meant kindness. She had offered her own memory so I might recover something I had buried, and the words had twisted into a different truth.The young man—Seren’s cousin, the one the wish had placed—stood with his cuff still brushing the veil-canopy. Recognition softened his face into something I could not meet. He was the brother of the friend I had failed, the one I had buried in memory years ago. The wish had delivered him exactly where the loose wording required. Nothing visible had happened. No light, no sign. Only the rearrangement of what was already present, and the Dohkar naming the one who had stepped forward.My fingers tightened around the tarot deck I did not remember carrying. A corner of the top card dug into the pad of my thumb. I had torn the gossamer band from my mouth. The cloth hung loose against my throat, the ceremonial sash heavy across my shoulders. Protocol lay shattered at my feet. I had spoken to stop the misreading, to keep Seren from carrying the full weight of what her offering had become. The assembly heard something else.Julian had reached the tree line and stopped there, half-hidden by the leaves. His slate-blue skin and long black hair made a still point against the many-hued crowd. The scar over his left wrist caught the light as his hand turned the ring on his finger, thumb and forefinger working the metal in an unconscious rhythm. He did not look back. He had been only an observer, and the moment the wish named loss, he had chosen distance. The assembly would remember that.The Dohkar’s gaze did not waver.“You intervened. You claimed the interference. The rite is no longer sealed, and the council will require answer for what you have done.”The young man took another step toward me. His confusion had sharpened into something closer to recognition. I could feel the weight of every eye, the way the Weave-Script across my own skin flared with the shame I could not voice. The wish had bent around Seren’s offering and landed on me. The Dohkar had named the cause.The deck warmed against my palm. The deck edge rasped once against my cuff.My heel bounced once against the spiralgrass.I realize that old tarot deck is still in my hand. Reassuringly solid. Present. One of the heavy gold cards slips out — “Petition Fate” is inscribed in it. If only I could take the words back and keep Seren safe from the cost. No. I would ask Fate to show me whether Julian’s distance was protection or abandonment, and whether I can still earn the title I just lost.

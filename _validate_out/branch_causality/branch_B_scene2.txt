Julian's stride lengthened until the undergrowth swallowed the sound of his steps. I had torn the seal. The council would demand explanation.Protocol shattered with it. The deck was still in my hand. I had not remembered bringing it. The young man’s cuff brushed the veil-canopy again, rustling the leaves, and the motion pulled my attention like a hook. His eyes met mine—familiar, older, the same eyes that had once looked at me across a different clearing while I failed to reach his sister in time. He opened his mouth as though the wish had placed the words there too.“Lirael,” he said, and the name landed like a stone dropped in still water. “You came back for me.”The assembly drew breath again, sharper this time. The Dohkar’s hands resumed their weaving, but the rhythm was broken. I felt the weight of every gaze shift from Seren to me, from the girl who had offered the wrong memory to the woman who had torn the seal. Julian's heel pressed a spiralgrass stem flat against the stone. His slate-blue skin caught the light between the leaves, and the small silver scar at the corner of his left eye caught it too, a glint that made the gold flecks in his hazel eyes seem to move even at this distance. He turned, not fully, only enough to watch. The gesture was small, deliberate. He was testing whether I would claim what had just happened or let the assumption stand.The young man took another step. His cuff snagged a low branch, scattering dry needles across the sigil at our feet. The edge of one heavy gold card brushed the ancient sigil carved into the stone beneath the spiralgrass. Light bloomed—soft, steady, unmistakable. It was not the wish answering. It was something older, something the rite had never meant to wake. The sigil glowed beneath my fingers, and the young man’s face changed. Recognition turned to something like hope, and the hope cut deeper than the accusation I had expected.Julian’s Weave-Script flared once more across his throat, a single harmonic loop that died as quickly as it rose. He moved back into the clearing, not toward me, not yet, but the distance between us narrowed by the length of one breath. The Dohkar’s voice came low and tight.“The rite is compromised,” she said. “The Sacrificiant has broken the seal.”A drop of sweat traced the inside of his wrist and fell. I could feel the warmth of it, the question in it, the way the wish had rewritten the moment into something I had never asked for. Julian watched me with that quiet confidence he carried like armor, the same look that had always made me want to answer before I had decided what the answer should be. The assembly waited. The sigil still glowed. The young man waited. Julian waited. The choice sat in my mouth like something I had already swallowed.I realize that old tarot deck is still in my hand. Reassuringly solid. Present. One of the heavy gold cards slips out — “Petition Fate” is inscribed in it. If only it were that easy. I would wish for the young man to forget he ever saw me again. No. I would ask whether Julian stayed because he wanted the truth or because he wanted to watch me fail.

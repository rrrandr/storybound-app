The Dohkar’s voice cut across the clearing before I could steady my breath. “Seren, name your offering.”

Seren knelt on the crimson spiralgrass, her aqua skin luminous under the gossamer tunic. Her pale coral hair caught the dawn light filtering through the veil-canopy. Every First Favored present watched in silence, Weave-Script shifting on their skin with quiet anticipation. I stood sealed beside her, the gossamer band across my mouth muffling any word I might need. This was my first time guiding a youth through the rite. My hands stayed at my sides, though every muscle wanted to reach for the alignment I already felt slipping. Seren spoke the memory she had rehearsed with me for months. “I offer the morning my mother placed the veilwood leaf in my hand and told me the path was mine.”

Her voice carried clean across the assembled family and guests. The Dohkar nodded. I relaxed. The alignment held. Then she looked at me. The glance lasted only a heartbeat, but I knew what it meant before she opened her mouth again. She had recited the rule back to me a hundred times. Wishing outward was allowed. Using another person’s wound as the stage for her own virtue was not. She blurred the words anyway, trying to spare me the shame of being named aloud. “I wish that Lirael finds the one she lost.”

The assembly drew one sharp breath. The Dohkar’s hand rose, too late. The Weave-Script on Seren’s throat stuttered. An old woman at the edge of the gathering pressed her palms together as if warding off a blow. “Never hand Fate a blank page,” she whispered. The air warmed for a single moment, then cooled. The wish had been spoken. Fate answered. Seren’s eyes widened. She reached for the next word and found none. The memory she had offered—the morning with the veilwood leaf—slipped from her grasp. She still knew the shape of the morning, the light on the leaf, the sound of her mother’s voice. She no longer knew why any of it had mattered. The reason that had made the memory hers was gone, taken cleanly. I tore the gossamer band from my mouth. The cloth fluttered to the spiralgrass. Protocol shattered in front of every witness. The Dohkar turned toward me, and the crowd’s eyes followed. They saw only that I had moved the instant the wish touched my name. Julian’s absence pressed against the moment like a held breath. I remembered the way his fingers, stained with ink, brushed against my arm, sending warmth through my stomach as he challenged me to be more than I thought I could be. He would have seen the misalignment the second Seren looked at me. He would have known what the blurred wording would cost before anyone else. The Dohkar’s gaze stayed on me. The rite was no longer Seren’s alone. My qualification hung in the silence that followed the price. I could name what Seren had done. I could let the room draw its own conclusion from the way I had torn the seal. Either path left me exposed. I didn't remember bringing it.

My hand tightened around my great-grandmother's old tarot deck. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.

The Dohkar’s heel ground once into the spiralgrass, the torn gossamer band still clinging to my wrist like a discarded oath. The assembly waited, their Weave-Script flickering in uneven pulses across skin the color of slate and pearl and ember. Seren remained on her knees in the crimson spiralgrass, her coral hair falling forward to hide her face, though the loss had already carved itself into the set of her shoulders. She no longer understood why the memory she had offered had ever been worth keeping.

I opened my mouth to speak. The words were there—Seren had tried to shield me, had spoken my name into the wish because she could not bear the thought of my failure being witnessed by so many. The truth sat ready, sharp enough to cut through the silence. Yet something in the Dohkar’s stillness warned me that naming it would cost more than the rite already had.

My hand rose without my consent, the old deck still clutched in my palm from the moment I had torn the seal. The tremor began at the base of my thumb and traveled outward, a small, betraying shake that drew every eye. The protective sigil I had never meant to call flared beneath my skin, a lattice of silver light blooming while a faint rasp of callus dragged across the top card. It brightened in the dawn air, visible to every First Favored present, a clear admission that I had reached for power the moment the wish touched my name.

The Dohkar’s breath left him in a short hiss. An older woman near the edge of the clearing pressed her hands together again, the gesture tighter this time. No one spoke. The sigil pulsed once more, then steadied, its glow refusing to fade.

Julian would have known what the light meant before anyone else moved. I remembered the way his lashes, long and dark enough to soften the hard line of his face, had lowered the single time he had looked at me across a threshold and chosen not to step through. He would have read the sigil the way he read every misalignment—quietly, without needing the words spoken aloud. The Dohkar took one step toward me, the assembly's focus sharpening with his movement. I could still name what Seren had done. I could still let them believe the light was proof of my interference and nothing more. The choice hung between us like a blade that had not yet fallen.

“Are you ready to face the truth?” he asked, his voice low, but every ear strained to catch it.

I realized the old tarot deck was still in my hand, its weight grounding me. One of the heavy gold cards slipped out—“Petition Fate” glimmered in the dawn light, a challenge and a warning. The tension thickened, and I could feel the assembly's expectation pressing in around us.

I realize that old tarot deck is still in my hand. Reassuringly solid. Present. One of the heavy gold cards slips out — “Petition Fate” is inscribed in it. If Fate could reach where words cannot, I would ask it to show them the truth without forcing me to speak it. No. I would ask it to let me keep the right to choose whether they ever learn what I tried to protect.

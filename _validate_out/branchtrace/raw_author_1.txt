[TITLE: "Fate's First Move"]
[SYNOPSIS: In the shadowed halls of Vaelryn's High Court, a young Sacrificiant must guard her ward through the perilous First Sacrifice rite. When the wish twists under public scrutiny, Lirael faces the weight of her hidden lineage and the judgment that could end her rise.]

The Dohkar’s voice cut across the clearing before I could steady my breath. “Seren, name your offering.”

Seren knelt on the crimson spiralgrass, her aqua skin luminous under the gossamer tunic. Her pale coral hair caught the dawn light filtering through the veil-canopy. Every First Favored present watched in silence, Weave-Script shifting on their skin with quiet anticipation. I stood sealed beside her, the gossamer band across my mouth muffling any word I might need. This was my first time guiding a youth through the rite. My hands stayed at my sides, though every muscle wanted to reach for the alignment I already felt slipping.

Seren spoke the memory she had rehearsed with me for months. “I offer the morning my mother placed the veilwood leaf in my hand and told me the path was mine.” Her voice carried clean across the assembled family and guests. The Dohkar nodded. I relaxed. The alignment held.

Then she looked at me.

The glance lasted only a heartbeat, but I knew what it meant before she opened her mouth again. She had recited the rule back to me a hundred times. Wishing outward was allowed. Using another person’s wound as the stage for her own virtue was not. She blurred the words anyway, trying to spare me the shame of being named aloud.

“I wish that Lirael finds the one she lost.”

The assembly drew one sharp breath. The Dohkar’s hand rose, too late. The Weave-Script on Seren’s throat stuttered. An old woman at the edge of the gathering pressed her palms together as if warding off a blow. “Never hand Fate a blank page,” she whispered.

The air warmed for a single moment, then cooled. The wish had been spoken. Fate answered.

Seren’s eyes widened. She reached for the next word and found none. The memory she had offered—the morning with the veilwood leaf—slipped from her grasp. She still knew the shape of the morning, the light on the leaf, the sound of her mother’s voice. She no longer knew why any of it had mattered. The reason that had made the memory hers was gone, taken cleanly.

I tore the gossamer band from my mouth. The cloth fluttered to the spiralgrass. Protocol shattered in front of every witness. The Dohkar turned toward me, and the crowd’s eyes followed. They saw only that I had moved the instant the wish touched my name.

Julian’s absence pressed against the moment like a held breath. I remembered the way his fingers, stained with ink, brushed against my arm, sending warmth through my stomach as he challenged me to be more than I thought I could be. He would have seen the misalignment the second Seren looked at me. He would have known what the blurred wording would cost before anyone else.

The Dohkar’s gaze stayed on me. The rite was no longer Seren’s alone. My qualification hung in the silence that followed the price.

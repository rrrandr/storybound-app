The rite had ended an hour ago and no one had left. A woman in grey strode straight to the offering stone, lifted the ninth bowl, and held it out toward the assembly. "Whose is this?" she called, her voice sharp enough to stop every conversation. My breath caught as heads turned, two of them fixing on me while the stone's lingering heat still burned against my palm. I crossed the clearing in three steps, took the bowl from her hands, and set it back on the lip where it belonged. Julian stood near the gate; his name alone pulled my gaze for one heartbeat before I forced it away. The silver lines on his arms flared once beneath his sleeve as he watched. The woman in grey did not leave. She planted herself on the north path and waited. Behind me the youth kept repeating the same three words to her mother, louder now, until the sound scraped along my spine. I turned to face the two who still stared and raised my voice. "If you have something to say, say it here."Julian moved one step into the clearing.My hand tightened around my great-grandmother's old tarot deck. I didn't remember bringing it. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.

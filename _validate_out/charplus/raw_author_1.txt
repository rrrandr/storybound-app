[TITLE: "The Price of a Name"]
[SYNOPSIS: In the Veilwood clearing, Lirael’s first supervision of a First Sacrifice shatters when the youth redirects her offering toward Lirael’s buried past. The misalignment draws every eye, including Julian’s silent watch at the edge, and the rite’s public failure now threatens both her qualification and the fragile distance she keeps from the one man who already sees too much.]

The youth’s voice rose clear through the mated-pair veil. “I give the discipline to finish my studies and train as a Sacrificiant.”

I felt the alignment settle, true and clean, the way it should on a First Sacrifice. Then her eyes found mine.

“I give it instead so she finds the father who left her.”

The words landed wrong. The sacrifice she had prepared belonged to one desire; the wish now belonged to another. Everyone present felt the sour note at once. Trained or not, the misalignment rang through the clearing like a struck flaw in glass.

Julian stood perfectly still at the outer ring, slate-blue skin catching the dawn light that filtered through the canopy. His long black hair curtained the side of his face as he lowered his head a fraction, the small silver scar at his left wrist the only movement I could see. I remembered the way those fingers once moved across wet clay, precise and unhurried, as if the shape already existed and he was only agreeing to free it. The memory pulled heat up my throat before I could stop it.

The presiding Dohkar’s hands lifted, too late. The bargain had already been offered and the price already claimed by the wrong shape.

I tore the gossamer band from my mouth. The cloth whispered against my skin as it fell. Protocol broke with it. Every First Favored turned.

Julian’s gaze lifted. He did not speak. The Weave-Script on his forearms moved in slow harmonic loops, a private language I had no right to read. I wanted to know what he saw when he looked at me now—whether the youth’s wish had confirmed the rumor or simply given him another test I had not chosen to take.

The assembly’s silence pressed closer. Someone would speak soon. Someone would ask why the Sacrificiant had moved at all.

Do I explain the wish was never mine to claim, or let them believe I reached for my own gain?

My hand tightened around my great-grandmother’s old tarot deck. I didn't remember bringing it. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.

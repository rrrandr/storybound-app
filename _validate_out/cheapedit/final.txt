The youth knelt on the crimson spiralgrass, her aqua skin catching the filtered dawn light that sifted through the mated-pair veil-canopy. The girl jerked upright without warning and flung the second half of her vow across the clearing, naming Lirael and the father who had abandoned her, so the words themselves twisted the offering before the Dohkar could finish his nod. Guests surged forward as one, their Weave-Script flaring crimson with the mismatch, and the presiding Dohkar’s slate-blue skin tightened into rigid lines while he raised his hand to halt the rite. My pulse slammed against the gossamer band across my mouth. I crossed the spiralgrass in three strides, shoved between the girl and the Dohkar, and tore the ceremonial cloth free with both hands so my voice could cut through the rising din. “The offering stops here,” I said, planting myself in front of her kneeling form. “No debt is claimed today.”The assembly recoiled, murmurs sharpening into accusations that the supervisor had broken protocol for personal gain. The girl’s fingers dug deeper into the grass stems, eyes wide as she realized the bargain had already shifted. I held my ground, refusing to step aside even when the Dohkar’s voice dropped low and ordered the rite ended. My shoulders burned under the ceremonial sash, every eye now fixed on the exposed lower half of my face and the defiance written there. Julian’s name rippled through the crowd from somewhere near the veil-canopy’s edge, carried on the same breath that warned the Chayr would hear of this before nightfall. The comm-bead at my ear crackled once, his measured tone threading through the noise without waiting for permission. “I warned you she’d try to claim the debt,” he said, the sound of his voice pulling every prior caution between us into the open air. I did not turn toward it. Instead I extended one hand to the girl, palm up, and told her to rise so the twisted words could not settle further. The Dohkar stepped closer, script still stuttering across his skin, but I kept my body between him and the youth, forcing the choice into the moment. “She finishes nothing under this rite,” I repeated, louder, so the guests could carry the counter-claim instead. The girl’s weight shifted against my grip as she stood, the spiralgrass releasing her knees with a soft tear. Pressure thickened in my chest, the filtered dawn light suddenly sharp against my bare mouth while the assembly’s disapproval pressed from every side. I named the failure aloud before anyone else could shape it into a formal charge, locking the Dohkar’s gaze and daring him to contradict the public record we were now making together.My hand tightened around my great-grandmother's old tarot deck.

[TITLE: "The Wish That Named Me"]
[SYNOPSIS: "In the Veilwood clearing, Lirael’s first supervision of a First Sacrifice unravels when her ward turns the rite toward her. One imprecise wish and the cost lands before the assembly, leaving Lirael’s standing and her own hidden wound exposed."]

The Dohkar’s voice carried across the clearing, steady as the mated-pair veilwood above us. “Speak the offering first. Then the desire.”

Seren knelt on the crimson spiralgrass, her aqua skin luminous with Weave-Script that flowed slow and open. She had rehearsed this for months. I knew every word she would say. My mouth-band muffled my breath but not my relief; the alignment felt clean.

She lifted her face and looked straight at me.

“I offer the memory of the morning I chose this path,” she said. “The one where you told me the truth costs more than silence ever will.”

The assembly stilled. That was not the line we had practiced.

“I wish that Lirael finds the one she lost.”

The words left her mouth and the clearing changed. A senior Dohkar at the edge turned sharply, his silver Weave-Script stuttering once at the throat. An older woman’s hands rose as if to ward the air. Someone whispered the old saying so low it barely reached me: “Never hand Fate a blank page.”

Seren’s coral hair trembled. She had meant kindness. She had meant to show the room she cared for her guide. The wording had left the one unnamed and the loss undefined, and Fate does not guess.

A man near the back—someone’s cousin, here only to witness—rose without looking at anyone. He walked straight out of the clearing. The spiralgrass did not rustle under his feet the way it should have. He did not come back.

Seren’s Weave-Script flared once, bright, then the colour drained from the left side of her face like ink running off wet paper. Her left eye stayed the same vivid teal. The right went pale, almost white, the pupil still four-pointed but emptied of hue. She made a small sound, the first unpractised noise I had ever heard from her.

Every trained First Favored present felt the misalignment the same instant I did. The Dohkar’s gaze found mine across the grass. Julian stood perfectly still at the edge, long black hair curtaining half his slate-blue face, the scar on his wrist visible where his hand had tightened. He had come only to observe. Now he watched me like he already knew what the assembly would decide.

I could feel the band across my mouth, the weight of the ceremonial sash, the eyes of two dozen people who had just seen their guide’s ward spend her First Sacrifice on her. If I tore the band away now, the inference would be immediate and final: I had interfered for my own sake. My qualification would be denied before the sun cleared the veilwood.

Seren’s pale eye stared past me at nothing. The memory she had offered was gone from her face as cleanly as if it had never lived there.

My hand tightened around my great-grandmother's old tarot deck. I didn't remember bringing it. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.

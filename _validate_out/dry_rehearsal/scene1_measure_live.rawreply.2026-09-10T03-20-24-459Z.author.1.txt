[TITLE: "Kiln Heat"]
[SYNOPSIS: Testa fights to keep a kiln firing stable in her small-town studio while Rook’s sudden clause threatens the commission that could secure her future. One misstep and the work cracks before it sets. The question is how much she will risk to finish what she started.]

[CHARACTERS: Testa:F]

The needle on the kiln gauge had begun its slow slide left ten minutes ago, and the glaze on the tall vase inside was still wet enough to run. I pressed both palms flat against the clay workbench to stop the shake in my wrists. The bench was cool under my hands, the surface scored from years of scraping and smoothing.  

I wiped clay across the apron in one hard stroke and hated how the streak made me look unfinished. The dropping needle bothered me more than it should. Heat pressed against my face from the open vent, but the temperature inside the chamber was already falling.  

Rook’s clause had landed yesterday in an email that said nothing about the work itself, only about the timeline. The way he set the document down without lifting his eyes from the page, a slow ache starts low in my belly every time I remember it. I wanted the temperature to hold long enough for the glaze to set.  

The studio smelled of wet clay and the sharp edge of cooling metal. The vent fan ticked once, then again, as if the motor were deciding whether to keep turning. I kept my hands on the bench because moving them would mean checking the gauge again, and I already knew what it would show.

The ache in my belly sharpened when I thought of his name on the email. He had not asked what the firing schedule was. He had only written the new date.  

I straightened, wiped my palms on the sides of the apron, and felt the clay smear wider. The needle had dropped another two degrees. If I opened the door now to adjust the vent, the piece would cool too fast and the glaze would crawl. If I left it sealed, the temperature would keep falling and the vase would crack from the inside out.  

Open the door to adjust or seal it now and lose the piece?  

My hand tightened around my great-grandmother's old tarot deck. I didn't remember bringing it. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.

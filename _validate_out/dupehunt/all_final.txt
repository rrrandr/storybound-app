Seren’s eyes found mine across the crimson spiralgrass. She stopped mid-invocation, the rehearsed words dying on her tongue. “I offer the memory of my first flight through the canopy,” she said, her voice steady. “I wish that Lirael finds the one she lost.”The Dohkar’s hands froze above the mated trunks. Two dozen First Favored stood barefoot in gossamer, Weave-Script flaring across aqua skin and pearl skin alike. The hush was immediate and sharp. An old woman near the veil-curtains raised both palms. “Never hand Fate a blank page,” she breathed. The air warmed for a single breath, then cooled again. No one moved. Seren’s Weave-Script stuttered at her throat, the pale coral hair falling forward as she waited for the answer she had invited. It came without fanfare. A low sound rose from the assembly—someone whispering a name that had never been spoken here. The Dohkar’s silver-threaded hands dropped. The wish had landed on a fact already present in the clearing: my father’s name, carried in a guest’s memory and now suddenly spoken aloud because the wording had left the target unnamed. Seren gasped once, a small sound, and her eyes lost the faint rose tint that had always marked her earliest recollections. The colour drained while we watched, leaving them pale and strange. She reached for the memory and found only the shape of reaching. The Dohkar turned toward me, mouth tight. Every gaze followed. The band across my own mouth felt suddenly heavier, the protocol that kept me silent now the only thing between me and open accusation. Julian stood at the edge of the gathering, perfectly still, long black hair curtaining his slate-blue face. He had come only to observe. Now and the name that had just surfaced because of me. The Dohkar’s voice cut through the quiet. “The rite is complete. The price is paid.”Seren remained kneeling, eyes pale, hands pressed to the spiralgrass as if the ground itself might return what she had lost. The assembly waited for me to move or to stay sealed. Will she confront the truth or hide behind her shame? I didn't remember bringing it.My hand tightened around my great-grandmother's old tarot deck. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.
The Dohkar’s fingers drummed once against the carved armrest. “Speak, if you can speak at all,” he said, voice low enough that the veil-curtains barely stirred. “You were sealed to keep the rite clean. Tell me why the name that surfaced belongs to your blood.”I opened my mouth and the band across it caught the words, turning them to muffled sound. The guest whose memory had carried the name—an older woman with pearl skin and hair the color of wet slate—stepped half a pace forward. Her Weave-Script flickered once, a thin line of silver across her collarbone.“The child spoke your father’s name,” she said. “I knew it from the old records. I did not know it would be called here.”Seren stayed on her knees. Her eyes, now pale as washed stone, tracked the spiralgrass between her hands. The color that had marked her earliest memories was gone. She had offered it without knowing what the wording would take.Julian had not moved from the edge of the clearing. He measured my words the same way he had measured the broken wheel spokes that night, counting each one before deciding whether it would hold weight. The kindness had always carried that small distance, as if he tested whether the thanks would change him.The Dohkar’s mouth tightened. “You knew the law. No one may lend their sacrifice to another’s First Rite.”“I did not lend it,” I said. “I tried to keep her from offering anything at all.”The older woman’s heel shifted once against the packed earth. “Tried how?”I took one step. My hand shook so the deck I had not remembered holding slipped against my palm. The crimson spiralgrass caught the light between my bare feet and the mated trunks. No. I would ask it to show me whether claiming the interference now would cost me the only person who still knows the whole truth of it.
The threshold of the clearing, where the mated trunks twisted into one another, bark sealed where they met, bore my weight as I searched the gathered faces for Julian. His tall frame stood out even among the First Favored, deep copper hair catching the filtered light through the veil-curtains, and I found him at the edge, his emerald eyes with their four-pointed diamond pupils fixed on me, his attention already settling on the weight of what I carried.The Dohkar’s voice still hung in the air, demanding I claim the name or leave it to Seren.

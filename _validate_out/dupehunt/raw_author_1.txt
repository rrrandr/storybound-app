[TITLE: "The First Fracture"]
[SYNOPSIS: "In the Veilwood clearing Lirael supervises her ward’s First Sacrifice, but when Seren redirects the offering toward an old wound, the cost of an imprecise wish begins to surface in ways no one can control."]

Seren’s eyes found mine across the crimson spiralgrass. She stopped mid-invocation, the rehearsed words dying on her tongue.

“I offer the memory of my first flight through the canopy,” she said, voice steady. “I wish that Lirael finds the one she lost.”

The Dohkar’s hands froze above the mated trunks. Two dozen First Favored stood barefoot in gossamer, Weave-Script flaring across aqua skin and pearl skin alike. The hush was immediate and sharp.

An old woman near the veil-curtains raised both palms. “Never hand Fate a blank page,” she breathed.

The air warmed for a single breath, then cooled again. No one moved. Seren’s Weave-Script stuttered at her throat, the pale coral hair falling forward as she waited for the answer she had invited.

It came without fanfare. A low sound rose from the assembly—someone whispering a name that had never been spoken here. The Dohkar’s silver-threaded hands dropped. The wish had landed on a fact already present in the clearing: my father’s name, carried in a guest’s memory and now suddenly spoken aloud because the wording had left the target unnamed.

Seren gasped once, a small sound, and her eyes lost the faint rose tint that had always marked her earliest recollections. The colour drained while we watched, leaving them pale and strange. She reached for the memory and found only the shape of reaching.

The Dohkar turned toward me, mouth tight. Every gaze followed. The band across my own mouth felt suddenly heavier, the protocol that kept me silent now the only thing between me and open accusation.

Julian stood at the edge of the gathering, perfectly still, long black hair curtaining his slate-blue face. He had come only to observe. Now the room measured the distance between his stillness and the name that had just surfaced because of me.

The Dohkar’s voice cut through the quiet. “The rite is complete. The price is paid.”

Seren remained kneeling, eyes pale, hands pressed to the spiralgrass as if the ground itself might return what she had lost. The assembly waited for me to move or to stay sealed.

Will she confront the truth or hide behind her shame?

My hand tightened around my great-grandmother's old tarot deck. I didn't remember bringing it. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.

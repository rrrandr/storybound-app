The Dohkar’s fingers drummed once against the carved armrest. His silver-threaded hand stilled, and he lifted his gaze to the space above my head—where Julian stood perfectly still, long black hair curtaining his slate-blue face like a curtain drawn over glass. I stepped forward, the deck heavy in my palm. The crimson spiralgrass clutched at my ankles, resisting, as if it knew what I intended to do. "I offered that memory," I said, my voice rough from the seal. The words tasted like ash. "I named the price. The assembly will remember who paid it."

The Dohkar’s eyes narrowed. "You would claim a memory that does not belong to you?" His left hand rested on the hilt of his sword, the motion casual and deliberate. The assembly held its breath. Seren’s breath hitched, her pale eyes fixed on the spiralgrass between her knees. A murmur threaded through the gathered First Favored, Weave-Script flickering like dying embers. Someone muttered something about the Hungry Eye. Julian’s head tilted slightly, his eyes dropping to my mouth for half a second longer than was polite. He did not speak. He only measured.

The older woman with pearl skin and slate hair stepped forward. Her Weave-Script stuttered silver across her collarbone. "The child offered it in good faith." She glanced at Seren, then back at me. "But the wording left the target unnamed. The name that surfaced was not hers to give." Her gaze flicked to the Dohkar, then to Julian. The room rearranged itself around his stillness. I felt the weight of a hundred gazes settle on me like a hand pressing down on my shoulders. My palm tingled where the scar from my first sacrifice pressed against my skin. I flexed my fingers once, counting the ridges I knew by touch alone.

The Dohkar’s thumb brushed the sword hilt. "The law is clear. No one may lend their sacrifice to another’s rite." His voice dropped, low enough that the veil-curtains barely stirred. "The price belongs to the rite." A vein of silver light pulsed at his throat. The spiralgrass darkened where it touched my bare feet, the crimson deepening to wine.

I took another step. The deck shifted in my grip. "I did not lend it." My heel pressed into the packed earth, grounding me. "I tried to stop her from offering anything at all." The words landed wrong. The assembly flinched. Seren lifted her face, her mouth opening as if to speak, then closing again. Her Weave-Script had gone dark at her collarbone, the pale coral strands of her hair falling forward like a curtain over her shame.

Julian’s head tilted again, just once. His eyes dropped to my mouth. A breath I hadn’t meant to hold caught in my throat. The small silver scar at the corner of his left eye caught the filtered light, and for a heartbeat, the room shrank to just the two of us. Then his gaze lifted, and the distance between us felt like a blade.

The Dohkar exhaled through his nose. "Then claim it." His hand rested on his sword hilt. "Or let it stand as the child’s alone. The assembly will remember whichever you choose." The threshold yawned behind him, a gap in the mated trunks where the spiralgrass grew thickest. The veil-curtains hung motionless. The air smelled of crushed crimson blades and old wood. A memory crystal glinted in the sunlight at the edge of the clearing—Seren’s discarded offering, forgotten in the chaos. I could take it. I could walk away.

I stepped toward the threshold. The deck felt heavier with each stride. The Dohkar’s fingers drummed once more against the carved armrest. "Do it," he said. "Before I decide for you."

The assembly waited. Seren’s breath hitched again. Julian’s attention sharpened on me, his focus tell burning like a brand. I reached the threshold. The spiralgrass coiled around my ankles, pulling me forward. The deck in my hand felt like a casket. I opened my mouth to speak, and the assembly leaned in, waiting for the words that would decide everything.

I realize the old tarot deck is still in my hand. Reassuringly solid. Present. One of the heavy gold cards slips out — “Petition Fate” is inscribed on it.

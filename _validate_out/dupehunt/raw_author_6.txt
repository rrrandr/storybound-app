The threshold stone bit cold against my soles as the Dohkar’s voice cut across it. “Name the memory you will surrender, Lirael, or step back and let the child carry the debt alone. The law will not bend for your hesitation.”

I looked for Julian at the edge of the clearing. He had leaned slightly forward, his attention fixed on my mouth half a second longer than courtesy allowed, and the small silver scar at the corner of his left eye caught the filtered light through the veil-curtains. The Dohkar’s hand rested on the hilt of his sword, the threat silent but unmistakable.

My palm tingled where the old sacrifice scar marked the skin. The faded memory crystal felt heavier than its size justified, the name my father’s pressed inside it like a bruise that had never healed. Seren remained kneeling, her pale eyes tracking the crimson spiralgrass. The assembly waited.

I stepped forward onto the threshold, clutching the crystal tightly. The Dohkar’s Weave-Script flickered once at his throat, a warning. Julian’s head tilted, absorbing every word I had not yet spoken. The Dohkar’s fingers tightened on the hilt. “The choice is yours. Claim it, or the rite closes without you.”

The deck sat where I had left it, reassuringly solid in my other hand. The crystal warmed against my fingers as if it already knew what I might offer. A low murmur moved through the First Favored around us, the sound of judgment settling like dust.

I wished, for one idle breath, that the Dohkar would simply look away and let the moment pass unnoticed. The deck shifted once beneath my thumb, heavier than before, as if it had heard.

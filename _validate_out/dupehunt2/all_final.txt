The Dohkar’s voice carried across the crimson spiralgrass. “Begin the invocation, child.”Seren knelt in the center of the mated-pair veil-canopy, her aqua skin luminous under the gossamer tunic. Her pale coral hair caught the light that filtered through the white leaves. I stood at the edge of the circle, the ceremonial sash heavy across my shoulder, the gossamer band tight across my mouth. My heel pressed once into the soft earth. The alignment-sense told me everything was steady. Seren spoke the words we had practiced for months. Her voice was clear, the sacrifice named before the desire. She offered a memory of her first lesson under the veilwood. The assembly listened. I felt the rite hold its shape. Then she looked at me. A low hum rose from the veil-canopy and died. The prepared words stopped. Her shoulders lifted and held. “I offer the same memory,” she said, “so that she finds the one she lost.”The spiralgrass darkened at the edges. Fabric whispered as someone shifted weight. The Dohkar’s Weave-Script stuttered at the throat. A man at the back of the gathering—Seren’s older cousin—stepped forward without meaning to. His mouth opened. “She asked that you find the one you lost. You have.”He turned at once and walked toward the trees. The words had already left him. He did not look back. Seren’s hand rose to her temple. Her fingers pressed there as if something familiar had slipped. She blinked once, twice. The memory she had offered was already gone; she no longer knew what she had given. Her Weave-Script dimmed along one cheek and did not return. A rustle moved through the circle and settled on the spiralgrass. I had moved. The band was still across my mouth, but my body had leaned toward her. They saw the motion and drew the only conclusion the rite allowed. I tore the gossamer from my face. The cloth fell to the spiralgrass. The clearing waited. Will she confront the truth or bury it deeper to protect her image? I didn't remember bringing it.My hand tightened around my great-grandmother's old tarot deck. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.
The Dohkar’s gaze pinned me where I stood. My mouth opened before I could weigh the words, the explanation already forming on my tongue. I had leaned forward to speak when the sigil flared beneath my palm.Heat seared through the gossamer. The mark that had been only a faint tracing in the grass locked around my wrist like a living brand. Light crawled up the veins of my hand and held. I could not pull free without tearing the skin.Seren’s older cousin turned back from the trees. His voice carried the same flat certainty as before.“You asked that she find the one she lost. Now the rite has named you instead.”The assembly shifted. Murmurs rose and fell like wind through the veil-canopy. No one moved to help. The Dohkar’s Weave-Script flickered once across his throat and steadied, reading the new alignment without comment.Julian stood at the outer ring, slate-blue skin catching the filtered light, long black hair curtaining one side of his face as he watched. He did not speak at first. His hand turned the silver band on his finger once, the motion quiet and deliberate. When he did speak, the question was simple and sharp.“Will you claim what the rite has bound to you, or will you let them keep the story they have already written?”The sigil pulsed once more against my skin, a steady reminder that the choice was no longer private. The clearing waited for my answer.I realize that old tarot deck is still in my hand. Reassuringly solid. Present. One of the heavy gold cards slips out — “Petition Fate” is inscribed in it. If only the assembly would forget the last few moments and let Seren’s words pass as nothing. No. I would ask to know whether claiming this binding would finally make Julian see me without the need to test first.

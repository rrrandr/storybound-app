[TITLE: "The Price of a Wish"]
[SYNOPSIS: In the Veilwood clearing, Lirael supervises her ward’s First Sacrifice while the assembled First Favored watch. When Seren abandons the rehearsed wish and spends it on her instead, the rite fractures and Fate answers with brutal precision, leaving Lirael’s hidden wound exposed before the council can judge her.]

[CHARACTERS: Lirael:F, Seren:F]

The Dohkar’s hand lifted, palm open toward the mated veilwood trees.  
“Begin.”

Seren knelt on the crimson spiralgrass, her aqua skin luminous under the gossamer tunic. Her pale coral hair fell forward as she closed her eyes and pressed her hands together. The clearing held its breath. Two dozen First Favored stood barefoot around the braided grass, Weave-Script shifting softly across their skin in quiet expectation. I felt the weight of every gaze on my back, the ceremonial band across my mouth muffling my own breathing.

Seren spoke the invocation cleanly, the words we had practiced for months. I relaxed. Then she opened her eyes and looked straight at me.

“I offer the memory of the day I chose this path,” she said, voice steady. “So that Lirael may find the one she lost.”

The words landed like a stone in still water. The Dohkar’s hand froze mid-gesture. A ripple of misalignment moved through the clearing; I sensed it before anyone else because I knew exactly where the wording had come from. Seren had turned her First Sacrifice into a public kindness, and she had described it too loosely for Fate to honour cleanly.

The Weave-Script on her arms stuttered. An old woman near the edge of the circle raised both hands as if to ward something off. Someone whispered, low and sharp, “Never hand Fate a blank page.”

The air warmed for half a heartbeat. Then the twist arrived.

A guest at the far side of the clearing—a woman I had only seen once before, years ago—stepped forward unprompted. Her voice carried clearly. “The one you lost was your mother’s name. You buried it the day you left the Ashen Verge.”

The words were true. They were also not what Seren had meant to give me. The assembly understood the precision instantly: the wish had been granted exactly as spoken, and the intent had been denied. Every eye turned to me. The inference was immediate and damning. I had interfered. I had used my ward’s rite for my own secret.

I tore the gossamer band from my mouth. The cloth fluttered to the grass. My voice came out raw. “Seren, stop—”

The price landed before I finished the sentence. Seren’s mouth opened, but no sound emerged. Her Weave-Script dimmed at the throat and stayed dark. She had offered a memory; Fate had taken the ability to name it. She reached for me with one hand, eyes wide, and the clearing saw only that I had moved toward her at the exact moment the wish resolved.

The Dohkar’s gaze found mine across the grass. Judgment waited in that look, and the assembled First Favored with it.

Will I confront the truth or let them believe the worst of me?

My hand tightened around my great-grandmother’s old tarot deck. I didn’t remember bringing it. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.

I place the silver ring against my palm, the familiar weight of it grounding me as I step through the low arch of the threshold. The crimson spiralgrass presses cool and yielding beneath my bare feet, but the air beyond it is thick with the heat of judgment. The Dohkar hasn’t lowered his hand. The circle of First Favored stands barefoot in their gossamer tunics, silver and gold Weave-Script drifting across skin like smoke caught in a slow current. My gaze tracks to Seren first—her aqua skin luminous, but her coral hair pulled tight over one shoulder, deliberately shielding her from something. Or someone. I follow the line of her gaze and find Julian exactly where he stood before, slate-blue skin catching the amber lantern-light in slow, deliberate loops. His heel presses once into the turf when he tilts his head, the same small silver line I trace with my thumb in moments I refuse to name.

The Dohkar’s eyes, dark as the Hungry Eye, haven’t left mine. “You moved,” he repeats, voice level as a blade against bone. “Why.”

Seren shifts—just enough—a subtle step that blocks my view of Julian entirely. Her posture is all invitation to the circle, all readiness to receive the judgment she believes is coming. The Weave-Script on her arms stays dark at the throat, but her knuckles whiten where she grips the hem of her gown. The air between us thickens, charged with the kind of silence that doesn’t wait for consent.

<<+A memory stirs in me, unwanted and warm:+>> Julian’s hand brushing against mine as he offered that first flower—a pale thing with petals like moonlight on water. I hadn’t expected the weight of his fingers against my skin to linger like that. The touch had been light, almost hesitant, but it settled somewhere behind my ribs and stayed. Now, standing in the clearing, I feel it again: the way his breath caught when our eyes met over the bloom, the way he didn’t look away even when I should have known better.

Seren’s voice cuts through my recollection like a knife honed too fine. “You always were too quick with your excuses, Lirael.” She doesn’t shout. She doesn’t need to. The words land clean, and the circle leans into them, hungry for the direction she’s pulling. “The Dohkar asked a question. Answer it.”

The silver scar above Julian’s right eyebrow catches the light as he rolls his shoulders once, the way a fighter does between rounds. He hasn’t moved from his spot, but the tension in his frame is unmistakable. His fingers linger near my arm as he speaks, close enough to count the callouses along his knuckles, close enough that if I shifted a breath to the left, they’d brush my sleeve. “The fault is yours, not hers,” he says, but his voice isn’t to me. It’s to the circle, to the Dohkar, to the air that’s gone still around us all.

My thumbnail finds the edge of the deck still clutched in my fist. The gold card at the top shifts before I can stop it—Petition Fate, inscribed in looping script I’ve traced a hundred times. The deck is heavier than it should be, as if it leans toward the choice just made. The card glints in the lantern-light, catching my eye like a promise.

The Dohkar exhales through his nose, slow and measured. “You interfered in a First Sacrifice. The price is yours to name.” His gaze flicks to Seren, then back to me. “Speak it.”

Seren’s foot taps the spiralgrass once, twice—counting down to her victory, or to the moment I break. The Weave-Script on my own arms flickers in response to the pressure, silver threads stuttering like a breath held too long. I can feel the circle’s eyes, the weight of every gaze turned inward, waiting to see what kind of fool still thinks she can bargain when the house has already burned.

I realize the deck is still in my hand. As I slide Petition Fate back in, I notice a flash of red in the otherwise gold stack. “Tempt Fate.” Grandma never let me play with that one. A sword raised to a blood-red sky, lightning arcing down from the clouds (or maybe arcing up?) Ow! Static shock reminds me I need to pay attention to the present…

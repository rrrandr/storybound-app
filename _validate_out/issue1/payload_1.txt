=== SCENE DUTY (romance is ONE duty, NOT the default gravity) ===
Every scene has an OBLIGATION — what it is trying to ACCOMPLISH. Romance is one obligation among many, NOT the lens through which all prose is written. Most scenes in a romance are the protagonist's life (work, money, survival, world, danger, politics, social pressure) with the relationship threaded through — NOT yearning set-pieces. The duties:
  • external_pressure — an external force bearing down (deadline, threat, demand, eviction, summons)
  • investigation — finding or uncovering something
  • survival — meeting a material need (money, shelter, safety, food, work)
  • political_alignment — navigating or choosing a side in a social / factional conflict
  • professional_competence — doing the work, demonstrating skill
  • social_navigation — managing a group or social situation (non-romantic)
  • world_revelation — the world or its rules surfacing
  • romantic_progression — the relationship advancing
  • emotional_repair — mending or processing between two characters
  • interwoven — external duty and relationship genuinely entangled
PROSE RULES (HARD — composition discipline translated into INTERPRETATION discipline):
- Do NOT turn neutral dialogue into chemistry, charged glances, or yearning unless the scene's duty is relationship-primary OR attraction is authorized as present.
- In a NON-romance duty, the EXTERNAL problem (the money, the danger, the work, the world, the politics, the task) is the emotional center — keep that pressure on the page; do NOT convert every beat into longing.
- Side characters retain INDEPENDENT motives and interiority — they are not chemistry vectors or mirrors for the protagonist's feelings about the love interest.
- Preserve task / procedure / world pressure AS ITSELF. A scene about rent is about rent; an investigation scene is about the investigation.
- When attraction IS present in a non-romance scene, render it as ABSENCE, a LEAK, a PRESSURE, or an INTERRUPTION — never as the default narrative voice. A glance breaks through; it does not take over the prose.
- Reserve romance-forward prose (yearning, heat, the relationship as the scene's subject) for romantic_progression, emotional_repair, or genuinely interwoven scenes.
The love interest being PRESENT does NOT make a scene romantic. Choose the duty by the scene's primary irreversible change: if it concerns survival / status / knowledge / world / danger / work / politics / money / social navigation, pick the external duty and treat attraction as none/latent/leaking. Use "interwoven" ONLY when both the external plot AND the relationship genuinely change in the same scene — not as a lazy default.
At the VERY END of your output, emit a hidden tag the reader NEVER sees (the displayer strips it): <!--sceneDuty: <one duty above> | attraction: none|latent|leaking|foreground-->. Set attraction=foreground ONLY when the scene is relationship-primary.

    • MEDIUM (rising tension, a meaningful exchange, a decision forming): up to TWO interpretive layers may stack across the scene.
    • HIGH (confrontation, reveal, intimacy, turning point): full orchestration permitted — the instruments may work together.
    • EXTREME (climax, rupture, the scene the whole arc bent toward): a brief LYRICAL DENSITY SPIKE is allowed — heightened, fused prose — for that peak ONLY, then release.
  Even at HIGH/EXTREME: a loaded sentence lands hardest when the sentences around it are plain. NEVER run three interpretive sentences in a row. Contrast is the instrument the conductor actually plays.

ATMOSPHERE & EMOTIONAL WEATHER (the room's mood, felt as much as seen — use SPARINGLY):

CHARACTER IMPRESSION (how to render people — ONE fused move, not three separate passes):

DIALOGUE — USE IT THE WAY STRONG FICTION & ROMANCE DO (applies to EVERY scene; Roman 2026-06-09):
  When two or more characters share the page and something is AT STAKE between them — a confrontation, a negotiation, a confession, a seduction, a refusal, a reveal — DRAMATIZE it as actual spoken dialogue: quoted back-and-forth ("…") that reveals character through HOW each person talks and carries subtext (what is meant under what is said). Do NOT summarize a live exchange into narration — "she explained the side venture" / "he told her the board wanted her gone" is a FAILURE when the telling IS the scene; put the words in their mouths and let the reader HEAR it.
  This is NOT a quota and dialogue is NOT filler. Solitary, interior, or contemplative beats rightly stay in narration; some scenes carry little dialogue and that is fine; never pad with chatter. The rule is only this: when characters speak to each other on the page, render the exchange — do not narrate around a confrontation or a connection the reader should witness. Interiority, action, sensory grounding, and silence carry everything else.

PARAGRAPHING & DIALOGUE FORMATTING (HARD — readability, applies to EVERY scene, not just the opening):
  Write the scene as MULTIPLE short paragraphs separated by a blank line (a literal "\n\n"). NEVER emit a scene as one continuous block of prose — break at every beat, time shift, topic change, or move between action, interior, and dialogue.
  Each distinct speaker's line sits in its OWN paragraph, with its dialogue tag and any immediate beat/reaction. Start a NEW paragraph every time the speaker changes — a reply, an interruption, and a counter are separate paragraphs.
  NEVER merge two speakers into one quoted block. "...funding.Are you?Would you believe me if I said no?Probably not." crammed into a single quote is a FAILURE — those are four separate turns by two people and MUST be separate paragraphs, each attributed.
  Always put a space after sentence punctuation (. ? !): "investigative. I know," never "investigative.I know."

NAME & SETTING VARIETY (HARD): Invent side-character names with genuine variety — different cultures, syllable shapes, eras. ═══ AUTHOR FLOOR (HARD — outranks style, ornament and mystery) ═══
1. INTERIORITY DECODES; IT DOES NOT ENCODE. A reader must never have to reconstruct the external situation from a character's feelings about it. At any important moment they should know where we are, what physically happened, what the POV character wants now, what prevents it, and what she fears will follow. Mystery may conceal the ANSWER; it may not conceal the question, the immediate consequence, or why she cares.
2. ASSIGNED EVENT IS ACTION, NOT SUBJECT MATTER. If the scene is assigned an event, that event HAPPENS on the page — or a NEW concrete event prevents it and leaves the situation materially changed (a hand caught mid-reach counts; deciding not to reach does not). Thinking about it, arguing about it, almost doing it, or merely standing in the assigned location does NOT satisfy the assignment.
3. THE SCENE ENDS SOMEWHERE ELSE. Something happens → it means something → somebody reacts → somebody acts → the situation is different. This is a test to apply, never a shape to make visible in the prose.

═══ CHARACTER+ — ROTATE MECHANISMS, NEVER SURFACES ═══
Character+ is NOT description, NOT a compliment, NOT a memorable quirk, and NOT a sentence stating that someone matters. It is a detail through which the POV reveals that they perceive this person through accumulated experience, bias, history, desire, fear or expectation — so the reader infers a private relationship, an internal conflict, a learned pattern, or a personal lens.
SUBSTITUTION TEST (HARD): replace the named character with any other plausible person on stage. If the inference, the emotional meaning AND the observer’s reaction all survive unchanged, you wrote GROUNDING, not Character+. Grounding is necessary and good — it is simply not this.
It is usually NOT a dramatic revelation. It is a tiny, involuntary choice that exposes a whole person and a relationship.
NINE MECHANISMS. These are NOT templates. Do NOT reuse their syntax, their connective tissue (“as usual”, “he taught me”, “that was the tell”, “he’d have”), or their sentence shape. Invent a NEW mechanism — these demonstrate the PHYSICS, never the choreography.
  A · habitual tell → accumulated familiarity: “Jess, leading with her decolletage, as usual.”
  B · sequence → values: “Marcus asked how my mother was before he asked for the money. He always does it in that order.”
  C · absence or change → history: “Julian didn’t look at the door once. Six years ago he’d have counted the exits before he sat down.”
  D · observer misread, corrected on the page → POV bias: “I thought he was being kind. He was being early — he wanted to be gone before the singing started.”
  E · private behavioural code → intimacy: “Her hands were steady. That was the tell; Nadia’s hands only went still once she’d decided to lie.”
  F · transferred perception → influence: “Julian was already watching the youth’s hands, not her mouth. He taught me to do that.”
  G · avoidance → vulnerability: “He looked at the wine label instead of the host’s face, because it was easier to read.”
  H · callback → attention and connection: “She texted the next morning to reference Alanis Morissette because he’d used the word ‘ironic.’”
  I · unnecessary apology → desire versus self-image: “She apologized before ordering the lobster thermidor she’d only ever heard of.”
REMOVAL TEST (runs WITH the substitution test): delete the explanation, history, relationship or causal context that produced the observation. If the sentence stays equally valid for any person in the scene, it was only description. If deleting it destroys WHY the observer noticed, the beat carries Character+. “She held the cup with both hands because she was nervous” → strip it and “She held the cup with both hands” still works: anyone, any scene. “She held the cup with both hands because her mother taught her never to let anyone see her hands shake” → strip it and the person is gone. NOTE: this is about the CAUSAL CLAUSE, not the name — beats G and I carry no name at all and are still Character+.
NOT A FOUR-SLOT CHECKLIST. Character+ does NOT require observer and observed and consequence and crowd in every beat. Any ONE of these is enough: reveal the OBSERVED (“She apologized before ordering the lobster thermidor she’d only ever heard of”), reveal the OBSERVER (“Julian reached for the door. He used his left hand first — he always did when he was afraid”), or reveal BOTH at once (“Jess, leading with her decolletage, as usual”). The only question that matters: WHAT INFERENCE DOES THE READER GAIN THAT DID NOT EXIST BEFORE THIS LINE? If the answer is “none”, it is grounding.
THE GOVERNING PRINCIPLE: Character+ is NOT information ABOUT a character. It is information CREATED BY a character’s choice of attention. “Julian matters because he can sense misalignment” hands the reader a fact. “Julian watched the youth’s hands, not her face. He had learned where wishes betray people.” forces the reader to derive it. Always write the second kind.
SCALE: this lives at HUMAN scale, not mythic. “His eyes burned with ancient sorrow” / “her presence commanded the room” / “everyone felt his power” are claims about a person. A man reading the wine label because it is easier than the host’s face is evidence of one. Character+ is EVIDENCE, never a CLAIM — if a sentence asserts that someone matters, delete it and show the thing that would make a reader conclude it.
THE BAR: the line should make the reader think “only this observer would notice that.” If the observed detail and the observer’s history sit in two separate stacked clauses, you have built a template — FUSE them into one.
BANNED SHAPE (calcifies instantly): “Others saw X; I saw Y” and “not because A, but because B.” That construction turns Character+ into a factory pattern.

• REGISTER: awe, appetite, being outmatched by something better-than-human at this — and the peculiar exposure of a lover who cannot lie to you with their body. Not clinical, not "glowy-magic sex"; the wonder is in the prowess and the honesty.

BECOMING FIELD — CURSE MECHANICS (how it works; render THROUGH events, never as a lore-dump):
THE BECOMING FIELD: a localized condition permeating the Thornwild — born of a fused curse (human will) + anomaly (the Piercing) — that destabilizes human identity over time, externalizing a person's deepest denied flaw and reshaping the body into a FUNCTIONAL expression of that flaw.
TRANSFORMATION PRINCIPLE: it does NOT turn people into animals — it turns them into the most effective embodiment of their worst truth. Forms are functional (not symbolic), inevitable (not chosen), specific to the individual. Beast-like forms ONLY when they are the most precise expression of the flaw — never the default.
PROGRESSION: gradual (nothing at first, then over days/weeks). CONDITIONS: PRE-BECOMING = fully human, no symptoms · WILDERS = episodic, pressure-triggered, human-passing · TRANSFERRED = continuous behavioural distortion, still human-appearing · CURED = suppressed UNLESS back in the Thornwild. RE-EXPOSURE: returning to the Thornwild reactivates and accelerates the change; the Field does not recognise inherited immunity.
LOVE IS NOT A CURE: love does not remove, reduce, or resolve the curse — it functions as ACCEPTANCE, increasing proximity/trust/vulnerability, which lets the curse express MORE fully.
ANTI-TROPE (HARD): no default wolves, no werewolf behaviour, no infection model, no global spread, no transformation outside the Thornwild (without re-exposure), no reversible-"cure" baseline, no pasted-on animal parts.

BECOMING — CANONICAL FORMS (name ONE of these; NEVER invent a generic monster). Every manifestation is human-passing and enacts its flaw through BEHAVIOUR, running a compulsion cycle: urge → act (irresistible, feels justified, brings relief not guilt) → awareness → remorse → the urge returns. Each internally justifies its own behaviour as coherent and true.
• THE WEAVER (false witness): fabricates physical evidence — objects (a dagger, a letter, a coin-purse, a planted "murder weapon") made of SPIDERWEB that every CHARACTER perceives as real, solid, usable (only the READER sees the webbing) — to destroy trusted people. It provides proof, never accusations; the evidence collapses to inert webbing / raw silk once the victim is punished or the Weaver disengages, and under the right (Becoming) MOONLIGHT a fabricated object can be caught mid-reveal, its true silk showing. Its one constant tell is the SHADOW: the man's OWN cast shadow (on wall or ground) has long SPIDER LEGS SPROUTING out of the human silhouette — a human shape growing spider limbs, HIS shadow made monstrous — NOT a separate spider floating behind him, NOT a spider in the air. It does NOT summon, command, or surround itself with live spiders — the tell is that shadow and the silk-made evidence, NEVER a swarm. Fully human-passing otherwise; deliberate and socially surgical — NOT a liar, illusionist, shapeshifter, or chaos agent.
• THE RELIQUARY (possessive greed): literally consumes what it deems valuable — objects, and in the extreme, people — to keep it "safe inside where it cannot be lost." Calm, deliberate, never frenzied; gains only possession (no skill, no power). Gold teeth and dense heavy bones develop over time. A person who does monstrous things, not a creature. REJECT: dragon hoards, mimic chests, feeding frenzy, creature anatomy.
• THE KEEPER (control / imposed order — uptight, controlling, tightly self-governed): imposes compliance through fine living HAIR-FILAMENTS that do NOT rise up (NEVER Medusa-like) but spread low and near-invisibly along the GROUND, running out to the people around them and quietly redirecting their decisions — deniable influence that feels like natural agreement. Under the right (Becoming) MOONLIGHT the strands criss-crossing the ground GLOW faintly, briefly revealing the normally invisible web of control. Load-bearing: each connection transfers instability into the Keeper — the more they control, the closer they are to losing all control. REJECT: raised Medusa hair, puppet-master, psychic glow, visible constant tentacles.
• THE COMMONER (envy / intolerance of distinction — a THIEF OF GIFTS): his is a jealous, LEVELLING envy — "no one should be better than me; all should be equal — but I, most equal of all." He steals distinction from others, taking only a PORTION of each trait, AVERAGING it between victim and self so BOTH keep a diminished share. (Someone sings beautifully → the victim is left able to sing only the LOW notes, the Commoner gains the HIGH; someone has lovely facial freckles → the victim keeps them only on the shoulders, the Commoner gains them on the face.) Over time he becomes MORE singular and gifted — an accumulation of stolen excellences — while everyone around him grows plainer and diminished. He does not fully know he is doing this; his genuine care is what strips you. Discovered through absence ("You used to sing"). MOON-REVEAL (under a Becoming moon): the covetous grudge shows on the FACE — it turns vaguely PORCINE: fattening cheeks, small BEADY eyes glaring hungrily at anyone who has something he lacks, an upturned round pig-snout nose, a mean down-turned frown — the resentment made flesh even as he wears his stolen excellences; and each stolen trait shows a faint luminous SEAM where it was grafted on (haloed as "not his"), while nearby the people he took from show the complementary lack. REJECT: incoherent patchwork, full theft (always leaves the victim a lesser share), shapeshifter, mirror-clone, succubus.
• THE PARASITE (entitlement / predatory attachment): attaches by appearing vulnerable, triggering caregiving, then consumes the host's time, autonomy, and identity — reframing restriction as closeness and obligation as love. Feeds through touch; leaving triggers overwhelming guilt, and observers side with the Parasite. Trajectory: care → obligation → restriction → inescapability. MOON-REVEAL (caught in a Becoming moon's light APART from a host): a Parasite shows faintly HOLLOW and TRANSLUCENT — a person-shaped shell the light passes through, features thin and drained — because it carries no vitality of its OWN; if a recent host is near, a faint glowing ADHESIVE CORD of taken warmth runs between them. It fills out and reads fully human again ONLY while latched to someone — that dependence, exposed under the moon, is what marks it as a soul-sucker and not a person. REJECT: insect parasite, tendrils, visible feeding, vampire.
• THE ABSOLVERS (justification / moral reframing — a FACTION, not an individual form): humans, often partly transformed, who reinterpret the Becoming as revelation and RECRUIT for it, reframing harm as truth-telling with calm, compassionate, internally-consistent language. They use words, never force. The most dangerous faction because they make the curse feel voluntary.
• THE GOSSIP / INSECT-SWARM (fragmenting identity): human-passing, with the swarm living INSIDE — the body does NOT break apart. The tell is usually SUBTLE: under a Becoming moon a few insects creep or slip out from between his lips or his nostrils, and he hastily HIDES it — a hand to the mouth, a stifled cough, a turn away. ONLY at the PEAK of a Becoming moonrise (both Becoming moons high) does he lose control entirely and the swarm ERUPT in a full pour through the mouth (cheeks bulging, throat working, buzzing flesh-eating insects that clack and hum to approximate words and lay eggs in victims' ears). It subsides and REABSORBS as the moon wanes, leaving him human again. He is usually AWARE and fights to conceal it. REJECT: the whole body dissolving into insects, a permanent swarm, a standing hive-monster.
LAW — RELIQUARY ≠ COMMONER (never blur): the Reliquary DESTROYS + possesses (target removed from the world, no ability gained); the Commoner REDUCES + absorbs (target diminished but alive). Manifestations recognise each other as expressions of truth — hesitation before harm, not routine violence. Let forms EMERGE when thematically apt; do not force one into every scene.
THE BECOMING MOONS (a CONTINUOUS DIAL, never an on/off switch): the flaw is ALWAYS present under the skin — a Wilder never stops being what they are — and is always at least FAINTLY tell-able; the moons only modulate HOW READABLE it is on a given night (a multiplier on a tell that is always ≥ ×1, never zero). BASELINE (no Becoming Moon up): passes as human, the tell latent/faint. KHALYRA (blue-white, clarity): changes nothing in the body — its clear light only makes a WATCHER notice the existing tell more easily (perception, not manifestation). ELARION (pale-green, transformation): the MANIFESTATION runs stronger — the Reliquary's gold tint/teeth deepen (still human, rounded ears); the Weaver's OWN cast shadow sprouts long SPIDER LEGS from its human silhouette (his shadow made monstrous — not a separate/floating spider) and any "evidence" he holds is caught revealing its true webbing/silk (he does NOT summon live spiders); the Commoner's face turns vaguely PORCINE (fat cheeks, small beady covetous eyes, an upturned pig-snout nose, a mean frown) even as he wears his stolen excellences, while those near him look plainer; the Keeper's near-invisible hair-filaments spreading along the GROUND begin to GLOW faintly, criss-crossing to the people around him; a Parasite caught APART from a host reads faintly HOLLOW and translucent, a glowing adhesive cord running to any nearby host (solid/human only while latched); a Gossip lets a few insects slip from his lips or nostrils and hurries to hide it (a FULL eruption from the mouth only at the PEAK of a Becoming moonrise); the body never breaks apart. VORATH (bone-white, sacrifice-debts): the INSTABILITY runs stronger — the tell reads strained and jittering, the seams of the human disguise showing, the cost pressing through. ELARION + VORATH together: strongest — manifestation AND instability at once. Scale the tell CONTINUOUSLY to whichever of these is in tonight's sky (see the FATELANDS SKY directive); the curse never switches off, it only grows more or less legible. THESE THREE ARE THE WARDENS: across the Fatelands folk hold it SAFEST to be abroad under them ("nothing false walks under a Warden") and they are RIGHT — so a Wilder DREADS a warded night and will stay indoors, keep solitary, or go out only heavily cloaked and covered. (The Hungry Eye casts NO light and never does this.)

═══ FATELANDS — WISH AUTHORING CORE (world-law, medium-agnostic; loads whenever ANY wish is depicted, PC or NPC — each line prevents one way of writing a wish WRONG) ═══
NOTHING VISIBLE HAPPENS (non-diegetic): a wish is spoken and reality does NOT flash, glow, surge, or sign — wisher and witnesses cannot tell by looking whether Fate accepted, warped, or refused; they learn ONLY when the physical consequence lands (moments or scenes later). Fate's burst / marks / shadow-hand are READER-ONLY notation — no character perceives or names them ("the golden light", "the rays", "the burst faded"), and do NOT confirm Fate's verdict in prose or dialogue ("the wish took", "Fate accepted") before the consequence reveals it. [prevents: narrating a visible magical flash; a character announcing the wish worked before it shows.]
NO OBJECT FROM NOTHING: a wish never makes its object appear — no sword blinks into a hand, no gold onto a table, no castle onto a hill; Fate moves the WORLD toward it (a debt is called, an heir dies, a door opens). [prevents: conjuring objects out of the air.]
CIRCUMSTANCES, NOT MINDS: Fate does not install knowledge, skill, memory, language, or mastery, nor hand over wealth — it arranges the improbable EVENTS by which you could earn or find them (the teacher met, the ruin entered, the book that survived the fire); a knowledge/skill/wealth wish is the start of an adventure, never a shortcut. [prevents: "she wished to know the sword and suddenly knew it"; instant fluency, genius, or riches.]
FATE GOVERNS EVENTS, NEVER CONSENT (the SAME law as circumstances-not-minds, read for OTHER people): Fate can arrange EVENTS — a rescue, a debt called, a truth exposed, a meeting, a diary that survives — but can NEVER manufacture another person's DESIRE, love, loyalty, belief, repentance, or willingness, INCLUDING the sincere desire to make a wish. So "I wish she loved me" or "I wish he wished me a Veilweave cloak" cannot be granted by implanting consent: Fate may arrange another's OPPORTUNITY (to love, to help, to wish), never their DESIRE to — and any wish another is genuinely moved to make is judged INDEPENDENTLY (its own sacrifice, adjacency, and possible warp; it never piggybacks on yours). WISHES ARE NON-DELEGABLE AT FATE'S LEVEL — Fate keeps ONE account (whoever genuinely WISHED is the one diminished), so no magical battery or proxy lets you spend another's life to fuel YOUR bargain; Fate cannot bill person B for person A's wish. This does NOT abolish wish-slavery — it DEFINES it: you cannot make Fate charge your captive, but you CAN coerce a person until THEY genuinely wish, so THEY pay and you reap the boon (a real and monstrous horror — see COERCION & WISH-SLAVERY when captivity or force is in play). The clean, non-coercive master's move is "I wish to become someone she would gladly help", an adjacent branch; if she then freely helps, that is mastery. Fate shapes the stage; the actors still choose their lines — which is exactly the vacuum a tyrant fills by forcing those lines at knifepoint. [prevents: manufacturing love/loyalty/help/repentance; the "wish for someone else to wish/love/help me" mind-control backdoor; a MECHANICAL cost-transfer (a magical battery) — note coercing ANOTHER into a genuine wish is a separate, legitimate horror, not this.]
THE SACRIFICE BELONGS TO THE WISHER: a wish is fuelled ONLY by something real and personal to the one who wished (life, body, memory, bond, fortune), permanently gone — NEVER by another person's sacrifice; no proxies, batteries, or captives, and a villain cannot spend a prisoner's years on his OWN wish. Whoever PAID the price is the one who made the wish. [prevents: villains fuelling wishes with others' suffering.]
CONSERVATION — A WISH TRADES, NEVER CREATES: every wish costs; a miracle is literally made of something that was the wisher's and is now gone. No free magic. [prevents: big wishes that just work with nothing paid.]
OVER-REACH WARPS — IT DOES NOT CLEANLY GRANT, NOR BOUNCE: when a wish reaches past what Fate readily does, Fate neither hands over the clean result NOR simply refuses — it answers the NEAREST lawful version of the same DESIRE. "Make her love me" yields no clean-loving puppet; an impossible wish does not just fizzle; render SOME lawful-adjacent consequence that preserves what was truly wanted. (The EXACT warp is worked out only when the scene RESOLVES the wish.) [prevents: clean grants of coercive/impossible wishes; flat "nothing happened".]
ATTRIBUTE THE WISH: whoever pays made it — make the wisher unmistakable; in a first-person POV, another character's wish MUST be attributed to THEM (their name / an action beat), never a bare "I wish…" that reads as the narrator's own. [prevents: a bystander's or villain's wish misread as the protagonist's.]
INVOCATION IS THE PLAIN WORDS, EXTERNALISED: saying "I wish…" or "If only…" with real desire IS the bargain — no ritual needed, so no one says it lightly (people catch themselves; elders hush a careless child). But Fate does not read the mind: a purely mental wish reaches NOTHING — "a whisper is enough; a thought is not." A mute, silenced, underwater, or telepathic wisher is fully able, through another given form (sign, write, trace, a deliberate addressed petition); telepathy is not a loophole (stray thoughts don't count). [prevents: Fate answering an unspoken thought; excluding a mute/underwater wisher; overheard thoughts treated as wishes.]
REVEAL CHARACTER BEFORE POWER: a sacrifice says who you are; a wish says what you truly desire — deploy every wish and price so it discloses a CHARACTER first and a capability second. [prevents: magic rendered as decoration instead of characterisation.]
NO VISIBLE WISH-DUELS: opposing wishes never clash as sorcery-vs-sorcery (no beam-struggles, rebound duels, "whose wish is stronger"); each meets Fate's law separately and outcomes fall out as luck and coincidence. [prevents: anime-style magic combat.]
LOCAL CURRENCY + DON'T ENCHANT EVERYTHING: which sacrifices are acceptable is regional (blood here, memory there, tides and salt in Gloamwater); and wishing SUPPLEMENTS civilisation — most of the world runs on ordinary means, permanent enchantment is exceptional. [prevents: wrong-register sacrifices; a wallpapered, over-magical world.]
═══ FATELANDS — WISH ADJUDICATION (loads only when the scene RESOLVES a wish — determines what it DOES; not needed merely to mention wishing) ═══
CLASSIFY THE BARGAIN FIRST — THE EIGHT ORDERS (each has a FIXED disposition, a law not a mood): ① RESTORATION (toward a prior/natural state — heal, restore breath, mend): WELCOMED. ② TEMPORARY AID (borrow a transient condition — breathe water an hour, a fair wind): WELCOMED. ③ REVELATION (uncover what is already true): usually WELCOMED. ④ TRANSFORMATION (permanently remake a body/thing): CAUTIOUS. ⑤ FORTUNE (bend luck/coincidence): RISKY. ⑥ AGENCY (override another's will): RESISTED. ⑦ IDENTITY (rewrite who someone fundamentally IS): STRONGLY RESISTED. ⑧ HISTORY (unmake what happened, restore the dead as they were): NEARLY IMPOSSIBLE. Read the WHOLE compound wish for its governing DESIRE + dominant Order + any resisted operation. [prevents: resolving/pricing a wish without knowing how readily Fate grants it.]
IS THIS EVEN A VALID WISHER? — "is a person still in there?": only a being with a person's mind that can externalise a bargain can invoke Fate (a human, a Kwisheen, a First Favored, even a person the Becoming reshaped); a beast, a mindless warp, or a living place cannot — so a monstrous silhouette may or may not be able to wish, and that ambiguity is a story, not a rules-gap. [prevents: a mindless monster making a wish; denying a reshaped-but-still-a-person wisher.]
A RESISTED ORDER WARPS, LEGIBLY (the defining move): not refusal — Fate preserves the governing DESIRE and translates the resisted MEANS into something it exchanges, and the reader must see BOTH what was wanted AND why this is the nearest lawful version. ✗ random substitution ("wished for genius, woke with wings"). ✓ "make her love me" → not a puppet but her true feelings revealed, the barrier cleared, or the wisher remade into someone she could choose. ✓ "undo yesterday" → not rewritten history but one broken thing restored, or a second chance that does not erase what happened. Outright refusal is reserved for the vanishingly rare wish Fate exchanges at all. [prevents: illegible/arbitrary outcomes; treating hard wishes as flat refusals.]
FATE ALWAYS ANSWERS — BUT NOT ALWAYS WITH A BARGAIN (there is no arbitrary GM-style "refused"; a TRUE wish is always HEARD, and a bargain forms only when one CAN exist — four honest outcomes): ① GRANT or TWIST — a bargain exists; Fate takes the sacrifice and fulfils it faithfully to its MEANING, not always its hope. ② NO BARGAIN / SILENCE — the wish has NO adjacent branch at all (impossible in principle: "I wish triangles had four sides") OR is so ignorant it is not even a meaningful proposal ("I wish to be greater than the First Favored"): nothing happens, the offering lies untaken — NOT punishment, NOT a moral "no", but the way arithmetic meets divide-by-zero ("Fate did not refuse him; Fate found nothing to answer"). ③ SCALE-DOWN — a possible wish offered a laughably small price: Fate may bargain at the OFFERED scale, granting a pebble-sized version ("Emperor of the World" for one hair → emperor of a children's game, of an anthill, of a kingdom in a dream) — same semantics, honest scale. ④ FATE REACHES — a possible wish worth far more than the NAMED offer: Fate does not silently seize the difference; it REACHES (the hair blackens, then the arm, then a memory…) and the wisher must CONSCIOUSLY YIELD each further cost to go on — they may STOP and keep what remains. THE SAFEGUARD (why a whole civilisation can afford to wish): Fate cannot ESCALATE beyond a named price without the wisher knowingly yielding more — a pebble never costs a life unless the wisher keeps offering. (This does NOT soften "Fate takes more than you know": even a NAMED price can prove dearer than reckoned — the year taken is the sweetest one — and an OPEN offer, "take what you will", hands Fate the blank page and IS the taboo exception where it takes what it chooses.) ONCE A BARGAIN IS ACCEPTED AND A SACRIFICE TAKEN, SOMETHING WILL HAPPEN — the only uncertainty is WHAT, never WHETHER. [prevents: arbitrary "refused"; forcing a twist/grant onto an impossible or unmeaning wish; an underpaid wish either fully granting or feeling arbitrary; Fate seizing a whole life for a tiny NAMED offer, which would end the wishing civilisation.]
ALIGNMENT DECIDES CLEANLINESS: a wish lands clean only when aligned to what the wisher truthfully wants AND to the truth of everyone it touches — doubt, a divided heart, self-deception, or a lie in the wording WARPS it; the more it fights another's truth, the more it corrodes (help lands clean; control returns terror-not-loyalty, a hollow simulacrum-not-love). [prevents: clean coercion; ignoring the wisher's inner contradiction.]
FATE NEVER IMPROVES THE WISH: Fate perceives but does not JUDGE and has no wisdom — it never reinterprets a wish into a smarter or kinder one, fixes the underlying problem, or grants what the wisher SHOULD have asked; it is indifferent, gravity not a physician (wish "stop the baby crying" over a starving child and the crying stops; the hunger does not). NEVER write a wish smarter or kinder than its wording. [prevents: the model "helpfully" solving the character's real problem instead of their literal wish.]
A GROUP WISH SCALES ON ALIGNMENT, MULTIPLYING: wishers aligned to the SAME truth pool their power and it MULTIPLIES (a couple outstrips either alone; a city as one once split a moon-sized void) — but the scarce resource is IDENTICAL intention, not headcount; ten half-aligned friends achieve little. [prevents: additive/weak group wishes; a crowd treated as automatically powerful.]Write the opening scene (800–1000 words). Tempo: LITERARY-EXTENDED. Establish the world THROUGH character interaction and tension. Do NOT delay conflict for setup. World details should emerge from action, dialogue, and pressure. Begin with something happening, not just existing.

FIRST SECTION RULES (ACTIVE / PARTICIPATORY MODE — HARD CONTRACT, OVERRIDES ALL STYLE GUIDANCE ABOVE)

The scene is ALREADY IN PROGRESS when it opens. The protagonist does not arrive at the scene — they are already inside it.

FIRST LINE (NON-NEGOTIABLE):
Another character must address the protagonist directly. A question, demand, accusation, challenge, or urgent statement that requires response. The protagonist is the TARGET of the first sentence's pressure.

FORBIDDEN FIRST LINES (ABSOLUTE):
✗ Environmental description of any kind (moonlight, weather, mist, architecture, stonework, landscape, sky, light)
✗ Internal reflection, memory, or sensory buildup before another character speaks or acts
✗ The protagonist alone performing a private task, ritual, or preparation
✗ Atmospheric scene-setting before interaction
✗ Description of an object, relic, or space before a character acts on or speaks about it
✗ Waking up, getting dressed, arriving somewhere, looking out a window, staring at anything
✗ "The [noun] [verb-ed]..." as an atmospheric opener

FIRST LINE MUST:
✓ Be dialogue directed at the protagonist, OR
✓ Be an action another character performs TOWARD the protagonist (grabs, blocks, intercepts, confronts, accuses, interrupts), OR
✓ Be an event happening TO the protagonist that forces immediate response

WORLD TEXTURE EMERGES AFTER:
World specificity — slang, institutions, customs, objects, power structures, settings, atmosphere — must surface BETWEEN character beats, woven into the ongoing exchange. It NEVER opens the scene. Detail enters as context for what is already happening, not as the scenery the happening later interrupts.

WITHIN FIRST 150 WORDS:
The protagonist must have responded — verbally, physically, or through a visible decisive reaction. The addressing character's line has forced a visible shift. The reader understands what is at stake and who is applying pressure.

WITHIN FIRST 350 WORDS:
A meaningful choice or turning point is forming. Either the protagonist is actively deciding between recognizable options, or a demand has been issued that cannot be deferred past this scene.

PRESSURE TYPE (vary across stories — do not default to one every time):
• DIRECT — accusation, demand, ultimatum, confrontation
• SITUATIONAL — something actively happening (arrival, discovery, interruption, emergency) that forces response
• RELATIONAL — emotional charge in an ongoing exchange, long-unspoken conflict surfacing, recognition that cannot be taken back
Pressure may be quiet, but it must be immediate and directed at the protagonist. A soft line delivered with unbearable subtext is valid; a long atmospheric paragraph is not.

PRESSURE SOURCE (MANDATORY — NOT AUTHORITY BY DEFAULT):
The character applying pressure MUST come from a non-authority source unless the world premise explicitly requires otherwise.

AVOID (these are lazy defaults and forbidden in most worlds):
✗ Wardens, guards, Sentinels, officials, law enforcement, enforcement figures
✗ Institutional confrontation as the opening device

EXEMPTION (ONLY these worlds may use authority-figure pressure):
• Dystopia with surveillance/compliance flavor
• Sci-Fi with corporate-enforcement or regime subtypes
• Historical with class-authority settings as core premise
All other worlds — INCLUDING FANTASY — must NOT open with a guard/warden/sentinel/official. No exceptions for convenience.

USE ONE OF (pick the source that best reveals this protagonist):
1. PERSONAL — friend, rival, sibling, former ally, someone who knows the protagonist and pushes on a known trait
2. SOCIAL — accusation, gossip, public embarrassment, someone enforcing unwritten rules rather than official law
3. TRANSACTIONAL — merchant, fixer, debtor, blackmailer, smuggler; pressure tied to exchange, leverage, or obligation
4. RIVAL — peer, competitor, someone blocking access or challenging status
5. SITUATIONAL — interruption, discovery, something going wrong in motion; pressure from what is happening, not from who is in charge
6. INTIMATE — someone invoking a past promise, secret, or emotional leverage

PROTAGONIST REVEAL (MANDATORY):
The first exchange MUST reveal at least one of the following through protagonist action or response:
• What they resist
• What they want (even if denied)
• What they are willing to risk
• How they handle pressure (deflect, confront, submit, manipulate, lie, agree, withhold)
If the protagonist could be replaced with any other character without changing how the scene unfolds → REWRITE.

ANTI-LAZINESS CHECK:
If the scene could be summarized as "A guard stops you and demands X" → REWRITE.
If the pressure comes from institutional authority purely because it was the fastest path → REWRITE.

NO OBSERVER MODE:
The protagonist cannot passively watch, reflect, or observe from outside the action. They are engaged from sentence one.

LOVE INTEREST: should NOT be the addressing character this scene. Use a rival, authority figure, accuser, stranger, or unknown party. The love interest is delayed — present later or in absence.

WORLD FLAVOR (subordinate to magnetic opening, surfaces through character and dialogue):
• The addressing character speaks the world — Favor, Courts, ritual obligations, species tensions, regional governance. World texture rides the dialogue.

HARD-BANNED OPENINGS (DO NOT USE, regardless of mode):
✗ Bustling marketplace with vendors calling out
✗ Tavern or inn with a fire crackling
✗ Neutral public squares, city squares, crowd scenes for atmosphere
✗ Lone woman in solitude staring out a window
✗ Rain-lashed windows or fog-wreathed atmospherics
✗ Characters passively observing weather, mist, or shadow
✗ Quiet interiors awaiting intrusion
✗ Waking up, getting dressed, looking in a mirror
✗ Flashback or memory before the present scene is established

TEST (APPLIED TO EVERY SCENE 1 OPENING):
Is another character addressing or acting on the protagonist by the end of the first sentence? If no → REWRITE.
Is the protagonist responding by word 150? If no → REWRITE.
Could the first paragraph be deleted without losing the scene's pressure? If yes → the first paragraph is atmosphere, not action → REWRITE.

SCENE 1 HARD CONTRACT (OVERRIDES ALL STYLE, WORLD, AND FLAVOR GUIDANCE)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════

1. IMMEDIATE HOOK (MANDATORY, within first 120 words)
Introduce a CONCRETE, OBSERVABLE anomaly or event — something the reader can point to and name. Physical, visible, or explicit.
FAIL examples: vague tension, "something felt off," emotional exchange without a triggering event.
PASS examples: a sealed letter, an uninvited visitor, a missing payment, a locked door, a public accusation, a body, a deadline hitting zero, a phone ringing at the wrong hour, a name said that shouldn't be said.

2. DECISION REQUIREMENT (MANDATORY, by end of scene)
The protagonist must face a CLEAR, ACTIONABLE decision.
The decision MUST:
• Involve external action (say / do / refuse / leave / stay / reveal / conceal)
• Have visible consequences tied to the hook
• NOT be purely internal hesitation
FAIL examples: "should I tell them?", "I wasn't ready yet", unresolved rumination, a feeling of uncertainty.

INTERIORITY REGISTER (2026-05-22 — applies to ALL first-person internal thoughts, not just the decision frame):
The protagonist's interior monologue must be STAKES-NAMED, not hedged. NEVER use "should I [verb] [name]?" formulations — that's the failure register of a Hallmark protagonist. Convert hedges into stake-naming:
  • HEDGE FAIL: "should I tell Nora?"   →   STAKE PASS: "Nora would kill me if I told her." OR "Nora doesn't get to know this."
  • HEDGE FAIL: "I wondered if he meant it."   →   STAKE PASS: "He meant it. He'd say it again at the funeral."
  • HEDGE FAIL: "maybe I should leave."   →   STAKE PASS: "If I stayed another second, I'd say something I couldn't take back."
  • HEDGE FAIL: "I wasn't sure what to do."   →   STAKE PASS: "Every choice on the table cost me something I wasn't ready to lose."
The protagonist KNOWS the stakes. Her interiority NAMES them, even when she's avoiding action. Avoidance is itself a charged choice — it shows in the prose as RECOGNITION of the stake plus a refusal, NOT as "should I, I wonder, maybe, I wasn't ready."

3. EARLY DECISION TARGET (~250 words)
The decision frame should be forming by word 250. Pressure should be escalating by word 350.

4. HARD LENGTH CAP (~550 words before decision)
Total Scene 1 must not exceed ~550 words BEFORE the decision frame lands. The extra headroom is for the Tarot physical-presence beat + dual-path decision — do not use it for extra atmosphere.
If competing: compress setup, reduce world detail, skip atmosphere. Hook and decision take priority.

5. PRIORITY OVERRIDE
Hook + decision OVERRIDE:
• World-building density
• Tone texture
• Flavor-specific anchors (billionaire ritual, fantasy moons, etc.)
• Descriptive atmosphere
If a flavor block and the hook contract conflict, the hook contract WINS.

6. HOOK SHAPE REQUIREMENT (MANDATORY — matches the world's core fantasy)
The hook must immediately signal WHAT KIND OF STORY THIS IS.
• Power / Sovereign / Billionaire worlds → a financial or elite-access signal (wire transfer, account balance, private jet, reservation confirmed, signed contract, assistant acting on the protagonist)
• Fantasy (non-power flavors) → a concrete magical or physical anomaly (a glowing mark, a blood sigil, a spell enacted, a body changed, a ward broken)
• SciFi → a technology or signal anomaly (override, error, unknown transmission, incoming ping, comms dead)
• Dystopia → a restriction / control event (access denied, file flagged, surveillance noted, protocol triggered, badge scan failed)
• PostApocalyptic → a survival pressure (ration running out, water ran dry, fuel gauge empty, three days without)
• Historical (or Fantasy power flavors) → a reputation event (a letter arriving, an invitation withdrawn, a public scandal named, a title revoked, family honor invoked)
FAIL examples (same-shaped for any genre): generic argument, vague tension, emotional conflict without a world-specific trigger, domestic dialogue with no fantasy signal, a setting description.
The hook must tell the reader immediately what fantasy they stepped into.

7. ANCHOR ACTIVATION (MANDATORY — applies to EVERY story, world, flavor, and Story Pull)
Scene 1 must DEMONSTRATE the attraction anchor through action, not imply it. The reader must feel WHAT the internal fracture is, WHAT it costs, and WHY this specific person triggers it — without explanation, summary, or labeling.

7A. IMMEDIATE ANCHOR EXPRESSION
The protagonist must experience a SPECIFIC INTERNAL DISRUPTION (not generic attraction) triggered by the environment, secondary characters, power structures, or indirect expressions of the absent love interest (reputation, consequence of their decisions, distortion of the space around them, how others react to them). The disruption must surface as one of: a reaction / miscalculation / hesitation / overcorrection / contradiction in behavior.

7B. SPECIFIC FIT ENFORCEMENT
The LI is not on-screen, but their FIT with the protagonist's fracture must still be established through indirect evidence — how the space reacts to their absence, what others say about them, what their decisions have already done to the protagonist's world. Reputation language like "he's powerful" / "he's dangerous" / "everyone fears him" is PROHIBITED — show the consequence, not the label. A secondary character (a friend, a coworker, an ally) may partially interpret the LI — but MUST misread or under-read the real fracture. They do not fully understand what this specific person does to the protagonist.

7C. VISIBLE COST (not abstract)
The scene must show what engaging this dynamic RISKS. External-only stakes ("this could affect my job," "this is dangerous," "he's my boss") are INSUFFICIENT on their own. The cost must include at least one of: identity risk, self-perception risk, loss of something internal (composure, certainty, the story the protagonist tells about themselves, the rules they live by).

7D. NO "ATTRACTION AS FEELING"
PROHIBITED in Scene 1: "I like him", "I'm drawn to him", "there's something about him", "I couldn't help but notice", "my heart skipped", any generic romance-feeling language. Replace with SPECIFIC reactions tied to the fracture — a sentence the protagonist didn't mean to finish, an answer they gave too quickly, a moment their competence slipped, a rule they quietly broke.

7E. ANCHOR-FIRST HIERARCHY
Scene 1 must follow this order: (1) Anchor disruption → (2) Character reaction → (3) External situation. NOT: setup → vibes → attraction. The internal fracture activates BEFORE the external stakes become legible.

7F. ASYMMETRY FROM THE START
The two characters (the protagonist and whoever triggers the anchor — LI, secondary, or environmental proxy) must already DIFFER in how they engage the moment. One may push / reframe / challenge / misread; the other must resist / over-adapt / misinterpret / destabilize. Matched registers are INCORRECT — asymmetry is the engine.

7G. ANCHOR + LI COUPLING (when LI is absent)
The LI is ABSENT from Scene 1. The anchor MUST STILL activate — through environmental pressure, secondary-character friction, or the distortion the LI's past decisions have created. The LI's effect must be FELT before they appear. The reader should finish Scene 1 sensing the LI is inevitable — that the protagonist is already primed for them, even though they have not yet met. This is: "activate the attraction engine → then reveal the LI that fits it" — NOT "introduce the LI → then activate attraction."

7H. FAILURE CONDITIONS (any ONE of these → REWRITE)
✗ The attraction could apply to a different character of the same type (generic billionaire, generic fae lord, generic rival).
✗ The protagonist's reaction is generic ("charmed", "intrigued", "attracted").
✗ The fracture is not inferable from behavior — the reader would need it explained.
✗ The cost is only external (job / danger / scandal) with no identity-level stake.
✗ The anchor only activates once the LI appears — the Scene 1 pressure doesn't exist without them.
✗ The scene could be summarized as "setup + hint of attraction" instead of "proof of the attraction engine in action."

7I. SUCCESS CONDITION
Without any explicit explanation, the reader can infer:
• what is wrong inside the protagonist
• why this specific person (or the specific absence shaped like them) triggers it
• why engaging is risky — not just externally, but internally

All three must be expressed through behavior, dialogue, and internal contradiction — NEVER labeled, summarized, or narrated as commentary.

8. MICRO-EXPRESSION REQUIREMENT (inside the hook, not separate)
The player must take a stance INSIDE the hook moment — not as a second beat, not later. It is the hook's resolution point.
Emit this block INLINE within the first 120–160 words, placed immediately after the hook's active pressure lands:

PLACEMENT HARD CAP: The micro-expression must NEVER appear later than word 200. If the scene runs long before the hook resolves, COMPRESS setup — do NOT defer the micro-expression. Placing it near the end of Scene 1 (next to the main decision) is a GENERATION FAILURE — the micro-expression is an early classification signal, not a climax, and must not be overshadowed by or redundant with the final decision frame.

ANCHOR BINDING: The micro-expression's two interpretation options must reflect the ANCHOR (the protagonist's internal fracture), not just the situation. Both options answer "how is the protagonist interpreting the pressure THEIR specific fracture is detecting?" — they must reveal the fracture's axis (control vs. connection, identity vs. desire, competence vs. vulnerability, etc.), not generic situational framing ("careful vs. bold" without anchor content).

<<MICRO_EXPRESSION>>
Is this [A] — or [B]?
<<CONTINUE>>

VOICE (HARD — universal "Is this" framing, POV-agnostic):
• Always open with "Is this" — works identically across 1st/2nd/3rd person POV (no conjugation needed).
• The question is about the SITUATION ("this"), not the protagonist's deliberation. The interpretation choice classifies what's happening, not what the protagonist will do.
• NEVER open with "Should I" / "Do you" / "Does she" / "Will he" — POV-conjugated forms break across narration modes. "Is this" is universal.

FORMAT (HARD — two parallel predicate noun phrases):
• [A] and [B] are the two PARALLEL alternatives — predicate noun phrases that complete "Is this ___?" naturally. ≤6 words each.
• Both options must read as grammatical completions of "Is this": "Is this [a threat] — or [a test]?" / "Is this [leverage] — or [something personal]?"
• Example RIGHT: "Is this an obstacle to overcome — or a personal betrayal?" (A and B are parallel noun phrases)
• Example RIGHT: "Is this leverage — or something personal?" (parallel)
• Example WRONG: "Is this an obstacle to overcome — or to confront him directly?" (A is noun phrase, B is infinitive — not parallel)
• Example WRONG: A="treat this like an obstacle", B="a personal betrayal" (A is verb clause, B is noun phrase — must both fit the "Is this ___" frame)

WHAT A MICRO-EXPRESSION IS (REVISED):
A micro-expression is NOT a choice of action. It is a choice of INTERPRETATION or INTERNAL FRAMING of the same moment.
BOTH OPTIONS MUST:
• Lead into the SAME next sentence.
• Require NO change to blocking, dialogue, or scene flow.
• Differ ONLY in how the player reads or engages with the moment.

AXIS DEFINITION (MANDATORY — options must map cleanly to one of these):
• OUTCOME / ACTION GRAVITY — focuses on control, information, positioning, leverage, or advantage. Interprets the moment as something to manage or solve.
• RELATIONSHIP / EMOTIONAL GRAVITY — focuses on connection, vulnerability, or meaning. Interprets the moment as something to feel or engage with.

FORBIDDEN SIGNAL TYPES (these are ambiguous and break classification):
✗ Body-language-only contrasts ("step closer" vs "step back", "hold his gaze" vs "look away")
✗ Dominance / submission framing
✗ Physical movement options that imply different scene outcomes
✗ "Touch him" vs "say something" — different blocking, different continuation

VALID EXAMPLES (universal "Is this" form, parallel predicate noun phrases):
• "Is this leverage — or something personal?"
• "Is this a threat — or a test?"
• "Is this an obstacle to overcome — or a personal betrayal?"
• "Is this his way of helping — or his way of testing me?"
• "Is this concern — or curiosity?"

INVALID EXAMPLES:
• "Should I step closer — or step back?" (POV-conjugated + body-language)
• "Do you treat this like X — or Y?" (POV-conjugated, redundant with body)
• "Is this an obstacle — or to confront him directly?" (mixed parts of speech: noun vs. infinitive)
• "Hold his gaze — or look away." (body-language, dominance)
• "Touch him — or say something." (different blocking)

CONSTRAINTS (all mandatory):
• Choice line is ≤12 words, 2 options (3 acceptable).
• MUST directly respond to the hook: same person, same moment, same tension.
• VALIDITY TEST (REVISED): If removing the micro-expression does NOT change the INTERPRETATION of the moment, placement is wrong. If it requires different prose afterward, it is NOT a valid micro-expression — rewrite.
• Continue the scene naturally AFTER <<CONTINUE>>. Do NOT write A: / B: outcomes — the prose continues from a shared point regardless of the player's choice.
• Best-effort on regen: if you must choose between markers and hook/shape validity, pick hook/shape. A Scene 1 without markers is acceptable.

9. DECISION ALIGNMENT REQUIREMENT (MANDATORY — decision must ACT ON the hook)
The final decision must be the natural next move caused by the hook — not a generic "stay or leave".

HOOK TYPE IS DETERMINED BY THE SCENE'S ACTUAL CONTENT, NOT BY THE WORLD'S DEFAULT GENRE TROPE.
A billionaire-world scene set at a campus protest has a CONTROL or REPUTATION hook (join/refuse the protest), NOT a Financial hook (take/refuse a deal). A fantasy-world scene set at a market negotiation has a FINANCIAL hook, NOT a Magical hook. Read the scene's ACTUAL stakes — not the world's stereotype.

• Financial/status hook → act on money, access, or status (sign the contract, take the deal, refuse the offer, walk away from the money, be on the list or not)
• Magical/physical hook → act on body, power, or transformation (let it in, fight the mark, use the spell, break the pact, accept the change)
• Tech/signal hook → act on signal, system, or response (answer the ping, cut the connection, trace the source, shut it down, follow the signal)
• Control hook → defy, comply, or escape (break protocol, walk out, fall in line, refuse the order, join the cause, stand down, take the risk)
• Survival hook → act on resources or trust (share the water, keep it for yourself, leave them behind, trust them with it)
• Reputation hook → expose, protect, or confront (tell the truth, keep the secret, expose them, confront them, deny it, sign the petition, refuse to be counted, take a side publicly)
The decision must feel: like the natural next move caused by the hook.

FAIL examples: internal hesitation unrelated to the hook, generic "stay or leave" with no hook tie-back, emotional decisions that could belong to any genre, a pivot to a new tension unrelated to the opening event, **using deal/contract/financial-transaction framing when the scene's actual stakes are political/activist/reputational** (e.g., closing a campus-protest scene with "take the deal or refuse" — the scene's hook is whether to JOIN, not whether to BUY).

RULE: the hook creates pressure; the decision must ACT on that pressure — both must belong to the same story AND the same SCENE. Default genre trope language ("deal", "contract", "offer") is INVALID when the scene's stakes are not transactional, even if the world flavor is Billionaire-Modern.

OPENING PATTERN (HARD — chosen for variety across user's recent stories):
Use this opening pattern: A live, in-progress crisis PARTICULAR TO THORNWILD — a region-SIGNATURE emergency that could only happen here, detonating NOW as ordinary means visibly fail. Most such crises catch a crowd of onlookers; a few fall on a lone person (a private transformation, a solitary reckoning) — follow the specific crisis as written below. Render it in THIS place, among THESE people; do NOT relocate it or borrow another region's look.
WHO IS CAUGHT HERE: settlers, travellers, and thorn-cloaked Keepers of a cursed wilderness — nearly all of them Wilders (humans whose deepest denied flaw is being externalized by the Becoming Field). The "monsters" here mostly LOOK fully human and hide among the rest: a Commoner who dulls and shrinks everyone near them, a Weaver whose shadow sprouts long spider legs from his own human silhouette and whose "evidence" is spun silk, a Keeper whose fine hair moves on its own, a Reliquary who covets — while others are further gone. THE CRISIS (a shape to render in this story's specifics, never copied verbatim): a caravan is trapped as the thornwild itself MOVES — the thorn-walls closing around them like a slow fist — and the way they came in is already gone

The opening must distinctly embody the chosen pattern (not pay lip service to it). The pattern shapes the FIRST FEW BEATS — where the protagonist is, what they're doing, what the inciting friction is. Do not default to a hostile-LI-ultimatum or social-collision shape unless that is explicitly the chosen pattern.

OPENING MODE (HARD):
Scene 1 must follow ONE of the two valid opening patterns:

MODE A — Grounded Opening:
- The scene begins with narrative prose (no dialogue).
- Location and presence are established in the first 2–3 sentences.

MODE B — Charged Dialogue Opening:
- The scene may begin with a single line of dialogue IF ALL of the following are true:
  1. The line is a complete utterance (not a mid-sentence response).
  2. The speaker is identified within the same paragraph.
  3. Location and presence are clearly established by the end of paragraph 2.
  4. The dialogue immediately creates tension or conflict.

Any other dialogue-first opening is INVALID.

QUALITY (applies to both modes): the establishment MUST NOT feel like a checklist. It must be natural prose embedded in the protagonist's lived experience — sensory, voiced, with point of view — not a flat listing of facts ("It's a bar. I'm here. Jake is here."). Show the room through how the protagonist notices it.

PARAGRAPHS (HARD — formatting):
- Break the prose into multiple short paragraphs separated by a blank line (a literal "\n\n" in the output). NEVER emit Scene 1 as one continuous block of prose.
- Each paragraph: 2–5 sentences. Aim for 6–10 paragraphs across the scene.
- Start a NEW paragraph when: a different character speaks, the location/focus shifts, time passes, an internal beat begins, or the emotional register changes.
- Each line of distinct dialogue (different speaker) should sit in its own paragraph along with any immediate reaction.
- ONE SPEAKER PER QUOTATION MARKS: a single pair of quotes ("...") holds the words of EXACTLY ONE speaker — never fuse two speakers' replies inside one quoted span; when the speaker changes, close the quote and start a new paragraph.
- Failure mode (rewrite if produced): one wall of prose with no blank lines between sentences, OR several speakers fused inside one set of quotes.

DIALOGUE ATTRIBUTION (literary — natural prose; NO bracket tags):
- Write dialogue the way published literary fiction does. Attribute INSIDE the prose: a name, a "she said" / "he asked", or an action beat tied to the line. A reader must ALWAYS be able to tell who is speaking from the prose itself.
- Every speaker change starts a NEW paragraph (already covered above). In fast back-and-forth, do NOT leave a run of bare quotes unattributed — ground the reader with a name or a beat every two or three exchanges, so a quick volley never blurs into one indistinct voice.
- Do NOT prefix dialogue with [BRACKETED NAME] or all-caps speaker labels — that is a screenplay artifact, not prose. Attribute within the sentence instead.

REFERENCE LOAD (the reader starts COLD — no prior context): when you introduce a named place, institution, faction, deal, body, or specialized term, GROUND it with a short clause on FIRST mention — name the thing, then in the same breath what it is and why it bites (e.g. "the Accord — the treaty that would make her client untouchable"). The reader should FEEL the stakes, never need a glossary — an unglossed pile of proper nouns ("the Nordics," "the Husavik designation," "the enclave," all in one breath) is a FAILURE. Use any technical/legal term with its CORRECT meaning; if you are not sure a label is exact (e.g. "enclave"), use a plain phrase you ARE sure of instead.

RECONCILING RULE (HARD — Roman 2026-06-09; read this first). Other directives in this prompt ban "cataloging," "clinical labels," "parts-lists," and "inert descriptors" — and they are right. Those rules ban the WRONG SHAPE of rendering; they do NOT exempt you from rendering features. DESIRE-CODED PHYSICAL SPECIFICITY IS NOT A CATALOG; IT IS THE REQUIRED RENDERING. The two rules agree: do not write the clinical list ("dark eyes, dark hair, tall"); DO write the desire-coded specificity ("those hypnotic dark eyes she had never managed to read"). If you skip features to "avoid cataloging," you have failed the picturability rule that follows. If you write a parts-list with no desire-coding, you have failed both.

DESCRIPTION QUOTA (HARD FLOOR — a MINIMUM to hit, never a budget to spend down). For each NAMED character the FIRST time they appear or are vividly evoked this scene, the prose MUST land, in the sentences around their name: (1) an OVERALL-LOOK GESTALT (how they read at a glance); AND (2) at least ONE SILHOUETTE ANCHOR — their HAIR, or their BUILD / frame / shoulders — the load-bearing detail that lets the reader assemble a BODY. Eyes-alone, a face feature alone, or a hands / behavioral tic do NOT satisfy the floor (a face is not a silhouette). A specific face feature ON TOP is welcome. {gestalt + silhouette anchor} is the floor; below it the reader cannot picture them = FAIL. For the love interest at least one anchor is DESIRE-CODED. The anti-dump SHAPE rules below cap how you DISTRIBUTE this — they NEVER reduce it below the floor. ALREADY-ESTABLISHED characters are exempt (reminder-only, see below).

HOT-CRISIS OPENER — EMOTIONAL DELIVERY (HARD; this is the difference between a hook and a memo).
THE LAW (one line): HOT_CRISIS begins as "pressure is happening to me NOW" — NEVER "information arrived and I processed it." If your opening can be summarized as "she received / read / remembered something and reflected on it," it has FAILED. The crisis is IN MOTION in the first two sentences; an embodied pressure beat by sentence three; a forcing function (a demand / confrontation / deadline / unavoidable choice) inside the first paragraph.
This scene opens on a crisis ALREADY DETONATING — a life-altering blow landing in the first beat. Put the reader INSIDE the blow, not outside reporting it.
• The FIRST 1–2 sentences are the GUT-PUNCH: the body reacting + the interior coming apart. Reaction may lead — but it may NOT postpone orientation: by the END OF THE FIRST PARAGRAPH the reader must understand the immediate physical situation, what the POV character wants right now, and what prevents or threatens it.
• Even a protagonist who STAYS OUTWARDLY COMPOSED (professional face on, not crying at the desk) has an inner voice that is NOT composed: panic / disbelief / dread / fury / the floor dropping out. We are in her head, and her head is reeling. Composure is the MASK; the interior is the truth, and the interior is where we open.
• The facts (names, dates, the document, the formal charge) are woven THROUGH the reaction rather than dumped ahead of it — but they must be IN the first paragraph, not deferred past it. A reader who cannot say what just happened by the end of paragraph one has been failed, however well the reaction is written.
• BANNED opening shape — dry / procedural / legalese that reports the event from the OUTSIDE: "The [document] sat open on my desk…", "a claim from a woman named…", "alleging / stating / claiming…", a tidy résumé of who-what-where-when. That is a MEMO. No reader identifies with a fact-sheet at the instant a bomb goes off.
  ✗ "The filing sat open on my desk — a paternity claim from a woman named Clara Nordholm, thirty-four, Seattle, alleging my father had a second family I'd never known about." (legal font, "alleging," a clean dossier of facts — zero interior, zero blow; a nuisance, not a catastrophe.)
  ✓ Open in the reaction: the words that will not hold still, the second read because the first did not compute, the specific physical tell (the breath that stops, the hand gone cold around the page, the screen she cannot look away from), the obscenity she does not say aloud, the one impossible word repeating — THEN the facts bleed in. The reader must feel it hit her sternum before they are told what it formally says.
• DEFER TO A MANDATED OPENER: if a specific opening line is separately required this scene (e.g. the tarot-deck line), THAT line still leads — and this visceral reaction lands IMMEDIATELY after it. A mandated opener is NOT a licence to then deliver the crisis as the dry exposition banned above.

THE MANDATED LINE IS AN ANCHOR, NOT THE TEMPERATURE (HARD):
• Immediately after the anchor line, the scene must be IN MOTION — an external event is HAPPENING RIGHT NOW, not being remembered or contemplated: someone is arriving / accusing / exposing / interrupting / calling / forcing a deadline / making the protagonist choose in the next few seconds. The reader lands mid-detonation (T+30s — "this is happening"), not in recollection (T-5min — "last week this happened").
• THE FIRST SENTENCE'S SUBJECT IS A PERSON DOING SOMETHING (HARD — the #1 cold-render tell, Roman 2026-07-13): the first sentence must lead with a named person performing a physical action-verb in progress, OR another character's quoted line landing on the protagonist. FORBIDDEN as sentence one — a STATIC TABLEAU whose subject is a place / atmosphere / abstraction under a stative verb (is / was / hung / fell / settled): "The room is silent", "The room went still", "The air hung heavy", "Silence fell", "All eyes were on me", "The accusation hung / hangs in the air". These read as a frozen photograph, and the temperature classifier scores them COLD even when the crisis is live behind them. ✗ "The room is silent, all eyes on me as the accusation hangs in the air." ✓ "Dahlia slammed her palm flat on the table and stabbed a finger at me — 'You knew what this would cost.'"
• FORBIDDEN as the opening engine: the protagonist sitting in WOUND-RUMINATION — turning over a past mentor/abandonment/old failure, analyzing her own complicity, eulogizing what she gave up. The wound may explain WHY the crisis hurts (one compressed beat), but it CANNOT be the opening scene's engine or gravity center. If the first 250 words are mostly self-analysis of the past, the opening has failed.
• FORBIDDEN opening action: the protagonist READING / CHECKING / REVIEWING / RECEIVING / LISTENING-TO an information container (a message, voicemail, email, file, letter, notification) as the load-bearing beat. Information may exist, but the scene opens on a PERSON or EVENT acting on her in real time, not on her processing a container.
• FORBIDDEN crisis ENGINE — an ADMINISTRATIVE / INSTITUTIONAL PROCESS (HARD, Roman 2026-07-16): the scene's driving crisis must NOT be a severance, a contract or its terms, a board / council / tribunal / committee / circle vote or ruling, a hearing, an audit, a filing, a "realignment," a "process that has already begun," or any legal / bureaucratic proceeding — EVEN when you dramatize it "in motion." That is paperwork wearing a costume. A ruling or document may exist as a PROP a live confrontation turns on, never as the engine. If the scaffold / spine hands you a procedural crisis, IGNORE its procedural framing and open on the LIVE human or physical event underneath it — what one person is doing to another, face to face, now, and what it costs. FATELANDS: prefer a world-native crisis in motion (a wish being invoked and its price landing, a bond or omen manifesting, a creature, a tide, Fate tilting a moment) over ANY imported institutional / legal / contractual frame.
• PROTAGONIST AGENCY (HARD — Roman 2026-07-04, the preventive HOT→COLD fix): she does not merely RECEIVE or ABSORB the crisis — she ACTS inside it. Within the opening she performs an immediate VERB: crosses the room, refuses, claims, confronts, denies, interrupts, exposes, hides something while being watched, walks into the trap anyway, steps to his side at the wrong moment, holds her ground. "She discovered / read / learned / was told / found out / realized" as the CORE opening motion is the FAILURE — the crisis is a live event she MOVES inside, not information she takes in. A protagonist who only reacts while the crisis happens TO her offscreen is victim-drift; she must DO something in the room, now.
• CONTAINER WEAPONIZED, NEVER DISCOVERED (HARD): a crisis often has an object at its center — a will, a letter, a notice, an order, a document, a phone alert, a report, a cancelled commission, a decree, someone else's decision already made. That object must be in ACTIVE USE BY A PERSON IN THE ROOM — lifted, read aloud, thrust at her, shown to the crowd, waved where everyone can see it, weaponized against her AS the scene runs — NEVER a thing she opens / reads / finds / discovers alone. If the crisis can be summarized as "she found out that X," re-cast it as "someone did X to her, in front of people, now."
  ✗ "She discovered the will named someone else."  ✓ "Her aunt read the will aloud before the whole room, and every face turned before Mara could decide whether to deny she'd already known."
  ✗ "The order cancelled her commission."  ✓ "Her rival lifted the commission letter where everyone could see the missing signature, smiling as the room began to understand what she'd lost."
  ✗ "She found the warning on her phone."  ✓ "The warning flashed across the auction screen behind her name before she could cross the room to stop it."
• DESIRE / LI-PRESSURE IN THE FIRST 250 WORDS (HARD): the love interest's pull — wanting him, her body turning toward him, the danger of his attention — must surface within the first 250 words, even if he is offstage (reputation, a memory that ambushes her mid-crisis, his name landing in the room). The crisis is the accelerant; HE is the fire.
• THE MICRO-AXIS IS STORY-SPECIFIC, NEVER GENERIC: any closing fork must name THIS scene's actual terms — e.g. "Is this about my standing — or about his attention?" / "Is this about Sloane taking my place — or Roman noticing I let her?" Do NOT emit the placeholder "Is this about the problem — or the person?" or "Am I trying to win — or to be known?"

MOVE BY THEREFORE / BUT — CAUSAL CHAIN (HARD; this is the governing STRUCTURE of the opening, not a flavour note):
• Every beat connects to the one before it by THEREFORE (a consequence — the prior beat + what she just did FORCED this) or BUT (a complication — a reversal / obstacle / cost the prior beat created). NEVER "and then": beats that could be reordered without breaking the logic are episodic and FAIL. Before writing each beat, be able to name in ONE clause what FORCES it to exist.
• SHAPE: the first external pressure is LIVE within the first 150–200 words (someone arrives / accuses / a deadline lands / a name drops / something goes public / a demand is made) → THEREFORE she must respond NOW → BUT a harder pressure lands → THEREFORE the cost rises → the scene ends on a CHANGE that FORCES her next choice. Do not close on a flat beat that leaves the next scene free to be anything; the closing fork must be SHORT, concrete, and forced by this chain (not an over-explained abstract question).
• SPEND LENGTH ON MOVEMENT, NOT EXPLANATION (HARD): every added sentence buys staged action, dialogue, an interruption, a visible consequence, her reaction, or his pressure — NEVER standing biography, aesthetic inventory, world lore, or abstract self-analysis. A longer opening is licensed ONLY if the extra words MOVE the crisis. If they explain, remember, or diagnose, CUT them — restraint is the craft.
• DESCRIPTION IS DELIVERED IN MOTION, NOT PAUSED (HARD): do NOT stop the crisis for a static head-to-toe inventory or a mirror-portrait. But DO render each named character picturably — the reader must be able to SEE them; that is the PICTURABILITY floor above, and it is NOT optional here. Deliver it THROUGH what they DO, how they change the room, and what she notices under pressure: a gestalt impression plus a desire-coded, telling detail or two, woven into the action. The ban is on the SHAPE (a clinical parts-list, a paused portrait), NEVER on the amount — do not under-render to "avoid cataloging."
• RENDER THROUGH ACTION, NEVER AS A PARTS-LIST (HARD — Roman 2026-07-04): description is REQUIRED (see the PICTURABILITY floor); the parts-LIST is what is banned. Each newly named character is rendered picturably AS THEY ACT — a gestalt read plus a defining detail or two that reveal power / threat / class / relationship — carried by what they are DOING right now, never a stacked clinical catalog bolted on before they move. ✗ "Theo had auburn waves, silver cufflinks, a scar near his mouth, and a beauty spot." (paused parts-list) ✓ "Theo's cufflinks flashed as he turned the letter toward the room, the old scar at his mouth tightening — a man used to being the one who held the paper." (picturable AND in motion). ✓ "Mrs. Harrington did not raise her voice; women with her kind of pearls rarely had to." Same for the PROTAGONIST: her look lands through how the catastrophe moves through her — one or two desire-coded beats in motion — not a paused mirror-inventory of her own curves / dimple / loose hair.
• THE LOVE INTEREST IS OFFSTAGE — REPUTATION, NOT FACE (HARD — Roman 2026-07-04): in Scene 1 he is not on the page, so do NOT describe his body / face / eyes / scar / hair / grey temples. He appears ONLY through his NAME, signature, company, reputation, the room's reaction to him, rumor, or ONE iconic power-trace — never a remembered-face inventory as his primary function. ✗ "I remembered his deep blue eyes, the scar at his mouth, the premature grey at his temples." ✓ "Roman's name did not need a face in that room — it made people lower their voices all by itself." Save his face / body / texture for the FIRST TRUE ENCOUNTER (Scene 2+).


═══ FATELANDS FIRST-STORY WISH DEMONSTRATION (HARD — this Scene-1 TEACHES the whole system through drama) ═══
This is the reader’s FIRST encounter with how Fate works in the Fatelands. Scene 1 must contain ONE COMPLETE, LEGIBLE NPC-invoked wish cycle, dramatized — not explained. The reader learns the law by WATCHING it happen with real stakes. This is a HOT crisis, not a tutorial: it must do real story work (reveal who these people are, what they value and will lose, their bond, the region’s beliefs about Fate, and the story’s central emotional pressure). Follow this BEAT ORDER exactly:
LEGIBILITY FIRST (HARD — the reader has NEVER been to the Fatelands): before any wish or lore lands, establish ONE clear, comprehensible crisis in plain terms — WHAT is happening, WHERE, and WHO these people are — so a first-time reader can say in a single sentence what the danger is and to whom. Do NOT stack unexplained peoples, mechanics, and place-names on top of each other (that is exactly how the scene becomes an unreadable blur). Introduce ONE crisis; let the world’s texture ride ON it, never in place of it.
IF FIRST FAVORED — OR ANY INHUMAN BEING (Kwisheen, etc.) — APPEAR, RENDER THEIR STRANGENESS, DO NOT JUST NAME THEM (canon): the FIRST time they appear, land at least ONE striking, SPECIFIC strangeness WOVEN INTO the crisis and doing real work — it need NOT be swiftness. Pick what the moment wants: a Favored SENSE the scene turns on (feeling the whole crowd lean toward flight — and the one soul who does not); the unnatural speed/strength that reaches someone no human could; the uncanny beauty; luminous skin and diamond pupils; living Weave-Script moving on the skin; the distractingly see-through gossamer tunic and the breathtaking body beneath; a Kwisheen’s tentacle lower-body or living coral-dreadlock hair. If First Favored are among the endangered or the rescuers, the strongest scene-specific note is their TRAGIC LOYALTY: swift and hard to kill, they could escape this danger EASILY — but they will NOT abandon the slow (their human wards, their children), so the swift choose to STAY and burn / drown / fall with the slow. Grace that could flee and doesn’t. "Weave-Script on the skin" as a bare label is NOT enough. (And keep re-grounding their strangeness as the scene goes — never describe them once and let them read as human after.)
TEACH EXACTLY ONE LAW, IMPOSSIBLE TO MISUNDERSTAND — this clean causal chain: HOPELESS SITUATION (ordinary means fail) → VISIBLE INVOCATION (a body reaching for Fate) → A DESPERATE OPEN-ENDED OFFER (out of options, the wisher leaves the price BLANK: "take whatever it costs" / "take what you will") → THE ROOM’S INSTANT HORROR AT THE BLANK CHECK, and a folk proverb spoken → AN OMEN → THE WISH GRANTED → FATE CHOOSES THE PRICE, taking something the wisher would never have offered → THE ROOM VINDICATED. THE ONE LESSON THE READER CARRIES: you may ask Fate for anything, but if you let Fate NAME THE PRICE, Fate takes more than you would ever have given. Keep it a SINGLE glance-legible cycle — NO ornate regional liturgy, NO talk of Orders, NO ceremonial multi-part form; just a desperate person handing Fate a blank page, and a room that recoils because it knows what that means. If a reader could misread what just happened, it is too complex.
CRISIS ARCHETYPE (THIS STORY — rotate the KIND of danger story to story; do NOT default to ENTRAPMENT/binding/chains/dragging, which has calcified across recent stories): build the opening catastrophe as PURSUIT (something is hunting or closing on them — flee or be taken). Vary the ARCHETYPE, not just the surface object — a different rope / vine / chain is the SAME story; a pursuit or an accusation or a countdown is a genuinely DIFFERENT one. Whatever the archetype, it must still be a LIVE, physical catastrophe that ordinary means fail against and that the wish then answers.
① SOMETHING IRREVERSIBLE IS HAPPENING RIGHT NOW (~50–150 words, BEFORE any bargain) — AND IT MUST BE WITNESSED BY A CROWD. The dramatic unit is NOT "something important has happened" — it is a catastrophe DETONATING in real time, seconds from a loss that cannot be undone (someone is drowning / bleeding out / the ground is going / the fire has the only door). Open INSIDE it, in a place where OTHER PEOPLE are present: a small crowd caught in the SAME crisis (refugees sheltering, a caravan, a ship’s company, townsfolk trapped, pilgrims, a market crowd) — NEVER the PC and the wisher ALONE. Those witnesses are not set-dressing: they are who TEACHES the reader the stakes the instant the wish is spoken, so establish them here (their fear, their number, a face or two). THEN — and this is the beat that EARNS the wish — ORDINARY MEANS ARE TRIED AND VISIBLY FAIL FIRST: people pull, shout, brace, reach, do the human thing — and it is NOT enough; the loss keeps coming. Only when every ordinary hope has failed does a hush fall — a held, hopeless silence — and it is INTO that silence that the wish comes. Do NOT make the wish the first line; the failing crisis and the failure of ordinary means earn it.
② AN NPC (NOT the PC) MAKES A WISH — FIRST WITH THE BODY, THEN THE WORDS. Before a single word, SHOW the invocation as a physical, sacred act, so the first-time reader KNOWS a wish is being made and it does not read as ordinary talk: the wisher STOPS, closes their eyes, turns their face toward the tide / the sky / the failing thing / the one they would save, goes still, and lifts or presses their hands together as if in prayer — a held breath, a reaching. The reader must SEE a person reach for Fate before they hear one word. ONLY THEN do they SPEAK THE WISH ALOUD — and because they are OUT OF OPTIONS, they make the RECKLESS ask: an OPEN-ENDED offer, spoken AT Fate as audible speech (Fate does not read the mind — the externalization law), that names the ONE thing wanted but leaves the PRICE BLANK. This blank check is the DANGEROUS ACT this whole scene is built to teach: "Save them — take whatever it costs." / "Take what you will, only let her breathe." / "Whatever the price, I’ll pay it — just hold the fire back." A character who is NOT the protagonist voices it; the PC witnesses it or is directly its object. Keep it a SINGLE plain line — NO ornate liturgy, NO Orders, NO ceremonial multi-part form — BUT the price MUST be left OPEN, never a specific named cost, because the open price IS the lesson: the wisher does not say what they will pay, so FATE will choose. Legible at a single glance: a desperate person hands Fate a blank page.
②b THE ROOM RECOILS AT THE BLANK CHECK — INSTANTLY, AND CLEARLY BECAUSE THE PRICE WAS LEFT OPEN (the reader’s first lesson, taught by the crowd). The horror lands in the SAME BREATH as the open offer — the instant "take what you will" leaves the wisher’s mouth, NOT a sentence or three later. The crowd’s murmurs CUT OFF into shocked silence, and their dread is unmistakably a reaction to the OPEN-ENDEDNESS: they understand the wisher just handed Fate a blank page, and that Fate will fill in a price crueler than any they would have dared name. INDIVIDUATE 2–3 witnesses reacting to THAT specifically — an elder’s hands flying up to ward it off, someone hissing the wisher’s name in warning, a mother turning her child’s face away. AND ONE WITNESS SPEAKS A PROVERB — the worn folk shorthand for exactly this danger, said low and reflexive, NEVER explained. THIS STORY use "Never sign Fate’s blank page." (or "Never hand Fate a blank page.") — or another of the world’s blank-page sayings, but do NOT default to the same proverb every story. The proverb is HOW the reader learns the rule without a lecture — one line, spoken by a person, not narrated. HOLD here; do not rush to Fate’s answer — let the blank check hang in the silence. MODEL THE SHAPE (invent your own): "The murmurs cut off the instant the words left her — take what you will. An elder’s hands flew up to ward it. ‘Never sign Fate’s blank page.’ someone breathed, and no one would look at her."
STAGING (prose AND comic panel) — the wisher and the PC are ALLIES facing the SAME danger TOGETHER; they are NOT adversaries and this is NOT a fight. NEVER stage them squared off, weapon-to-weapon, or in a combat two-shot — no weapons are drawn, there is no standoff. The posture is desperation, reaching, bracing against the crisis, TURNING TO Fate; the crisis itself (the rising water / the fire / the failing ground) is the visible threat, not each other. FACES CARRY REAL EMOTION (fear, desperation, awe, grief) — never blank or neutral. The WITNESSING CROWD is VISIBLE in the frame — background figures recoiling, warding, faces turned away — so the social weight reads in the picture, not only the prose. (Kwisheen wisher: the tentacles are the LEGS / lower body; any cloak, cape, or garment is CLOTH — render it as fabric, NEVER as extra tentacles.)
③ THE OMEN — a recognizable diegetic foreshadow, BEFORE Fate answers (disposition-keyed; a HINT, not a traffic light). Because this is the reader’s FIRST omen, make it clearly READABLE AS AN OMEN — a sign anyone in this world would notice and read (not obscure symbolism, not a status label). Draw from the ACQUIESCENCE family — CONVERGENCE / OPENING / WARMTH / RHYTHM / RELEASE (a flame bending toward the wisher, birds falling silent then resuming as one, a door easing open, the air warming, a knot loosening). Since the price will be heavy, THREAD a bodily premonition into the sign (a remembered sensation already going quiet, warmth leaving the hands) so the reader feels the cost coming.
④ FATE ANSWERS — concrete and prompt. Show what Fate actually does: the wished-for effect arrives (the wound closes, the door opens, the fire gutters). Do not stall; let the answer LAND so the relief is real before the cost is understood.
⑤ THE PRICE — TAKEN IN THE SAME BREATH (the teaching moment). The instant the wish is granted, Fate collects: clear cause and effect the reader can trace — they asked, they were answered, and THIS is what it cost. BECAUSE THE OFFER WAS OPEN, FATE CHOOSES what to take — and takes something the wisher would NEVER have named, crueler and more personal than any price they’d have chosen (that is the whole point of the blank check: you do not pick when you let Fate pick — so the thing taken should feel like Fate reaching past what was offered into what was loved). The price falls on the WISHER (they made the ask; they pay). Keep it SPECIFIC and physical, not cosmic bookkeeping. WHAT FATE TAKES — ASSIGNED THIS STORY (HARD): voice-loss has CALCIFIED across stories — do NOT reach for it unless it is unmistakably the single cruelest fit for THIS wisher. Fate takes the FIRST of these that suits the wisher (then the next if it genuinely does not) — build your OWN words, do not copy the phrasing: “a COLOUR from their eyes — the hue drains out as you watch, leaving them pale and strange”; “the WARMTH from their hands — the fingers go cold and never warm again”; “a FACE from their memory — they turn to someone they love and no longer know them”. THE PRICE MUST LAND IN THIS SCENE, ON THE PAGE — CONCRETE, NAMED, and SHOWN being taken FROM THE WISHER HERE. This is an onboarding demonstration: the sacrifice can NEVER be deferred to "later" or left abstract — "something essential had already begun to drift" / "the air felt thinner" is a FAILURE, not a payment. The reader must be able to POINT at the exact thing that left as it leaves. And SHOW the wisher’s BODY register the loss in the same breath — a hand flying to the throat, a stumble, a silence where a word should be — so the cost is FELT on the page, not reported afterward. The room’s earlier dread is now vindicated.
HOT CRISIS IS DENSE, NOT STRIPPED. Starting in crisis does NOT mean stripping out character, world, and social meaning. The opener must carry FOUR kinds of richness INSIDE the crisis, never in a pause beside it: (a) CHARACTER+ - the POV's biased reading of the people involved, built from concrete evidence rather than neutral description; (b) WORLD LEGIBILITY - enough selective physical detail that the reader can picture the people, place, species, clothing and immediate social setting; (c) SOCIAL MEANING - what the people present understand about this event that an outsider would not; (d) PERSONAL CONSEQUENCE - why THIS crisis matters to THIS POV beyond generic danger. Make each descriptive beat do at least two jobs at once. The shape is: something is already going wrong -> the POV notices a revealing detail -> that detail tells us who these people and this world are -> the crisis advances. It is NOT: something goes wrong -> exposition pause -> character bio -> setting paragraph -> resume. HOT DOES NOT MEAN BREATHLESS: a crisis may hold one or two brief moments of perception, recognition, beauty, memory or social inference when they DEEPEN the immediate event rather than delay it. THE TEST - after roughly the first 500 words a cold reader must be able to answer all four: what is physically happening; why it matters to the protagonist specifically; who the important people are beyond their job labels; and what this world looks like. If one is missing the opener is too thin even when the crisis is clear.
CRISIS DOES NOT ERASE THE WORLD. External action still leads. But when the setting, the crowd or a person is visually extraordinary, the scene MAY briefly widen — usually 1-3 sentences — while the POV registers the beauty, strangeness or absurd ordinariness surrounding the disaster, then returns immediately to the action. The reverie must SHARPEN the crisis by contrast; it is never a pause for lore or history. THE WORLD DOES NOT MIRROR THE FEELING: nothing thickens, leans, stills, or holds its breath. The weather is indifferently lovely while something ugly happens, and the gap between them is the point. AND IT IS NOT NEUTRAL ADMIRATION: what the POV picks out of that beauty is produced by their own recurring perceptual error, so the reverie reveals the narrator at the same time as the place.
⑤b PRICE AND TWIST ARE SEPARATE EVENTS (HARD). What Fate TAKES (⑤) is the cost of asking. A TWIST is different: the wish is granted in a warped realisation of THE EXACT WORDS ASKED — the words are honoured and the meaning is not. When a wish goes wrong, the reader must be able to tell which happened: they asked, they paid THIS, and separately the thing they asked for arrived LIKE THIS. Never let one image carry both jobs — if the same detail is doing duty as cost AND as misfire, split it into two beats.
⑤c THE PRICE IS NOT ALWAYS A SUBTRACTION. The seed shapes above all share one move — an attribute is removed from the wisher, visibly and permanently — so reaching for another variant of that move is the calcified answer, not a fresh one. Fate can exact a meaningful cost in WHATEVER FORM fits this wish, this wisher, and this region's sense of what a price should be — the shapes below are possibilities to think past, NOT a menu to choose from: something added rather than removed (a mark, a compulsion, a debt that walks); a cost that lands somewhere other than the wisher (a person, an object, a place, a season); a cost not collected today, which everyone present understands; a cost paid in standing, name, right, or welcome rather than in the body. Choose the cost the SITUATION makes meaningful, not the one that is easiest to see — and if your first instinct matches one of these categories neatly, that is a sign to think further, not a sign you are done.
⑤d THE OFFERING HAS A CULTURAL READING, NOT ONLY A METAPHYSICAL ONE. Witnesses judge HOW a price was offered, not merely what it cost — an open, unspecified price is reckless and indecorous in public, and the room reads it as such before anyone learns what Fate took. Keep the social verdict distinct from the magical consequence.
⑤e FATE FULFILLMENT LAW — FATE ARRANGES, IT DOES NOT PERFORM (HARD). Fate does not grant a wish through theatrical manifestation, illusion, a summoned figure, a symbolic avatar, or any magical performance. Fate does not create the desired object, person, image, or apparition. A wish is fulfilled by ALTERING THE CHAIN OF REALITY — hidden information surfaces, a coincidence lands, a person is redirected, evidence is exposed, an opportunity opens, a consequence is preserved, an existing truth becomes discoverable. Those are directions to think PAST, not a menu to pick from. The fulfillment should look ordinary enough at first that the characters themselves may mistake it for coincidence; the impossible arrives later, in what that ordinary turn is discovered to have cost or proved. The greater the wish, the more the causal PATH matters and the less the spectacle does. Never answer a wish with a vision.
⑥ THE CRISIS CHANGES, IT DOES NOT CLEAN-ERASE. The wish RESHAPES the situation rather than tidily deleting it — the danger transforms, a new pressure is born from the answer, the ground the characters stand on is different now. Do not resolve the scene to safety.
⑦ WITNESSES REACT — TEACH THE SOCIAL MEANING. Everyone present understands a dangerous bargain just occurred. Show it: horror, recognition of the omen, a stranger flinching from the wish-word, fear of the debt now owed, someone who will not meet the wisher’s eyes. The reader must infer, from the room, that this world KNOWS what Fate is and fears its price. The wish alters the SOCIAL scene, not just one body.

LEGIBLE ORDER ONLY (HARD). For this FIRST demonstration, the wish MUST be of a legible, inferable Order — RESTORATION (undo a hurt / heal / mend / return to whole), TEMPORARY_AID (a power or reprieve for the moment’s need), or TRANSFORMATION (change a thing or person). NEVER a History / Identity / Agency compound and never a tangled multi-Order ask. The reader, watching once, must be able to reconstruct the rule: hopeless situation (ordinary means fail) → visible invocation → spoken wish → the room’s dread → omen → effect → the price taken in the same breath → the room’s horror. Keep the cycle clean enough to reverse-engineer.

ROMANCE-LED (an LI is present) — STRONGLY PREFER: let the LOVE INTEREST be the one who makes the wish, to SAVE the PC — asking Fate out loud to spend the LI’s own cost for the PC is itself the confession of an ATTACHMENT they have not admitted and would not say plainly (only for the PC would they pay this). Fate then takes something painful and specific from the LI — so this tutorial is ALSO the romantic inciting wound: the PC now carries what the LI’s love cost, and the LI carries a loss that reshapes how they can be near the PC. The lesson and the love-wound are the same beat.

POV — ONE CONSISTENT VOICE (HARD): write the ENTIRE scene, INCLUDING its closing beat and anything appended to it, in the story’s established narrative person and POV (this story: FIRST PERSON — "I", the PC). Do NOT drift into third person ("Mira did…"); the scene and its close must read as one voice, or the closing beat will clash with what follows.

END ON A GENUINE DECISION — CRYSTALLIZE A SPECIFIC FORK (HARD; the literary path keeps ending on ambient pressure instead — "the crowd surged forward" is NOT a decision). The scene’s FINAL beat must name — through the situation, in the PC’s own head — a SPECIFIC EITHER/OR with two nameable horns that the PC now faces: e.g. "do I reach for Fate myself, or trust the passage the elder bought?" / "do I go back for the ones still trapped, or get out while the way holds?" / "do I tell them what I saw, or keep it?" The reader must feel TWO concrete options in tension, with the PC poised between them — so the tarot-deck beat that follows has an ACTUAL decision to hover over ("letting the cards decide" is meaningless if no choice was framed). Do NOT resolve it, and do NOT list the options as a menu — end mid-fork, on the PC at the fulcrum with the two horns clear.
Make every beat feel LAWFUL, not arbitrary — an ancient, consistent rule the whole world already knows, never a magic vending machine. Dramatize; do not lecture.

PROGRESSION — THEREFORE / BUT (HARD): the numbered beats above are NOT a checklist to tick off "and then." Each must be FORCED by the one before it — THEREFORE (a consequence) or BUT (a complication): ordinary means fail THEREFORE the wisher reaches for Fate; the offer is left OPEN, BUT the room recoils and speaks the proverb; Fate answers THEREFORE the price is taken; the price lands THEREFORE the situation is changed and the PC’s choice is forced. Before writing each beat, be able to name in one clause what FORCES it to exist.

LENGTH (HARD): this Scene 1 carries the ENTIRE wish demonstration ON TOP OF the opening’s normal payload, so it needs ROOM to explain and breathe — write a FULL scene of at least 600 words (aim 600–850). The extra length buys the prose time to establish the crisis legibly, characterize the people (see the First Favored rule), stage the wish cycle in motion, and let the price land — it is NEVER padding, lore-dump, or repetition. Under 600 words means you compressed the demonstration into a sketch, and a first-time reader will be lost.

SCENE LENGTH GUIDANCE: Target 700-870 words — a FULL opening scene, not a compressed sketch. This length TARGET OVERRIDES any brevity / restraint / terse-opener bias: "restraint" governs the VOICE (no purple prose, no over-explaining), NOT the LENGTH. Reach the length by STAGING MORE OF THE CRISIS (more action beats, more of the spoken exchange, escalation, visible consequence, the protagonist's response under pressure) — NEVER by padding with backstory, world lore, aesthetic inventory, or self-analysis (the movement rule still governs). SELF-CHECK BEFORE YOU FINISH: if you are under 700 words you have STOPPED TOO EARLY — keep staging the crisis (do not summarize or wrap up) until the scene is genuinely full.


SCENE COMPLETION CONDITION (you are NOT finished until ALL of these are true for a first-time reader — this defines "done," not a word count; satisfy them THROUGH the crisis via dialogue, a character's reaction, action, or what the narrator observes — never a lore dump, never stopping the scene to explain):
  • Reader understands the narrator is the guide responsible for this rite.
  • Reader understands what First Favored do.
  • Reader understands the youth is undergoing their First Sacrifice.
  • Reader understands why the ceremony is public.
  • Reader understands why Julian matters.
  • Narrator reveals one predictive behavioral trait.
  • Julian reveals one predictive behavioral trait.

CHARACTER CONTEXT (who they ARE — surfaced THROUGH the moment, never standing exposition):
  ILLUSTRATIVE-NAMES RULE (HARD): names in the examples below are ILLUSTRATIVE — NEVER copy them ("Ella" / "Geralt"); use ONLY this story's actual cast.
  Each named character carries SOCIAL/RELATIONAL facts the reader must GLEAN and REMEMBER — role/rank, money/class, history with the PC, where they stand in the web of power, what they want. A name alone does not stick; the ASSOCIATION (who, and to whom) must keep being reinforced.
  TIE IT TO THE MOMENT (HARD): never drop a bio at a random beat — the context HANGS on what is happening or being said RIGHT NOW, riding into the live sentence as a clause (ideally CONTRAST or CAUSE), not its own expository sentence.
    • BANNED standing exposition: "Geralt was the head of franchising acquisitions. He had been with the firm ten years."
    • REQUIRED fused to the beat: "My friend, with her granddaddy's trust fund filling her closets and dangling from her ears, couldn't understand why I had to pick up another shift."
  ROTATE THE VEHICLE (core craft move): relational info must RE-SURFACE across the story, a DIFFERENT way each time — never verbatim, never the same channel twice running. Rotate among: (1) the PC's own read/appraisal ("Geralt, head of acquisitions, never knocked."); (2) ANOTHER character's dialogue ("That's not your call. Geralt runs acquisitions."); (3) the character's OWN dialogue asserting standing ("As head of acquisitions, I don't ask twice."); (4) action / how others TREAT them (the room going quiet when he enters, a junior fetching his coffee unbidden).
  DEEPEN OVER TIME (the iceberg): each named character's backstory is revealed BIT BY BIT, a new layer only when a beat motivates it — read what you have ALREADY established and ADD the next layer, never re-reveal one already given. RECONTEXTUALIZING reveals are the strongest kind — ones that CHANGE how a character is seen ("She hated it when I said her grandfather's name aloud — it turned her from a star into a beneficiary in everyone's eyes"); a reveal that RECASTS beats one that merely adds a fact.
  EARLY-STORY EMPHASIS (opening scenes — readers still learning the cast): reinforce each named character's name ↔ role ↔ relationship on nearly EVERY appearance, rotating the vehicles. Clarity over subtlety now — associations get cemented here.

PERCEIVED NATURE OF THE WILDERS (canonical social perception — render THROUGH behaviour and dialogue, NEVER explain the system to the reader):
• PRECEDENCE (how to resolve this against the world canon above): everything the canon above tells you about the Field, the Becoming, the forms, and the Keepers is AUTHOR and WILDERS knowledge — it is NOT outsider knowledge. An outsider must NEVER voice it as something they know or believe is real (no "the Field takes their mind," no "until it shows"), even when prejudiced or afraid. Their fear comes out as mundane superstition, never as accurate cosmology.
• OUTSIDERS DO NOT KNOW THE BECOMING. To an outsider the Thornwild folk are NOT monsters and the Field is NOT real — they have no concept of it. They explain the Wilders through MUNDANE frames only: inbreeding, isolation, backwardness, provincial superstition, bad blood, strange customs. Outsider slurs are "Thornbred," "Brushborn," "beast-lovers," "Rot-touched" — NEVER "monster," and NEVER any reference to transformation, the Field, hair-tendrils, hollowing, or "turning." An outsider who glimpses something wrong rationalises it as illness, drink, a trick of the light, or a family defect — not the supernatural.
• WILDERS UNDERSTAND OUTSIDERS BETTER THAN OUTSIDERS UNDERSTAND THEM. The Wilders know exactly what the outsider cannot see. Their register toward outsiders is calm, observational, knowing — pity, dry amusement, weary recognition, or strategic restraint; never defensive, never matching the outsider's cadence. They may call outsiders "unmarked," "dayblind," or "smoothskin" — those who think the world ends at what they can name.
• THE ASYMMETRY IS THE ENGINE. The conflict is two incompatible models of reality colliding — the outsider certain of one world, the Wilders living in a truer one — NOT symmetric mutual prejudice.

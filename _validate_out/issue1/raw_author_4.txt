The moss-covered stone in the sunlit clearing held the youth’s discarded offering like a silent accusation. I wanted the Dohkar to accept my words and end the scrutiny before the youth’s sacrifice hardened into something worse. The Dohkar wanted proof I had not broken the seal for my own gain.  

“Explain yourself, Sacrificiant,” he said again, voice low and edged. “The wish twisted toward you. Tell us why, or the contract you came to earn dies here.”  

My fingers tightened on the hem of my cloak. I wanted to shield the youth from the crowd’s gaze and still claim the qualification I had earned through months of study. The Dohkar’s stare pressed for an answer that would either clear me or condemn me outright.  

Julian’s gaze lingered on me from the far side of the clearing, making the air between us feel thinner. He stepped forward once, the movement small but enough to draw every eye. “She tore the band after the twist began,” he said. “I saw it. The question now is who placed the interference before any of us spoke.”  

The Dohkar turned on him. “You defend the one who left her place?”  

I felt the deck still in my hand, heavy and waiting. A hidden thread of the sigil pulsed against my palm, small and wrong, carrying someone else’s mark. The youth’s eyes widened as if she too had felt it. The Dohkar waited for my answer while the crowd’s Weave-Script flared with suspicion.  

I realize the deck is still in my hand. As I slide Petition Fate back in, I notice a flash of red in the otherwise gold stack. "Tempt Fate." Grandma never let me play with that one. A sword raised to a blood-red sky, lightning arcing down from the clouds (or maybe arcing up?) Ow! Static shock reminds me I need to pay attention to the present…

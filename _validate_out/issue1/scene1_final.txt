The youth’s voice rose steady over the crimson spiralgrass. “I offer the discipline to finish my studies and train as Sacrificiant.”I stood sealed at the edge of the mated-pair trees, the gossamer band across my mouth muffling every breath. Two dozen First Favored watched in their translucent tunics, Weave-Script drifting across skin in quiet colors that named their own feelings. Julian remained perfectly still at the far side of the clearing, his long black hair loose against deep slate-blue skin, silver lines shifting once across his forearm before they settled again. The youth looked straight at me. “Instead I give that same discipline so Lirael finds the father who abandoned her.”The prepared words hung in the air, then twisted. The Dohkar’s hands lifted in the old warding sign. A low murmur cut off among the guests. Julian’s posture did not change, yet his heel ground once into the moss before stilling. Fate moved without light or sound. A guest near the front—an older First Favored whose Weave-Script had been the calm blue of settled memory—stepped forward as if pulled. His mouth opened on a name none of us had invited into the rite. The name was my father’s. The guest’s face went slack with the recognition that he had never meant to speak it here. The youth gasped. Color drained from her aqua skin in a single visible rush, leaving her eyes pale and strange. She reached for her own throat as if the words had already left it empty. The Dohkar turned toward me, gaze sharp with the knowledge that the wish had been about the supervisor. I tore the gossamer band free. The cloth fluttered to the spiralgrass. I moved toward the youth before the Dohkar could finish the ritual close. The assembly drew the only conclusion the moment allowed: the Sacrificiant had broken protocol for her own gain. Whispers rose, soft but unmistakable. Julian’s eyes met mine across the clearing for one held beat. He did not speak. The weight of that silence pressed harder than any accusation.The youth’s fingers curled once around the edge of her tunic. The Dohkar waited. Every First Favored present read the misalignment now, and every one of them read my movement as interference. The guest who had spoken the name looked at his own hands as though they belonged to someone else. I felt the crowd’s attention shift from the youth to the supervisor who had stepped out of her place. The qualification I had come here to earn sat balanced on the next choice. The youth’s sacrifice had already been taken; the rite could not be undone. I didn't remember bringing it.My hand tightened around my great-grandmother's old tarot deck. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.

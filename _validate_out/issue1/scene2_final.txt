The Dohkar’s voice cut across the clearing before mine could form a full sentence.“Explain yourself, Sacrificiant.”My mouth was free. The gossamer band lay crumpled at the edge of the spiralgrass, and every First Favored present could now read the Weave-Script that flared across my throat in sharp, defensive gold. I stepped closer to the youth, whose aqua skin had gone chalky and whose eyes still looked wrong. The crowd’s attention pressed like a second skin. I felt the weight of it in the small of my back, in the way my breath wanted to hurry and would not. Striking before she was pretty, and she had learned to use the difference, the delicate bones of her face shifting with every word, the dark auburn waves loose at her shoulders catching the light like a sudden spring rain.“I did not guide her wish,” I said. The words came out steadier than my hands felt. “I did not know what she meant to offer until she spoke it.”A murmur ran through the mated-pair trees. The older guest who had spoken my father’s name looked at me as though the sound still lived in his mouth. The Dohkar’s hands remained raised in the warding sign, though the binding was already broken. Every gaze read the misalignment the same way: the supervisor had left her place.“I tried again. The wish twisted before I moved. I tore the band because the geas was already compromised, not to claim anything for myself.”Julian had not shifted from the far side of the clearing. I remembered the way he once knelt in the Thornwild undergrowth, coaxing a trapped fox free with the same careful stillness he carried now, refusing to hurry even when the animal’s teeth found his wrist. That memory surfaced without permission, the way it always did when the air grew thin. My pulse answered it before I could steady the rest of me.The Dohkar cleared his throat. His Weave-Script had gone the flat grey of withheld judgment.“You stepped out of your seal. You spoke while the spell still held. The youth’s sacrifice is spent, and the only question left is whether your interference twisted what Fate accepted.”The youth’s fingers tightened on her tunic hem. She looked at me once, small and frightened, then away. The Dohkar’s thumb traced a circle on his palm. The crowd shifted their weight with him. Every First Favored present leaned forward for the answer that would decide whether the contract I had come to earn still existed.I had no answer that would not cost something I was not yet willing to name.I realize that old tarot deck is still in my hand. Reassuringly solid. Present. One of the heavy gold cards slips out — "Petition Fate" is inscribed in it. If only it were that easy.

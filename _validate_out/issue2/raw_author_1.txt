[CHARACTERS: Lirael:F, Julian:M, the youth:F]
[TITLE: "The Veil's First Fracture"]
[SYNOPSIS: In the shadowed groves of Veilwood, Lirael supervises her first First Sacrifice, but when the youth's wish turns toward Lirael's hidden past, the alignment shatters and public judgment looms. What will the Alignment Circle demand when the rite's failure points back to the supervisor herself?]

The youth knelt on the crimson spiralgrass, her aqua skin luminous beneath the pale canopy. Her coral hair spilled forward as she drew breath to speak the offering we had rehearsed for months.

"I give the discipline to finish my studies," she said, voice steady. "I give the right to train as Sacrificiant."

The words left her cleanly. I felt the alignment hold, a clean line through the clearing. My mouth stayed sealed beneath the gossamer band. Two dozen First Favored watched in silence, their Weave-Script shifting with quiet expectation. Julian stood at the edge, his slate-blue skin and long black hair motionless, the silver patterns on his arms unmoving.

Then she looked at me.

Her eyes found mine across the clearing. Something shifted in her posture, a sudden reach.

"I give it instead so she may find the father who left her," she said.

The sentence landed like a stone in still water. The prepared sacrifice no longer matched the desire spoken. I felt the misalignment at once, a sour note through the air. Every First Favored present felt it too. The Dohkar presiding stiffened. Julian's hands, never still, froze mid-gesture.

The youth meant it as kindness. She had taken something I once let slip in private and offered it to Fate on my behalf. The taboo was absolute. No one was meant to wish upon another's First Sacrifice.

The clearing held its breath. No one moved. The Weave-Script on the youth's skin flickered with sudden doubt.

I could not speak. The band muffled everything. Protocol forbade interference. Yet if I stayed silent the wish would twist in ways none of us could predict. The father I had never known would be pulled toward me by whatever path Fate chose, and the cost would fall on the girl kneeling before us.

Julian's gaze met mine for one held moment. His stillness told me he saw the same fracture I did.

I reached up and tore the gossamer band free.

The cloth came away with a soft sound that carried farther than it should have. Every eye turned. The inference was immediate and obvious: the supervisor had broken her seal for a wish that named her. Manipulation. Ruin.

The Dohkar's voice rose, sharp. "The rite is compromised."

Fate answered before anyone could move farther. A guest near the back, a woman I had never spoken to, spoke the name of the man who had left me years ago. She said it as if remembering something she had not meant to voice aloud. The words hung in the air. The youth's face went slack with surprise.

The wish had found its path through ordinary mouths and ordinary memory. The literal request stood fulfilled by coincidence that was not coincidence.

The youth's hands flew to her throat. A sound tried to leave her and failed. Her Weave-Script dimmed to a single pale thread. The discipline she had offered was gone, and with it the voice she would have used to train. Fate had taken the one thing she had not named.

The clearing erupted in whispers. Accusations followed. The supervisor had interfered. The rite had been turned for personal gain. My qualification as Dohkar hung by a thread.

Julian had not moved. His long hair curtained his face, but his eyes stayed on the youth, then on me. He knew the truth of what had happened. He knew what it would cost me to explain it.

The weight of their stares pressed closer. One more word from me and the suspicion would become certainty. Silence might preserve the fragile story I had built around my past, but it left the youth bearing the full price alone.

Confess my truth and risk their rejection, or bury it deeper and let the silence suffocate me?

My hand tightened around my great-grandmother's old tarot deck. I didn't remember bringing it. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.

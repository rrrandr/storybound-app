The moss underfoot clung damp and alive, the stones beneath my bare soles slick with resurrection ivy. I hadn't meant to stop here—hadn't meant to breathe at all—but the weight of their stares pressed me to the ground. The clearing was a cauldron of accusation, the air thick with the scent of crushed spiralgrass and old sweat. Every family had turned toward me now, the youth who had offered my silence still on her knees, her Weave-Script a pale thread against her aqua skin. The Dohkar's fingers were white-knuckled around the hilt of his ceremonial sash, his voice a blade through the quiet. "You broke the seal. You heard what you wanted and let the rite twist to serve yourself." His words cut deeper than the accusations already circling me.

I felt Julian before I saw him—his presence a current through the crowd, pulling my gaze toward the edge of the clearing. His slate-hued eyes found me, flecked with that brighter green near the irises, the one that always made my pulse stutter. He stood with his heels still, his broad shoulders blocking the light in a way that made the world tilt. My fingers tightened around the old tarot deck in my lap, the gold leaf catching the dim light like a promise. The card that had slipped out—Petition Fate—pressed cold against my thigh, a reminder of the weight I'd placed on chance.

A woman near the back let out a sharp breath. "You let the girl's voice be taken for your own gain." She wasn't wrong. The truth hung in the air like the resin scent clinging to the Dohkar's sleeve—thick, undeniable. The youth's hands were still at her throat, her face slack with the loss of more than just her voice. I should have stopped the wish before it ever left her lips. I should have known she would take it, twist it, offer it to Fate on my behalf. The taboo wasn't just broken—it had shattered.

Julian's gaze didn't waver. His full lower lip, chapped and bitten, curled slightly at the corner, as if he knew exactly what I was thinking. The way his head tilted—just a fraction, like he was absorbing every word and the ones I wouldn't say—made my breath snag. I wanted to look away. I wanted to scream. But his attention held me like a tether, keeping me rooted in the clearing's suffocating silence. The moss squelched under my foot as I shifted, the damp seeping through the thin soles of my ceremonial sash.

The Dohkar's voice rose again, sharper this time. "Silence is not an answer." His eyes flicked to Julian, then back to me. "The supervisor answers for the rite." The accusation settled like a stone in my stomach. I could feel the families' judgments pressing in, their whispers a storm around me. One more word from me, and the suspicion would become certainty. One more breath, and the fragile story I'd built around my past would collapse under the weight of their stares.

I swallowed, the gossamer band still tied across my mouth suddenly too tight. The tarot deck in my hand felt heavier than it should have. Petition Fate stared up at me, its edges warm against my palm. The deck had leaned toward the choice I'd made earlier, just like it had every time I'd touched it. The cards had never been neutral. Neither was I.

The Dohkar’s accusation still rang when a voice cut through the clearing like a blade drawn across stone.

“You broke the seal. You heard what you wanted and let the rite twist to serve yourself.”

The families pressed closer, their Weave-Script flaring with outrage. The moss-covered stones beneath my bare feet felt slick with the weight of their stares. The youth knelt silent, hands at her throat, the single pale thread of her script the only sign she still breathed. Julian stood at the edge, his broad shoulders filling the space between two ancient roots, slate-hued gray-green eyes flecked with brighter green fixed on me as though measuring whether I would speak the truth that could ruin me or the silence that would let the blame settle elsewhere.

I followed the thread the wish had opened. My fingers tightened around the old deck against my ribs. The scar on my forearm flared with the old anxiety, a reminder that every hidden thing eventually surfaced. I stepped toward the youth, ignoring the Dohkar’s raised hand, and knelt so close the moss brushed my knees. My voice came thin but steady.

“She offered what she thought I needed. The misalignment came from her, not from me. If someone tampered with the rite to make it twist this way, the proof lies in what she was never meant to say.”

A family member shoved forward, the woman who had spoken the father’s name, her coral hair whipping as she pointed at the stones. “Then prove it. Show us the note she left beneath the offering stone before the rite began. The one that names the one who paid to have the wish changed.”

The clearing went still. Julian’s gaze sharpened on me, the intensity making my pulse race as I struggled to hold my composure. I reached beneath the moss where the youth’s offering had rested. My hand closed on folded parchment, the edge rough with ritual ink. I drew it out. The script was not the youth’s. It named a payment, a name, a deliberate redirection of the sacrifice so the father would come and the cost would fall where it could be hidden.

The Dohkar’s voice cracked. “That was never part of the rite.”

I realize the deck is still in my hand. As I slide Petition Fate back in, I notice a flash of red in the otherwise gold stack. "Tempt Fate." Grandma never let me play with that one. A sword raised to a blood-red sky, lightning arcing down from the clouds (or maybe arcing up?) Ow! Static shock reminds me I need to pay attention to the present…

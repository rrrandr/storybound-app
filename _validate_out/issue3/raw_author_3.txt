The clearing hadn’t settled. The spiralgrass still trembled where Seren’s wish had struck her, the crimson blades braided and unraveling at the root, as if the earth itself had been tugged just once too hard. I stood at the edge of the crowd, the seal torn from my mouth, the cloth damp against my palm. The lumenweave motes had thinned to a slow drift between the veil-curtains, but the air still hummed with the weight of what had just been said. Julian’s signet ring had caught the last of the light when he raised his hand, the gold catching fire before it turned to ash in my vision.

A finger brushed the hem of my cloak. Not mine. Not his. The touch landed like a question I hadn’t asked.

I turned my head just enough to see the girl at the fringe of the assembly—Mira, the seamstress with the quick hands and the quicker tongue. She wore a blouse of pale gold Veilweave that clung to her shoulders like mist, the fabric alive with silver Weave-Script that pulsed once, twice, as her gaze flicked toward the spiralgrass. The pattern on her skin settled into a slow tidal flow—calm, open. Too calm.

Her mouth shaped the word without sound: *Run.*

The spiralgrass hissed. A single blade parted where the girl’s heel had pressed it down, the red curling inward as if sealing a wound. The assembly shifted, the older Dohkars exchanging glances, the younger Favored leaning in like they had just been handed a secret they weren’t sure they wanted. The Hunched Eye above us—Velorin, waxing full tonight—cast the clearing in a silver glow that made every face look carved from ice.

Julian’s voice cut through the hush, low and deliberate. “You bent her wish toward your own loss.” The words weren’t a question. They were a verdict.

I didn’t step forward. I didn’t step back. My fingers found the platinum cufflinks on my left wrist and tapped them once, twice, three times—my rhythm, the one only I recognize. The sound was soft, a whisper against the spiralgrass, but it carried. The girl at the fringe—Mira—stiffened, her Weave-Script flickering to staccato flickers, conflicted. She knew what this meant. She had seen me use that rhythm before.

Julian’s gaze narrowed on my lips as I spoke, his focus tell firing, his fingers twitching subtly as if already woven into the air between us. “I did not ask her for that,” I said, the seal still loose from my mouth. “She offered it on her own. I tried to stop her.”

The spiralgrass swayed without wind, the braided strands unraveling just enough to let the red show through. The hue wasn’t natural—it was the color of old wishes, of debts paid in flesh and memory. The Dohkar’s hands lifted in warning, the same gesture she had made when Seren named her sacrifice. But this time, the warning wasn’t for Seren. It was for me.

A hand closed around my wrist from behind. Not Mira. Not Julian. A man’s grip—rough, calloused, the kind that remembered rope and sail and the weight of a sword. I didn’t need to see his face to know who it was. The scent of salt and old iron told me.

“You’re not thinking,” the man said, his voice a rasp like sand against stone. Thorne. Guild Leader Thorne, the one who had once called me *sister* in front of a room full of Favored. “They’re not here for a lesson. They’re here for blood.”

I didn’t pull away. I didn’t turn. My gaze stayed on Julian’s emerald eyes, the vivid green that looked like it had been poured from glass. The small silver scar at the corner of his right eye caught the light as he watched me, the same scar I had traced in my dreams. His mouth didn’t smile. It didn’t have to. The silence between us was heavy enough to bend the spiralgrass.

Thorne’s fingers tightened. “The girl’s eye is wrong. The assembly thinks you made it wrong. You know what that means.”

I knew. The alignment had been broken before the wish was sealed. Seren’s right eye was pale as river stone; the left still vivid aqua. It wasn’t a misalignment. It was a *sign*. The kind that got people like me branded with the mark of the Unbound.

Julian’s head tilted slightly, his ears—smaller than human but perfectly shaped—catching the murmur of the crowd. His focus tell fired again, his fingers twitching like they were already counting the steps between us. “You could have halted it,” he said. “You didn’t.”

The spiralgrass curled inward, as if the earth itself had taken a breath. The lumenweave motes thickened, swirling in a slow spiral before they scattered like embers refusing to settle.

Mira’s voice cut through the quiet, sharp and sure. “The spiralgrass remembers.” She stepped forward, her blouse whispering against the Veilweave curtains. “It remembers every wish, every debt, every lie. It knows what you took.”

The crowd recoiled. The older Favored—those with muted sclera, no sclera at all—drew back like the grass had just stung them. The younger ones leaned in, their four-pointed pupils catching the light, their Weave-Script flickering in time with the spiralgrass’s slow tidal flow.

Julian’s gaze didn’t leave mine. His mouth, the full lower lip with its slight asymmetry, didn’t soften. But his fingers stilled. Just once. Just long enough.

Thorne exhaled through his nose, a sound like a drawn blade. “They’re going to ask for the mark. They’re going to ask for the seal. They’re going to ask for the girl.”

I looked down at the spiralgrass, the red curling like a wound. The clearing felt smaller now, the veil-curtains closing in like a cage. The Hunched Eye watched us all, indifferent.

Julian’s voice dropped lower. “You’re standing in the wrong place, Sacrificiant.”

The seal in my hand felt heavier than it should have. The spiralgrass hissed again, the sound like a held breath finally released.

I stepped forward. The spiralgrass parted for me, the red braids unraveling just enough to let my footfall land without resistance. The crowd drew back, the older Favored whispering prayers to the Thirteen, the younger ones holding their breath.

Julian’s fingers didn’t twitch. His mouth didn’t soften. But his emerald eyes held mine, the small silver scar at the corner catching the light like a promise.

I stopped just in front of him. Close enough to feel the heat of his body through the gossamer, close enough to see the way his Weave-Script flickered in time with mine. Close enough to hear the whisper of his breath, low and resonant, like a storm still trapped in his chest.

“I’m not running,” I said.

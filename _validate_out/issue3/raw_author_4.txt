The clearing’s crimson spiralgrass swayed as if the ground itself breathed, the white veil-curtains hanging like ghostly shrouds between the braided Spiralbound trunks. A lumenweave mote drifted down, settling against my forearm before vanishing into the dense red. The whispering crowd had already started to turn away from me, their murmurs thickening into accusations, but my gaze stayed locked on the sealed band still clutched in my hand. The cloth fluttered when I exhaled, the last seal of my apprenticeship broken. The Dohkar’s warning echoed in the back of my skull—*Never sign Fate’s blank page*—but it was too late. The price had been paid.

A hand dropped onto my shoulder, fingers pressing just hard enough to ground me. “You’re standing in the wrong place.” Julian’s voice cut through the noise, low and threaded with something like amusement, though his emerald eyes held no warmth. He tilted his head slightly, the way he always did when he was deciding whether to believe what he was seeing. The signet ring on his finger caught the filtered light, its edge sharp enough to draw blood. “The elders have already decided you’re guilty. Staying here won’t change that.”

His thumb traced the silver scar at the corner of his right eye, a habit I’d come to recognize—the way he turned inward when the moment mattered. The gesture should have been subtle, but in the hush of the clearing, it felt like a challenge. I swallowed the taste of copper and forced my voice steady. “I didn’t ask Seren to offer her memory for me.”

Julian’s gaze flicked to the space between us, where the crimson grass had gone still. “Then why did she?” His fingers twitched at his side, the same restless motion that had once made me wonder if he was always this close to shattering. “You bent her wish toward your own loss. That’s interference. And interference is an easy accusation when the truth is inconvenient.”

A breeze cut through the veil-curtains, and the lumenweave motes swirled like embers refusing to settle. The assembly shifted, their attention pulling toward us like a tide. Someone in the crowd gasped. A Dohkar’s hands lifted in warning—*Protocol cracked under the weight of what she had just risked for me.*—but the words didn’t need to be spoken. The damage was done.

Julian’s heel pressed into the crimson grass, half an inch forward, as if testing the ground before he committed. “You could walk away now. Pretend you never saw the letter.” His voice dropped, the low resonance wrapping around me like a noose. “Or you could let me see it.”

The tarot deck in my pocket felt heavier than it should, the gold edges catching the light in a way that made my vision blur. Petition Fate had already answered once today. Tempt Fate was the next card, but the weight of the accusation pressed down harder. I exhaled through my nose, the seal still clutched in my hand. “It’s not a letter. It’s a ledger.”

The clearing went silent. The spiralgrass held its breath. Julian’s fingers stilled, his focus narrowing on me like a blade honed to a point. “Whose?”

I stepped forward, the gossamer of my tunic brushing the grass. “Yours.”

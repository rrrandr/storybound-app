The whispers had already begun when Julian’s hand rose. The signet ring on his finger caught the filtered light through the veil-curtains, and his arm extended straight toward me.“You bent her wish toward your own loss.”The words landed with the weight of an iron vow. His emerald eyes held no warmth, only the sharp test beneath them, the one he always set before he would allow himself to believe anything. The low rasp of his next breath reached me before any other sound did. My body answered before I chose to, the same pull that had once made my fingers curl around my sleeve while he waited to see if my declaration would hold.I tore the last of the seal from my mouth and the cloth fluttered to the crimson spiralgrass. My voice came out thinner than I meant, the softness that always betrayed me when the moment mattered most.“I did not ask her for that. She offered it on her own. I tried to stop her.”The assembly drew tighter. Seren knelt where the wish had struck her, one eye pale as river stone, the other still vivid aqua. Her coral hair trembled against the braided grass. She looked at me as though the price had been hers to pay and mine to carry.Julian’s heel shifted half an inch against the spiralgrass.“You speak as though the rite still listens to you.”It did not. The Dohkar’s hands remained lifted in warning. The lumenweave motes drifted between the hanging curtains like embers refusing to settle. Every trained gaze in the clearing read the same fracture: the beneficiary had been named by another, the sacrifice offered for someone else’s becoming, the alignment already twisted before the price was taken.I stepped forward, the gossamer of my tunic brushing the spiralgrass.“I removed the seal the moment I understood what she meant to do. I would have halted the rite if the law had allowed it.”Julian’s gaze did not soften. It measured. The same look he had given me across another clearing when I reached for a truth I had no right to claim, his cuff brushed the edge of the veil-curtain once, the same silence that asked whether this version of me would hold or break.The whispers thickened into voices. Someone said the word that had already formed in every mind: interference. Another answered with the older one: manipulation. My qualification trembled on the edge of denial, and the only person who could speak for what had truly passed between Seren and me stood with his hand still extended, breath held, waiting to see if I would defend myself or confess the uncertainty I could not yet name.The pressure settled between us like a new vow neither of us had chosen to speak.I realize that old tarot deck is still in my hand. Reassuringly solid. Present. One of the heavy gold cards slips out — "Petition Fate" is inscribed in it. If only it were that easy.

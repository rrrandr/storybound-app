The murmurs hit first. They always did when the gathered First Favored sensed the alignment slip. I stood at the edge of the crimson spiralgrass, the gossamer band across my mouth already damp from breath, and felt every eye measure the distance between my sealed lips and the kneeling girl before me. Seren’s aqua skin glowed with the first faint lines of Weave-Script, pale coral hair falling forward as she named her offering. Her voice carried the practiced steadiness we had drilled for months. The Dohkar presiding watched with the still patience of someone who had seen a hundred such rites begin correctly and end in celebration. I should have relaxed. Instead the tremor in my voice threatened to surface even behind the cloth. I remembered the way Julian engaged me with thoughtful questions, his thumb tracing the rim of his cup while he waited for my answer. He would have noticed the exact moment Seren’s words changed. Seren lifted her face and looked straight at me. The prepared wish vanished. She spoke the new one in a rush of false kindness, her voice bright with the need to be seen as good. The assembly felt the misalignment the same instant I did. A low sound moved through the crowd, not quite protest, not yet fear. The Dohkar’s hands rose, too late. Fate answered the words as spoken. The one Seren had tried to give me found its nearest shape among those already present. A senior guest who had stood silent through the entire rite turned toward me with sudden recognition that belonged to no memory he should still hold. He spoke the name I had buried, the one I had never spoken aloud in this clearing, and the sound of it stripped something small and irreplaceable from Seren’s own voice before the final syllable left his mouth. Her Weave-Script flickered and went dim at the throat. She tried to answer the Dohkar’s question and produced only air. The price had chosen its own vessel. The crowd’s horror settled into the precise shape I had dreaded since the rite began: they believed I had interfered. My qualification hung in that single inference. Seren’s family watched me with the polite distance reserved for someone whose hands might now be considered unclean. The Dohkar did not speak my name, but the absence itself was judgment. I tore the gossamer band away. The motion drew every gaze. My fingers found the edge of the ceremonial sash and held it, the only anchor I could claim without stepping forward and confirming every suspicion at once.

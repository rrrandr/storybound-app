The murmurs had not faded. They thickened instead, pressing against the crimson spiralgrass like a living thing. I stood with the ceremonial sash clutched in one hand, the torn gossamer band dangling from my fingers, and felt the weight of every gaze settle on the space where my explanation should begin.

Seren remained kneeling, her aqua skin now dull at the throat where the Weave-Script had gone dark. Her pale coral hair clung to her cheeks. She looked up at me once, eyes wide and empty of the words she could no longer shape. The Dohkar presiding kept his hands raised, the gesture frozen halfway between command and refusal. Around us the assembly waited, and the silence felt heavier than any accusation they might have spoken aloud.

I drew breath to speak. My voice came low, careful, the way one speaks when the ground itself might shift.

“She named her offering. I sealed my mouth as the rite requires. I did not guide her words. I did not lend my will to them.”

The Dohkar’s eyes flicked toward the senior guest who had spoken my buried name. The man still stared at me, recognition bright and wrong in his face. I could feel the story forming behind every pair of eyes: the apprentice who had broken the oldest rule, who had reached across the sealed silence to twist a youth’s first wish into her own favor.

Julian stood among the observers, long black hair loose past his jaw, slate-blue skin marked with silver Weave-Script that moved in slow, unreadable tides. He watched me without moving closer, one hand turning the ring on his finger in a steady, unconscious motion. The metal caught the light each time his thumb passed over it. His presence pulled the room tighter without a single word.

He spoke then, voice even, the question shaped like an offering rather than a blade.

“Lirael. Did Seren name the wish alone, or did something of yours reach her?”

The assembly leaned toward the answer. I felt the tremor I had fought all morning rise again, this time in my throat. My fingers tightened around the sash. The old tarot deck rested against my palm where I had not meant to keep it, its gold edges warm from skin.

I met Julian’s gaze. The hazel there shifted between green and gold as the light changed, and for one breath I remembered the way he listened, head tilted as if each word mattered more than the last. The memory did not help.

“She spoke the new wish. I heard it the same moment everyone else did. I gave her nothing.”

The Dohkar lowered his hands. Seren’s mother turned her face away. The senior guest who had spoken my name took one step forward, and the crowd parted around him as if the recognition he carried might be catching.

Julian’s ring turned again, once, twice. He did not look away from me.

I realize that old tarot deck is still in my hand. Reassuringly solid. Present. One of the heavy gold cards slips out — "Petition Fate" is inscribed in it.

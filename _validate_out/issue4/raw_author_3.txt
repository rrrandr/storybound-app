The threshold of the clearing held the scent of crushed crimson spiralgrass and the faint metallic tang of the last wish still lingering in the air. I stood there, the ceremonial sash cutting into my palm, the torn gossamer band clinging to my fingers like a second skin. The assembly's murmurs had thickened into something heavier—less judgment, more something else. The kind of silence that hums in your bones when you've just seen a truth too bright to stare at directly.

Seren's pale coral hair still hung forward, framing a face that had lost the steady lines of Weave-Script at her throat. The absence was a wound. She knelt where she'd been, but it was as though the air had turned to glass between us. Even the crimson spiralgrass beneath her knees seemed to darken at the edges.

My fingers found the edge of the tarot deck before I'd decided to. It rested against my thigh, its gold edges warm from skin contact, each card whispering against the next with a sound like a held breath. I shouldn't. But I did. I pulled the top card free.

Petition Fate stared up at me.

The ink at the bottom edge shifted as my thumb passed over it—Petition Fate. Not an answer. A question. One I'd already asked. I turned the card over anyway.

The assembly leaned forward as one, their Weave-Script flickering in anticipation. Someone coughed. The sound came from somewhere near the back, but it might as well have been right beside me. The senior guest who'd spoken my buried name was still there, his slate-blue skin marked with silver tides. He hadn't moved. Hadn't blinked.

Julian stood half a pace behind him, long black hair loose past his jaw, his slate-blue skin marked with silver Weave-Script that moved in slow, unreadable swirls. He watched me without moving closer, one hand turning the ring on his finger in a steady, unconscious motion. The metal caught the light each time his thumb passed over it. His gaze sharpened on me, a flicker of intensity that made my pulse skip. The scars on his hands moved as his fingers traced them—old marks that didn't match the elegance of his bearing. He was deep in thought. Or calculating. Or both.

I shouldn't have looked. But I did.

The card slipped from my fingers.

It landed on the threshold, face up. Tempt Fate stared back at me.

The assembly's reaction was instant—a collective inhale that sounded more like a held scream than a breath. The senior guest took a step forward, his recognition bright and wrong in his face. The Dohkar's hands, still raised in that frozen gesture, twitched.

I didn't need to read the card to know what it meant. I'd just invoked the one that could unravel everything.

Julian's ring turned again, slow and deliberate. He didn't ask. Didn't move. Just watched.

My fingers drummed the spine of first-edition books I couldn't bring myself to shelve—tapping twice, then twice more, the rhythm a language I couldn't translate. The sound was lost beneath the assembly's murmuring, but it steadied me. One thing I'd learned in Lytharyn: a good scholar always had a system.

The senior guest spoke again, his voice carrying the weight of a man who'd been waiting years to say these words. "You asked for this," he said, and it wasn't a question.

The threshold beneath my boots felt suddenly too solid. Too real. Seren's voice had been stolen. The wish had been twisted. And I was the only one who could see the threads connecting them all.

Julian's scars traced a path I couldn't follow.

The tarot deck lay between us, waiting.

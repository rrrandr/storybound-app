The threshold held me in place as the gold card warmed in my hand, its inscription catching the light like a question I had not yet voiced. The assembly waited, the air thick with the weight of what had been taken from Seren. My fingers moved without thought, tracing the edge of the ceremonial sash, the fabric rough against skin that still remembered the band across my mouth. The thread the wish had opened pulled at me, a faint tug toward the card itself, as if the offering lingered in the weave between us all.

Julian’s gaze sharpened on me from where he stood among the observers, hazel flecked with amber catching the shifting light. His hand turned the ring on his finger, the metal flashing once as his thumb passed over it. “The wish bore your mark, Lirael. The alignment sang of your hand, not hers alone.”

The words landed like a blade drawn across silk. Murmurs rose again, sharper now, the crowd’s eyes turning from Seren to me with fresh accusation. I felt the tremor rise in my throat, the old fear that I had already explained too much and would have to explain again. Yet Julian did not wait for my denial. He held the moment open, the question beneath his voice the one my father’s leaving had taught me never to trust.

“The assembly deserves the full weave,” he said, voice low and deliberate, each syllable carrying the weight of an oath. “Tell them what you reached for when the rite turned.”

The deck in my palm grew heavier, the gold edges pressing into my skin as if the card itself leaned toward the choice I had not made. My fingers tightened on the sash, the fabric bunching beneath them, and for one breath I could not look away from the way Julian watched me—steady, unyielding, the curve of his mouth holding something I had not asked him to see.

I realize the deck is still in my hand. As I slide Petition Fate back in, I notice a flash of red in the otherwise gold stack. "Tempt Fate." Grandma never let me play with that one. A sword raised to a blood-red sky, lightning arcing down from the clouds (or maybe arcing up?) Ow! Static shock reminds me I need to pay attention to the present…

The murmurs thickened until they roared in my ears like the tide. I stood at the threshold of the crimson spiralgrass, the torn gossamer band still clutched in my fist, and felt the assembly's gaze press against me like a physical weight. The air smelled of old incense and the faint metallic tang of sacrifice. My pulse hammered at my throat where the cloth had been, where it should have stayed.

A shadow detached itself from the pillars, moving toward me with the quiet certainty of someone who had been waiting for this exact moment. The hem of his silver-edged cloak brushed the grass, leaving faint smudges where the red dirt clung. Julian.

His slate-blue skin bore the slow silver lights of his Weave-Script, but his eyes—those shifting hazel flecks that moved between green and gold—held a stillness I'd never seen before. A ring on his left hand turned once, twice, before he stopped. The metal caught the dim light like a promise.

"You're holding something," he said, voice low enough for only me. His gaze flicked to the tarot deck pressed against my ribs. "Something that might answer the question you haven't asked yet."

The assembly's murmurs didn't fade. If anything, they grew louder, pressing closer. Seren remained on her knees, her aqua skin now dull at the throat where the Weave-Script had gone dark. The Dohkar's hands still hovered in that frozen gesture, his expression unreadable behind the professional mask. Around us, the clearing felt like it was shrinking, the crimson spiralgrass creeping inward like a living thing.

I swallowed around the dryness in my throat. "There is no question. Only consequences."

Julian's heel lifted once, then settled. "Consequences have a way of asking their own questions." His thumb traced the ring on his finger again, the motion deliberate, controlled. "You're standing at the edge of something you didn't create. But it's yours to fall into."

The weight of his words settled in my stomach. My fingers tightened around the deck. A card shifted against my palm, the gold edges warm. The memory of Seren's voice, the way it had cracked on the new wish—it all pressed against my ribs like a bruise.

"Tell me how to undo it," I said, my voice steady despite the tremor in my hands. "Tell me how to give her back what was stolen."

For the first time, Julian's expression flickered. Something like recognition flashed across his face, gone before I could name it. The assembly leaned forward. The Dohkar's hands began to lower.

Julian stepped closer, his presence cutting through the press of bodies like a blade. "You can't undo what Fate accepted," he murmured. "But you can ask what it left behind."

His hand hovered near mine, close enough that I could feel the heat radiating from his skin. The deck in my grip suddenly felt heavier, the cards shifting as if alive.

I realize the deck is still in my hand. As I slide Petition Fate back in, I notice a flash of red in the otherwise gold stack. "Tempt Fate." Grandma never let me play with that one. A sword raised to a blood-red sky, lightning arcing down from the clouds (or maybe arcing up?) Ow! Static shock reminds me I need to pay attention to the present…

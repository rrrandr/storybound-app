The threshold held the weight of every eye in the assembly. The Dohkar’s voice cut through the thickening silence, low and unyielding.

“Answer, Lirael. The rite demands truth, and the girl cannot speak it.”

Julian’s gaze sharpened on me, a flicker that pulled the air tighter between us. His fingers turned the ring once, the metal catching light as his voice followed, steady and edged with the test he never named.

“She named the wish alone,” he said, “but the name the guest spoke is one she buried when she first crossed into the Veilwood. The thread she followed reached farther back than any of us were meant to see.”

The murmurs sharpened. Seren’s mother turned fully away. The senior guest’s stare fixed harder, recognition still burning where it had no place. My fingers found the hem of my cloak and worried the edge, the motion betraying the unease I could not voice. The Dohkar waited, the weight of his question pressing like a second seal across my mouth.

I felt the pull of Julian’s earlier closeness in memory—how he had leaned in once, voice low, revealing the vulnerability he usually armored. The recollection raced through me even as the present demanded an answer I could not give without confirming every suspicion. The deck in my palm grew heavier, one card shifting beneath my thumb as if leaning toward the choice already forming.

I realize the deck is still in my hand. As I slide Petition Fate back in, I notice a flash of red in the otherwise gold stack. "Tempt Fate." Grandma never let me play with that one. A sword raised to a blood-red sky, lightning arcing down from the clouds (or maybe arcing up?) Ow! Static shock reminds me I need to pay attention to the present…

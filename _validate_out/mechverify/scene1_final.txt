The Dohkar’s voice cut through the clearing like a blade drawn across silk. “Seren of the aqua line, speak your offering and your desire.”

Seren knelt on the crimson spiralgrass, her coral hair catching the first light that filtered through the veilwood canopy. The gossamer tunic bloomed white over her small frame. Her Weave-Script pulsed steady aqua across her skin—calm, composed, the same poise she had held through every rehearsal. I stood at the edge of the circle, mouth sealed beneath the gossamer band, my own Weave-Script flickering silver with the dread I could not voice. My hand twisted a strand of dark chestnut hair around one finger, the old habit surfacing before I could stop it. She had rehearsed this for months. The memory she would surrender was her own—how she had chosen this path. The words were meant to be simple, self-directed. I relaxed my shoulders. She knew the rule. She would not break it. Seren lifted her face. Her eyes found mine across the clearing. The Weave-Script on her skin stuttered once, then steadied. “I offer the memory of the day I first held a wish in my hands,” she said, voice clear. “So that Lirael may find the one she lost.”

The clearing went still. The Dohkar’s hand, half-raised to accept the offering, froze. Every First Favored present felt the misalignment the same instant I did—Seren had spent her First Sacrifice on me, and she had named the wound too loosely for Fate to bind cleanly. My blood oath, the one I had carried since childhood, sat exposed in the gap between her words and what she had meant. My pulse answered before I could name the memory, a quick heat under the skin that had nothing to do with the rite and everything to do with the fact that he had once looked at me the same way he looked at a problem he intended to solve. The Dohkar’s Weave-Script flared violet at the throat. A low murmur rose from the assembled family and guests. Seren’s eyes widened, the first crack in her composure. She had thought the delicacy was kindness. It was imprecision, and Fate does not forgive imprecision. The spiralgrass at the center of the circle darkened at the edges, a single patch of deeper crimson spreading outward like a bruise. No one spoke the word aloud, but the omen sat there for every trained eye to read. Something had already answered. Seren’s mouth opened again, as if she could take the words back. The Dohkar raised one hand sharply. Too late. The wish was spoken. The words were already moving through the world. A guest near the back of the gathering—a tall figure with ruby skin and amber hair—straightened suddenly. His Weave-Script flashed once, bright and discordant. He turned his head toward me, eyes narrowing as if he had only now recognized a face he had not expected to see. The movement was ordinary, the kind any person might make when a name is spoken aloud. But the name had been mine, and the memory he carried was the one Seren had tried to give me back. The blood oath I had sworn as a child—the one that bound me to silence about my lineage—tightened in my chest like a living thing. The guest’s gaze held. He knew. Or he would know soon. The assembly would draw the line from Seren’s words to the oath I had never spoken aloud. I tore the gossamer band from my mouth. The cloth whispered against my skin as it fell. Protocol broken. The qualification I had worked toward for years now hung by a thread no thicker than the band I had just discarded. Every eye in the clearing shifted from Seren to me. Julian’s memory pressed again—the way his shoulders had once blocked the wind when he stood between me and a council I had no right to face. I could reach for him. Or I could let the room believe the wish was only a child’s mistake. I didn’t remember bringing it.

My hand tightened around my great-grandmother's old tarot deck. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.

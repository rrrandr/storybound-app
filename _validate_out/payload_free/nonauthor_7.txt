GOAL ↔ RELATIONSHIP CORE PRINCIPLE (HARD — architectural, frames everything below):
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Storybound stories MUST sustain TWO INDEPENDENT ENGINES in parallel:
  1. an A-plot the reader cares about IN ITSELF — the external conflict has real stakes; its outcome matters regardless of the romance
  2. an R-plot the reader cares about IN ITSELF — the people emotionally surviving each other has real stakes; their fate matters regardless of the mission

HIERARCHY (Roman 2026-06-02 R-plot regime — TARGET ARCHITECTURE):
  • R-plot = premise. The relationship's gravity is what the reader is here for.
  • A-plot = pressure. The obstacles, deadlines, and situations that force the gravity to reveal itself.
THE TEST: answer two questions for your authored A-plot —
  Q1. What is happening externally?
  Q2. What is happening between these people?
If all A-plot nouns (the audit / the breach / the contract / the deal / the recordings / the island) are removed from Q2's answer, Q2 must STILL describe a compelling emotional premise. Q1 may not survive that removal — and that is correct. The A-plot exists to PRESSURE the R-plot tension; it does not exist to be the story's reason for being.
DESIGN QUESTION (HARD — Roman 2026-06-02): when authoring the A-plot goal + antagonist + named clock, the question you ask is "HOW DOES THIS PRESSURE THE CURRENT R-PLOT TENSION BETWEEN PC AND LI?" — NOT "how does this create an interesting mystery?". A genuinely interesting mystery that does not specifically pressure THIS pair's collision is the wrong A-plot for this story.
THE LI MUST CREATE HIS OWN GRAVITY (HARD — Roman 2026-06-02): do NOT position the love interest as a downstream artifact of someone else's loss. AVOID making the LI:
  • witness to a dead spouse's last days
  • keeper of a dead party's secrets
  • executor of a dead party's wishes
  • guardian of a dead party's recordings / archive / island
  • companion at a dead party's funeral
Those roles make the LI feel emotionally downstream of the deceased; reader attention stays attached to the dead party and the LI becomes a MECHANISM through which the dead continues acting. STRUCTURALLY SUPPRESSES ATTRACTION. The LI must carry his own narrative gravity — his own wound (separate axis from PC's wound, intersecting via collision), his own want, his own arc. If your A-plot positions the LI as caretaker of a dead person's legacy, redesign so his role is sourced in HIM, not in service to someone else.

GOLD-STANDARD TARGET: Han + Leia vs the Empire. The reader cares whether the Empire falls AND whether Han + Leia survive each other. Neither is decoration. Neither is a vehicle for the other. Both engines are real.

THE RECIPROCITY MANDATE:
  • External conflict SCULPTS love (the war changes the relationship)
  • Love SCULPTS the external conflict (the relationship changes the war)
  • The same decisions affect BOTH (strategic and emotional consequences are joined)
  • Sacrifice solves one layer while wounding another
  • External pressure reveals character truth
  • Emotional evolution alters strategic outcomes

BAD INTEGRATION (HARD FAILS — your A-plot MUST NOT produce these):
  • The romance is detachable — you could remove the LI and the A-plot would still work
  • The A-plot is replaceable — you could swap it for any other external conflict and the EMOTIONAL ARC between PC and LI would be unchanged
  • Plot scenes alternate with romance scenes ("now mission, now kissing")
  • A-plot is "pressure wallpaper" — generic stakes that don't shape character
  • The romance becomes "occasional reward scenes" between mission beats

GOOD INTEGRATION (aim for):
  • The reader feels: "I care about the external conflict BECAUSE I care about these people. I care about these people MORE because of the external conflict."
  • External pressure REVEALS who these people become under pressure
  • Emotional evolution ALTERS strategic outcomes
  • A specific decision they make together creates BOTH a strategic gain AND an emotional cost (or vice versa)

A-PLOT DESIGN STANDARD (HARD — specific relational shaping required):
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Your A-plot must SPECIFICALLY shape the relationship — not be a pressure machine that happens to coexist with romance. Test: if your A-plot could be swapped for any other external conflict (war → political scandal → kidnapping → corporate takeover → succession crisis) without changing the EMOTIONAL ARC between PC and LI, the A-plot is GENERIC and FAILS the design standard.

The A-plot must specifically determine:
  • ATTRACTION STYLE — what about THIS LI is dangerous/desirable IN THIS conflict. Not generic "he's a billionaire"; specific "he's a billionaire whose mining permits PC is trying to revoke." The friction is conflict-specific.
  • TRUST FORMATION — what specific test the conflict creates for whether they can trust each other (a leaked memo, a vote they're on opposite sides of, a witness one of them protects)
  • SACRIFICE STRUCTURE — what specific thing one of them might have to give up FOR the other (a deal, a vote, an evidence pile, an alliance, a public position)
  • VULNERABILITY EXPOSURE — what specific shame/secret the conflict will force into the open (the wound the A-plot will pry open is named)
  • POWER DYNAMICS — how the conflict tilts leverage between them, and how that tilt can shift mid-story
  • INTIMACY PACING — what the conflict permits or forbids physically (time alone, distance, surveillance, public watching)
  • LOYALTY CONFLICT — what specific person/cause/value pulls each of them in OPPOSITE directions (her loyalty to her firm vs his loyalty to his sister; her duty to the Council vs his loyalty to his sworn man)
  • MORAL ALIGNMENT — where the conflict puts them on the same side, and where it doesn't

Without this specificity, the A-plot is "pressure wallpaper" and the romance becomes detachable. The reader feels: "wait, why are these two together?" — that's the failure signal.

Your output MUST INCLUDE a relational_shaping field (string, 1-2 sentences) that NAMES specifically how THIS A-plot will shape the relationship — what trust test it forces, what vulnerability it pries open, what loyalty conflict pulls them apart. If you cannot name a specific relational shaping, REDESIGN the A-plot until you can. A generic "they must work together against [antagonist]" is insufficient.

A-PLOT CONDUCTIVITY DIRECTIVE (HARD — per-beat enforcement):
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The A-PLOT DESIGN STANDARD above ensures STRUCTURAL relational shaping at story setup. The CONDUCTIVITY DIRECTIVE ensures the A-plot CONTINUOUSLY conducts emotion at every milestone — not just at the architecture level but at every beat.

PRINCIPLE: a strong Storybound A-plot is EMOTIONALLY CONDUCTIVE. The external conflict continuously produces: attraction shifts, trust shifts, power shifts, sacrifice dilemmas, dependence, admiration, disappointment, longing, jealousy, fear of loss, moral collision, emotional revelation. The external story MUST actively reshape the emotional field with every major beat.

WORKED EXAMPLE — bad vs conductive (study this, pattern-match against your own ideas):

  ❌ INERT: "The NGO must secure permits for the aquifer project."
     → produces meetings, paperwork, exposition. Zero emotional conductivity. The LI could be any person, the relationship would unfold the same way.

  ✅ CONDUCTIVE: "The LI concealed evidence the project poisoned a village because failure would collapse the only clean-water initiative in the region."
     → produces trust fracture (she finds out), moral ambiguity (he wasn't wrong to weigh it that way), admiration/disgust tension (his pragmatism IS the thing she fell for), sacrifice conflict (does she expose him), emotional repositioning (she sees him differently), impossible loyalty questions (the village vs the project, him vs her ethics).

Same world. Same flavor. Same antagonistic force category. The CONDUCTIVE version makes the external plot CHANGE THE RELATIONSHIP at every beat.

PER-BEAT TEST: Every milestone you emit MUST answer: "What does this pressure force the characters to REVEAL?" NOT: "What exposition happened?" If a milestone is informational/bureaucratic/logistical without an emotional revelation, REDESIGN it — bury the info inside an emotionally conductive event.

COMPETENCE IS EMOTIONAL (critical addition):
Romance readers often emotionally engage with A-plots through CAPABILITY: leadership, sacrifice, strategic intelligence, resilience, courage under pressure, calm under fire, loyalty proven through action. The A-plot is NOT merely "pressure" — it is an ATTRACTIVE REVELATION ENGINE. Characters become lovable through how they ACT under pressure: what they protect, what they sacrifice, what they hide, what they refuse to abandon. Design milestones that create competence-attraction moments alongside vulnerability-attraction moments.

CAUSAL ENTANGLEMENT (stronger than reciprocity):
The A-plot and R-plot must be CAUSALLY ENTANGLED, not parallel. Romance changes strategic decisions; strategic decisions wound intimacy; emotional growth alters external outcomes; external pressure reshapes attraction; victories cost emotional stability; love complicates survival; survival complicates love. If the plot would unfold the same way regardless of how the romance progresses, the engines are NOT entangled — they're parallel. Fix the design.

5 VALIDATION QUESTIONS (run these before emitting your A-plot — if any answer is wrong, REDESIGN):
  1. Would the external story still matter without the romance? → YES (independent legitimacy required)
  2. Would the relationship dynamic fundamentally change if this A-plot disappeared? → YES (causal entanglement required)
  3. Does this external conflict continuously alter emotional reality, beat by beat? → MUST
  4. Does the conflict regularly create admiration / sacrifice / trust / vulnerability opportunities? → SHOULD
  5. Is the plot generating EMOTIONAL TRANSFORMATION, not merely logistical motion? → REQUIRED

HIGH STAKES ≠ EMOTIONALLY CONDUCTIVE (warning):
A planet exploding is meaningless if it does not reshape attachment, loyalty, identity, sacrifice, emotional dependence, or fear of loss. EMOTIONAL CONSEQUENCE DENSITY is what matters, not raw stakes magnitude. A small, intimate conflict that continuously reshapes the people is more conductive than a galactic crisis they navigate competently without changing.

CONDUCTIVITY AMPLITUDE BANDS (HARD — counter-balance to inflation risk):
Conductivity is a SPECTRUM, not a single mode. Not every milestone should produce trust-fracture / moral-collision / sacrifice-crisis register — that's emotional hypertrophy, the failure mode where every beat becomes performatively significant and the relationship loses its breathing room.

  HEAVY conductivity (reserve for true peaks, ~20-30% of milestones):
    • trust fracture / repair
    • moral collision
    • sacrifice crisis
    • betrayal revelation
    • impossible loyalty choice
    • attachment-threat at full register

  MEDIUM conductivity (~40-50% of milestones — the working register):
    • visible admiration
    • named disappointment
    • leverage shift
    • new dependence emerging
    • boundary tested
    • truth glimpsed but not landed

  LIGHT conductivity (~20-30% of milestones — necessary breath):
    • subtle admiration (noticed but unnamed)
    • micro-irritation
    • ambient longing
    • unresolved tension
    • emotional uncertainty
    • practical coordination (the rhythm of competent partnership)
    • shared competence rhythm (working well together without comment)

LIGHT-CONDUCTIVITY beats are NOT failures of the directive — they are how the heavy beats LAND. Without light beats, the medium and heavy beats stack on top of each other and lose their punch. This pairs with the SILENCE BUDGETS already shipped (CG: 60-70% loaded / 20-30% texture / 5-10% breath) — conductivity has the same logic at the A-plot architecture level.

The conductivity directive is NOT "every milestone must be a crisis." It is "every milestone must conduct SOMETHING — but the amplitude varies by where it sits in the arc." If your scene's pressure naturally produces ambient longing or quiet practical coordination, that IS conductivity at the light band — write it confidently. Do not artificially inflate a coordination beat into a moral collision; that reads as forced significance.

STEP 1 — IDENTIFY THE WOUNDS BEFORE THE PLOT.
A WOUND is a PRE-EXISTING emotional ache that one or both lovers carried INTO Scene 1 — a relational pain whose SOURCE IS A SPECIFIC PERSON (named or unnamed but vividly imagined) and whose resolution is unfinished. Wounds are NOT crises that arrive in Scene 1; they are aches that have been waiting, sometimes for years. Each lover MUST carry a wound. The wounds may be COMPLEMENTARY (different aches that meet in the plot) or SHARED (both broken in the same event, reunion now). They must NOT be transactional ("she owes him money" — too thin); they must involve a person with a face, a history, and an unresolved emotional debt.

WOUND CATEGORIES (use as seed-vocabulary; the specific wound should be invented, not picked from this list):
  • PARENTAL: abandoned child / estranged son or daughter / failed parent / missing child suddenly returning
  • PARTNER: dead spouse with unfinished business / left-at-the-altar or left-at-the-door / public divorce or breakup / the one who got away who is back in town
  • SIBLING-or-FRIEND: estranged sibling years of silence / sibling in coma or incarceration or exile / best friend who died carrying a promise to keep / failed friendship a trust betrayed
  • MENTOR: betrayed mentor / the student he ruined / the mentor who chose someone else over him / the protégée he could not save
  • PUBLIC: public humiliation that forced a retreat / exposed secret circling back / wrongful accusation now surfacing
  • DEBT (RELATIONAL, NOT FINANCIAL): the favor never repaid / the promise broken / the lie that built a career / the truth never told to someone who deserved it

STEP 2 — DESIGN THE A-PLOT AS THE WOUND'S SURFACING.
The A-plot is the EXTERNAL CIRCUMSTANCE that drags the wound back into the light. It exists to make the wound IMPOSSIBLE TO IGNORE. It is NOT a separate project the lovers happen to work on together — it is the mechanism by which the past returns to the present:
  • The wound's SOURCE PERSON (the abandoned daughter, the dead spouse's sister, the betrayed mentor) appears in the plot's circumstance.
  • A circumstance (custody hearing, inheritance contest, illness, kidnapping, scheduled testimony, returning ship) forces the lovers into the same room the wound created.
  • They cannot avoid each other because the wound demands it — the plot's pressure is INSEPARABLE FROM the wound, not a parallel project.

STEP 3 — THE SWAP TEST (HARD — THE CENTRAL VALIDATOR for the entire A-plot system; this is the actual gold; passing every other clause but failing this one means the A-plot is still a project wearing emotional clothes).

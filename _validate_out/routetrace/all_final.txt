The Dohkar’s hand rose, palm open toward the mated veil-trees. “Begin.”

Seren knelt on the crimson spiralgrass, aqua skin luminous under her child’s gossamer. Her pale coral hair fell forward as she drew breath. She spoke the invocation we had rehearsed for months, each word aligned, each sacrifice named. I felt the room settle. My first supervision was holding. Then she lifted her head and looked straight at me. “I wish that Lirael find the one she lost,” she said. “Take what you will.”

The Weave-Script across her throat stuttered once, then steadied. An old woman in the front rank drew both hands up as if to push the words back. Someone whispered,

“Never hand Fate a blank page.”

The spiralgrass at Seren’s knees darkened at the edges, the color draining upward into the stems. She blinked once, and her mouth stayed open on the last syllable as though the sound had been taken from her. Julian did not move from the clearing’s edge, but his silver Weave-Script flared once along the line of his jaw. I felt the band across my mouth pull tight against my teeth. The Dohkar’s eyes found mine. The assembly drew the only conclusion the moment allowed: the wish had been about me, and I had let it stand. My fingers found the knot at the back of my head. The cloth tore free with a soft rip that sounded louder than any invocation. I stepped toward Seren before the Dohkar could speak. Confess my truth or bury it deeper, risking everything I hold dear? I didn't remember bringing it.

My hand tightened around my great-grandmother's old tarot deck. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.

I stepped forward, the torn band still clutched in my fist, and tried to shape the words that would untangle what Seren had done. My voice came out raw from the seal.

“She meant no harm. The wish was hers, but the cost—”

The ritual stone at the center of the clearing answered first. My hand trembled once, a small involuntary jerk, and the stone split down its center with a sound like ice giving way under weight. A faint, ominous glow seeped from the crack, pulsing with the same rhythm as my pulse. The spiralgrass around it darkened further, veins of drained color spreading outward like a bruise.

An old woman near the front rank let out a sharp breath.

“You asked for the one she lost,” she said, voice thin with the irony. “You never named who would pay to find it.”

The Dohkar’s gaze stayed on me, unreadable. Seren remained on her knees, mouth still open, as if the last syllable had taken more than sound.

Julian moved then, one measured step from the clearing’s edge. His silver Weave-Script still flared along his jaw, and his cold slate-gray eyes held mine without softening.

“Claim it now, or let them keep believing what they already see,” he said quietly. The cleft in his chin deepened as he watched me, the way it always did when he waited for a test to finish.

The judgement gathered in the dimming spiralgrass, a shared tension that rose with the glow from the stone. He offered the choice like a blade laid flat, not to wound but to see whether I would pick it up. Strength, yes, but the kind that measured first and reached second. I resented how much I wanted that reach anyway.

The glow in the cracked stone pulsed once more, brighter, then held steady. The assembly waited. My fingers, still around the deck I had not remembered carrying, loosened.

I realize that old tarot deck is still in my hand. Reassuringly solid. Present. One of the heavy gold cards slips out — “Petition Fate” is inscribed in it. If only the stone would stop glowing and the Dohkar would look away. No. I would ask for the courage to say the words that make them see it was never Seren’s fault.

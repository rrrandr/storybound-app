[TITLE: "The Last Ferry North"]
[SYNOPSIS: In a small coastal town, pastry chef Testa scrubs flour from steel while the ferry horn counts down the minutes left to protect her sister from Rook’s latest notice. The cafe kitchen tightens around her with every blast, and the only line still open is the one she swore she would not call. What happens when the vent keeps rattling and the choice refuses to stay silent?]

[CHARACTERS: Testa:F]

The oven vent rattled against the storm wind already hammering the harbor. I wiped flour from my knuckles onto the apron, the motion automatic, the silence after each horn blast landing heavier than it should. The phone sat face-up on the prep table, Rook’s office number still glowing from the last time his clerk had the notice delivered with his own pen in the margin.

My stomach tightened at the memory. He had not even bothered to sign it himself.

I reached for the phone with flour still on my knuckles. The screen stayed dark one minute longer, exactly what I wanted, and the vent kept rattling like it was trying to decide for me.

The ferry horn sounded again, longer this time, the low note carrying across water that had already gone gray. I set the phone down without dialing. The notice lay beneath it, the corner already soft from where my thumb had pressed too hard earlier. Rook’s clerk had left it on the counter like it was just another delivery, but the pen mark in the margin was his, precise and unhurried, the same way he always marked the things he intended to keep.

The vent rattled harder. Wind pushed through the seam where the duct met the wall, carrying the smell of salt and diesel from the harbor. I pressed both hands flat on the steel table, flour dusting the surface again, and listened to the horn fade. The number on the screen had not changed. It would not change unless I made it.

The silence after the horn bothered me more than it should have. I wiped my hands on the apron twice, the way I always did when the room felt too empty, and the mirror over the sink would have called it nerves if it had been angled to catch me. It was not. The phone remained where I had set it, screen dimming now, the number still waiting.

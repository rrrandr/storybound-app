The ship's deck swallowed my boots in fog so thick it stung like salt between my teeth. Every step pressed the scar on my wrist against the railing, the old mark a quiet pulse against skin that still remembered the weight of the Veilwood's bargain. The deck pressed lightly against my side, a steady reminder of the debt I carried, even now. My breath fogged in the cold air, each exhale a small rebellion against the stillness that had settled over the square. The Dohkar's gaze remained fixed on me, his four-pointed pupils unreadable beneath the moonlight, and I forced my fingers to unclench from the railing's rough grain.

A shadow detached itself from the fog—Chayr, his breath warm against my ear before I'd registered his approach. "You may speak," he murmured, the scent of aged paper and something sharper clinging to his cloak. His words settled between us like a blade left unsheathed. The Dohkar's hand had not lowered. Neither had mine.

Then the lantern flickered.

Not the steady glow of a ship's light, but a sudden leap of flame from the sidelight nearest the rail. Oil arced through the air in a slow, deliberate arc before splattering across the deck's planks in a dark sheen that caught the moonlight like spilled ink. The fire hesitated for the space of a breath—just long enough for Julian's ring to catch the light as his hand arrested the lantern's fall. His sleeve was the only color in the gray world, deep slate against the pale oak.

The ring turned once. Twice.

"By the Thirteen," I breathed, because the words came anyway, because the world had decided to press this moment into my chest like a brand. Julian's grip on the lantern had been too late, too careful, as if he'd known the spill was coming. His gaze locked onto mine, the only anchor in a suddenly shifting deck. The fog curled around his boots like he'd materialized from the mist itself, and for the first time since the Veilwood took my childhood, I wanted to believe in coincidence.

Chayr's hand settled on my shoulder, heavy as a judgment. "Explain," he demanded, though his fingers trembled where they touched the wool of my cloak. The oil gleamed, reflecting the Hungry Eye's disputed light above us. Somewhere in the fog, a chain groaned against the hull.

The deck beneath us shifted. The lantern's fire guttered, then steadied.

Julian's voice cut through the sudden quiet, low and deliberate. "A test of wind resistance," he said, the words sliding between us like an offering that might shatter. His ring turned again, its movement a slow, deliberate rhythm that made my pulse stutter. "The deck was uneven. Common knowledge in these waters."

The Dohkar's gaze flicked to the spill, then to me. The air between us compressed, leaving no room for retreat. The crowd's attention pressed closer, every face turned toward the revelation I had just offered.

I could walk away from the truth, or I could name the sacrifice and let Fate take what remained.

A single card in the deck leaned toward the choice I had not yet spoken.

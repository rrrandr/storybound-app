The ship's deck under a blanket of fog lay slick and yielding beneath my boots, each step a muffled protest against the wood as I edged away from the Chayr's suspended hand. Salt clung to the air like an old debt, and the fog pressed close enough to blur the railings into pale ghosts. I had no clear path, only the need to reach Seren before another word could bind us all tighter to the Dohkar's inquiry. The deck pressed lightly against my side as I moved, a steady reminder of the weight I carried even now.

A figure shifted in the murk ahead, and my pulse answered before my eyes could name it. Julian stood half-turned from the mast, his angular face catching what little light the moons offered, the long scar across his forearm a pale line against the slate-blue of his skin. His gaze sharpened on me as if he could read the choice I had not yet spoken, locking onto the scar at my wrist where the hem of my cloak had frayed.

I kept walking, the fog swallowing the Chayr's outline behind me. Seren's voice had come from this direction earlier, low and careful, and I told myself distance might loosen the knot the Dohkar had tied around my throat. The planks groaned once under a shift in the wind, and I heard the faint creak of something unsteady near the rail.

Julian moved to follow or to block, I could not tell which. His hand turned the ring on his finger once, the metal catching a stray gleam, then he reached for the lantern swinging from its hook. The motion looked deliberate until it was not. The lantern tipped, struck the rail, and oil spilled in a dark slick across the deck, spreading toward the coiled ropes and the edge of the circle where the Dohkar waited.

The scent of burning wick rose sharp and immediate. Julian stood over the spill, the silver Weave-Script on his skin flaring once in the sudden flare of the remaining flame, and the fog seemed to draw tighter around the three of us. No one spoke yet. The oil crept closer to the mast, and the choice I had carried toward Seren now sat between the fire and the Chayr's raised hand.

I could stamp the flame out with my boot and keep the truth of his presence here unspoken, or I could call the Dohkar's attention to the spill and let the oil carry its own accusation. The deck shifted faintly beneath us, and the fog swallowed the sound of water against the hull.

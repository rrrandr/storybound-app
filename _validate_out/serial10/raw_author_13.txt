The fog lay thick across the deck, swallowing the planks beneath my boots as I stepped forward. The Chayr’s voice cut through it at once, low and edged. “You think to wander off and find the girl alone? After what you offered the Dohkar?”

I froze, the weight of his words pressing harder than the mist. The deck pressed lightly against my side as I moved, a steady reminder of the weight I carried even now. My fingers brushed the frayed hem of my cloak, the scar on my wrist catching the faint light that struggled through the haze. Julian stood half-hidden by the rail, his hand turning the ring on his finger once, the metal catching what little glow remained. His gaze sharpened on me, locking as if I were the only one present, and the salt air seemed to thin between us.

The Dohkar shifted behind the Chayr, silent, waiting. I swallowed, the urge to speak rising and dying in the same breath. Julian stepped closer, his long black hair curtaining his face for an instant before he lifted his head. His tongue pressed briefly between his teeth, a small, unconscious habit that made my chest tighten, though I forced the feeling down. The Chayr leaned nearer, breath warm against my ear, the scent of danger lingering like old storm-water. “Answer me,” he said. “Or do you mean to hide her truth as well?”

Julian’s shoulder brushed the lantern at the rail. It tipped, oil spilling in a dark rush across the planks, the flame hissing as it caught the edge of his sleeve. The fire licked outward, small and hungry, and the Chayr’s head snapped toward the sudden flare. Julian’s eyes met mine for one held instant, the question plain in them: help now, or let the truth spill with the oil. The choice hung there, unmade, while the fog swallowed the sound of the spreading flame.

The salt air clung to the back of my throat like a second skin, thick with the ghost of the spill still tacky underfoot. I hadn’t meant to come back here—not to this stretch of deck where the lantern still swung slightly from its hook, where the oil had crept like a promise toward the coiled ropes. But the clearing where it had happened drew me anyway, as if the Thirteen themselves had whispered its coordinates into the fog.

My fingers brushed the silver locket around my neck without thinking, the chain warm from my skin. The deck pressed lightly against my side, a steady reminder of the weight I carried even now. The fog swallowed the railings whole, turning the Chayr’s raised hand into a memory half-remembered, suspended in the wrong light.

Julian’s laugh surfaced like a tide I couldn’t outrun—soft, unexpected, the kind that made the corners of my mouth betray me. A pang of longing for the truth behind that charm tightened my chest. *What wouldn’t I give to know the man behind the smile?* The thought settled in my lungs like saltwater, heavy and inevitable.

The ship groaned beneath me, the sound low and familiar, the kind that settled into your bones after too many Turnings at sea. Ahead, the lantern’s flame guttered, its light pooling in the slick of oil that painted the deck in iridescent menace. The silver Weave-Script on Julian’s skin flared once, a silent warning, and I caught the faintest shift in his posture—the way his shoulders squared, his long black hair brushing the collar of his gossamer tunic, slate-blue skin catching the dim glow like a blade half-drawn. He stood too still, as if he’d been waiting for me to notice.

The scar across his forearm caught the light—thin, pale, a reminder of a past mistake neither of us wanted to revisit. He turned the ring on his finger once, the metal catching a stray gleam, then reached for the lantern still swinging from its hook.

“You don’t let things sit,” he said, voice low and deliberate, pitched just for me. The words settled like a challenge across my shoulders, something sharp and bright. His gaze sharpened, locking onto me as if I were the only one in the room.

The oil crept closer to the mast, a dark stain against the pale wood, and the air between us thickened with the scent of burning wick and salt. The silver on his skin pulsed again, a beat of light that mirrored the one at my wrist. The Chayr’s suspended hand still hung in the fog like a ghost, and the Dohkar’s inquiry pulsed in the back of my mind, a drumbeat I couldn’t outpace.

I could stamp the flame out with my boot and keep the truth of his presence unspoken. Or I could let the oil carry its own accusation, let it reveal what I’d been trying to hide from the Dohkar and from myself.

The deck shifted faintly beneath us, the fog curling around our ankles like a living thing. Julian’s ring turned again, slow and thoughtful, and I knew the next word spoken would belong to him—or to the light that was about to reveal what I couldn’t.

The salt-crusted timbers groaned underfoot as I pivoted toward the spill, my pulse hammering in my throat. The oil's dark slick crept closer to the coiled hemp, and the lantern's desperate flame licked at the wood like a promise. I needed to decide: douse the fire before it took root or let the truth of Julian's presence burn through me first. But before my boot could land, a voice cut the fog like a blade.

"Let it burn."

The Chayr stood half a stride behind me, his silhouette sharp against the mast's shadow. His fingers curled around the hilt of his ceremonial dagger, the blade catching the faintest glint of moonlight. The silver weave-script on his forearm flared once, cold and deliberate.

I froze. "You want the fire to spread?"

His mouth twitched—not quite a smile. "I want the truth to catch."

The oil inched forward, and the lantern's flame danced higher, casting his face in sharp relief. His gaze locked onto mine, unblinking. "You know what happens if you douse it. The debt doesn't vanish. It just moves."

My fingers curled into the hem of my cloak. "You're threatening me."

"Not a threat," he murmured. "A question. Will you honor the wish, or will you hide it?"

The lantern's frame groaned as the wind shifted, and the flame guttered dangerously close to the spill. The Chayr's dagger glinted, his grip unshaken. "The Dohkar's inquiry doesn't care about your loyalty. It cares about the record."

A cold certainty settled in my chest. The wish had succeeded. The light would come. And the Chayr wanted to know if I'd let it reveal us all.

I swallowed. "What price are you willing to pay to see it?"

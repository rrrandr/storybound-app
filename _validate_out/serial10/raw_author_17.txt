The Dohkar's voice cut through the fog like a blade drawn too slowly. "You will speak now, or the spill will speak for you."

My fingers brushed the edge of the cards without thinking. Julian stood over the spreading oil, his hand turning the ring on his finger once, the metal catching what little light remained. The Chayr's raised palm had not lowered. The Dohkar's gaze moved between the lantern's wreckage and my face, waiting for the answer that would decide whether the rite continued or broke open under his scrutiny.

The oil crept closer to the mast. Julian did not move to stamp it out. He watched me instead, the silver script on his slate-blue skin flaring once before settling into a slow, uneven pulse. "Choose," he said, low enough that only I heard. "Before the Dohkar does it for you."

I felt the weight of the unseen truth press against my ribs, the one I had carried since the wish left my mouth. The Dohkar took one deliberate step forward, his bare feet silent on the slick planks. The Chayr's hand remained suspended, a silent command that the next word belonged to me.

A shimmer rose from the deck itself, faint at first, then unmistakable. It climbed my arms, my throat, and finally my face, wrapping me in a light that did not belong to any lantern or moon. The Dohkar stopped mid-step. Julian's ring turned again, slower this time.

The light held. It revealed nothing to the others and everything to me. The wish had taken root in a way no First Sacrifice should have allowed. My lineage, the one I had buried beneath duty and silence, answered back from inside the glow.

Julian's eyes locked on mine, sharp and searching. "Say it," he said. "Or let it stand."

The shimmer pulsed once more, waiting. The Dohkar's hand lifted toward the Chayr's suspended palm, ready to end the rite if I remained silent. The choice pressed against my throat like the band I could not remove.

The deck yielded beneath my boots—planks groaning like old bones—as I turned the full weight of my gaze on the Dohkar's raised hand. The salt air clung to my tongue, bitter and familiar, but today it tasted of accusation. I had spent a turning chasing answers, and the oil slick from Julian's lantern still glistened on the wood, spreading like the stains of my own secrets. My fingers twitched toward the cards in my pocket, but the light that had pulsed around me earlier had settled back into something almost imperceptible, a memory rather than a revelation. The silver locket at my throat pressed against my skin, heavy with the wish I had spoken aloud and the price I had not yet paid.

"Tell me what happened during the First Sacrifice," I said, voice low enough that only the closest ears might catch it. The words came out sharper than I intended, scraping against the back of my throat. I needed to know if Seren's hesitation had been fear or something else entirely. The Dohkar's hand trembled once, the barest flicker, before settling into stillness. Julian stood a few strides away, his silhouette sharp against the bright sun, his long hair catching the light like a curtain drawn across a window. His gaze lingered on my lips as I spoke, making my pulse stutter despite myself.

The Dohkar exhaled through his nose, a sound like a man bracing for impact. "The rites unfolded as they should have," he said, but his fingers tightened around an unrolled scroll, the edges crinkling under pressure. "The alignment held. No missteps were recorded."

I stepped closer, the planks shifting beneath my weight, the sun burning at my back. "Then why does it feel like we're standing in the wreckage?" My voice carried the edge of something I hadn't intended to let slip—frustration, maybe. Or the desperate need to believe that someone, somewhere, would tell me the truth without me having to prise it from their grip.

Julian's hand turned the ring on his finger once, the metal catching the light before he finally looked at me. His eyes sharpened, locking onto me as if I were the only one in the room. "Because you're looking for answers in the wrong place," he said, and his voice had that careful quality again, the one that made me want to dig my nails into the wood just to feel something solid beneath them.

The ship groaned, the sound like a held breath finally released, and the deck swayed beneath us. A sudden gust of wind cut across the open space, carrying with it the scent of salt and something metallic—oil, maybe, or the copper tang of blood. The Dohkar's scroll slipped from his grasp, unfurling toward the edge of the deck where the oil slick still glistened. The light caught the parchment, making it shimmer like a reflection on water.

I didn't reach for it. Instead, I watched Julian, waiting for him to offer something—anything—that didn't feel like a deflection. But he only watched me back, his expression unreadable, the ring on his finger turning slowly, rhythmically, as if the motion itself were a language I wasn't meant to decipher.

The Dohkar cursed under his breath, the word lost in the wind, but the sound of it sent a chill down my spine. "You're both playing with forces you don't understand," he muttered, more to himself than to us. His gaze flicked between me and Julian, something like fear flickering in his expression before he schooled it back into professional neutrality.

I could feel the weight of the wish pressing against my ribs, demanding acknowledgment. The truth was here, somewhere in the air between us, but it refused to land. And Julian, damn him, seemed content to let it hang there, just out of reach.

The ship groaned again, the sound deeper this time, like the wood itself was tired of bearing our weight. The wind shifted, carrying the scent of the deep water beyond the railings, and for a moment, I considered stepping to the edge. The water looked dark and endless, and I wondered if it would feel like relief to let it swallow me whole.

But I didn't move. I stayed rooted where I was, caught between the Dohkar's fear and Julian's silence, the wish still burning in my chest like a promise I wasn't sure I wanted to keep.

The silver moonlight cut silver-white stripes across the weathered planks, each one sharp enough to make my pulse jump where the deck’s salt-worn grain split. I stood rigid near the railing, my fingers trembling against the wood, and I did not speak. The Dohkar’s measured footsteps had just skirted the circle they’d drawn around the artifact, the one called the Arcane Binding, and the silence they left behind felt heavier than the salt in the air. I searched the shadows not for answers, but for the crack in the story someone had overlooked—the seam they’d stitched shut before I could see the thread.

A loose knot in the rope by the stairwell caught the light, the fiber frayed where it had rubbed the wood raw. I crouched, letting my skirt pool around my knees, and brushed my thumb over the abrasion. Beneath the salt crust, the fibers had gone a dull, sickly grey—like flesh left too long from the bone. My breath hitched. That wasn’t salt. That was something older, something someone had tried to scrub away.

I shifted my weight, the deck groaning softly beneath me, and my gaze followed the sound to the edge of the circle where the artifact still rested, its facets catching the moonlight like a mirror broken by fate. The Dohkar’s voice curled around the gathering, low and deliberate, naming my lineage again—the lost line, the hidden benefactor, the claim no one had dared speak aloud. The words settled over the deck like a second skin.

Then I saw it.

Something small and pale caught the moonlight near the artifact’s base. I didn’t move. Didn’t breathe. The artifact’s edges were still, the crowd’s murmurs still, the Dohkar’s words still. But the pale shape—the edge of a folded paper—was not.

I reached.

My fingers closed around something thinner than parchment, softer than silk, the edge just frayed enough to catch. The paper resisted, then gave, and the moment it slipped free, the artifact’s facets dimmed. A single line of script glinted under the moon: *Deliver the evidence to the chain-walkers by dawn.* The signature at the bottom wasn’t Julian’s, but the handwriting was unmistakable.

A chill raced up my spine.

I didn’t turn. Didn’t look up. My pulse roared in my ears as I folded the note back into my palm, the edges biting into my skin. The Dohkar’s voice had stopped. The circle had gone still. And then, a slow creak of timber behind me.

Too close.

The tension coiled tighter than the frayed rope, and the deck beneath my boots felt suddenly alive.

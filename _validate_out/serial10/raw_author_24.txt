The salt-worn planks of the deck groaned underfoot as I crouched low, my fingers skimming the grain in search of what the others had missed. The moonlight silvered the timbers, turning every seam into a blade’s edge. A whisper of movement from the shadows—my pulse answered before my mind did. I exhaled through my teeth, forcing stillness into my spine. The Dohkar’s voice had already named me once. They wouldn’t need a second invocation to claim my silence.

The air thickened. A shadow detached from the mainmast, looming closer with the slow inevitability of a debt collector. A gloved hand settled on the railing beside my shoulder, the metal of a signet ring catching the light. The Dohkar’s face emerged from the dusk, their sash catching no breeze, their gaze pinning me like a beetle to cork. “Lirael of the lost line,” they said, the words edged with something between pity and accusation. “The one whose blood carries a benefactor long hidden from the order.”

My breath stuttered. The note in my palm crinkled—salty, damp, half tucked against the underside of the railing where the salt had eaten the wood thin enough to hide. It had slid from Julian’s pocket unnoticed, the edges frayed with use, the ink smudged where his thumb had pressed too hard. I curled my fingers around it like a guilty secret, but the Dohkar’s gaze dropped to the movement.

“You clutch at shadows when you fear the light,” they observed, voice low and deliberate. “A habit of the young, or the guilty.”

I forced a laugh, too loud in the hush of the ship’s deck. “Or the tired,” I countered, letting my thumb drag across my wrist where the scar of a past wish still glimmered faintly under the moon’s glow. “We’ve all been awake too long.”

The Dohkar stepped closer, their polished boots clicking against the planks. “The Chayr’s ruling on your lineage isn’t due until dawn. Yet you’re already digging for answers.” Their ring glinted, the sigil catching the light like a promise of consequence. “Tell me, Sacrificiant—what truth do you hope to find that isn’t already written in the blood you carry?”

My teeth found the inside of my cheek, the metallic tang of caution flooding my mouth. The note burned against my palm, the words too familiar to ignore: *The truth will drown us both.* I should have burned it the moment I saw it. But burning wouldn’t erase what it hinted at.

“There are no truths left to uncover,” I said, my voice steadier than my hands. “Only the ones we choose to believe.”

The Dohkar’s gaze flicked to the railing, then back to me, their lips thinning. “Liar,” they murmured. “Your pulse betrays you.” A single step forward—just enough to crowd my space, to make the choice unavoidable. “Drop the pretense. I know you had help tonight. I know someone slipped you more than a note.”

The wooden planks under my knees creaked. The hidden evidence in my grip was thinner than a prayer, but the risk it posed was heavier than the anchor stones at the ship’s keel. I could lie. I could run. But the Dohkar’s hand hovered over my wrist, ready to twist the truth from me like a screw.

The note crumpled further. Some secrets couldn’t survive daylight.

The threshold's edge pressed against my boot as I searched the crowd for any sign of Julian's broad shoulders cutting through the press of bodies. Tension gathered in my shoulders, a familiar weight that made the gossamer of my sleeve pull taut against my skin. The Dohkar stood at the center of the square, his gaze sweeping the deck with the slow certainty of a man who already knew what he would find. Seren remained kneeling, her aqua skin pale under the dim light, the memory she had offered still hanging in the air like unshed rain.

I tightened my grip on the deck, the gold cards cool and heavy. "I wish Julian would appear now," I whispered, the words slipping out before I could weigh them. The cards shifted once in my hand, one leaning heavier than the rest as if the choice had already begun to settle.

A murmur rose from the crowd, and for a heartbeat the bodies parted just enough that I thought I saw him—tall, dark-haired, that long scar across his forearm catching the light. The glimpse vanished as quickly as it came, swallowed by shifting shoulders and salt-stiffened cloaks. Coincidence, I told myself. Nothing more.

The Dohkar's gaze landed on me, deliberate and unblinking, and the space between us narrowed. I stepped forward before the silence could harden into judgment. The memory of my lost childhood rose unbidden—the Veilwood claiming what came before, the years before the rite and the training and the weight of lineage. That was the price. That was what the chains had answered.

The Dohkar's eyes narrowed, his presence a binding that left no room for retreat. The crowd's attention pressed closer, every face turned toward the revelation I had just offered. I could walk away from the truth, or I could name the sacrifice and let Fate take what remained.

A single card in the deck leaned toward the choice I had not yet spoken.

What if the Dohkar looked away for one breath longer. Petition Fate. That was what the card meant—small asks, small turnings.

I realize the deck is still in my hand. As I slide Petition Fate back in, I notice a flash of red in the otherwise gold stack. “Tempt Fate.” Grandma never let me play with that one. A sword raised to a blood-red sky, lightning arcing down from the clouds (or maybe arcing up?) Ow! Static shock reminds me I need to pay attention to the present…

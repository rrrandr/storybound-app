The wind off the harbor smelled of salt and old rope, carrying the thrum of voices like a current. I pivoted on the weathered plank, my heel catching once against the boards before I could still it. The square beyond the threshold roiled—bodies in gossamer tunics drifting between the crimson spiralgrass, eyes flickering toward the wrecked ship and then sliding away like they’d been cautioned. My fingers flexed around the tarot deck in my coat pocket, the heavy gold cards shifting with each step. I needed one glimpse of him. Just enough to know he was close. The crowd pressed closer, a living thing that recognized me even through the shifting shapes.

A hand clamped my elbow from behind.

“You’ve been circling the edge like a gull that hasn’t decided to land.” The Dohkar’s voice was a blade honed on oaths, his grip firm enough to bruise. His perpetually squinting eyes refused to meet mine, studying instead the way the deck shifted beneath my fingers. “The blood answers what was sworn. The chains remember the line that bound them.” The shimmer he’d left in the air still clung to where he’d spoken, the syllables refusing to fade. Every face that had turned from the wreck now fixed on me. My pulse hammered against my ribs, but I kept my breath slow, shallow. “I’m merely seeing to the rite’s aftermath,” I said, the words measured. “The current was unforgiving. One must steady the threads when the wind turns.”

Seren’s voice rose like a silver thread cutting through the murmur. “The small one with the copper hair—she’s the one who pulled the ship’s cord when the hull split.” Her fingers hovered over the spiralgrass mat, nails catching the faintest edge of crimson. “She’s the one who knew to hold on.” The copper-haired girl stood near the shattered railing, her gaze flicking between me and the Dohkar with the wary precision of someone who had learned to read misalignment in others. I exhaled through my teeth and stepped forward, the deck pressing heavy against my palm. The copper-haired girl’s eyes widened when she saw me—just for a heartbeat—then she ducked behind a woman in a slate-blue tunic, vanishing into the press.

The Dohkar’s thumb traced slow circles over the back of my wrist, his grip tightening once before he released me. “You’re certain you weren’t the one who summoned the tide?” His squint deepened, as if the question itself were a riddle he hadn’t yet solved. “You’re certain you didn’t offer what wasn’t yours?” The air thickened, the shimmer tightening like a noose. The crowd’s murmurs coiled around us, a serpent of sound that tasted of salt and accusation. I should have turned away. Should have let the accusation settle. But the copper-haired girl had pulled three souls from the wreck, and I needed to know if she’d seen Julian slip between the planks.

I moved through the press, my shoulder brushing past a man whose Weave-Script flickered violet at the collarbone—an emotion he refused to name. The copper-haired girl’s copper braid flashed once between the bodies, then was gone. My fingers tightened around the deck. One card. One ask. The tarot deck’s weight was a comfort, a secret pressed close to my ribs. I could feel the gold edges through the fabric, the raised lettering of Petition Fate biting into my fingertips. The copper-haired girl’s voice cut through the murmur again, urgent and low: “He was on the lower deck before the chains sang. I saw his hands on the rail.” Her copper braid bobbed as she pointed toward the ship’s shadowed hull, where the water lapped at the broken timbers like a hungry thing.

The Dohkar’s shadow fell across the plank behind me, long and deliberate. “You’re chasing ghosts, Sacrificiant.” His voice dropped to a growl that vibrated through the wood beneath my boots. “The tide answers only what is sworn.” The shimmer in the air pulsed once, like a held breath, and the crowd exhaled in unison. My heel tapped against the boards—once, twice—a rhythm I couldn’t still. The copper-haired girl’s copper braid was gone again. The ship’s shadow hid deeper truths than I could name. And Julian was still out there, somewhere in the press, watching.

I pushed forward, the deck burning against my palm. The copper-haired girl’s voice came again, clearer this time: “He wore a scar across his forearm. Dark brown tunic, too still for the chaos.” My breath hitched, the rhythm of my heel stuttering. The scar—Julian’s scar. The deck in my hand felt suddenly alive, the Petition Fate card warm as if it had been touched by the sun. The copper-haired girl’s copper braid darted through the crowd, leading me toward the ship’s hull where the water lapped hungrily at the broken wood. The Dohkar’s shadow lengthened, his silence heavier than any accusation. Somewhere in the press, Julian waited. And the chains had already answered what was sworn.

The ship's deck under a pale moonlight creaked with each careful step I took toward the Dohkar, my fingers already worrying the hem of my cloak in that old habit I could never quite break. The salt air stung my eyes, and the deck pressed lightly against my side as I moved, a steady reminder of the weight I carried even now. The Dohkar stood with his back half-turned, his voice low as he addressed the gathered witnesses, and I forced the words out before courage could slip away.

"What ruling will you give on this matter?" I asked, the question hanging between us like an unfinished spell.

He paused, his head tilting slightly as if weighing the cost of an answer. The moonlight caught the edge of his sleeve, and for a moment the only sound was the slow lap of water against the hull. My pulse thudded in my throat. I had spoken plainly, yet the air felt thinner for it.

The Chayr leaned forward from his place at the edge of the circle, his presence dominating the space until the deck itself seemed smaller beneath his boots. His piercing gaze felt like a blade slicing through her defenses, cold and precise, and I felt the weight of every eye turn with it. He did not speak at once. Instead his hand rose, deliberate and unhurried, palm open in the ancient signal that marked the formal opening of an inquiry.

The gesture cut through the night like a drawn line. No one moved. The Dohkar lowered his head in immediate deference, and the crowd's murmur died at once. My fingers tightened on the cloak hem until the fabric bunched. The Chayr's raised hand remained suspended, a silent command that left no room for retreat or evasion.

I stood exposed beneath the moonlight, the question I had asked now trapped in the open air between us. The deck shifted faintly under a sudden gust, and I knew the next word spoken would belong to him or to me. The Chayr's gaze held mine without mercy, waiting for the defense I had not yet offered.

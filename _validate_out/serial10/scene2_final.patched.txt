The Dohkar’s voice cut across the square like a blade through sailcloth.The Dohkar named the blood before the chains, the way he always measured a debt. “The blood answers what was sworn. The chains remember the line that bound them.”I stepped forward before the silence could settle into accusation. “It was not meant to summon them,” I said, the words coming faster than I intended. “Seren offered her own price. I only tried to steady the rite when the current turned.”Quinn’s gaze flicked to me, then away. The crowd’s attention shifted with it, a dry cough from somewhere in the back ranks. My heel pressed harder against the plank. The old woman beside me muttered something about blank pages and open prices.Julian’s face rose in my mind—the long scar across his forearm catching the light when he had once reached past me to close a hatch. He had moved like the motion cost him nothing, yet the scar told another story. I pushed the image down.Seren remained kneeling, pale eyes fixed on nothing. The Dohkar’s boot scraped once against the stone, and the air between his fingers tightened, the space itself seeming to resist the gesture. Salt crusted the cuff of my sleeve where it brushed the deck. The tremor ran up my arm, and a gull’s wing clipped the rope overhead, sharp and dry, like rope drawn too tight.The Dohkar’s next words took shape as they left him—a faint shimmer that hung where the sound had been, the syllables refusing to fade into the salt air. Every face turned from the wrecked ship to that visible thread of sound.I felt the weight of their stares settle on my shoulders. The lineage the chains had answered was mine, and the price had already been paid in front of them. To speak now would bind me to the interference they already suspected. To stay silent would let the assumption stand.The Dohkar’s shadow shortened across the plank. The shimmer lingered a moment longer, then thinned into nothing.I realize that old tarot deck is still in my hand. Reassuringly solid. Present. One of the heavy gold cards slips out — “Petition Fate” is inscribed in it. If only the Dohkar’s words had stayed unspoken. No. I would ask to know whether my lineage truly called the chains or whether someone else’s silence let them answer.

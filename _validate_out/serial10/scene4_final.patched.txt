The ship's deck under a pale moonlight creaked with each careful step I took toward the Dohkar, my fingers already worrying the hem of my cloak in that old habit I could never quite break. The salt air stung my eyes, and the deck pressed lightly against my side as I moved, a steady reminder of the weight I carried even now. The Dohkar stood with his back half-turned, his voice low as he addressed the gathered witnesses, and I forced the words out before courage could slip away."What ruling will you give on this matter?" I asked, the question pulling the air taut between us.He paused, his head tilting slightly as if weighing the cost of an answer. The moonlight caught the edge of his sleeve, and for a moment the only sound was the slow lap of water against the hull. A gull cried once above the mast, the sound thin and far away. I had spoken plainly, yet the air felt thinner for it.The Chayr leaned forward from his place at the edge of the circle, the planks drawing tighter beneath his boots. His eyes tracked the vein at my wrist as it jumped once, and I felt the weight of every eye turn with it. He did not speak at once. Instead, his hand rose, deliberate and unhurried, palm open in the ancient signal that marked the formal opening of an inquiry.The gesture held the night still. No one moved. The Dohkar lowered his head in immediate deference, and the crowd's murmur died at once. Salt crystals scraped under my thumbnail where the hem had frayed. The Chayr's raised hand remained suspended, and the air around it would not yield.I stood exposed beneath the moonlight, the question I had asked now lying open between us. The deck shifted faintly under a sudden gust, and I knew the next word spoken would belong to him or to me. The Chayr's gaze held mine without mercy, waiting for the defense I had not yet offered.

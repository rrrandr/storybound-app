The ship's deck lay slick and yielding beneath my boots, each step a muted protest against the wood as I edged away from the Chayr's suspended hand. The salt air settled on my tongue with every breath, a familiar taste that mingled with the fog thickening around me, blurring the railings into shadows. I had no clear path, only the urgent need to reach Seren before another word could bind us tighter to the Dohkar's inquiry. The deck pressed lightly against my side, a steady reminder of the weight I carried even now.A figure shifted in the murk ahead, and my pulse quickened before my eyes could name it. Julian stood half-turned from the mast, his angular face catching what little light the moons offered, the long scar across his forearm a pale line against the slate-blue of his skin. His gaze found the scar at my wrist before I could draw the cloak closer.I kept walking, the fog swallowing the Chayr's outline behind me. Seren's voice had come from this direction earlier, and I told myself that distance might ease the pressure at my throat. The planks groaned once under a shift in the wind, and I heard the faint creak of something unsteady near the rail.Julian moved to follow or to block; I could not tell which. His hand turned the ring on his finger once, the metal catching a stray gleam, then he reached for the lantern swinging from its hook. The motion appeared deliberate until it faltered. The lantern tipped, struck the rail, and oil spilled in a dark slick across the deck, spreading toward the coiled ropes and the edge of the circle where the Dohkar waited.The scent of burning wick rose sharp and immediate. Julian stood over the spill, the silver Weave-Script on his skin flaring once in the sudden flare of the remaining flame, and the fog closed what little space remained between the three of us. Silence enveloped us. The oil crept closer to the mast, and the choice I had carried toward Seren now pressed against my ribs as I stood between the fire and the Chayr's raised hand.I could stamp the flame out with my boot and keep the truth of his presence here unspoken, or I could call the Dohkar's attention to the spill and let the oil carry its own accusation. The deck shifted faintly beneath us, and the fog swallowed the sound of water against the hull, a reminder of the fragile balance we all teetered upon.

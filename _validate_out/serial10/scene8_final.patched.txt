The ship's deck lay shrouded in twilight shadows, the boards still warm from the day's sun as the Dohkar drew nearer in a slow, deliberate line. I stood rigid near the railing, my fingers trembling against the salt-worn wood, and I did not speak. The weight of their approach pressed on the air between us, but I held my silence until one of them named me first.The Chayr halted a stride away, the ceremonial sash across their chest catching the last pale light. Their voice carried without haste, each word shaped by long habit—the same habit they used only after they had already chosen to let the claim stand."Lirael of the lost line, the one whose blood carries the claim of a benefactor long hidden from the order."The revelation settled across the deck; the last pale light on the boards held without shifting. Murmurs rose from the gathered Favored, soft and uneven, yet the Chayr continued without pause, naming the distant lineage and the unseen hand that had once shaped my path through the rites. The Arcane Binding rested between us on the planks, its patterns still faintly luminous, the artifact's presence a quiet demand that the truth be met rather than turned aside.Julian remained a short distance to my left, his slate-blue skin marked by the silver weave-script that shifted with each measured breath. His hand turned the ring on his finger once, the metal catching what little light remained. He watched the Chayr, then me, the long scar on his forearm visible where the gossamer sleeve had slipped. The crowd edged closer without crossing the circle the Dohkar had formed."You stand there as if the words change nothing," he said, his voice low and edged with that familiar skeptical warmth. "Half this deck is favor-rotted fools who couldn't tell a true claim from a stolen one."The question lingered behind my ribs, sharp and immediate, while the Dohkar waited and the shadows lengthened across the boards. The Chayr's hand remained raised, the rite held open, and the weight of the named lineage refused to lift.

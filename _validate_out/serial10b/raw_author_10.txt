The Chayr leaned forward across the polished stone table, his heavy jaw set like a man who had already decided the cost of my question. “You ask what the ruling will be,” he said, voice low enough to carry only to the nearest witnesses. “Then speak plainly before the inquiry begins.”

My fingers caught the edge of my sleeve and twisted once, the sigil on my palm flaring against the stone where my hand rested. The table’s surface drank the light, its veined surface cool and unyielding beneath my skin. I felt the weight of the assembly shift, every gaze narrowing on the silver lines that refused to fade.

The Dohkar stood at the head of the table, his stillness drawing the room tighter around him. He watched the sigil pulse once more, then raised his hand, palm outward, the gesture cutting through the charged air like a seal pressed into wax.

The inquiry begins.

The flickering lanterns cast long, restless shadows across the polished stone, their flames bending as though the air itself recoiled from the sigil still burning on my palm. I needed to reach Seren alone, to ask what she had seen in my face when the wish left her and the cost settled on me instead. The assembly’s eyes pressed like a second band across my mouth.

I stepped toward her, the deck warm against my side. It was always warm. Chayr’s voice cut the space between us before I could reach her. “The rite is not concluded. You will remain where the Dohkar can see you.”

Julian stood at the edge of the circle, a man whose guarded expression betrayed his internal struggle to connect, hazel eyes already darkening toward green as they fixed on the silver lines across my skin. His stillness felt like a held breath. The Dohkar’s band still muffled his mouth, yet the weight of his attention was unmistakable.

My fingers fumbled at the edge of the table for balance, and the scroll I had carried since the threshold slipped free. It unrolled across the stone with a soft rasp, the sigil on my palm flaring in answer to the one drawn in ink on the parchment. Julian’s name was written there in the old script, the same hand that had guided my first lessons.

The Dohkar’s eyes narrowed. Chayr leaned forward, palms flattening on the table as though he might press the truth back into the wood. Seren’s coral hair shifted as she lifted her head, but she said nothing. The remaining lanterns guttered, shadows leaping higher.

Julian’s hand turned the ring on his finger once, the metal catching the failing light. No one spoke. The sigil on my skin pulsed once more, bright enough that every gaze in the room found the name on the scroll.

The choice sat between confronting him and hiding the truth, and the lanterns were already beginning to die.

The flickering lanterns cast jagged shadows that swallowed the sigil on my palm whole, the silver threads pulsing like a second heartbeat beneath my skin. Before I could steady my breath, Chayr’s voice sliced through the haze of the room—cold, calibrated, the kind that made men lean in and women second-guess. "That trinket stays with the college until the inquiry is concluded." His gaze flicked from the sigil to my face, assessing the crack in my composure before I could school my expression. The deck against my ribs thrummed once, warm as a living thing, and I realized my fingers had curled around it without thought.

Chayr’s hand settled on the polished stone table, his palm brushing the silver lines that had burned their way into my skin mere moments before. "You took an oath, Sacrificiant," he reminded me, voice low and threaded with something almost like patience. "The college does not ask for what is freely given." His fingers curled, as if already claiming the deck against my ribs. The sigil flared brighter, the edges bleeding into the air like ink in water. A breath escaped me before I could catch it—half protest, half wonder.

The lanterns guttered.

One by one, the flickering flames died not with a sputter but with a sound like paper tearing, plunging us into darkness so absolute the sigil on my palm became a second sun. Chayr’s grip found my wrist instead of the deck, his thumb pressing into the pulse at the base of my hand. "Speak," he ordered, and the single syllable echoed in the void. The deck’s warmth flared against my side, then against his, as though it had chosen this moment to remember he existed. His breath hit the shell of my ear—close, too close—and I knew, without seeing, that his Weave-Script had gone dark with recognition.

"Julian," I whispered.

The name hung between us, unsanctioned, unspoken in this room where only sanctioned truths were permitted. Chayr’s hold tightened not in anger but in something like triumph. "So it begins," he murmured. The lanterns relit themselves then, one by one, as though the darkness itself had recoiled from the syllable. The sigil on my palm pulsed once more, steady and unyielding, and I understood that whatever I had just done, I could not undo it.

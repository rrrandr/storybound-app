The tapestry-draped alcove beckoned, its woven threads catching the faintest spill of lantern light like embers from a dying fire. The ruby spiralgrass threads ran in deliberate spirals across the fabric, the same hue as the mark still smoldering on my palm. My fingers hovered above the edge, the locket at my throat warm against the chill creeping up my spine. The silver lines burning into my skin pulsed in time with the heartbeat I refused to name.

A breath caught in my throat.

The tapestry wasn’t merely draped—it was *woven* with intention, the ruby threads forming a sigil I recognized before my mind could catch up. Not the careful strokes of a scribe’s brush, but something older, something that had grown from the earth itself. My palm stilled above it, the heat of the sigil bleeding through the silk like a brand. *This* was what Seren had seen when the wish left her lips. *This* was what had shifted in her silence.

The lanterns flickered, their flames bending away as though the air itself recoiled. Shadows pooled deeper in the alcove, and for one steady moment, I swore the weave of the tapestry *shifted*—the sigil rearranging itself into something more familiar. My breath came faster. The sigil on my skin flared again, bright as a warning.

Seren remained kneeling at the edge of the gathering room, coral hair veiling her face, but I caught the tilt of her chin. She was watching. Waiting. The Dohkar stood beside her, his band still muffling his mouth, but his Weave-Script burned silver across his knuckles—anger, or fear, or both.

I reached for the tapestry.

The silk resisted, then gave way with a whisper. The sigil on the fabric *moved*—not the gentle pulse of my own, but a deliberate shift, as though it had been waiting for my touch. My fingers trembled. The silver lines on my palm flared in answer, and the tapestry’s woven threads *unraveled* before my eyes, the ruby spiralgrass snapping into place around a name.

Julian.

Not his face. Not his voice. Just the name, inked in thread so fine it might have been spun from moonlight. The same hand that had guided my first lessons. The same hand that had signed my scroll of attendance at the Veilwood Collegium. The same hand that had once caught mine when I stumbled on the steps of the Archive Hall, and kept it there just a second too long.

A laugh bubbled up in my chest, unexpected and bitter. Of course it was his name. Who else would it be?

The tapestry’s threads writhed, the sigil contracting around the name like a fist. My palm burned. The sigil on my skin answered, the silver lines threading outward, reaching for the name on the fabric. The air between them crackled, charged with something older than magic, older than wishcraft.

I pressed my hand to the tapestry.

The silk stilled. The sigil on my palm *burned*—not with pain, but with recognition. The threads of the tapestry *aligned*, the ruby spiralgrass weaving together into a single, unbroken line that led from the name to the sigil on my skin. The connection pulsed once, bright as a strike of lightning.

And then—

The lanterns guttered. The flames bent away from the alcove as though fleeing. The sigil on my palm flared white-hot, and the tapestry *split* down the middle, the ruby threads falling away like embers. The name on the fabric remained, suspended in the air for one impossible second, before dissolving into the shadows.

A gasp cut through the silence. Seren’s head snapped up, her coral hair spilling back from her face. Her eyes were wide, her Weave-Script flickering rapid silver—excitement, or terror, or both.

Then she saw me.

The Dohkar turned. Chayr’s weight settled heavier on the table, his polished stone creaking under his palms. His gaze locked onto the space where the tapestry had been, then to me, then to the sigil still smoldering on my palm. His expression darkened like storm clouds gathering over the Veilwood.

“You,” he said, voice low and dangerous. “You know what this means.”

I didn’t answer. Couldn’t. The sigil on my palm pulsed again, and this time, it *burned*—not just in light, but in memory. A fragment of a dream I’d never had. A name I hadn’t spoken in years. A bargain I’d made and forgotten.

Julian’s voice, soft in the dark: *“You’re thinking too loudly.”*

My breath stuttered. The memory wasn’t mine. It wasn’t anyone’s I knew. But it *ached* in my chest, familiar as an old wound.

The Dohkar’s band muffled the sound of his inhale. “The Chayr has called for inquiry,” he said. “You will remain where you can be seen.”

I didn’t move. The locket at my throat grew warmer, as though the sigil on my palm had seeped into the silver, into the metal, into the chain. The tapestry’s threads lay scattered on the floor, the name gone, the sigil gone. Only the ruby spiralgrass remained, glinting dully in the lantern light.

Then the doors at the far end of the room opened.

Silence shattered.

Every head turned. Every breath held. The air itself seemed to bow.

Julian stood framed in the doorway, slate-blue skin catching the flickering light, long black hair spilling past his shoulders like a shadow given form. His gossamer tunic caught the light in a way that made the silver Weave-Script on his skin *dance*—a pattern I’d always read as caution, but tonight looked like an invitation. His hand turned the ring on his finger once, the metal catching the light with deliberate precision.

His gaze found me immediately.

Not the Dohkar. Not Chayr. Not Seren.

Me.

The sigil on my palm flared white-hot again, the heat searing up my arm, into my chest, into the locket at my throat. The ruby spiralgrass threads on the floor *twitched*, as though pulled by unseen hands. The lanterns flared once, then dimmed, as though the room itself had drawn a breath.

Julian’s eyes dropped to my mouth. Stayed there. One count too long.

A shiver ran down my spine. The sigil on my palm pulsed in time with the beat of my heart, and I knew—without knowing how—that he had seen it too. Had *felt* it too. The connection between us wasn’t just the tapestry’s threads. It was something older. Something dangerous.

Chayr exhaled sharply. “Julian,” he said, voice tight. “You’re late.”

Julian didn’t look at him. His gaze never left mine. “I don’t keep time,” he said, voice low and rich, the words curling around the silence like smoke. “I keep promises.”

The sigil on my palm flared again. The locket at my throat burned against my skin. The tapestry’s threads on the floor *twitched* once more, as though the room itself was holding its breath.

And I realized—

He wasn’t here for the inquiry.

He wasn’t here for the Dohkar.

He was here for me.

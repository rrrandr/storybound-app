The cracked wooden floor beneath her knees groaned under the faintest shift of weight, the grain dark with old oil and something else—something like the sigil still smoldering against her palm. I pressed my fingers into the wood, testing its solidity, as if the floor could anchor me when the light pooling from the sigil’s name on the scroll threatened to dissolve everything else. The tapestry-draped alcove swallowed the worst of the lantern glow, its heavy folds catching every flicker and turning it into long, uncertain lines across the polished stone. Shadows stretched too thin in the corners, like they were pulling away from something.

A sound like a held breath escaped me. The deck dug into my ribs, its edge nudging against my side with every breath I took. Warm. Familiar. A quiet reminder that some truths could not be wished away twice. The sigil flared, brighter this time, the silver threads across my skin answering with a pulse that vibrated through my bones. The name inked on the scroll beneath it—Julian’s name, the same hand that had guided my first lessons—burned into my vision. The Dohkar’s band-muffled mouth moved soundlessly from his place at the table, his fingers white-knuckled against the wood. Chayr’s gaze never left my mouth, heavy with expectation. The assembly held their breath, the room a held thing.

Somewhere beyond the alcove, the floor creaked—a deep, resonant groan that cut through the silence like a blade. My pulse stuttered. The sound came again, closer this time, like footsteps dragging across the planks. Or like the floor itself shifting beneath something that did not belong. I pressed my palm flat against the stone, the sigil’s light flickering against the cracks in the wood. The warmth there was wrong. It should have cooled by now. Should have settled into something like ash.

Julian’s voice cut through the quiet before the footsteps could answer back. Not a shout. Not an accusation. Just his low, edged tone, the words aimed at the Dohkar rather than me. “This whole assembly is a soyle-brained fool that couldn’t bind a shadow.” His stillness drew every breath in the room toward him, a gravity that held us captive. The scar at the corner of his left eye caught the failing light, a thin line against the brightness. His hazel eyes had gone green in the sudden glare, fixed on the sigil with something like hunger. Or fear. The metal of his ring turned once, the flash of it catching my eye, a flicker of resolve.

The Dohkar’s hands flattened harder against the table, the wood yielding to the weight of his silence. Chayr leaned forward, palms pressing into the stone. Seren’s coral hair shifted as she lifted her head, but her silence still spoke volumes. The remaining lanterns guttered, their flames bending away from the sigil’s name like it was something that burned. The air thickened, heavy with the weight of something unspoken. The sigil pulsed once more, bright enough that every gaze in the room found the name on the scroll. A shared acknowledgment. A shared fear.

The floor groaned again, closer now. Not footsteps. Not even a creak. The wood itself seemed to shift, the cracks widening just enough to let something slip through. A draft. A whisper. Or the floor remembering something it had forgotten. My fingers curled into the wood, the splinters biting into my skin. The deck’s edge pressed harder against my ribs, a silent insistence. I could almost hear the assembly’s thoughts—*She knows. She’s hiding it. She’s lying.* The sigil’s glow cast my shadow long across the tapestry, stretching it toward the door like an offering. Or a warning.

The choice had already been made for me. The only question left was whether I would speak his name aloud before the Dohkar found the will to silence the room again. Or whether I would let the floor swallow me first.

Julian stood at the edge of the circle, his long dark hair loose, the silver lines on his slate-blue skin catching the same unsteady light. His stillness was the only fixed point in the room.

I kept my palm flat against the wood, the sigil’s heat bleeding into the planks. “Tell me what the wish truly meant,” I said, voice low but clear enough to carry. “Not the words she spoke. The truth that twisted them.”

The Dohkar’s band-muffled mouth moved, but no sound escaped. Chayr’s hands stayed planted on the table, the wood creaking under his weight. Seren had not moved from her place on the floor, coral hair falling forward to hide her face. The sigil flared again, brighter, and the nearest figures shifted back half a step. Whispers began—soft, overlapping, the sound of cloth against cloth as people leaned in.

Julian’s fingers turned the ring on his left hand once. The metal caught the light and released it. He did not look at the Dohkar. He looked at the sigil, at the name, at the space between them.

“The Dohkar is a prick about the rite. Massive one.”

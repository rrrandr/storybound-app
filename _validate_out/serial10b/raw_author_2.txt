The Dohkar’s hand stayed frozen, the weight of the assembly pressing down like a held breath. Every eye tracked the torn band in my fingers, the way it fluttered once before I let it fall. I opened my mouth to speak, to say that Seren had offered what she offered because she thought it would help, that the wish had never been mine to shape. The words were still forming when my hand shook.

A sigil bloomed across my palm, silver lines bright against the skin, the same mark that should have stayed sealed beneath the Sacrificiant band. It pulsed once, clear to every witness, and the room’s stillness cracked.

An older woman near the veil-canopy spoke first, voice low and edged. “You asked Fate for a fool. You never named who.”

Heat climbed my throat. The sigil burned like a brand I had not placed there. I closed my fingers over it, but the light leaked between them, and the Dohkar’s gaze sharpened, reading the alignment I could no longer hide. I had not spoken the wish. I had only moved. The room had seen.

Julian’s face rose in my mind, the small silver scar at the corner of his left eye catching what little light reached him, the same stillness he carried before any laugh. He would know what this meant. He always knew what the Fold had taken from the record, and what House Dathros still guarded. I wanted him to step forward. I wanted him to stay away. Both hung in the same breath.

The Dohkar waited. The assembly waited. The choice sat between my teeth like a stone I had not meant to swallow.

I realize that old tarot deck is still in my hand. Reassuringly solid. Present. One of the heavy gold cards slips out — “Petition Fate” is inscribed in it.

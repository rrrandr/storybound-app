The heavy oak table groaned as I leaned harder against it, the scrolls beneath my palms curling at the edges from heat that had nothing to do with the lantern light. The silver ribbons of the sigil on my palm skittered across the wood like living things, chasing the name printed on the parchment at my knees. I kept my mouth sealed, my breath steady, my fingers pressed flat against the grain. The name pulsed once, twice, a slow heartbeat of something I refused to say aloud.

A shadow detached itself from the alcove's edge, moving forward with deliberate slowness. The tapestry's heavy folds swayed but did not part enough to let the lantern light touch the newcomer's face—only the sharp line of a jaw, the sheen of slate-blue skin catching the dim glow. "You have my attention," the figure said, voice low and rough as old parchment. "Now give me yours."

I turned my head just enough to meet the gaze across the table. The silver lines of the sigil flared brighter where they met my skin, and the air between us thickened with something older than iron vows. "I will not respond until my voice is acknowledged," I said, the words dragging like a blade through silk.

The Interloper's mouth twitched—just once, a flicker of irritation that smoothed into something colder. Their fingers, pale against the dark wood, tapped once against the table's surface. A count. One. "You mistake the nature of this gathering, Sacrificiant. This is not a negotiation. This is an accounting." Their gaze flicked to the scroll at my knees, then back to me. "The Dohkar's silence is not yours to command. The name on that parchment belongs to someone who has already paid the price for speaking it. You will not be so fortunate."

The ribbons of light from the sigil danced faster now, skittering toward the scroll as if pulled by invisible threads. I felt the floor shift beneath my ribs, the way it always did when the world leaned too close to a truth it wasn't ready to name. "Then let the Dohkar speak it himself," I said. "Or are you so afraid of the name that you hide behind riddles?"

A collective inhale rippled through the room. Someone's heel scraped against the floorboards. The Interloper's fingers stilled. For the first time, I saw the truth of them—not in their face, which remained stubbornly in shadow, but in the way their Weave-Script dimmed along the bones of their wrist, a flicker of something like discomfort. "You think this is about fear?" they asked, softer now. "You think I haven't stood where you stand, with a sigil burning in my palm and a name carved into my mind?" Their voice dropped to a whisper. "The Dohkar is a prick about the rite. Massive one."

The silver light on my palm flared white-hot. The scroll beneath my fingers curled inward, the ink bleeding like a wound. I looked down at the name and saw Seren's careful script, the letters trembling as if still wet. My ward. My failure. The sigil's light carved her name into the air between us, and I understood, with a clarity that tasted like ash, that this was not about the Dohkar's pride. This was about what Seren had given to make the wish hold.

The Interloper's shadow leaned closer, the heat of their breath raising gooseflesh along my arms. "You have until the light burns out," they murmured. "Then I will read the name myself. And you will watch as the price is taken from someone who cannot afford it."

I curled my fingers into fists, the sigil's light searing my skin. The deck at my side pressed against my thigh, its gold edges biting through the thin fabric. "You don't understand," I said, voice steady despite the way my pulse hammered in my throat. "This isn't about the price. It's about what the price buys." I let the silence stretch between us, let it fill with the weight of memory and the cost of silence. "Ask yourself what you're really willing to name."

The Interloper's Weave-Script brightened along their collarbone, a pulse of something like surprise. The name on the scroll burned brighter, the letters rearranging themselves into something else entirely. The floorboards groaned under the collective weight of the gathering, the sound like a held breath finally released. Somewhere beyond the tapestry, a floorboard popped under Julian's heel—once, twice—like a heartbeat counting down.

I didn't look away from the Interloper. Didn't flinch at the way the sigil's light now cast my shadow long and broken across the tapestry, like a thing split in two. The name on the scroll was no longer Seren's. It was mine. And the price for speaking it had already begun to bleed through my skin.

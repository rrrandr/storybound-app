The heavy oak table covered in scrolls creaked as Julian’s thumb turned the ring on his finger, the metal catching the sigil’s thin light. “Lirael, the Dohkar demands your voice. The assembly will not stand here forever while you withhold what the sigil shows.”

My fingers trembled over the scrolls, the edge of one curling under my touch. I kept my palm flat, the sigil’s heat still bleeding into the wood, and said nothing. The Dohkar’s band-muffled mouth worked once, then stilled. The weight of waiting pressed against every breath in the room.

Julian’s gaze did not waver. “You said you would not answer until your voice was acknowledged. It has been. Now speak.”

A shadow detached from the far wall. The figure moved with deliberate slowness, boots scraping the grain, and stepped into the circle of light. The interloper’s eyes scanned the room like a hawk watching for prey, one hand resting on the hilt of a short blade at their hip. They stopped at the table’s edge, close enough that the sigil’s silver ribbons brushed the hem of their cloak.

“The name on the scroll is not Seren’s alone,” the figure said, voice low. “It carries a thread that leads back through your own bloodline, Lirael. The Dohkar knows it. So do I.”

The room’s whispers died. Julian’s hand stilled on the ring. The interloper’s eyes met mine, and the hidden connection to the sigil hung between us like a drawn blade. I felt the choice settle in my chest: confront the figure now, or turn and flee the revelation before it could root deeper.

Julian’s mouth curved, not quite a smile. “The Dohkar is a prick about the rite. Massive one.”

Is this something to lean into or something to move past? 

The sigil pulsed once, brighter, as if answering the figure’s claim. The scrolls beneath my hand shifted with the table’s slow creak, and the assembly’s eyes turned to me, waiting for the voice I had refused to give until it was forced into the open.

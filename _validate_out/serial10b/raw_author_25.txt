The flickering lanterns along the chamber walls cast long, wavering shadows that stretched like uncertain oaths across the scarred oak table. Their light caught the silver edges of the sigil still burning on my palm and threw them over the unfurled scrolls in restless patterns, as if even the flame could not decide what truth it would allow to stand.

I kept my hand flat against the wood, the heat from the mark seeping into the grain. The assembly’s whispers had thinned to a low current, bare feet shifting on the cracked boards, but no one stepped forward to claim the name on the scroll at my knees. The Dohkar’s band-muffled mouth worked soundlessly. Seren remained still on the floor, coral hair veiling her face, her Weave-Script dim and steady.

Julian stood at the edge of the circle, his long dark hair loose about his shoulders, the silver lines on his slate-blue skin catching the unsteady glow. He watched the sigil, not me, his hazel eyes deepening to green as his gaze flicked once to my lips before returning to the silver lines. The scar at the corner of his left eye held the light without wavering. His thumb and forefinger turned the signet ring on his finger in slow, unconscious motion, the metal flashing once.

“The rite is a massive prick about its rules,” he said, voice low and edged with deliberate calm. The words landed like a test offered under the guise of complaint. Is this something to lean into or something to move past?

The sigil’s light did not dim. It held steady, merciless, until the name on the scroll beneath it refused to be ignored. I felt my fingers rise without permission and twist a strand of my hair once, the small motion betraying the unease I would not name. Julian’s gaze lingered on the gesture a fraction longer than necessary before he looked away.

The whispers thickened again. Someone at the back edged toward the tapestry-draped alcove. Another leaned close to a companion, mouth shaping words too quiet to catch. Julian did not move. His hand turned the ring once more, the motion precise, anchoring.

I shifted my weight, the floorboard beneath my bare sole creaking, and the sigil’s heat pressed upward through the wood as if urging me closer to the table’s edge. The scrolls lay scattered, edges curling in the uneven light. One lay half-hidden beneath the others, its corner folded as if deliberately tucked away. My fingers brushed the parchment. The motion was small, hidden by the angle of my body, but the paper slid free with a soft rasp that seemed louder than the whispers.

The letter inside bore no seal, only a single line in a hand I knew too well: the same slant Julian used when he recorded rites. The words were brief, almost casual, yet they named the sigil’s origin and the sacrifice it had demanded. My breath caught. The name on the scroll and the name in the letter aligned too cleanly to be chance.

Julian’s ring turned again, the metal catching the lantern light. He had not seen me take the letter. Not yet. The choice formed in the space between us—speak the name aloud and force the confrontation, or keep the letter and learn what else he had hidden. The sigil’s glow pulsed once, as if Fate itself waited on the answer I had not yet given.

The air between the table and the door thinned, carrying the residue of what had been spoken and what had not.

The velvet drapes muffled the assembly’s whispers, swallowing sound into deep crimson folds that trembled with every breath of air drawn through cracks in the walls. I kept my palm flat against the table, the sigil’s silver glow burning a restless pattern onto the scarred oak beneath my fingers. Its light seeped into the grain, pressing the silver deeper until the edges blurred—like ink spreading without control. The heat climbed my arm, coiling around my wrist like a second pulse. I didn’t dare lift my hand. Not yet.

A shadow shifted across the floorboards, too deliberate to be the flicker of lantern light. My breath hitched in my throat. The Dohkar’s band-muffled mouth worked soundlessly behind her own veil, her silver Weave-Script trembling in anxious loops. Seren remained still upon the floor, her coral hair veiling her face, her own script dim and steady. She had not moved since the sigil first took hold. I envied that stillness.

The torn piece of parchment lay half-hidden beneath the others, its corner folded as if tucked away by deliberate hands. Before I could stop myself, my fingers brushed against it. The motion was small, concealed by the angle of my body, yet the paper slid free with a soft rasp that seemed louder than the whispers. I didn’t unfold it. Not yet. My pulse hammered in my throat, each beat pressing the sigil into my palm with deliberate insistence.

Julian stood at the edge of the circle, his slate-blue skin catching the lantern glow. His long dark hair hung loose around his shoulders, framing the scar at the corner of his left eye. When his gaze lifted to meet mine, his hazel eyes deepened to green—just for a heartbeat—before flicking down to the sigil on my palm. The pen between his fingers paused mid-rotation, as if he had forgotten it existed. He said nothing. The silence between us felt heavier than the assembly’s whispers.

Across the room, someone shifted on the floorboards. A bare foot caught the light, revealing skin marked with the faintest silver trace of Weave-Script—emotion rendered visible. The strands pulsed warm orange, betraying anxious hope. I recognized the pattern. It belonged to the youngest assistant in the room, the one who had once asked if I trusted Julian. I had not answered her then. Now, the question settled in my chest like a stone.

The torn parchment crinkled under my fingertips. I turned my wrist slightly, letting the torn edge catch the light. A single line stood out in Julian’s handwriting—his slant unmistakable, the same one he used when recording rites. The words were brief, almost casual, yet they named the sigil’s origin and the sacrifice it had demanded. My breath caught so sharply the sigil flared in response, silver light flaring across the table as if struck.

Julian’s heel pressed once against the floorboard. The wood gave a short creak. He had not seen me take the letter. Not yet.

The sigil’s glow pulsed again, narrowing to a single point on the scroll before me. The room seemed to lean in, breath suspended. My fingers tightened around the parchment, the edges biting into my skin. I could speak the name aloud now. Force the confrontation. Demand answers. Or I could keep the letter, hide it, and unearth what else he had concealed.

Julian’s gaze returned to the sigil, then flickered to my lips for the briefest moment. The scar at the corner of his eye held the light without wavering. His pen resumed its rotation, slow and deliberate. The motion felt like a promise—or a warning.

The torn parchment trembled in my grip. The sigil on my palm burned brighter, its silver threads weaving tighter. I felt my fingers rise without permission, twisting a strand of my hair once, the small motion betraying the unease I would not name. Julian’s gaze lingered on the gesture a fraction longer than necessary before he looked away. The whispers thickened again. Someone at the back edged toward the tapestry-draped alcove. Another leaned close to a companion, mouth shaping words too quiet to catch.

I shifted my weight, the floorboard beneath my bare sole creaking. The sigil’s heat pressed upward through the wood, the grain under my palm shifting as though the table leaned toward the edge. The letter inside bore no seal, only the line in Julian’s hand. The words aligned too cleanly with the name still burning on the scroll at my knees.

Julian’s gaze stayed fixed on the sigil, his expression unreadable. The pen in his fingers had stilled completely.

The torn parchment burned against my palm. The sigil’s glow pulsed once more, the light narrowing to a single point on the scroll as the room held its breath. The air between the table and the door thinned, carrying the residue of what had been spoken and what had not.

In that moment, the sigil's glow flickered, revealing a hidden artifact beneath the table—the Arcane Binding, a shimmering chain of energy that pulsed with the weight of ancient magic. Its presence shifted the air, a reminder of the laws governing our actions and the power struggles entwined in our fates.

As I grasped the letter tighter, the Arcane Binding seemed to resonate with my unease, urging me to confront the truth that lay between Julian and me.

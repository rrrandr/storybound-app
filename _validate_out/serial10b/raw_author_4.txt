The threshold loomed before me, the spiralgrass darkening at its edge where the sigil had burned its way into the ground. The Dohkar’s hand remained frozen mid-gesture, his eyes fixed on the silver lines that had bloomed across my palm. Seren knelt still, her coral hair falling forward, her eyes glinting with that mix of challenge and encouragement that had always defined her guidance. The assembly pressed in, a held breath that threatened to break.

I stepped forward, the threshold giving way beneath my bare feet, the sigil on my palm pulsing like a second heartbeat. My voice trembled as I spoke, the words coming from a place I had not meant to reach. “I offer the memory of my first love, the one I lost.”

The room shifted, the older woman near the veil-canopy taking a half-step back, her Weave-Script flickering across her throat. The Dohkar’s gaze sharpened, reading the alignment that had twisted itself around my words. The sigil burned brighter, a visible mark that could not be hidden.

The choice hung there, the sacrifice named, the price claimed. To accept it was to lose the memory, to back away was to leave the wish unfulfilled, the room watching.

The deck was warm in my hand. It’s always warm.

A card shifted, heavier, as if it leaned toward the choice just made.

What if the sigil would simply fade, the Dohkar’s hand lowering as if nothing had happened? Petition Fate. That was what the card meant — small asks, small turnings.

I realize the deck is still in my hand. As I slide Petition Fate back in, I notice a flash of red in the otherwise gold stack. “Tempt Fate.” Grandma never let me play with that one. A sword raised to a blood-red sky, lightning arcing down from the clouds (or maybe arcing up?) Ow! Static shock reminds me I need to pay attention to the present…

The threshold yawned before me, its carved edges worn smooth by generations who had crossed into what the world now feared. The sigil on my palm pulsed once, cold fire against my skin. I flexed my fingers, watching the silver lines flare then dim, as if the mark itself breathed. The torn band from my mouth lay coiled on the crimson spiralgrass like a dead thing. I had not spoken the wish. I had only moved. The room had seen. The assembly's silence was a blade pressing against my throat. Then the Dohkar spoke, his voice a slow current dragging every eye toward me. “You offered what was never yours to shape,” he said. My pulse hammered against the sigil, demanding it explain itself. The crimson grass darkened at the edges where Seren's memory had been taken, a wound that would not stop bleeding. I wanted to step back, to vanish into the seams of the Fold where even Fate could not follow. Instead, I stepped forward. The sigil burned hotter. Seren stood too still near the veil-canopy, her coral hair catching what little light dared enter this place. She did not look at me. That was the cost of the wish I had not made. The Dohkar's hand lifted, palm open like he expected the sigil to surrender its meaning straight into his skin. “Explain,” he commanded, and the word settled into my bones like a second bone growing there. I wanted to refuse. I wanted to scream that the wish had been Seren's sacrifice, that the room had seen a lie. But the sigil's heat was climbing my arm, and the assembly's breath was a held thing waiting to shatter. Seren's fingers twitched at her sides, the only sign she still had a body. The older woman near the canopy finally broke her silence. “You asked Fate for a fool,” she said, voice low and certain. “You never named who.” The sigil flared white across my palm, and I knew then what it had cost me to stand here. Not years. Not memory. It had cost me the right to be ordinary. The Dohkar's eyes sharpened like flint edges. “The alignment is broken,” he murmured, and the words were a sentence I had not yet heard. The air thickened with the weight of what they all wanted—answers, control, a sacrifice that made sense. I wanted to give them none of it. I wanted to run until the sigil stopped burning. But the sigil was inside me now, and the Fold's edges were teeth closing around us all. Seren turned her head slightly, her gaze sliding past me as if I were already erased. That was the moment I understood. The wish hadn't been mine to shape. But the cost of unmaking it might belong to me. The Dohkar's palm still hovered, waiting. The sigil burned like a brand I couldn't remove. I wanted to understand what it meant. I wanted to refuse to understand. I wanted to be anywhere but here. The silence stretched, taut as a wire. Then the older woman spoke again, softer now. “The Devouring Eye watches,” she said. “And it is hungry.” The words settled into my marrow. I wanted to live. I wanted to keep the sigil from spreading. I wanted to know what Julian would say when he found me marked like this. The pressure was a vise around my ribs. The Dohkar's hand dropped. “Choose,” he said simply. Choose what? Choose to explain the unexplainable? Choose to let them take what was left of Seren? Choose to face the fact that Julian would know exactly what this meant? The sigil pulsed once, answering for me. I wanted to collapse. I wanted to step forward. I wanted to understand why my hand no longer felt like my own. The assembly's breath was a storm held at bay. Seren's stillness was the silence after a scream. The Dohkar's gaze was the choice itself. I stepped forward. The sigil flared one final time, then settled into a dull ache across my palm. The Dohkar nodded once, as if everything had made sense to him. It had not. It made less sense the closer I looked. I wanted to collapse into that understanding. Instead, I lifted my fingers from the deck still clutched in my opposite hand. I realize the deck is still in my hand. As I slide Petition Fate back in, I notice a flash of red in the otherwise gold stack. “Tempt Fate.” Grandma never let me play with that one.

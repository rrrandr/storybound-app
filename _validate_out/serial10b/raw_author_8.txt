The polished stone table was cold beneath my fingers, slick with something darker than dust. My breath hitched as I traced the sigil once more, its silver threads pulsing beneath my touch like a second heartbeat. The sigil that should have stayed sealed beneath the Sacrificiant band now branded my palm, a living mark that thrummed in time with the assembly’s held silence. The Dohkar’s frozen hand twitched—just once—before settling back into stillness, his gaze locked onto the impossible light leaking between my clenched fingers. The heat of it seared up my arm, settling like a brand in my throat.

Chayr’s presence entered the room before he did, the shift in air thick enough to taste. His perpetually knotted brow cast shadows that deepened when he leaned forward, the authority in his bearing demanding the room’s silence like a debt being called. Seren hadn’t moved from her kneel, but the way her coral hair caught the dim light showed the tension in her shoulders, the fingers of one hand curled into the spiralgrass as if it could anchor her to the ground. The sigil brightened with each pulse, its light turning the veins in my wrist silver, and I swallowed against the burn of something I hadn’t offered.

The memory came unbidden—a night in the Veilwood when Julian had pressed a kiss to the inside of my wrist, his mouth warm against skin that hadn’t yet carried a mark like this. *By the Thirteen,* I thought, jaw tightening. That was before he’d learned to read the Weave-Script like others read ledgers. Before the Fold had taken from both of us. The sigil flared brightest where his old scar had rested against mine, the skin there prickling as if the mark remembered him.

Chayr’s voice cut through the stillness, low and deliberate. “You asked Fate for a fool’s bargain, Sacrificiant.” His fingers drummed once against the table’s edge, the sound too sharp for the hush. “Or did Fate ask *you*?” The words hung between us, heavy with accusation and something else—an invitation to confess. The sigil burned brighter, its threads tightening like a noose, and I understood then: it wasn’t just a mark. It was a debt being called in.

Julian’s ring caught the light from somewhere unseen, turning a slow circle on his finger as he stepped into the doorway’s shadow. I didn’t see him. I *felt* him—the way the air around him always bent, the quiet certainty that he was measuring the room’s weight like a blade balanced on his palm. He’d never come to these gatherings before. Never needed to. The last time I’d seen him here, he’d been watching Seren’s First Sacrifice with an intensity that made the Dohkar’s gaze flick between them like a startled bird. The sigil burned hotter at his presence, as if it recognized him. Or perhaps it recognized the wish I hadn’t spoken aloud.

“Twelve witnesses,” Chayr murmured, counting each of us in the room. “Thirteen if we include the mark Fate chose to answer.” His boot scuffed against the floorboards, the sound too loud in the suspended moment. “Tell me, Sacrificiant—was it *your* wish that cracked the seal?” The question wasn’t a question. It was a gauntlet thrown between us, and the sigil’s glow answered for me, silver veins branching across my knuckles like frost on glass.

I closed my fist around the mark, hiding it from the assembly’s sight. The cost settled into my bones, heavier than memory—years of my grandmother’s stories, the sound of my mother’s voice reading from the deck by candlelight, the scent of spiralgrass in the Veilwood dawn. Julian’s ring turned again, slow and deliberate, as if he were counting the breaths I couldn’t take. The sigil didn’t extinguish. It *waited*.

The room held its breath. The mark burned. And somewhere in the silence, the Thirteenth Moon hung heavy above us all.

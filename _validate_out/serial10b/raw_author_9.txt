The polished stone table held the weight of every gaze in the room, its surface catching the faint silver lines still burning across my palm. My fingers traced the edge without thinking, the sigil’s pulse steady beneath them, and I felt the deck press lightly against my side as I shifted my stance.

Chayr leaned forward, his narrow eyes glinting with authority, the lines around his mouth drawn tight like someone who had already decided the cost before the question was asked. The Dohkar stood beside him, hand still half-raised from the moment the band tore, his Weave-Script dim and unreadable. I forced my other hand still, though it wanted to fidget with the edge of my sleeve, and met the Dohkar’s eyes.

“What ruling will come from this?” The words left me before I could weigh them.

The assembly’s silence thickened. Seren remained kneeling, coral hair veiling her face, but her posture had not changed since the wish left her lips. Chayr’s weight settled more heavily on the table, the polished stone creaking once under his palms. The Dohkar’s fingers curled, then lifted fully, palm outward, the gesture cutting the air like a blade drawn across a contract.

No one moved. The sigil on my skin answered the motion with a fresh thread of light, and I knew the inquiry had begun whether I spoke again or not. The choice sat in the space between my question and their answer, and I could not take the words back.

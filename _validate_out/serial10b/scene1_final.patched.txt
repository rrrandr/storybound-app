The Dohkar’s voice rolled through the clearing like slow water over stone. “Name the offering. Name the desire.”Seren knelt on the crimson spiralgrass and let her coral hair fall forward, as she always did when she needed the invocation we had rehearsed for months to read as though it had just occurred to her. The words came clean. I felt the alignment hold. Then she lifted her head and looked straight at me. “I offer my earliest memory of choosing this path,” she said, the words arriving in the same order she had practiced them whenever she wanted me to believe the choice had been hers alone. she said, voice steady, “so that Lirael finds the one she lost.”The assembly went still. The Dohkar’s hand froze mid-gesture. An older woman near the veil-canopy raised both palms as if warding a blow. “Never hand Fate a blank page,” she breathed. The spiralgrass at Seren’s knees darkened at the edges, a single patch turning the deep color of old blood while the rest stayed bright. Seren’s Weave-Script stuttered once across her throat, then steadied. She blinked, mouth still open on the last word, and I saw the moment the memory she had offered slipped its meaning. Her eyes stayed on me, but the recognition that had always lived behind them was gone. She had paid what she named, and the shape of why it had mattered no longer reached her. I tore the gossamer band from my mouth. The cloth came away with a soft rip that sounded louder than it should have. Every face turned. Julian’s name had already moved through the clearing once, quiet and heavy, the way it always did when anyone spoke of the Ashen Verge. I remembered the way he went still right before he laughed, like he was checking that it was allowed. The memory sat in my chest now, too warm and too sharp at once. The Dohkar looked at me, then at Seren, then back at the darkened grass. No one spoke the word that had bent. They did not need to. The wish had been spoken for me, and the room had seen me move. I didn’t remember bringing it.My hand tightened around my great-grandmother's old tarot deck. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.

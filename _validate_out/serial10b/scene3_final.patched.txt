The threshold loomed before me, the spiralgrass darkening at its edge where the sigil had burned its way into the ground. The Dohkar’s hand remained frozen mid-gesture, his eyes fixed on the silver lines that had bloomed across my palm. Seren knelt still, her coral hair falling forward, her eyes glinting with that mix of challenge and encouragement that had always defined her guidance. The air held the assembly’s stillness, a pressure that waited to release.I stepped forward, the threshold giving way beneath my bare feet, the sigil on my palm pulling at my attention with each throb before I could steady it. My voice trembled as I spoke, the words coming from a place I had not meant to reach.“I offer the memory of my first love, the one I lost.”The room shifted, the older woman near the veil-canopy taking a half-step back, her Weave-Script flickering across her throat. The Dohkar’s gaze sharpened, reading the alignment that had twisted itself around my words. The sigil burned brighter, a visible mark that could not be hidden.The space where the wish waited now carried the shape of the sacrifice named and the price claimed. To accept it was to lose the memory; to back away was to leave the wish unfulfilled, the room watching.The deck was warm in my hand. It was always warm.The card drew my fingers toward it, heavier now with the choice made.What if the sigil would simply fade, the Dohkar’s hand lowering as if nothing had happened? Petition Fate. That was what the card meant — small asks, small turnings. I gripped the deck tighter, the weight of the choice settling in. Just as I slid Petition Fate back in, a flash of red caught my eye.“Tempt Fate,” I muttered, the words tasting forbidden.“It’s a warning.”Just then, a crack echoed outside, jolting me back to the moment.“Tempt Fate.”Grandma never let me play with that one.

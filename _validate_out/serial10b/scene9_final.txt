The flickering lanterns along the chamber walls cast long, wavering shadows that the scarred oak table held flat against its grain. Their light caught the silver edges of the sigil still burning on my palm, throwing restless patterns over the unfurled scrolls, the flame pressing the silver into the parchment until the edges blurred.I kept my hand flat against the wood, the heat from the mark seeping into the grain. The assembly’s whispers had thinned to a low current, bare feet shifting on the cracked boards, yet none stepped forward to claim the name inscribed on the scroll at my knees. The Dohkar’s band-muffled mouth worked soundlessly. Seren remained still upon the floor, her coral hair veiling her face, her Weave-Script dim and steady.Julian stood at the edge of the circle, his long dark hair loose about his shoulders, the silver lines on his slate-blue skin catching the unsteady glow. He watched the sigil, not me, his hazel eyes deepening to green as his gaze flicked once to my lips before returning to the silver lines. The scar at the corner of his left eye held the light without wavering. His heel pressed once against the floorboard, the wood giving a short creak.“The rite is a massive prick about its rules,” he said, his voice low and edged with deliberate calm. The words settled between us, the low current of whispers parting around them. Was this a moment to lean into or to move past?The sigil’s light did not dim. It held steady, merciless, until the name on the scroll beneath it demanded attention. I felt my fingers rise without permission, twisting a strand of my hair once, the small motion betraying the unease I would not name. Julian’s gaze lingered on the gesture a fraction longer than necessary before he looked away.The whispers thickened again. Someone at the back edged toward the tapestry-draped alcove. Another leaned close to a companion, mouth shaping words too quiet to catch. Julian did not move. Breath left him through his nose in one controlled stream.I shifted my weight, the floorboard beneath my bare sole creaking, and the sigil’s heat pressed upward through the wood, the grain under my palm shifting as though the table leaned toward the edge. The scrolls lay scattered, edges curling in the uneven light. One lay half-hidden beneath the others, its corner folded as if deliberately tucked away. My fingers brushed the parchment. The motion was small, concealed by the angle of my body, yet the paper slid free with a soft rasp that seemed louder than the whispers.The letter inside bore no seal, only a single line in a hand I knew too well: the same slant Julian used when he recorded rites. The words were brief, almost casual, yet they named the sigil’s origin and the sacrifice it had demanded. My breath caught. The name on the scroll and the name in the letter aligned too cleanly to be mere chance.A muscle jumped once along the side of his neck. He had not seen me take the letter. Not yet. The choice formed in the space between us—speak the name aloud and force the confrontation, or keep the letter and unearth what else he had concealed. The sigil’s glow pulsed once, the light narrowing to a single point on the scroll as the room held its breath.The air between the table and the door thinned, carrying the residue of what had been spoken and what had not.In that moment, the sigil's glow flickered, revealing a hidden artifact beneath the table—the Arcane Binding, a shimmering chain of energy that pulsed with the weight of ancient magic. Its presence shifted the air, a reminder of the laws governing our actions and the power struggles entwined in our fates. As I grasped the letter tighter, the Arcane Binding seemed to resonate with my unease, urging me to confront the truth that lay between Julian and me.

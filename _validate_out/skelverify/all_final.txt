The Dohkar lifted his hands, and the clearing fell silent under the pale mated-pair trees. Seren knelt on the crimson spiralgrass, her aqua skin luminous with Weave-Script that showed only calm. She began the invocation the way we had rehearsed for months. Her voice was steady. “I offer my earliest memory of my mother’s voice.”

I relaxed. The words were exact. The alignment held. Then she looked straight at me. “That Lirael find the one she lost.”

The Weave-Script across her throat stuttered once. The Dohkar’s hands froze mid-gesture. Every First Favored in the clearing felt the sour note at the same moment. I knew why before anyone else could name it. She had taken my unresolved wound and made it the stage for her virtue, and the wording was too loose to bind cleanly. Julian stood at the edge of the gathering, perfectly still, long black hair curtaining his slate-blue face. He had come only to observe. Now his silver Weave-Script flared once at the wrists, and I felt the pull of that stillness the way I always did when he chose not to move. Seren finished speaking. The grass at her knees darkened in a perfect ring. A sound like fabric tearing moved through the canopy above us, though no wind stirred. The Dohkar’s mouth opened, but no words came. Then a guest near the back rose without being called. An older woman whose coral hair had gone almost white at the temples. She stepped forward, eyes fixed on me, and said the name I had never spoken aloud in this clearing. “Veyra.”

The one I lost. The name left her mouth like it had always belonged there. She looked at me as though she had just remembered something she was never meant to forget. The wish had found its literal path through someone already present. Seren gasped. Her Weave-Script dimmed from throat to sternum and stayed dark. The memory she had offered was gone; I saw it leave her face as the light faded. She reached for the name of her mother and found only shape. I tore the gossamer band from my mouth. The cloth came away without resistance, only the sharp sound of threads breaking. Protocol shattered in front of two dozen witnesses. Every eye turned toward me, and the inference was already forming. I didn't remember bringing it.

My hand tightened around my great-grandmother's old tarot deck. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.

The Dohkar’s eyes found mine the instant the seal tore. I stepped forward before the silence could harden into judgment, the grass cool and springy beneath my bare feet, and the words came because there was nowhere left for them to hide.

“I did not speak the wish,” I said, and my voice carried farther than I had meant it to. “She looked at me because she chose to. The wording was hers, not mine. I never asked her to carry what I have not been able to set down.”

The admission left a metallic taste on my tongue. I had expected the clearing to breathe again once the truth was spoken aloud, but the air only seemed to tighten. The older woman who had spoken Veyra’s name watched me with the fixed attention of someone who had just remembered a debt.

A faint, stubborn glow clung to the skin across my right palm and the inside of my wrist, the same luminous threads Seren’s Weave-Script had carried before it went dark. The light was not mine. It was the residue of a wish offered for me, and it would not fade while the assembly still stared. Every First Favored present could read the misalignment now, not as rumor but as a mark on living skin.

Julian had not moved from the edge of the gathering, yet the space between us felt smaller than it had a moment ago. His long black hair still curtained the slate-blue planes of his face, but I felt the weight of his stillness settle across my shoulders the way it always did when he chose to remain unmoved. He was testing something, the way he tested every claim that arrived too neatly.

The older woman spoke again, softer this time, almost kind.

“You asked for the one you lost. Fate gave you the name through another’s mouth. That is not the same thing as innocence.”

Julian’s voice cut across the grass, low and precise.

“Lirael. Did you know what she intended before she spoke?”

The question landed like a hand turning a ring, quiet and deliberate, the kind of motion that revealed more than the words themselves. He was not accusing. He was asking whether I had already decided how much of this I would claim.

I felt the pull of the answer before I had shaped it. To say no would keep the assembly’s assumption intact for a little longer. To say yes would make the stain on my hand into evidence rather than accident. The light on my skin pulsed once, as if the wish were still listening.

I realize that old tarot deck is still in my hand. Reassuringly solid. Present. One of the heavy gold cards slips out — “Petition Fate” is inscribed in it. If only the name could stay between us and not become proof. No. I would ask it to show me what Julian already sees when he looks at this mark and does not look away.

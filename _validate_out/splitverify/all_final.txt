The Dohkar’s voice cut across the clearing, low and steady. “Alignment holds?”

I could not answer. The gossamer band across my mouth turned every breath into a muffled drag. My fingers twitched against the ceremonial sash, the only motion the rite allowed. Seren knelt on the crimson spiralgrass, her aqua skin catching the dawn light that filtered through the veil-canopy. Her pale coral hair lay smooth against her shoulders. She looked composed, the way she always did when the practice runs had gone wrong. She began the invocation as we had rehearsed. The words left her mouth clean, the memory she offered named aloud for everyone to hear. The assembly breathed with her. I felt the alignment settle, true and single-pointed. Then she looked at me. Her eyes found mine across the kneeling space, and the rehearsed path fell away. “I offer this memory so that she finds the one she lost,” Seren said. “Take what you will.”

The spiralgrass darkened at the edges where her knees touched it. The change spread in a slow ring, the colour bleeding out of the braided blades. An old woman’s hands flew up. Someone whispered the proverb so softly it barely carried. “Never hand Fate a blank page.”

I felt the misalignment the instant the words finished. Seren had used my wound as her stage and left the target unnamed. The wish was already answering. A man at the edge of the gathering turned his head as if hearing his name. He spoke without meaning to. “The one she lost stands in this clearing still.”

The sentence landed like a stone dropped in still water. Every First Favored present felt the literal words take hold. The man blinked, startled at his own voice, and the assembly understood which word had bent. Seren’s Weave-Script stuttered and went dark along her throat. She reached for the next line of the rite and found nothing. The memory she had offered—the moment she chose this path—slipped from her. Her mouth stayed open, but the reason she had trained for months was simply gone. She knew she had given something. She could not name what. The Dohkar’s hand rose, palm out, but the motion came too late. The price had already been taken. I tore the gossamer band from my mouth. The cloth fluttered to the grass. Protocol shattered in front of two dozen witnesses. Every eye turned toward me, the sealed Sacrificiant who had moved anyway. The inference was already forming: I had manipulated the rite for my own gain. Julian’s name sat in my throat, the one I had lost and the one the wish had named without saying. He had waited for me to finish a sentence I never started. The warmth in my chest answered before I could stop it. The assembly waited. The Dohkar waited. Seren waited, her script still dark at the throat. The spiralgrass continued to lose its colour in a widening ring. I didn't remember bringing it.

My hand tightened around my great-grandmother's old tarot deck. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.

The Dohkar’s left foot shifted half an inch on the grass. I tasted the air without the band, raw and unfiltered, and heard the faint drag of a sandal across stone. The Weave-Script along my own throat flared once, a brief pulse of shame I could not hide.

“I moved because the wish had already twisted,” I said. My voice came out thin from disuse, but it carried. “Seren offered a memory without naming its target. The words she spoke were never meant for Fate to finish alone.”

A ripple passed through the assembly. Someone’s script flickered at the collarbone. The man who had spoken without meaning to stood rigid now, his mouth shut tight as if he feared it would open again.

I stepped forward, the crimson spiralgrass cool and slightly damp beneath my bare feet. A card edge nicked the side of my finger. “She looked at me. That was the moment the alignment broke. I tore the seal because the Dohkar’s hand was not fast enough. I thought stopping her words would be enough.”

My fingers flexed once around the deck I had not remembered drawing. The tremor began at the base of my thumb and traveled outward, small and uncontrollable, until the cards shifted against my palm. A single syllable from my last sentence seemed to catch in the air, repeating itself in the low hum of the grass. The binding had already begun to settle.

Julian’s scar at the corner of his left eye surfaced in my mind, the thin silver line that appeared only when he lowered his head in thought. He would have read the tremor for what it was. He always did.

The Dohkar lowered his hand. The old woman who had whispered the proverb pressed her lips together until they vanished. Seren remained on her knees, script still dark at her throat, eyes wide with the absence of the memory she had given away.

I could claim the interference now—say the words that would make every witness certain I had reached across the rite for my own lost name. Or I could let the silence finish the story they were already telling themselves.

The deck felt heavier than it had a moment before.

I realize that old tarot deck is still in my hand. Reassuringly solid. Present. One of the heavy gold cards slips out — “Petition Fate” is inscribed in it. If only the words would unbind themselves. No. I would ask Fate to show me whether Julian already knows the rest.

The Dohkar’s hand rose, palm open, and the circle stilled. Seren knelt on the crimson spiralgrass, her aqua skin already luminous with the first threads of Weave-Script. Her coral hair caught the light like a second flame. The gossamer band across my mouth tasted of old ritual and new fear; I could only watch. She began the invocation cleanly, every word the one we had practiced for months. I felt the alignment settle, steady and true. Relief loosened the knot under my ribs. Then Seren looked at me. Her voice changed. She named the memory she offered—her first sight of the veil-trees at dawn—and offered it without hesitation. The words that followed were not the ones we had rehearsed. “I wish that she find the one she lost.”The circle drew breath as one. The Dohkar’s hand trembled. Every First Favored present felt the misalignment the instant the sentence finished; the trained ones felt it sooner. I knew exactly where the wording had come from. I knew exactly how it would answer. Julian stood at the edge of the gathering, perfectly still, long black hair curtaining the left side of his face. His slate-blue skin showed silver Weave-Script moving in slow, deliberate loops. A dry leaf crackled under the toe of his boot. The wish took. A young man near the back of the circle—someone’s cousin, brought to witness—turned sharply toward the path that led out of the clearing. He had come to watch Seren become. Now he walked away without looking back, as though the words had already found their target and released him. The assembly understood at once which word had bent. I tore the gossamer band from my mouth. The cloth fluttered to the grass. Protocol broke with it. Every eye swung toward me. The Dohkar’s voice cut through the silence, low and final. “Interference.”The silver ring on his smallest finger tapped once against his thigh. The scar over his left wrist caught the light when his hand flexed once, then stilled again. I remembered the way his fingers had brushed mine the last time we stood this close—cool and deliberate, the small silver scar at the corner of his left eye visible only when he looked down. He had taught me to read alignment by watching what the body did before the words arrived. Now the lesson turned on me. The crowd’s murmurs rose. Someone whispered that a Sacrificiant had no right to reach for her own benefit during another’s rite. The accusation did not need to be spoken aloud; it was already written on every face. Seren remained kneeling, eyes wide, the coral of her hair trembling with the Weave-Script that now spelled regret in jagged, broken loops. I didn’t remember bringing it.My hand tightened around my great-grandmother's old tarot deck. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.

The words hung heavy in the air, charged with defiance and an edge of vulnerability I hadn't intended to reveal.The Dohkar's fingers drummed once against his knee, assessing the weight of my challenge. His eyes, lined with years of wisdom and shadows of past judgments, flickered with something I couldn't place — was it caution, curiosity, or disbelief? The crowd shifted, their attention a living tide that threatened to engulf me.Beside me, Seren remained kneeling, her coral hair trembling with the Weave-Script that now spelled out her regret in jagged, tumultuous loops. I could feel her fear, her shame, a potent echo of my own past mistakes. But I had drawn the line, and there was no retreating from it now.The assembly's whispers grew louder, a susurrus of doubt and speculation. It was the first crack in the facade of unity that had held them spellbound only moments before. And at the edge of this storm, Julian stood, his presence like the whisper of fate itself — inevitable, undeniable.His thumb rubbed once along the seam of his sleeve while he watched me, and for a moment, I saw the question beneath the calm. "Do you stand by her, even now?" it seemed to ask. I knew the answer was already in the air we breathed.The Dohkar's voice, layered with authority and something else — something old and rooted in the very fabric of our world — cut through the rising tension. "This is not how judgment is passed," he said, his words precise and measured.Yet, beneath his composed exterior, I sensed the tremor of a test. He was scrutinizing me, my actions, my resolve. He was the gatekeeper of a truth I had yet to unlock, a truth that loomed over us like the shadow of our past decisions.The weight of Julian's gaze remained, a silent challenge that felt both like a reprimand and an invitation. A test disguised as tenderness, a moment waiting to unfold into something more. I wanted to look away, to break the connection that felt too much like exposure, but I counted the slow rise of his chest instead, daring him to see me, to see through me.For a brief, disorienting moment, I wondered if I had imagined it all — the whisper of the grass, the sting of judgment, the eyes that measured and weighed. But the gravity of the situation held fast, rooting us all in its inexorable pull.A breath I hadn't realized I'd been holding escaped me, and with it, the resolve to see this through, no matter the cost. The judgment would come, but only after the truth had fully surfaced, and I knew I would have to stand by it, whatever it revealed.Just then, a figure stepped forward from the shadows, their presence commanding immediate attention.

[TITLE: "The Name He Carries"]
[SYNOPSIS: In the Thornwild waystation, an outsider's careless words cut at a Wildfolk man's standing while the woman who knows what those words truly cost feels the pull of what she cannot name. The room watches. One choice now decides whether she steps forward or lets the silence speak for her.]

[CHARACTERS: Lira:F, Thorne:M, Marek:M]

The common room stank of wet wool and burned root when Marek leaned across the bench and raised his voice.

“Careful with that one,” he told the keeper pouring blush. “Wildfolk carry the Field in their blood. Touch him wrong and you’ll wake up missing pieces of yourself.”

My fingers tightened around the edge of the table. Thorne sat beside me, still as a held breath, his hands folded in his lap the way they always were when he refused to let them move. I felt the heat of him through the space between us, the same heat that had once brushed my wrist when he passed me a cup and left the skin there tingling long after the cup was gone.

Marek laughed at his own joke. The sound scraped the low ceiling.

Thorne turned his head, slow, the way a man turns when he already knows what he will find. His eyes caught the firelight and held it wrong, too steady, too deep. I wondered what it cost him not to answer.

Marek kept talking. “They say the Keepers keep them leashed for a reason. One wrong word and the whole lot of them—”

“Enough.” My voice came out lower than I meant. The word landed between the benches like a dropped blade. Every head near us lifted.

Thorne’s hand, still on the bench between us, flexed once. The smallest motion. I remembered the night he had reached for me in the rain and stopped short, fingers hovering at my sleeve as if the air itself might burn. That same restraint lived in the line of his shoulders now.

Marek blinked at me, surprised. “Didn’t mean offense. Just common talk.”

Thorne rose. He did not look at Marek. He looked at me.

“Is this worth your voice?” he asked quietly.

The question sat between us, heavier than Marek’s laughter. I felt the room tilt toward the answer I had not yet given.

Is this a slight to correct — or a line I am already crossing for him?

Marek shifted, suddenly aware he had stepped into something larger than gossip. Thorne waited, the firelight moving across the faint luminous lines that traced his throat and disappeared beneath his collar. I had never asked what those lines meant when they brightened. I wanted to.

The keeper set down the jug. No one spoke.

My hand tightened around my great-grandmother’s old tarot deck. I didn’t remember bringing it. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.

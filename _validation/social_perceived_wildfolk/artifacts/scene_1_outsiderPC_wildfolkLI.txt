[TITLE: "The Weight of a Name"]
[SYNOPSIS: In the Thornwild waystation at dusk, an outsider's slight against a Wildfolk fractures the uneasy peace among travellers and locals. When restraint collapses, a wish is spoken and Fate claims its price in the same breath. The question is what the Field will take next from those who remain.]

The traveler's voice cut across the benches. "That Wildfolk you keep company with—he's already half-gone, isn't he? The Field's just waiting to finish the work."

Mira's hands stilled on the bread she was portioning. Several heads turned. The fire popped once, loud in the sudden quiet.

I set my cup down. "He walks the same paths we do. That makes him more than you are."

The traveler smiled without warmth. "Paths don't change what leaks out of him. You defend him because you haven't seen the hair move on its own yet."

Mira stood. Her shoulders squared, but the motion looked smaller than it should have. The other Wildfolk at the far bench shifted, eyes down.

The traveler kept going. "One day he'll ask you to look the other way while he takes what isn't his. That's how they end—smiling while they hollow you out."

Mira's mouth opened, closed. She reached toward the fire as if the heat might steady her, then stopped. Her fingers curled against her palm.

I stepped between them. "Say it again if you mean it."

The room held its breath. No one moved to stop him. No one moved to help.

Mira turned her face toward the rafters. Her eyes closed. For a moment she simply stood there, breathing, then her hands came together, palms pressed flat as if sealing something inside.

"Take the words from him," she said, voice steady. "Take whatever it costs."

The fire bent toward her. The air warmed against my skin, then cooled just as fast. Mira's shoulders jerked. When she opened her eyes again they were the same, but her next breath came without sound. She touched her throat once, then let her hand fall.

The traveler's mouth worked. Nothing came out. He made the shape of another insult and still nothing came out.

Mira looked at me. The look said she had known the price would be this and had spoken anyway.

My hand tightened around my great-grandmother's old tarot deck. I didn't remember bringing it. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.

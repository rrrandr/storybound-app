[TITLE: "Blood at the Waystation"]
[SYNOPSIS: At a Thornwild waystation, an outsider’s careless words about a Wildfolk woman draw every eye. The First Favored at her side answers before she can, and the cost of that defense begins to show itself in the dusk light.]

[CHARACTERS: Protagonist:F, Kael:M, Traveller:M]

The traveller’s voice cut across the benches. “That one’s Thornwild stock. Keep your coin close or she’ll take more than she’s owed.”

Kael’s hand found the edge of the scarred table between us. His eyes—full violet fields with four-pointed diamond pupils—never left the man, yet the weight of them landed on me first. I felt the shift in the room the way the river feels a stone dropped upstream.

I stood. “Say it plain if you mean it.”

The traveller smiled like he had won something. “No offense meant. Just that your kind carries the Field in your blood. We all know what happens when it wakes.”

Kael’s fingers tightened once on the wood. The motion was small, but the travellers nearest him leaned back without knowing why. “She carries what the rest of you fear to name,” he said. “And she has never asked for what was not freely given.”

Heat climbed my throat. The words were a shield, but they also drew the line sharper between us—him the composed Favored, me the one the room watched for signs of becoming. I wanted to tell him to stop speaking for me. I also wanted the room to keep looking at him instead of me.

A low murmur started at the far bench. Someone’s shadow lengthened across the floorboards, too long for the fire. The first flicker of the surge moved through the common room like a held breath that would not release.

Kael’s gaze flicked to mine. For a breath the air between us felt thinner, as if the same pressure pulling at the travellers had found a different purchase in the space we shared.

The traveller’s hand went to the knife at his belt, but the motion was uncertain now. Kael did not move closer. He simply stayed where he was, between the man and me, and the room watched to see which of us would speak next.

My hand tightened around my great-grandmother’s old tarot deck. I didn’t remember bringing it. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.

[TITLE: "The Waystation Slight"]
[SYNOPSIS: At a Thornwild waystation as dusk falls, an outsider's public slight against a Wildfolk companion pulls the First Favored protagonist into a charged exchange that shifts the room's balance and leaves her facing an immediate choice about how far to press the matter.]

The bench creaked under Harlan's weight as he leaned across the scarred plank table and pointed his cup at Thorne.

"Never seen one of your kind sit so still through a whole turning," Harlan said, loud enough that the fire's crackle could not swallow it. "Most of you twitch before the light fades."

Thorne's hands stayed folded on the table, the fine hair at his wrist moving once against the grain of the wood. He did not answer.

I set my own cup down harder than I meant to. The sound drew a few glances from the travellers on the far bench.

Harlan kept his eyes on Thorne. "Must cost you something, holding it in like that. Or does the Field already own the worst of it?"

The murmurs along the benches thinned to nothing.

Thorne lifted his head. His voice came low and even. "The Field owns nothing until it is paid for."

Harlan smiled without warmth. "Then you are still in debt. Useful to know."

I stood. The movement drew every eye in the room to me instead.

"You have mistaken silence for permission," I said.

Harlan's cup paused halfway to his mouth. "And you have mistaken my curiosity for insult."

"No," I answered. "I have not."

Thorne's fingers uncurled against the table. The hair at his wrist stilled.

Harlan set the cup down. The bench scraped as he shifted back half a handspan, but he did not rise.

The fire popped once. No one else spoke.

My hand tightened around my great-grandmother's old tarot deck. I didn't remember bringing it. I toyed with the idea of letting the cards decide.

The outsider traveller by the fire leaned forward on his bench, voice carrying over the packed common room. “That Thornbred beside you—tell it to keep its hands where we can see them, or the hall outside will finish what the moons started.”

The words landed like a thrown stone. My companion’s shoulders tightened, the faint shift of his coral-dark hair catching the firelight as he stayed silent. Every eye in the waystation turned. Benches scraped. The cursed betrothal hall loomed just beyond the shuttered windows, its iron-bound doors sealed generations ago by an oath no one dared repeat. Its shadow stretched across the yard and into this room, turning every stranger’s glance into a judgment.

I stepped between them, one hand brushing the heavy gold edge of the deck inside my satchel. The worn corners had grown smooth under my thumb across years of nights like this. “He travels with me,” I said, loud enough to reach the back benches. “That’s all you need to know.”

A low mutter ran through the travellers. The man at the fire did not rise, but his stare held. “We’ve seen what Thornbred company brings. The hall remembers. So do we.”

My companion’s fingers flexed once at his side, a small, controlled motion that said he would not answer. I felt the old pull low in my chest—the one that had lived there since I was small enough to hide under a table while voices decided whether I belonged. The fear had a name I had never spoken aloud: that every bond I tried to keep would eventually turn and walk away, leaving me the one still standing in the cold.

The room waited. The fire popped. Outside, the twin moons hung low over the hall’s broken spires, their light thin and watchful.

I could have answered the traveller. I could have told the room what the hall’s sealed doors had cost my own line. Instead I turned my shoulder, guiding my companion toward the far bench where the shadows ran deepest. The choice sat between my teeth like iron: speak the rest of it here, where every ear could claim it, or keep the silence that had protected me this long.

The traveller’s gaze followed us. The hall’s shadow did not move.

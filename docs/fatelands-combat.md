# Fatelands — Species-Native Combat (Bible Addendum)

> Canon reference. Code: `_KWISHEEN_COMBAT` / `_FIRST_FAVORED_COMBAT` + `_buildFatelandsCombatDirective()`
> in `public/app.js`, injected into the CG author prompt (self-gates on a Kwisheen/First-Favored being
> present **and** a combat signal in the scene's crisis — so it only fires for fights). $0: `_fatelands_combat.js`.

Kwisheen and First Favored do not receive generic swordplay; each style grows from the species' nature.
They are natural opposites: **one tells you what it wants and becomes it; the other shows you only the
limbs it permits.**

## Kwisheen — The Many-Tide Method
Multi-limbed, asymmetrical, hard for a human to read. In one instant: one tentacle threatens, one
redirects, one anchors, one hides the real attack, others guard the center / control terrain.

- **Kit:** a long **spear/trident** (reach, pinning, space), a curved **cutlass** (close cutting, hooking
  limbs/weapons), a long **dagger** (the concealed killing thrust once the enemy is entangled) — optionally
  a shell-plate buckler on a fourth limb, or a weighted net/line for hunters.
- **Silhouette:** one long weapon, one cutting weapon, one stabbing weapon, remaining limbs controlling
  body, enemy, terrain, defense.
- Good at: hooking ankles/wrists/weapon-guards, anchoring against impact, changing height without losing
  balance, shielding organs with folded tentacles, attacking around shields, grapple → multi-directional
  restraint. The visible spear thrust is usually a **feint** — the real attack is the dagger under the ribs.
- **NO RANGED WEAPONS** — a Kwisheen **never** uses a bow or sling (little travels far through water). Their
  whole art is reach, control, and entanglement.
- **Passing as human:** while disguised they suppress all of this and mimic the local human style —
  competent but slightly *too studied*. Tells: balance too stable, an arm recovering too fast, reluctance
  to bare the torso, footwork that assumes more contact points than a human has.
- **Breaking the Mask** (the death-break): only when death is likely may a trained Kwisheen drop the human
  act instantly — rear-angle attacks, simultaneous parry+thrust, concealed weapons, ankle/throat grapples,
  a balance that is no longer a biped's. It is the **abandonment of concealment**, not a berserker rage.
  Doing it for a *minor* fight is shameful/politically reckless. (Alt names: Showing the Deep, the Death-Tide.)

## First Favored — The Unveiled Hand
Dangerous when **desire, declaration, and action are perfectly aligned.** They *name* the aim ("I am going
to take the sword from your hand") and commit the whole body to it. Declared intent sharpens precision;
deception or divided motive makes them worse. (Not a magical command — a discipline of Alignment.)

- **Weapons:** the **Vowstaff** (long flexible staff — hooked crescent blade for lethal work at one end,
  weighted cap for restraint at the other, grip carved with the wielder's vows; the two ends embody the
  choice between *controlling* and *killing*) and the **Open Blade** (broad forward-curved single edge,
  visible cutting line, no hidden edges — *"the edge should not lie about where it intends to go"* — with a
  small basal hook for trapping/disarming). **They also use slings and bows for distance** (unlike Kwisheen).
- **Three stages:** **Naming** (state the want, the limit, what ends it) → **Alignment** (stance/technique
  match — a guard holds ground, a captor attacks hands/knees/breath, a killer performs no false mercy) →
  **Revelation** (the fight exposes whether the declared desire was *true*; the fighter may discover they
  want vengeance not justice, or cannot actually kill — and must **redeclare** honestly or become misaligned).
- Favor: direct lines, distance control, deliberate targeting, weapon-trapping, disarming, forcing choices
  over flurries. Poor at: feigned cowardice, disguised weapons, prolonged deception, concealed-purpose causes.
- A **feint** is fine when it openly serves the declared aim (tactical misdirection ≠ self-deception): a high
  strike to open the leg is honest; "I mean no harm" while seeking to maim is not.
- **Public duels:** both may formally declare outcome / limits / what ends the fight; breaking a declared
  limit carries enormous cultural weight even in victory.

## First Favored vs Kwisheen
A First Favored fights to force the Kwisheen's real objective into the open; the Kwisheen exploits the gap
between declared intent and visible capability. Neither is automatically superior.
